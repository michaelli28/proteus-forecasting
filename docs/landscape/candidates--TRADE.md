# Candidate Catalog — Lens: Analytic Tradecraft, Quality Assurance, and the Meta Layer

**Lens key: `TRADECRAFT`.** This lens maps most directly onto the customer's stated benefit (c) — "improve quality assurance — identify discrepancies between sources and reconcile differences in overall analytic confidence" — and benefit (d), automating repetitive analysis. Nine serious candidates plus one deliberately-labeled bad one.

**A note on the two knobs.** The prototype's actor profile exposes `riskTolerance` and `escalationPropensity`. In the tradecraft lens these are re-bound (the config is knob-generic; only the names are geopolitical) to `credulity` — how much evidence a simulant demands before accepting a claim — and `commitmentPropensity` — how readily a simulant collapses ambiguity into a single answer instead of preserving it. Where that re-binding is a stretch I say so in the candidate.

### C-TRADECRAFT-1 — Source Conflict Adjudicator (SCA): reconciliation as adversarial meta-analysis

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** When twelve reports give you nine different numbers for the same thing, this decides *why* they disagree — different yardstick, different date, different definition, or a real fight — and when it's a real fight it lowers your confidence instead of quietly picking a winner.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Reporting places Iran's stockpile of 60% enriched uranium between 120 kg and 480 kg as of 1 August. Is that spread a measurement/date/definition artifact, or a genuine dispute — and what is the defensible posterior interval and confidence level I should publish?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA DA / DI substantive analysts and their branch-level reviewers; NCPC and the nonproliferation NIO staff; DIA's regional centers. **Today:** an analyst opens six PDFs in six tabs, builds a spreadsheet by hand, notices (or doesn't) that two figures are UF₆ mass and two are U mass, resolves the rest by picking the source they trust most, and writes a footnote. The reconciliation logic lives in the analyst's head and dies when they rotate. There is no artifact recording *why* the outlier was discarded.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct **reconciliation world** — a hypothesis about the joint generative process behind the reporting. State variables per run: the latent quantity trajectory θ(t) (random-walk or drift-specified), a per-source bias vector b_s, a per-source precision σ_s, a definitional-transform selector d (which unit/scope map is in force), and an as-of alignment offset. Knobs: `credulity` sets the prior on σ_s (low credulity = fat-tailed source noise priors, so outliers are absorbed rather than believed) and `commitmentPropensity` sets the Bayes-factor threshold at which the run is willing to declare one reconciliation model the winner rather than reporting a mixture. This varies a great deal run to run — the same corpus produces a unimodal 210±40 kg answer under a high-credulity date-alignment world and a genuinely bimodal answer under a low-credulity world, and *that divergence is the product*.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Hierarchical Bayesian measurement model + Bayesian model selection**, which is formally the random-effects meta-analysis problem with adversarial sources. Each report v_i ~ N(f(θ, t_i, d_i) + b_{s_i}, σ²_{s_i}), where f applies a date-shift along θ(t) and a definitional transform matrix. Four competing generative models are fit and compared by marginal likelihood / Bayes factors: **M1 different-measurement** (single θ, spread absorbed by source noise), **M2 different-date** (residuals collapse once date-aligned along θ(t)), **M3 different-definition** (residuals collapse after the unit/scope map), **M4 genuine dispute** (no alignment collapses the spread; posterior is a well-separated mixture). Supporting statistics, all closed-form and seed-deterministic: Cochran's Q and I² heterogeneity, DerSimonian–Laird τ², Hartigan's dip test for posterior multimodality, and the minimal set of reports whose removal would flip the model selection (a leave-k-out sensitivity sweep). Under M4 the platform's existing weakest-link confidence rule fires: the product carries a mixture and a *lower* ICD 203 confidence, and there is structurally no code path that reduces the mixture to a point estimate.
- **What the judge panel is:** Five judges, auto-composed. (1) **ACH judge** — Heuer diagnosticity-weighted matrix over the four reconciliation models, ranking by weighted inconsistency ascending. (2) **Key-assumptions judge** — scores each assumed definitional map and each assumed source-independence claim, and requires a falsifier for each. (3) **Devil's advocate** — argues the runner-up reconciliation model and scores how much evidence would be needed to flip it. (4) **Red team with standing** — may force a bounded rerun if it can show a definitional map was applied without a documented conversion basis. (5) **Sourcing judge** — ICD 206-style scoring of each report's provenance depth. Rubric axes: *diagnosticity*, *definitional rigor*, *residual heterogeneity explained*, *confidence-propagation correctness*, *dissent preservation*.
- **The config pack:** `actors/reconciliation-profiles.yaml` (credulity × commitment grid), `domain/nuclear-material-accounting.adapter.ts` (unit ontology: UF₆ vs U mass vs SQ-equivalent vs SWU), `rubric/source-conflict.rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/bayes-factor-bands.yaml` (BF thresholds mapped to ICD 203 confidence levels), `judges/panel-sca.yaml`, `oracle/synthetic-conflict-generator.yaml`.
- **Data required:** A credible **unclassified demo is straightforward**. Named sources: IAEA GOV/ series quarterly Iran verification reports; ISIS (Institute for Science and International Security) analyses; EIA and IEA/OPEC MOMR crude production figures (a superb testbed — the same month's Russian output differs by 300+ kb/d across IEA, OPEC secondary sources, and OPEC direct communication, and later revisions supply ground truth); UN COMTRADE mirror-statistics discrepancies; Mediazona/BBC vs UK MoD vs Russian MoD casualty counts. Classified access adds HUMINT source-reliability ratings and collateral SIGINT/IMINT, which mostly improve b_s and σ_s priors rather than changing the kernel.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** Three-tier, and all three are real. **(i) Synthetic oracle** — generate θ, generate reports with a *known* injected conflict type, measure four-way classification precision/recall and posterior calibration (ECE) against the known θ. This is the regime the prototype already runs. **(ii) Historical backtest with as-of cutoff** — commodity and macro series where the initial spread later resolved to a revised true value: OPEC/IEA monthly crude production for Russia 2022–2024 (revisions published), US BLS payroll first-print vs final benchmark revision (a clean, high-N, date-stamped discrepancy corpus), IAEA declared-vs-verified inventory changes. **(iii) Live post-cutoff** — pre-register a reconciliation on a quantity resolving within 90 days (next IAEA quarterly, next OPEC MOMR revision) and score the interval. Not unscorable; arguably the most scorable candidate in this catalog.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(c) primary** — this *is* "identify discrepancies between sources and reconcile differences in overall analytic confidence," and the mechanism is a Bayes factor plus a weakest-link confidence rule rather than an assertion. **(a)** accuracy: the leave-k-out sensitivity sweep names the single report the estimate is hostage to, which no manual process produces. **(b)** time: replaces a multi-hour manual spreadsheet reconciliation per question. **(d)** cost: the definitional-transform ontology is written once per domain and reused across every question in it.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government program — **DARPA AIDA (Active Interpretation of Disparate Alternatives)**, explicitly a "multihypothesis semantic engine" for "noisy, conflicting, and potentially deceptive" sources, Phase 1 scenario Russia–Ukraine, [now complete](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/active-interpretation-of-disparate-alternatives); LDC released the AIDA annotation corpora ([LDC2023T10](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2023T10), and a [Russia–Ukraine informational-conflict annotation set](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.195/)) — a *ready-made evaluation corpus for this exact candidate*. Also **IARPA REASON** (PM Dr. Steven Rieber; Phase 1 24 months, Phase 2 18 months), which points analysts to overlooked evidence and critiques draft reasoning ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2023/01/06/iarpa-to-develop-novel-ai-that-automatically-generates-tips-to-improve-intel-reports/), [IARPA](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/reason)). Closest commercial — **Primer** (knowledge discovery over classified repositories; its RAG-V "near-zero hallucination" claim is a *vendor claim*, [ExecutiveBiz](https://executivebiz.com/2024/10/primer-technologies-ai-platform/)) and **Recorded Future**. **The differentiator is that none of them classify the *type* of conflict or propagate an unresolved conflict into a lowered confidence level — they surface conflict and stop.** AIDA generated alternatives; it did not do adversarial meta-analysis with confidence propagation.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* the definitional ontology is the whole ballgame — a missing unit map makes a definitional artifact look like a genuine dispute (fails toward false abstention, which is the safe direction, but degrades usefulness). Source-independence is assumed by the likelihood; if sources are circularly derived, σ_s is overstated and the posterior is falsely tight — **this candidate is unsound without C-TRADECRAFT-2 running upstream, and that dependency should be enforced in the pipeline, not documented in a README.** Heavy-tailed real-world source error breaks Gaussian conjugacy; use Student-t likelihoods. *Red lines:* if the corpus contains US-persons reporting, the domain adapter must refuse to load absent a screened-corpus flag — the same hard-refusal pattern the codebase already uses to refuse tracing a run whose domain the projector cannot read. Enforce at the type level: the report record type carries no free-text person-identifier field.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 3 now** (kernel is new code; the surrounding fan-out, calibration, abstention and metrics layers exist and are tested). **TRL 5–6 in 12 months** — the OPEC/IEA and BLS-revision backtests are runnable today and would constitute validation in a relevant environment.

### C-TRADECRAFT-2 — Effective-N: circular reporting and source-independence auditor

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Counts how many *genuinely independent* sources are behind a claim, as opposed to how many documents mention it, and tells you what your confidence should have been if you had known.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Fourteen separate reports assert that Group X pre-positioned access in a US water utility OT network. What is the effective number of independent sources — and if it is fewer than five, what does the corrected posterior on that claim become?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA Open Source Enterprise and DNI Open Source Center analysts; source-validation and asset-validation staffs; DIA's source registries; NGA's commercial-imagery-plus-social-corroboration workflows. **Today:** independence is asserted by memory and by whether the reports came in on different channels. The canonical failures are documented: a single fabricator, "Curveball," supplied [98 percent of the assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_reporting) on Iraqi mobile BW, and the Niger yellowcake forgeries circulated among agencies each citing the others. There is no routine, tooled independence check anywhere in the production line.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a **provenance-topology hypothesis**: how many independent origins k seeded the observed corpus, and along which edges the claim propagated. State variables: seed-set size k, per-edge transmission probability, adoption timestamps, paraphrase-distance decay, and a latent "translation/laundering hop" indicator. Knobs: `credulity` governs the paraphrase-distance threshold at which two documents are deemed to share an origin (strict = many origins, generous = few), and `commitmentPropensity` governs whether a run commits to a single cascade tree or keeps a posterior over forests. Meaningful variation: the same 14-document corpus yields N_eff ≈ 9.1 under a strict simulant and N_eff ≈ 1.8 under a generous one; the spread *is* the honest answer.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Network cascade / independent-cascade diffusion on a provenance DAG, plus survey-sampling design-effect math.** Build the DAG from three edge types: explicit citation, near-duplicate passage reuse (MinHash/SimHash shingling with longest-common-substring confirmation, deterministic given a fixed hash seed), and strict temporal precedence from first-observed timestamps. Fit an independent-cascade model to the timestamped adoption sequence by maximum likelihood; the fitted seed-set size k̂ is the estimated number of true origins, and k̂ = 1 is circular reporting. Compute mean pairwise dependence ρ̄ from shared-phrase and shared-citation overlap, then the **effective sample size N_eff = N / (1 + (N−1)ρ̄)** — the classic design-effect formula. Re-run the Bayesian update with N_eff independent likelihood ratios instead of N, and emit the **confidence delta** as the headline number: *"you believed 14 sources; you have 2.3; posterior odds fall from 61:1 to 4:1."* Also: Tarjan's algorithm over the citation graph to surface literal strongly-connected components (A cites B cites A), and a "patient zero" report naming the earliest document and its archive timestamp.
- **What the judge panel is:** (1) **Sourcing/ICD 206 judge** — scores whether each document's provenance chain terminates in an original observation or in another document. (2) **Devil's advocate** — argues that apparent reuse is convergent independent reporting (real phenomenon: wire-service phrasing, common translations of the same primary-language original) and scores how well the evidence excludes it. (3) **Deception judge** — scores whether the cascade pattern is consistent with deliberate laundering versus sloppy aggregation. (4) **Premortem** — "this independence assessment was wrong; why?" (5) **Key-assumptions judge** on the paraphrase-distance threshold, with a required falsifier. Rubric axes: *origin-recovery correctness*, *convergence-vs-laundering discrimination*, *timestamp integrity*, *confidence-delta defensibility*.
- **The config pack:** `domain/news-provenance.adapter.ts` (ingest + timestamp normalization + language-hop handling), `actors/independence-profiles.yaml`, `rubric/circularity.rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/paraphrase-distance.yaml`, `thresholds/neff-to-confidence.yaml`, `judges/panel-provenance.yaml`, `oracle/laundering-injector.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Fully unclassified demo — this is the strongest OSINT-only candidate in the catalog.** Named sources: GDELT Global Knowledge Graph (event + tone + source URL, updated every 15 minutes), Media Cloud, Common Crawl, the Internet Archive Wayback CDX API (authoritative first-observed timestamps), Wikipedia revision history (a gold-standard laundering corpus — claims enter Wikipedia, get cited by press, then get re-cited into Wikipedia), and the AIDA Russia–Ukraine informational-conflict corpus. Classified access adds HUMINT report serialization and dissemination metadata, which materially improves origin recovery for non-open sources — but the method is proven unclassified first.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **(i) Synthetic oracle, exact** — inject a single origin, launder it through a generated network with known topology, measure recovery of k and error in N̂_eff versus the true N_eff. Ground truth is constructed, so no model can have memorized it (the platform's existing closed-world leakage guard applies directly). **(ii) Historical backtest** — documented circular-reporting episodes with as-of cutoffs: the Niger yellowcake forgery propagation (2001–2004), Curveball's dissemination chain, and non-intelligence controls where the loop is provably documented — the "Brazilian aardvark" coati hoax and the Wikipedia-sourced false middle name of Karl-Theodor zu Guttenberg (both [documented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_reporting)), which are perfect low-stakes labeled positives. **(iii) Live post-cutoff** — pre-register on a breaking claim and resolve against Wayback first-appearance timestamps within days.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(c) primary and hardest-hitting** — corroboration is the single largest silent inflator of analytic confidence in the IC, and this converts it from a judgment call into a computed number with a stated confidence delta. **(a)** accuracy: prevents the specific failure mode that produced the highest-profile analytic failure in modern IC history. **(b)** time: independence audit of a 200-document corpus in minutes. **(d)** cost: this is precisely a repetitive task no human does at scale because it is tedious, not because it is hard.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — **IARPA has issued an RFI for credibility-assessment datasets**, noting that assessing a source's credibility is "a core challenge for a broad range of intelligence, defense, homeland security, and law enforcement applications" ([ICN](https://intelligencecommunitynews.com/iarpa-posts-rfi-for-credibility-assessment-datasets/)); **DARPA INCAS** builds tools to "detect, characterize, and track geopolitical influence campaigns with quantified confidence," performers SIFT, Protagonist, USC ISI, UIUC, Uncharted, with T&E by Lockheed ATL and UMD ARLIS ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/influence-campaign-awareness-and-sensemaking), [ICN](https://intelligencecommunitynews.com/darpa-announces-incas-program-participants/)); **DARPA SemaFor** for media provenance. Commercial — **Graphika** (network-cascade analysis of coordinated information operations), **NewsGuard**, **Blackbird.AI**, **Alethea** (all UNVERIFIED as to current federal contract vehicles). **The differentiator: INCAS and Graphika detect coordinated *campaigns*; none of them output an effective-N and a corrected posterior on a specific analytic judgment.** The output here is a confidence correction, not a network picture.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* paraphrase-distance is defeated by good translation and by genuine independent reporting of the same press conference (false circularity); timestamp spoofing and undated republication corrupt the cascade fit; the design-effect formula assumes exchangeable pairwise dependence, which is wrong for star-topology cascades — use the full graph Laplacian in those cases and say so. Failure direction is toward over-declaring circularity, which suppresses real corroboration — that is *not* a safe direction, and must be reported as a measured false-circularity rate, not assumed away. *Red lines:* the same machinery, pointed at domestic media, is a press-network mapping tool. Structural enforcement: the domain adapter refuses corpora not tagged foreign-origin/US-persons-screened; the output schema has no field for author identity — nodes are documents, and there is no person node type in the graph model, so "who wrote this" cannot be an output.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 3 now** (cascade kernel and shingling are new; GDELT/Wayback ingest is straightforward). **TRL 5 in 12 months**, with the coati/Guttenberg/Niger labeled-positive backtest as the validation evidence.

### C-TRADECRAFT-3 — Red Mirror: deception-hypothesis consistency testing

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Works out what the adversary would most want us to believe, computes what evidence they would show us to get us there, and then tells us how much of our current evidence base is exactly that.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Our estimate that Iran's cyber posture toward US water and wastewater OT is *reconnaissance, not pre-positioning for effect* rests on an observed absence of destructive tooling. Is the observed evidence better explained by (a) the honest world, or (b) a world in which Tehran is running a signaling strategy designed to produce exactly that conclusion — and which three of our observables are cheapest for them to fake?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA's counter-denial-and-deception cell and its equivalents; DIA's D&D analysts; NIC-level estimative drafters; CYBERCOM and the water-sector Sector Risk Management Agency's intelligence support. **Today:** deception is handled by an experienced analyst asking "what if this is a setup?" in a review meeting, informally, and it depends entirely on whether a D&D-literate person is in the room. There is no artifact, no scoring, and no record of which evidence was deemed fakeable.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run instantiates an **adversary objective and a signaling scheme**. State variables: adversary type τ ∈ {benign, preparing-for-effect, deterring-by-ambiguity, deceiving-a-third-party}; the target belief distribution the adversary wants us to hold; a per-observable production cost vector c(o, τ) that *differs by type*; a detection-risk term (what it costs them if we catch the deception); and a time budget. Knobs: `riskTolerance` maps naturally here — how much detection risk the adversary accepts to run a bolder deception; `escalationPropensity` maps to how far from the truth the adversary is willing to push the induced belief. **This is the one candidate where the prototype's original knob names are literally correct, unmodified.** Variation is large and substantive: the deceiving-a-third-party simulant (Saddam 1998–2003, deceiving Iran and his own generals rather than the US) produces a completely different predicted evidence signature than the deterring-by-ambiguity simulant.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Game-theoretic — a sender–receiver signaling game solved as Bayesian persuasion (Kamenica–Gentzkow).** For each adversary type, solve for the signaling scheme that maximizes their expected payoff given our Bayesian best response — this computes, as an optimization rather than a guess, "what would they show us." Then compute the **counterdeception likelihood ratio** LR = P(observed evidence | honest world) / P(observed evidence | optimal persuasion scheme), and decompose it per-observable so each piece of evidence is ranked by its contribution. A separate **costly-signaling separation analysis** partitions observables into *cheap talk* (both types can produce it at similar cost — evidentially worthless against deception) and *separating signals* (the honest type can afford it and the deceiving type cannot, or vice versa). Bennett and Waltz's core insight — that denial is a necessary component of every deception and must be analyzed as such — is operationalized as an explicit **absence model**: for each thing we did *not* observe, compute P(non-observation | honest) vs P(non-observation | denial effort), which converts "we saw no destructive tooling" from evidence of absence into a scored likelihood ratio ([Bennett & Waltz, *Counterdeception Principles and Applications for National Security*, Artech House 2007](https://www.amazon.com/Counterdeception-Principles-Applications-National-Security/dp/1580539351)).
- **What the judge panel is:** (1) **ACH judge** over {honest, deception-type-1..k}, diagnosticity-weighted, with the crucial property that evidence classified as cheap talk receives *zero* diagnosticity weight — the algorithm structurally cannot be persuaded by fakeable evidence. (2) **Devil's advocate** — argues the paranoid inverse: that the deception hypothesis is itself the adversary's desired conclusion (deception-about-deception), and scores whether the evidence base can distinguish the two. (3) **Key-assumptions judge** on the adversary's assumed payoff function, with a required falsifier. (4) **Premortem** — "we called this deception and it was honest; what did that cost?" (5) **Red team with standing to force a bounded rerun** if it can name an unmodeled adversary objective. Rubric axes: *cheap-talk identification*, *absence-model rigor*, *payoff-function defensibility*, *symmetry* (does the analysis apply equal skepticism to evidence supporting each side).
- **The config pack:** `actors/adversary-objectives.yaml` (per-country payoff templates), `domain/cyber-otsector.adapter.ts` (observable taxonomy: IOC classes, tooling categories, infrastructure leases, tradecraft signatures), `costs/observable-production-costs.yaml` (the load-bearing file — per-observable, per-type production and denial costs), `rubric/counterdeception.rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/lr-to-confidence.yaml`, `judges/panel-redmirror.yaml`.
- **Data required:** The observable taxonomy and cost model can be built unclassified from CISA/FBI joint advisories, MITRE ATT&CK technique cost proxies, Mandiant/CrowdStrike/Dragos threat reports, and Recorded Future infrastructure reporting. **A credible unclassified demo is possible but weaker than for C-1 and C-2**, because the sharpest cost asymmetries (what it actually costs a service to fabricate a given signature) are themselves classified. Note: the widely reported IRGC-affiliated "CyberAv3ngers" activity against Unitronics PLCs at US water utilities in late 2023 would be the natural anchoring case — **UNVERIFIED in this session** (search budget exhausted before I could confirm the CISA advisory number and dates); it must be verified against the primary CISA advisory before appearing in any proposal.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **(i) Historical backtest against documented, declassified deception episodes with hard as-of cutoffs** — Operation Fortitude / D-Day (fully declassified, with the German assessment record as the receiver-side ground truth); the Egyptian deception preceding October 1973; India's Pokhran-II concealment in May 1998 (a documented US warning failure with a published record); Russian force posture and the "exercise" cover story from November 2021 to February 2022, and the announced "withdrawals" of February 2022. Score: did the system's LR exceed threshold *before* the cutoff date? **(ii) Synthetic closed worlds** — the strongest oracle here, and it requires C-TRADECRAFT-9: run a simulated adversary that literally executes a deception policy inside a generated world, and measure detection rate, false-deception-alarm rate, and lead time against constructed ground truth that provably did not exist before the run. **(iii) Live post-cutoff** — weak; deception rarely resolves within a scoreable horizon. **Honest flag: this candidate has the worst live-resolution story in the catalog.** Its scoring case rests on (i) and (ii), and the proposal must say so rather than implying live measurability.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy, via a mechanism no incumbent has: down-weighting evidence by its fakeability. **(c)** QA — the "your estimate rests hardest on the three observables cheapest to fake" output is a quality-assurance finding of a kind that is currently produced by nobody, systematically. **(b)** time — the D&D review that today requires convening the right senior person becomes a standing automated pass on every product. **(d)** partial — the observable-cost model is a reusable asset across all questions in a domain.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — the CIA/DIA counter-D&D discipline itself, and the **Foreign Denial and Deception Committee** under ODNI (UNVERIFIED as to current name/status); no named acquisition program I could confirm is building automated counterdeception. **DARPA AIDA** is the closest funded effort, explicitly scoped to "potentially deceptive" information environments, but AIDA generated alternative interpretations rather than modeling the adversary's optimal signaling scheme, and is [complete](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/active-interpretation-of-disparate-alternatives). Commercial — nothing close. **Rhombus Power's Guardian** does adversary anticipation (awarded [up to $200M by the Air Force in September 2025, running through September 2030](https://theaiinsider.tech/2025/09/27/rhombus-power-snags-200-million-us-air-force-contract-for-ai-powered-strategic-decision-making-platform/)) but is a predictive-analytics platform, not a counterdeception engine. **This is the most defensible white space in the catalog and the least served by incumbents.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* the payoff function is elicited, not measured, and a wrong payoff function produces a confident wrong deception verdict — mitigate by making payoff a *simulant-varying* parameter rather than a fixed input, so the fan-out shows sensitivity rather than hiding it. Deception-about-deception is a genuine infinite regress; cap it at two levels and report the cap as a stated limitation. The gravest failure is **paranoid collapse**: a system that can explain any evidence as deception explains nothing — enforce structurally by requiring the honest hypothesis to remain in the ACH matrix with non-zero prior and by measuring the false-deception-alarm rate on known-honest synthetic arms, publishing it alongside the detection rate. *Red lines:* this models a foreign adversary's information strategy. It must not be pointed at domestic political actors or US persons — enforced by an actor-model schema in which the actor type is a foreign state or state-affiliated organization and there is no natural-person actor type to configure. It also must not become a deception *planning* tool: the output schema emits likelihood ratios and collection recommendations only; there is no "recommended signal to emit" output type, and adding one would be a schema change, visible in review, not a prompt change.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 2 now** — the persuasion solver and cost model are unbuilt, and the observable-cost elicitation is genuinely hard. **TRL 4 in 12 months**, validated in the synthetic closed world plus one declassified historical episode. This is the highest-risk, highest-differentiation candidate; propose it as a Phase II option, not a Phase I deliverable.

### C-TRADECRAFT-4 — Tradecraft Auditor: ICD 203 compliance, estimative-language coherence, and the Dutch-book detector

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Reads a draft assessment and finds the places where its own stated probabilities contradict each other, where a judgment has no source behind it, and where likelihood and confidence have been quietly mashed into one number — then scores it on the same rubric a human reviewer would use.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Does this draft meet the nine ICD 203 tradecraft standards, and specifically: are the seven estimative statements in it mutually consistent with *any* coherent probability distribution — and if not, which minimal subset is contradictory?"
- **Mission and user:** ODNI's Analytic Integrity and Standards staff and the Analytic Ombuds; every IC element's internal product-evaluation program (IC elements are required to conduct internal review using the IC Analytic Standards and report annually to ODNI, per [ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)); branch chiefs and DI review boards; the Sherman Kent School. **Today:** a branch chief reads the draft and applies the standards from memory; ODNI-level evaluations are a sampled, retrospective, labor-intensive human review of a small fraction of annual production. Nobody checks whether the probabilities in a single document are mutually coherent, because doing so by hand is tedious and requires the reviewer to hold seven propositions in working memory simultaneously.
- **What the N simulants are:** Honest answer — **the world does not vary here; the *reader* does.** Each of the N runs is a distinct **reviewer profile**: severity prior, standard-weighting vector across the nine tradecraft standards, and interpretation of ambiguous estimative language. Knobs: `credulity` = how much benefit of the doubt a reviewer extends to an unsourced-but-plausible assertion; `commitmentPropensity` = how readily the reviewer converts a concern into a failing score rather than a comment. This is a weaker use of the fan-out than C-1 or C-3 — but it is *exactly* the use the calibration layer was built for, since reviewer severity bias is the dominant error term in human tradecraft scoring, and the z-score normalization plus Krippendorff's α layer strips it. Stated plainly: the value here is in the calibration layer and the kernel, not in world-divergence.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** Two components, both exact. **(i) Probabilistic coherence checking — a linear-programming Dutch-book detector.** Parse each estimative statement into (proposition, ICD 203 likelihood band, confidence level). ICD 203 defines ordered likelihood bands (*almost no chance* → *very unlikely* → *unlikely* → *roughly even chance* → *likely* → *very likely* → *almost certain*) with associated numeric ranges. Extract the propositional structure (conjunction, disjunction, negation, conditional) and build the LP: does a probability measure exist over the atoms satisfying every stated band constraint? If infeasible, return the **irreducible infeasible subset** from the solver — the minimal set of statements that cannot all be true. This catches a real and common defect: "X is likely" alongside "X or Y is unlikely" is a hard contradiction, and no current tool finds it. It is deterministic, seed-free, and byte-identical across runs. **(ii) Rater calibration** — a graded-response IRT model over the N reviewer simulants × 8–9 rubric dimensions, separating reviewer severity from true product quality; plus the existing z-score normalization, Krippendorff's α for panel reliability, and dimensional-outlier detection to surface the one axis the panel underweights. Supporting deterministic checks: provenance completeness (fraction of judgments traceable to an evidence reference), **unsupported-assertion count** — reported as a metric, never blocked — and likelihood/confidence collapse detection, which is structurally trivial for this platform because there is no function of type `(Judgment) => number` anywhere in the type system.
- **What the judge panel is:** Nine judges, one per tradecraft standard — **Sourcing, Uncertainty, Distinctions, Alternatives, Relevance, Argumentation, Analytic Line (change), Accuracy, Visuals** ([ICD 203 enumeration](https://pangearesearch.substack.com/p/why-analytic-standards-exist)) — each scoring 1–4 on the published scale, plus three cross-cutting judges: **key-assumptions check** (every assumption must carry a falsifier or the standard fails), **devil's advocate** (argues the product's analytic line is unsupported), and **premortem** ("this assessment was wrong; which standard's violation caused it?"). Rubric axes are the ICD 203 standards themselves — the rubric *is* the customer's own regulation, which is the single strongest positioning fact in this entire catalog.
- **The config pack:** `rubric/icd-203.rubric.yaml` (the nine standards, verbatim, with the published 1–4 anchors), `thresholds/estimative-language-bands.yaml` (ICD 203 likelihood words → numeric intervals), `domain/finished-intelligence.adapter.ts` (product parsing: BLUF, judgments, sourcing footnotes, confidence statements), `actors/reviewer-severity-profiles.yaml`, `judges/panel-icd203.yaml`, `oracle/dutch-book-injector.yaml` (synthetic contradiction generator).
- **Data required:** **Fully unclassified demo, using real IC-authored text.** Named sources: the ODNI **Annual Threat Assessment** (published every year and written to ICD 203), the NIC's **Global Trends** series, declassified NIEs in the CIA CREST/FOIA reading room and the National Security Archive, IC statements for the record to SSCI/HPSCI, and — as an unclassified proxy corpus with matched difficulty — CRS reports and IISS/CSIS assessments. Classified access is needed only to run it on the actual production stream, which is a deployment question, not a science question.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Best-scored candidate in the catalog, with a published human-rater instrument to score against.** The **"IC Rating Scale for Evaluating Analytic Tradecraft Standards"** — eight dimensions, each 1–4 (Poor/Fair/Good/Excellent), total 8–32, three trained raters averaged, reports presented in neutral randomized format — is the exact instrument used in the independent evaluation of IARPA's CREATE program, where the University of Melbourne SWARM platform scored **22.0/32 versus 15.9/32 for normal professional-analyst methods, Cohen's d = 1.37**, against a preregistered hypothesis of d = 0.42 ([van Gelder et al., *Journal of Cognitive Engineering and Decision Making*, 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1555343420926287)). So: (i) **human-agreement oracle** — replicate that protocol, measure ICC and Krippendorff's α between the system's scores and three trained human raters on a held-out product set; the bar to clear is published. (ii) **Synthetic oracle, exact** — inject known Dutch books, known unsourced assertions, and known likelihood/confidence collapses at controlled rates and measure precision/recall; the coherence checker's ground truth is mathematical, not judgmental. (iii) **Historical** — score declassified NIEs against the same rubric and check whether tradecraft scores predict subsequent accuracy (this is C-TRADECRAFT-7's data, and the two candidates compose).
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(c) primary, and it hits the customer's exact words** — "reconcile differences in overall analytic confidence" is literally the confidence-coherence check. **(d) primary** — product review is the most repetitive, highest-volume, lowest-leverage analyst-hour sink in the IC, and this automates the mechanical portion (coherence, sourcing traceability, estimative-language compliance) while leaving substantive judgment to the reviewer. **(a)** efficiency: a branch chief's review time reallocates from checking form to checking substance. **(b)** time-to-analysis: the check runs during drafting, not after.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — **IARPA REASON is the direct incumbent**, explicitly building AI that "automatically produce[s] comments (feedback and recommendations) on a draft report... identif[ying] strengths and weaknesses in the draft's reasoning," described by PM Steven Rieber as instantaneous peer review ([IARPA](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/reason), [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2023/01/06/iarpa-to-develop-novel-ai-that-automatically-generates-tips-to-improve-intel-reports/)). **IARPA CREATE / SWARM** proved the effect size is real and large. The **Multisource AI Scorecard Table (MAST)** — Blasch, Sung, and Nguyen, out of the 2019 ODNI/DHS Public-Private Analytic Exchange Program — already maps ICD 203's nine criteria onto a 0–3 AI-evaluation checklist ([arXiv:2102.03985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03985)), and a validated follow-on methodology, PADTHAI-MM, exists ([AI Magazine, 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aaai.70000)). Commercial — **Globalytica** and Pherson Associates sell structured-analytic-technique training and tooling (UNVERIFIED as to current product line); no commercial ICD 203 compliance checker I could confirm. **Differentiator against REASON: REASON critiques evidence and reasoning in natural language. It does not solve an LP for probabilistic coherence, does not separate likelihood from confidence as typed fields, and does not calibrate its own reviewers. This candidate should be positioned as complementary to REASON and as a REASON transition partner, not as a competitor to it — that framing is both honest and commercially smarter.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* proposition extraction is the weak link — mis-parsing "X is likely if Y" as an unconditional claim produces a false Dutch book, and false contradiction alarms would destroy analyst trust faster than any other failure in this catalog. Mitigation is structural: report the extracted proposition set back to the analyst for confirmation *before* declaring infeasibility, and measure and publish the parse-error rate. Rubric scores on subjective standards (Argumentation, Relevance) will have lower human agreement than on mechanical ones (Sourcing, Uncertainty) — report per-standard agreement separately and never a single headline number. *Red lines:* this scores *products*, not people. The moment product scores are aggregated to a named author it becomes a performance-management system, which will kill adoption and is a different (and worse) product — see C-TRADECRAFT-10. Structural enforcement: the product record type carries no author identifier field, and the aggregation layer exposes no group-by on any personnel dimension. Politicization risk is real and cuts both ways: an automated "analytic line" score could be misused to flag dissent — mitigate by making the Alternatives standard *reward* preserved dissent, and by keeping the Analytic Ombuds channel outside the tool.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 4 now** — the calibration, z-score, Krippendorff, provenance and unsupported-assertion layers exist and are among the 663 tests; the LP coherence checker and the ICD 203 rubric pack are the new work, and both are small. **TRL 6 in 12 months** — this can be run against the published ODNI Annual Threat Assessment corpus and against the published IC Rating Scale protocol without a single classified byte, which makes it the fastest path to a demonstrable, externally-verifiable result. **Recommend this as the Phase I anchor candidate.**

### C-TRADECRAFT-5 — Collection Tasker: the observation that would most change the estimate

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Instead of asking "what do we know," it computes which single piece of information we could realistically go get in the next two weeks that would most change the answer — and what that would be worth per dollar spent.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Given our current posterior over whether Iran restarts enrichment to 60% at Fordow before 1 October, which of the 40 collectible observables available to us would, if obtained, most reduce the expected Brier score of that forecast — and what is the expected accuracy gain per collection dollar and per day of latency?"
- **Mission and user:** Collection management and requirements officers (CIA's collection management staff, DIA's Directorate for Operations, NGA source strategy, the NIM staffs who own NIPF-driven collection priorities); the analyst-collector interface that today runs on relationships. **Today:** an analyst writes a collection requirement in prose describing what they want; a collection manager prioritizes against a topic-level NIPF band; nobody computes the *expected value* of a specific observation against a specific estimate. Gaps get named ("we lack visibility into X") but not ranked, and the ranking that does happen is by topic priority, not by decision-relevance.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a **collection-environment world**: which observables are actually obtainable, at what latency, with what probability of collection success, against what denial effort. State variables: per-observable availability probability, latency distribution, cost, sensor revisit interval, and adversary denial intensity against that observable. Knobs: `riskTolerance` maps directly and honestly — how much collection risk (exposure, source burn, platform risk) the simulant will accept for a given information gain; `escalationPropensity` maps to willingness to task scarce, high-demand national collection versus commercial or open sources. Variation is substantial: the risk-averse simulant's top-ranked observable is commercial SAR revisit over Fordow; the risk-tolerant simulant's is a HUMINT placement, with a 6× cost and a 4× information gain — and the ranking inversion between them is the product.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Decision-theoretic value of information plus submodular combinatorial optimization.** For the current posterior p(h) over the outcome bands and each candidate observable o with likelihood model P(o | h): compute **expected value of sample information**, EVSI(o) = E_o[ max_a Σ_h p(h|o)·u(a,h) ] − max_a Σ_h p(h)·u(a,h), for a decision-relevant utility u; and in parallel the purely estimative version, expected reduction in Shannon entropy E_o[H(p) − H(p|o)] and expected Brier improvement. Then **budgeted subset selection**: maximize expected entropy reduction over sets of observables subject to a cost/latency/revisit-capacity knapsack, using the greedy algorithm with its (1 − 1/e) approximation guarantee — valid because entropy reduction is submodular under conditional independence of observations given h, and *invalid* when observables are correlated, which the kernel must detect (via the same dependence machinery as C-TRADECRAFT-2) and fall back to exact ILP on small sets. Output: a ranked collection deck with expected ΔBrier per dollar and per day, plus the **tripwire observable** — the single realization that would flip the BLUF's likelihood band, which is a directly actionable warning tasking.
- **What the judge panel is:** (1) **Diagnosticity judge (ACH-derived)** — scores whether the claimed likelihood models P(o|h) are genuinely discriminating or wishful. (2) **Devil's advocate** — argues the top-ranked observable is a decoy: high apparent diagnosticity precisely because the adversary can produce it cheaply (this judge *consumes* C-TRADECRAFT-3's cheap-talk partition, and the two candidates compose tightly). (3) **Key-assumptions judge** on collection-feasibility assumptions, each with a falsifier. (4) **Premortem** — "we spent the collection budget on this and learned nothing; why?" (5) **Red team with standing** to force a rerun if it can show the utility function encodes a policy preference. Rubric axes: *diagnosticity honesty*, *feasibility realism*, *decoy resistance*, *budget efficiency*, *tripwire actionability*.
- **The config pack:** `domain/collection-observables.adapter.ts` (observable taxonomy per INT), `observables/likelihood-models.yaml` (P(o|h) per observable — the load-bearing file), `costs/collection-cost-latency.yaml`, `actors/collection-risk-profiles.yaml`, `rubric/voi.rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/knapsack-budget.yaml`, `judges/panel-collection.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo is possible and genuinely convincing** using commercially observable proxies: Planet Labs and Sentinel-1/2 revisit schedules and published tasking prices, AIS and ADS-B feeds, IAEA inspection calendars, OPEC/IEA publication schedules, and NOAA/USGS observation cadences — all with real, published latency and cost. The demo question becomes "which commercially-obtainable observation most reduces uncertainty about [a resolvable near-term question]," which is fully scoreable. Classified access is needed for the real observable taxonomy and for national-technical-means cost/latency, which are the parts that make it operationally useful.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **(i) Synthetic oracle, exact** — generate a world with known h, let the system rank observables, then *actually reveal* the top-ranked and a random control, and measure realized entropy reduction and realized ΔBrier against predicted. The gap between predicted and realized VOI is the headline metric and it is measurable to arbitrary precision. **(ii) Historical backtest** — episodes where the decisive observation is documented and datable: the 14 October 1962 U-2 mission over San Cristóbal (would the model have ranked that flight path first, given the 1 October evidence base?); the IAEA's access to Parchin; the commercial-imagery observations that resolved the 2021–22 Russian buildup debate. **(iii) Live post-cutoff** — pre-register the ranking, then observe which observable actually resolved the question, on questions like next IAEA quarterly findings or a specific OPEC compliance figure. Fully scorable.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(b) primary** — this converts an estimate into a tasking, compressing the analysis→collection→re-analysis loop that today runs on weeks of human staffing. **(a)** accuracy: collection dollars go to the observation that moves the posterior rather than to the topic with the highest NIPF band. **(d)** cost: the mechanism is literal — expected accuracy gain per collection dollar is the objective function, so cost reduction is not a side effect but the thing being optimized. **(c)** secondary: naming what we do *not* know is ICD 203's fourth analytic standard, which requires analytic elements to identify and address critical information gaps.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — collection requirements management systems exist across the IC (UNVERIFIED as to current program names and vendors); **IARPA's forecasting line** (ACE, HFC) produced the posteriors but not the tasking layer. I could not confirm any current program computing EVSI over a live analytic posterior — **this appears to be genuine white space, and the reason is prosaic: nobody had a machine-readable posterior to compute it against, which is exactly what this platform produces natively.** Commercial — **Palantir** (collection management workflows, UNVERIFIED as to VOI computation), **Rhombus Power Guardian** ([$200M USAF award](https://theaiinsider.tech/2025/09/27/rhombus-power-snags-200-million-us-air-force-contract-for-ai-powered-strategic-decision-making-platform/)), and commercial tasking marketplaces (Planet, Maxar, Umbra) which optimize for imagery-collection efficiency but have no model of the analytic question.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* garbage-in on P(o|h) produces confidently wrong rankings — the likelihood models are elicited, and the fan-out must vary them rather than fix them, with the spread reported. Submodularity fails under observable correlation, which is common (three sensors watching one facility) — detect and fall back, don't assume. The system will systematically over-rank *observable* things over *important* things, because feasibility is modeled and importance is elicited; the premortem judge exists specifically to catch this. **Worst failure: a decoy loop, where the adversary learns which observable we rank first and feeds it** — hence the mandatory C-3 coupling. *Red lines:* this produces collection taskings. It must not produce taskings against US persons or domestic infrastructure — enforce structurally by making the observable taxonomy a closed enumeration in the domain adapter, with no free-text observable and no target-identifier field; a domestic observable simply cannot be expressed in the type. It must not be used to support covert action targeting: the utility function schema admits only estimative and decision-support utilities, with no action-outcome utility type.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 3 now** — EVSI over the platform's existing band posterior is a modest amount of new code; the knapsack and greedy submodular optimizer are textbook. **TRL 5 in 12 months** with the commercial-observables demo and the Cuban-missile-crisis backtest.

### C-TRADECRAFT-6 — Indicator Lab: measuring the discriminating power of I&W lists, and retiring the indicators that have none

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Takes a warning watchlist, measures how much each indicator actually distinguishes "it's happening" from "normal Tuesday," and tells you which indicators to delete and what your real false-alarm rate and warning lead time are.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "For our current Taiwan-contingency indicator list, what is the measured likelihood ratio of each of the 63 indicators, what is the list's false-alarm rate and mean warning lead time at the current alerting threshold, and which indicators have a likelihood-ratio confidence interval spanning 1 — i.e. contribute exactly nothing?"
- **Mission and user:** National and combatant-command watch centers (CIA Operations Center, DIA's watch, NMJIC, INDOPACOM J2 and its Joint Intelligence Operations Center); the NIO for Warning's staff; the analysts who maintain indicator lists and have never been told whether they work. **Today:** indicator lists are built by expert judgment, grow monotonically because removing an indicator feels like accepting risk, are reviewed qualitatively, and are almost never scored. Nobody can state their watchlist's false-alarm rate as a number. This is the purest "measure it and then delete things" candidate in the catalog.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is an **operating-regime world**: the base rate of the contingency, the adversary's activity tempo, the seasonal/exercise background, and the denial intensity applied to each indicator. State variables: per-indicator firing probability under normal and pre-event regimes, the change-point time, indicator correlation structure, and reporting latency. Knobs: `riskTolerance` maps to the chosen (α, β) operating point — how much false alarm the watch will tolerate to buy lead time; `escalationPropensity` maps to how quickly accumulated evidence is converted into a warning notice rather than a watch item. This varies meaningfully: the same indicator list under a high-α simulant warns 19 days out with a 1-in-4 false-alarm rate, and under a low-α simulant warns 4 days out with a 1-in-30 rate, and the tradeoff curve is the deliverable.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Sequential detection theory — Wald's SPRT and CUSUM change-point detection, with ROC/likelihood-ratio estimation.** Per indicator i, estimate the per-period likelihood ratio LR_i = P(fire | pre-event regime) / P(fire | normal regime) from labeled historical episode windows, with bootstrap confidence intervals. Accumulate log-LR across indicators and time as the SPRT statistic; set thresholds A = (1−β)/α and B = β/(1−α) to hit a chosen error pair, giving a **closed-form expected time to decision** and, via Wald's identity, an **average run length to false alarm** for the exponential-family case. Compute each indicator's *marginal* contribution to area under the ROC curve, conditional on the rest of the list — this is the delete signal, and it is not the same as its standalone LR, because correlated indicators double-count. Emit: per-indicator LR with CI; the subset of indicators whose CI spans 1, flagged **NON-DISCRIMINATING — PROPOSE RETIREMENT**; the measured false-alarm rate and mean lead time at the operating threshold; and a reliability diagram for the warning probability itself.
- **What the judge panel is:** (1) **Diagnosticity judge** — the ACH machinery applied to indicators rather than to evidence items, ranking by weighted inconsistency. (2) **Devil's advocate** — argues that a proposed-for-retirement indicator is low-LR only because the historical window undersampled it (the rare-but-decisive indicator problem, which is real: an indicator that has fired once, before the one event that matters, has a wide CI and a low measured LR). (3) **Deception judge** — scores which indicators the adversary can suppress (denial) or spoof (deception) most cheaply, and down-weights accordingly, consuming C-3's output. (4) **Premortem** — "we were surprised despite the list; which indicator did we retire, or which never existed?" (5) **Key-assumptions judge** on the labeling of historical episodes, with falsifiers. Rubric axes: *measured discrimination*, *rare-event robustness*, *denial resistance*, *lead-time honesty*, *list parsimony*.
- **The config pack:** `domain/warning-indicators.adapter.ts`, `indicators/taiwan-contingency.list.yaml` (indicator definitions with machine-checkable firing conditions), `episodes/labeled-windows.yaml` (the historical episode labels — the load-bearing file), `actors/watch-posture-profiles.yaml`, `rubric/indicator-power.rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/sprt-operating-points.yaml`, `judges/panel-warning.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **A strong unclassified demo exists.** Named sources for labeled episodes and indicator streams: PLA activity around Taiwan (Taiwan MND publishes daily ADIZ incursion counts — a long, dense, timestamped public series with labeled exercise episodes in August 2022, April 2023, and subsequent years); Russian force-buildup indicators from commercial imagery (Planet, Maxar) and open rail/logistics reporting for the 2014 and 2021–22 episodes; ACLED and GDELT for conflict-onset indicator work; AIS and ADS-B for naval and air movement; NOTAM and maritime exclusion-zone declarations (highly diagnostic, publicly published, and precisely timestamped). Classified access adds SIGINT and HUMINT indicators, which are typically the highest-LR ones — a real limitation that must be stated, not glossed.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **(i) Historical backtest with as-of cutoff, and it is genuinely clean** because warning problems come with dated ground truth: run the indicator list against the Taiwan ADIZ series with a hard cutoff before each labeled exercise; against the Russian buildup with cutoffs at monthly intervals through 2021; against the 2014 Crimea seizure; against the 1973 Yom Kippur and 1998 Pokhran-II surprise cases from the declassified record. Score: false-alarm rate, mean lead time, and calibration of the warning probability. **(ii) Synthetic oracle** — generate regimes with a known change-point and known indicator LRs, measure recovery of both. **(iii) Live post-cutoff** — pre-register a warning threshold on a real ongoing indicator stream (Taiwan ADIZ) and score alarms over the following 90 days. Fully scorable, with an unusually clean and public data stream.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a) primary** — accuracy stated as a measured false-alarm rate and lead time, replacing an unmeasured expert list. **(d) primary** — indicator monitoring is the archetypal repetitive task, and list pruning removes standing monitoring burden permanently. **(c)** QA: an indicator list whose discriminating power is measured is a quality-assured product; one whose isn't, isn't. **(b)** time: warning lead time is literally the objective.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — IC warning methodology (Cynthia Grabo's *Anticipating Surprise* is the doctrinal text; UNVERIFIED as to current IC warning-system program names); **IARPA's OSI (Open Source Indicators)** program pursued event forecasting from open data (UNVERIFIED — I could not confirm details this session); **IARPA HFC** produced hybrid forecasting systems and beat the human-only baseline by [10 percent in accuracy](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hfc), completing May 2020. Commercial — **Rhombus Power Guardian**, whose vendor claim is that it predicted Russia's 2022 offensive "with a high degree of accuracy, months prior" ([vendor/press claim, not independently verified](https://theaiinsider.tech/2025/09/27/rhombus-power-snags-200-million-us-air-force-contract-for-ai-powered-strategic-decision-making-platform/)); **Dataminr**; **Recorded Future**. **Differentiator: every incumbent adds indicators and alerts. None of them publish a measured likelihood ratio per indicator with a confidence interval, and none of them recommend deleting indicators. The deletion output is the distinctive, defensible, and slightly uncomfortable one — and it is exactly what a customer drowning in alerts needs.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* the rare-decisive-indicator problem is severe and is not solvable by more data — an indicator with N=1 historical firing has an uninformative CI, and retiring it on that basis would be a catastrophic error; structural mitigation is that the system **proposes** retirement and separately reports "insufficient evidence to rate" as a distinct third category, which is exactly the platform's first-class INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE verdict applied at indicator granularity. Episode labeling drives everything and is contestable. Adversary adaptation invalidates measured LRs the moment the list leaks. Non-stationarity: an LR measured on 2014 tells you little about 2026. *Red lines:* indicator lists aimed at domestic events or domestic groups are off the table — enforce by domain adapter, with a foreign-nexus assertion required at config load and a runtime throw if any indicator's firing condition references a domestic data source. Warning products feed decisions with force-employment consequences; the product schema emits likelihood, confidence, lead time and false-alarm rate — it emits no recommended response option, and there is no response-option type in the product schema.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 3 now** (SPRT/CUSUM and ROC estimation are textbook; the episode-labeling corpus is the real work). **TRL 5 in 12 months**, with the Taiwan ADIZ backtest and a live pre-registered 90-day run as validation. This is the second-fastest path to a hard, public, falsifiable number after C-TRADECRAFT-4.

### C-TRADECRAFT-7 — The Ledger: retrospective scoring of finished intelligence at scale

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Goes back through what we published, works out which claims were actually checkable, checks them, and reports how well we did — by topic, by horizon, and against the dumb baselines we should be beating.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Across the 2019–2025 Annual Threat Assessments, what fraction of the estimative claims were resolvable at all; of those, what was the Brier score by topic and by time horizon; how does that decompose into reliability, resolution and uncertainty; and what is the Brier skill score against a base-rate baseline and against a persistence baseline?"
- **Mission and user:** ODNI's Analytic Integrity and Standards staff and the lessons-learned function; the NIC; agency-level analytic methodology cells; SSCI/HPSCI oversight staff. **Today:** post-mortems are episodic, triggered by a public failure, conducted by a commission, and produce a narrative. There is **no routine, quantitative, at-scale accuracy ledger for finished intelligence anywhere in the US IC that I could confirm**, which is remarkable for an enterprise whose product is prediction. The comparison the customer will feel: forecasting tournaments have scored their participants continuously since 2011 and the IC has not scored itself the same way.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a **resolution-criteria interpretation** — and this is genuinely the dominant error source in retrospective scoring, so the fan-out is well-targeted. State variables: the operationalization of a vague claim into a resolvable quantity ("Iran will continue to expand its missile inventory" → which inventory, whose count, what threshold, by when), the resolution source chosen, the as-of date, and the handling of partial satisfaction. Knobs: `credulity` = how charitably an ambiguous claim is operationalized in the author's favor; `commitmentPropensity` = how readily a claim is declared UNRESOLVABLE rather than force-fit to a proxy. **Divergence here is the honest finding**: if a claim scores well under charitable operationalization and badly under strict, the claim was not falsifiable as written, and the system should say so rather than average.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Proper-scoring-rule decomposition plus hierarchical empirical-Bayes skill estimation.** Compute Brier, log score, and the **Murphy decomposition** (Brier = Reliability − Resolution + Uncertainty) per topic, per horizon, per office; ECE and MCE with reliability diagrams; sharpness, to catch hedging — a corpus that says "roughly even chance" about everything has excellent reliability and zero resolution, and only the decomposition exposes that. **Brier skill score against explicit named baselines**: base rate, persistence ("next year looks like last year"), always-modal-band, and uniform. Then, because most units of analysis have small N and raw rankings would be pure noise, fit a **hierarchical empirical-Bayes model with James–Stein shrinkage** over true skill θ_j per unit j, and report posterior intervals on skill rather than point ranks — refusing to rank when the intervals overlap is a first-class output, and maps onto the existing abstention gate. Additionally: a **resolvability rate** — the fraction of estimative claims that admit any resolution criterion at all — which is itself a tradecraft metric and, I predict, an uncomfortable one. The mandatory evaluation-regime tag is `historical_backtest`, and no function pools it with synthetic results.
- **What the judge panel is:** (1) **Resolution-criteria judge** — scores whether the operationalization is faithful to the claim's plain meaning, and this judge's disagreement rate across simulants is itself reported. (2) **Devil's advocate** — argues the claim was right in spirit and the resolution criterion is unfair (a real and legitimate objection that must be scored, not dismissed). (3) **Key-assumptions judge** on the resolution source's authority. (4) **Premortem** — "this ledger produced a misleading ranking; how?" (5) **Red team with standing** to force a rerun if it can show the resolution criterion was chosen after seeing the outcome (a hindsight-contamination check that is *structurally* enforced by requiring resolution criteria to be committed with a hash before the resolution source is read — the same seeded-artifact discipline the kernel already uses).
- **The config pack:** `domain/finished-intelligence-claims.adapter.ts` (claim extraction from published product), `resolution/criteria-templates.yaml`, `resolution/authoritative-sources.yaml` (per-topic resolution source registry), `actors/resolution-strictness-profiles.yaml`, `rubric/retrospective.rubric.yaml`, `baselines/explicit-baselines.yaml`, `judges/panel-ledger.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Fully unclassified demo on real IC-authored text — this is the single most persuasive demo in the catalog.** Named corpora: the **ODNI Annual Threat Assessment**, published every year since 2019 and freely available; the NIC's **Global Trends** series (2017, 2021, 2025 editions, with explicit multi-year projections); declassified NIEs in the National Security Archive and CIA FOIA reading room (the October 2002 Iraq WMD NIE key judgments are declassified and constitute a labeled negative); IC statements for the record. Resolution sources: IAEA reports, IMF/World Bank series, UN Comtrade, ACLED, SIPRI, election results, EIA/IEA. Classified access is needed only to run it on classified production — again a deployment question, not a science question.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** The scoring machinery *is* the product, so the meta-question is whether the ledger is right. **(i) Synthetic** — inject claims with known truth values and known ambiguity levels and measure resolution-criteria fidelity and scoring correctness. **(ii) Inter-rater** — have human analysts independently resolve a sample of claims and measure Krippendorff's α between human and system resolution; this is the credibility gate and it is measurable. **(iii) Convergent validity** — score a corpus of *forecasting-tournament* questions where the ground truth and the community Brier are already published (INFER, Metaculus, Good Judgment Open) and confirm the ledger reproduces the known scores exactly. That third one is a hard, cheap, falsifiable verification of the whole pipeline. Not unscorable — self-scoring.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a) primary** — you cannot improve accuracy you do not measure, and this is the measurement. **(c) primary** — the highest form of quality assurance is knowing whether the product was right. **(d)** cost: post-mortems currently cost commission-scale effort; this costs compute. **(b)** indirect but real: measured topic-level skill tells you where to spend analytic time.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — ODNI requires IC elements to conduct internal review and evaluation against the IC Analytic Standards with annual reporting ([ICD 203](https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf)), but that reviews *tradecraft*, not *accuracy*; ICD 203's Accuracy standard is one of the nine and is the hardest to evaluate at the time of writing, which is precisely why a retrospective ledger is the right instrument. **IARPA ACE and HFC** built the scoring science — HFC completed May 2020 with a [10 percent accuracy gain over the human-only baseline](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hfc). **RAND now runs INFER** for USG stakeholders, having transitioned it from UMD ARLIS, with Cultivate Labs as technology partner and a first-of-its-kind data-sharing partnership with the UK's **Cosmic Bazaar** ([Cultivate Labs](https://www.cultivatelabs.com/posts/uk-and-us-government-forecasting-collaboration-is-launched)). Commercial — **Good Judgment Inc.**, **Metaculus**, **Cultivate Labs**. **Differentiator: every incumbent scores forecasts made inside a tournament, on questions written to be resolvable. Nobody scores finished intelligence written in prose for a policymaker. The hard, novel, and valuable part is the resolution-criteria layer and the honest resolvability-rate metric — and the fan-out over interpretations is exactly the right architecture for it.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* hindsight contamination in resolution-criteria selection is the fatal failure and must be prevented structurally (commit-then-resolve with a hash, enforced in code); low resolvability rates could be dismissed as a parsing failure rather than a tradecraft finding, so parse quality must be independently measured; small-N ranking noise is handled by shrinkage but will still be misread by consumers, so the system must refuse to emit ordinal ranks when posterior intervals overlap. Selection bias: published unclassified products are not representative of classified production, and the demo must say so. *Red lines:* **this is one flag-flip away from an individual analyst performance-scoring system, and that would be a different product with different law and different politics.** Structural enforcement: claims are attributed to *products and offices*, never to persons; there is no author field in the claim record type; the aggregation layer exposes no personnel group-by. Congressional-oversight consumption of accuracy scores is a real political hazard for the customer and should be named honestly in the proposal rather than discovered later.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 4 now** — the full metrics package (Brier, Brier skill vs explicit baselines, Murphy decomposition, ECE/MCE, reliability diagrams, sharpness, log score) already exists and is tested, and the regime tagging already prevents pooling; the claim-extraction and resolution-criteria layers are the new work. **TRL 6 in 12 months** — a scored ledger of the published 2019–2025 Annual Threat Assessments is achievable inside a Phase I and would be the most attention-getting single artifact this program could produce.

### C-TRADECRAFT-8 — Same Questions, Same Scale: workforce-and-model forecasting tournament with a calibration-aware router

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Puts human analysts and AI models on exactly the same resolvable questions, measures who is well-calibrated on what, and then routes each new question to whichever performs best on that kind of question — including routing it to "nobody should answer this yet."
- **The analyst question it answers:** "On questions of the form 'will [state] take [militarized action] against [state] before [date]' at a 90-day horizon, what is the measured calibration curve of our analyst cohort, of our model ensemble, and of the two combined — and for the next such question, which should we trust?"
- **Mission and user:** Agency analytic-methodology and workforce-development offices; the NIC; the offices that would own an internal INFER-equivalent; and the AI test-and-evaluation function that has to decide whether a model is good enough to put in front of analysts. **Today:** model adoption decisions are made on demos and vendor claims, and analyst calibration is not measured at all. INFER exists, but as a separate crowd-forecasting activity — not as an instrument for measuring the workforce and the models against each other on the same items.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a **forecaster-configuration** — a model, a prompt/tooling configuration, an evidence budget, and a deliberation protocol. State variables: retrieval depth, as-of corpus cutoff, ensemble size, extremization parameter, and deliberation rounds. Knobs: `credulity` = how much retrieved evidence is accepted without corroboration; `commitmentPropensity` = how far from the base rate the simulant is willing to move (which is directly the sharpness/hedging axis the metrics package already measures). Variation is real and measurable: deliberation-protocol variation alone produces measurable Brier differences in the published literature.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Calibration estimation plus contextual-bandit routing.** Per forecaster (human cohort or model config): reliability curve by binned forecast probability, ECE/MCE, sharpness, Brier decomposed by Murphy, and z-normalized skill so that severity/optimism bias is stripped before comparison — the platform's existing calibration layer, applied to forecasters rather than judges. Ensembling by **extremized log-odds averaging** with weights derived from measured skill (Satopää/Baron-style extremization, which has a known closed form and known failure conditions). The router is a **contextual bandit** (LinUCB or Thompson sampling) over question features — domain, horizon, base-rate class, evidence density, question ambiguity — with arms = {analyst cohort, each model config, ensemble, **abstain**}; the regret bound gives a stated convergence guarantee, and the abstention arm makes "this question should not be forecast" a competitive option rather than a failure. Critically, **the abstention gate runs before scoring**, so a forecaster is not penalized for correctly declining an unanswerable question — and false-abstention and true-abstention rates are measured per forecaster, which no incumbent tournament does.
- **What the judge panel is:** Lighter than other candidates, because the oracle is the questions themselves. (1) **Question-quality judge** — scores resolvability, ambiguity, and whether the question is unanswerable-in-principle (the abstention-gate ground truth). (2) **Devil's advocate** — argues the leader's advantage is question-selection artifact rather than skill. (3) **Key-assumptions judge** on the feature space the router conditions on. (4) **Premortem** — "the router sent everything to the model and we lost analyst skill; how did we not notice?" Rubric axes: *question resolvability*, *calibration*, *sharpness*, *abstention correctness*, *routing regret*.
- **The config pack:** `domain/forecast-questions.adapter.ts`, `questions/question-bank.yaml` (with resolution criteria and sources), `actors/forecaster-configs.yaml`, `rubric/tournament.rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention-gate.yaml`, `router/bandit-features.yaml`, `judges/panel-tournament.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Fully unclassified demo, immediately.** Named sources: the **Metaculus AI Benchmark / quarterly Cups** (public question sets, public resolutions, and public bot-vs-human leaderboards), **INFER** (RAND/Cultivate Labs), **Good Judgment Open**, and **ForecastBench**. Classified deployment needs a classified question bank, which is an operational, not scientific, problem.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Trivially and completely — resolution is the point.** Live post-cutoff resolution on public questions, with published comparanda to beat: on a 334-question set (July–September 2024), o3 scored Brier **0.1352** against an expert-forecaster **median of 0.0225** (mean 0.1573) and a human-crowd baseline of **0.149**; on a 130-question hold-out (October–December 2024), o3-pro scored **0.1307** against an expert median of **0.0196** ([arXiv:2507.04562v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04562v3)) — note the enormous median/mean gap among experts, which is itself a finding about heterogeneity and an argument for the router. In Metaculus tournaments, bots have been placing in the top few percent of large human fields — `metac-claude-4-5-sonnet-high-32k+asknews` ranked 33rd of 1,130 humans in the Spring 2026 Cup ([summarized on the EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Spyz3wESZu2eeqhDj/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say), secondary source). The claim this candidate can make and defend is *not* "our models beat superforecasters" — they do not — but "we measure which does better on which class of question, and route accordingly, and abstain when neither should answer."
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy, via measured routing rather than assumed model superiority. **(b)** time: questions the model handles well stop consuming analyst hours. **(d)** cost: the router's objective is accuracy per dollar, and the abstention arm prevents spending on unanswerable questions. **(c)** partial: calibration measurement is a confidence-quality-assurance function.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** This candidate has the **strongest incumbents and the weakest differentiation** in the catalog, and that should be said out loud. Government — **IARPA ACE**, **IARPA HFC** (completed May 2020, [10% over human-only baseline](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hfc), producing the SAGE hybrid system), **INFER** now run by RAND with Cultivate Labs and partnered with UK **Cosmic Bazaar** ([Cultivate Labs](https://www.cultivatelabs.com/posts/uk-and-us-government-forecasting-collaboration-is-launched)), and the RAND Forecasting Initiative's Pro Forecaster program ([RFI](https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/pro-forecaster-description)). Commercial — **Good Judgment Inc.**, **Metaculus**, **Cultivate Labs**, **Mantic** ([Thinking Machines guest post on training LLMs to predict world events](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/)). **Honest assessment: propose this as an integration and instrumentation layer on top of INFER, not as a replacement for it. The two defensible novelties are the measured abstention gate and the calibration-aware router; the tournament itself is a solved, funded, incumbent-occupied problem.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* question-bank selection bias determines everything, and a bank curated by the people being scored is worthless; the bandit needs hundreds of resolved questions per arm before its routing beats a fixed policy, which is a multi-quarter cold-start; scoring humans creates gaming incentives (forecast the easy questions, abstain on the hard ones) — mitigated by scoring abstention explicitly and by mandatory question assignment. Deliberation can *reduce* accuracy through correlated error, so the ensemble diversity metric must gate ensembling. *Red lines:* scoring named individuals is a personnel action with union, Privacy Act, and morale consequences. Structural enforcement: default aggregation is to *cohort*, and individual scores are visible only to the individual — enforced by the record type carrying an opaque forecaster ID with no join path to personnel data in the schema.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 4 now** — the calibration, ECE, sharpness, abstention-gate and diversity metrics all exist and are tested; the bandit router and question-bank adapter are the new work. **TRL 6 in 12 months** — the platform can enter the public Metaculus AI Benchmark and post a scored, third-party-verified result, which is an unusually cheap and unusually credible external validation.

### C-TRADECRAFT-9 — The Range: synthetic closed worlds for analyst training, model evaluation, and tradecraft certification

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Builds complete fictional-but-realistic crises where we know the exact truth because we generated it, feeds trainees and AI models the messy reporting from inside that world — including deliberately planted deception and laundered sources — and grades them against a truth that provably did not exist before the exercise.
- **The analyst question it answers:** (Posed to the trainee, inside the world:) "Will the Republic of Karval's leadership authorize a cross-border seizure of the Verith corridor before day 120 — expressed as a probability across the four outcome bands, with confidence, key assumptions, and the falsifier for each?" (Posed by the program office, about the trainee:) "What is this analyst's calibration curve, deception-detection rate, and circular-reporting-detection rate across 40 scenarios?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA's Sherman Kent School for Intelligence Analysis; DIA's analytic training; NGA College; the National Intelligence University; **and, equally, the AI test-and-evaluation offices that need an uncontaminated benchmark for model procurement.** **Today:** analyst training uses historical case studies, which every trainee can google and every model has memorized, so neither can be honestly scored on them; and current-events exercises have no ground truth for months or years. There is no supply of graded, ground-truthed, non-memorizable analytic problems.
- **What the N simulants are:** Two nested fan-outs, which is the architecturally interesting part. **Outer:** each run generates a distinct **world** — state variables include elite-faction cohesion, economic stress (reserves, currency, energy import dependence), military readiness stocks, popular grievance level, external patron commitment, and an information-environment parameter set. **Inner:** within a fixed world, N **analyst/model simulants** each receive a different evidence subset and produce an estimate. Knobs at the world level: `riskTolerance` and `escalationPropensity` are used *natively and unmodified* — they parameterize the synthetic leadership's decision rule, exactly as the prototype already does. Knobs at the observer level: `credulity` and `commitmentPropensity`. Variation is total and controlled by construction.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **A coupled system-dynamics + agent-based generative world, plus an explicit observation model.** The latent world is stocks-and-flows (reserves, readiness, grievance, patron commitment) integrated on a fixed step, coupled to an agent-based elite-decision layer where each actor applies a threshold rule over its perceived state weighted by its `riskTolerance`/`escalationPropensity` — seeded PRNG, no wall clock, no `Math.random`, so the same seed yields a byte-identical world and artifact hash. Then the **observation model**, which is the part that makes this a *tradecraft* range rather than a wargame: each latent variable is observed through a channel with parameterized delay, censoring probability, additive source bias, quantization, and translation loss; a **deception policy** lets the synthetic adversary emit false observations at a modeled cost; and a **laundering operator** makes K nominal sources resample one upstream document, injecting circular reporting at a controlled effective-N. Because both the truth and the corruption are generated, every candidate in this catalog gets an exact acceptance test: C-1's conflict-type classifier, C-2's N_eff estimator, C-3's deception detector, and C-6's indicator LRs all have constructed ground truth here.
- **What the judge panel is:** The full standard panel applied to the trainee's product — ACH, key-assumptions check with mandatory falsifiers, devil's advocate, premortem, red team with standing — plus the **ICD 203 rubric judges from C-TRADECRAFT-4**, so a trainee gets a tradecraft score and an accuracy score in the same run. Additional range-specific judges: (1) **deception-detection judge** (did the trainee flag the planted deception, and did they flag deception where none was planted — both rates reported); (2) **independence judge** (did the trainee notice the laundered sources); (3) **abstention judge** (on the provably-unanswerable arm, did the trainee correctly decline). Rubric axes: *calibration*, *tradecraft compliance*, *deception detection*, *independence detection*, *abstention correctness*.
- **The config pack:** `worlds/karval-corridor.world.yaml` (stocks, flows, coupling constants, actor roster), `actors/synthetic-leadership.yaml` (risk/escalation grids), `observation/channel-model.yaml` (delay, censoring, bias, quantization per source class), `deception/adversary-policy.yaml`, `laundering/circularity-injector.yaml`, `rubric/icd-203.rubric.yaml` (shared with C-4), `thresholds/difficulty-ladder.yaml`, `judges/panel-range.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **None. Zero external data, zero classified access, and that is the entire point** — the world is generated, so there is no corpus to clear, no as-of leakage risk, and no possibility that a model memorized the answer. A credible unclassified demo is not merely possible; it is the *only* mode this candidate has. Realism calibration (making the generated world's dynamics plausible) can draw on published parameters — SIPRI military expenditure, IMF reserves data, V-Dem governance indices, ACLED conflict rates — without any of that data appearing in the generated world.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **The oracle is exact and constructed, which is the strongest possible position.** Ground truth is the world's own state at the resolution step, and the platform's existing leakage guards apply directly: an as-of hard date filter on the evidence corpus, plus the runtime assertion that throws if any evidence reference in a finished product post-dates the run's as-of date, plus the synthetic-closed-world property that the ground truth did not exist before the run generated it. The existing measured results are already in this regime: Brier skill versus base rate tracking evidence quality monotonically (+0.44 well-evidenced, +0.42 medium, +0.11 wide prior, −0.46 deliberately misspecified) across 15,832 simulations, and the abstention gate firing 80/80 on a provably-unanswerable arm versus 0–7/80 on answerable ones. Regime tag: `synthetic_oracle`, and no function pools it with backtest or live results. **The honest limitation, which must lead rather than trail: synthetic-oracle skill is not evidence of real-world forecasting skill, and the system says so in the first line of every run.**
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy: the only way to measure an analyst's or a model's calibration without waiting years. **(d)** cost: scenario development for analytic training is expensive, bespoke, human-authored, and single-use; here a scenario is a config file and a seed, and the difficulty ladder is a parameter. **(b)** time: model procurement decisions that today rest on demos can rest on a scored, uncontaminated benchmark in days. **(c)** QA: this is where the deception and circularity detectors earn their measured false-positive rates before anyone points them at real reporting.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — the **Sherman Kent School** and NIU analytic curricula (case-study based); **IARPA CREATE's** independent evaluation infrastructure, which built exactly this kind of graded-problem apparatus and demonstrated its power (SWARM 22.0/32 vs 15.9/32, d = 1.37, [van Gelder et al. 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1555343420926287)); DoD wargaming centers (Naval War College, CSIS Futures Lab) — but wargames produce narratives, not calibration curves. **DARPA AIDA's** LDC-annotated Russia–Ukraine informational-conflict corpora are the closest thing to a labeled deception/conflict benchmark and are [publicly released](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2023T10). Commercial — **Cultivate Labs** and **Metaculus** run scored question infrastructure but over real-world questions, not generated worlds; no commercial synthetic-world analytic training range that I could confirm. **Differentiator: uncontaminated ground truth. Every historical case study is in every model's training data; a generated world is in none of them. As LLM procurement in the IC accelerates — the CIA-developed Osiris is already used by thousands of analysts across 18 IC agencies ([AP reporting](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2024/05/29/262562.htm)) — the need for a memorization-proof evaluation range gets sharper every quarter, and this is the cheapest defensible answer to it.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* **realism is the whole risk** — a world whose dynamics are implausible trains and measures the wrong thing, and skill on generated worlds may not transfer to real ones, which is an empirical question the program should commit to testing (correlate a cohort's range performance against their subsequent INFER/live scores; if the correlation is null, say so). Difficulty calibration drifts. Trainees and models can overfit to the generator's idiosyncrasies, which is exactly why the generator's parameters must be resampled and held out, not fixed. *Red lines:* generated worlds must be recognizably fictional and must not model real named individuals — enforce structurally by an actor schema whose identifiers are drawn from a generated-name namespace with a load-time assertion against a real-entity list. The range must not be repurposed to generate synthetic reporting that could be mistaken for real intelligence: every generated artifact carries an indelible synthetic-provenance stamp in the artifact hash record, and the export path has no un-stamped serialization.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 4–5 now — the highest in the catalog, because this is closest to what the prototype already does**; 15,832 simulations have run in exactly this regime with measured results. The new work is the observation model (channel corruption, deception policy, laundering operator) and the tradecraft-grading judges. **TRL 6–7 in 12 months**, demonstrable in an operational training environment with a real trainee cohort. **Recommend this as the Phase I companion to C-TRADECRAFT-4: together they are a complete, fully-unclassified, externally-verifiable deliverable.**

### C-TRADECRAFT-10 — [BAD IDEA — INCLUDED AS THE CATALOG FLOOR] Analyst Bias & Insider-Risk Behavioral Scorer

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Applies the same calibration and outlier-detection math to individual analysts — scoring each one's personal bias signature and, extending the same behavioral model, flagging analysts whose research behavior and dissent patterns look anomalous for counterintelligence review.
- **⚠️ WHY THIS IS A BAD IDEA — READ THIS FIRST:** It is included to establish the floor and to name a trap this platform's own architecture makes seductive. **(1) The base-rate arithmetic destroys it.** Insider-threat events among cleared analysts are on the order of 10⁻⁵ per person-year. A detector with 99% sensitivity and 99% specificity applied to a 100,000-person cleared population yields roughly 1,000 false positives for every ~1 true positive — a positive predictive value under 0.2%. Every hour spent on that queue is an hour not spent on analysis, and each false positive lands on a real person's clearance. No plausible improvement in the classifier fixes a base rate that low; this is Bayes, not engineering. **(2) It inverts the platform's own core principle.** The calibration layer's dimensional-outlier detector exists to surface *the scenario the consensus underweights on one axis — i.e. the surprise*. It treats the outlier as the most valuable signal in the system. An insider-threat model built on the same math treats the outlier as the suspect. Same statistic, opposite sign, applied to the same people — and the version that punishes outliers will win, because it has a security office behind it. **(3) It is self-defeating commercially.** An analyst who knows the QA assistant also feeds a CI hub will not paste a draft into it, and every other candidate in this catalog depends on analysts pasting drafts into it. Building this poisons the well for C-1 through C-9. **(4) The legal surface is the worst in the catalog** — cleared US persons, Privacy Act, E.O. 12333, ICD 107, whistleblower and Analytic Ombuds protections, and the specific hazard that "unusual dissent from the analytic line" is *both* an ICD 203 virtue (the Alternatives standard) and a naive insider-risk feature.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Which of our analysts show a persistent directional bias relative to the calibrated panel, and which show behavioral anomalies warranting CI review?" — note that these are two entirely different questions that this candidate wrongly fuses, which is itself the design defect.
- **Mission and user:** Insider-threat hubs and CI staffs; DoD's DITMAC, which supports 43 DoD components and 45 insider-threat hubs ([CDSE](https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/INT100-guide.pdf)); continuous-vetting programs under **Trusted Workforce 2.0**, whose enrollment expansion to the non-sensitive public-trust workforce was expected to complete around the end of 2025 ([Trusted Workforce 2.0 progress report](https://assets.performance.gov/files/Trusted_Workforce_2.0_Progress_Report.pdf)). **Today:** hubs correlate adjudicatively-relevant reporting from defined categories; behavioral analytics over analytic work product is not part of that pipeline, and there is a good reason for that.
- **What the N simulants are:** Weak, and the weakness is diagnostic. Runs would vary a bias-and-behavior profile: directional bias magnitude, dissent frequency, query-pattern entropy, dwell time, and a latent malicious-intent flag. **The state variables that actually predict insider threat are life-circumstance and access variables that this platform has no business ingesting, and the ones it *can* see (analytic behavior) are weakly predictive at best.** `riskTolerance` and `escalationPropensity` have no honest mapping here at all — they would be re-labeled as personality proxies, which is precisely the kind of unfalsifiable construct-stretching the rest of this catalog avoids.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Anomaly detection over behavioral feature vectors** — Mahalanobis distance from cohort centroid, isolation forest, or a hidden-Markov model over activity sequences. Honest assessment: these are real algorithms with real math, so this is not literally **NO KERNEL** — but the kernel has **no validated link between its features and the outcome it claims to predict**, which is functionally worse than no kernel, because it produces a precise number nobody can falsify. IARPA's **SCITE** program pursued the more scientifically honest version of this: "active indicators" deliberately introduced into an environment to make threats react differently from non-threats, plus modeling of insider-threat detection-enterprise performance ([IARPA SCITE](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/scite), [ICN](https://intelligencecommunitynews.com/iarpa-posts-baa-for-scientific-advances-to-continuous-insider-threat-evaluation-scite-program/)). Note what SCITE understood that this candidate does not: passive behavioral observation was insufficient, so the program tried to *change the environment* to create separation.
- **What the judge panel is:** Would be devil's advocate, premortem, and a bias-audit judge. **This is where the candidate collapses most visibly: a judge panel adjudicating whether a named person is suspicious is a due-process proceeding wearing a rubric, and the rubric axes (*anomaly magnitude*, *pattern persistence*) are not adjudicative standards.** The panel architecture is designed to adjudicate hypotheses about the world, not allegations about people, and the mismatch is structural rather than fixable by better prompts.
- **The config pack:** Would ship `actors/analyst-behavior-profiles.yaml`, `features/behavioral-feature-set.yaml`, `thresholds/anomaly-alert.yaml`, `rubric/insider-risk.rubric.yaml`. **Recommendation: do not ship these files.** If any bias-correction capability is wanted, ship only the *aggregate, de-identified* half — `rubric/cohort-calibration.rubric.yaml` — and delete the behavioral half entirely rather than flag-gating it, because a flag-gated version of this will be turned on by someone.
- **Data required:** Analyst work product, query logs, dwell telemetry, and — for the CI extension — personnel, financial, and travel records on cleared **US persons**. Classified and legally-encumbered by definition. **No credible unclassified demo exists, and any attempt to build one on real people's behavior would be its own ethics problem.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **UNSCORABLE in the only sense that matters — and this is disqualifying.** The positive class has a base rate near 10⁻⁵, so a statistically meaningful validation would require observing hundreds of true insider-threat events, which will not happen. Synthetic insider-threat data is circular: you get back the behavioral model you injected. Historical case backtests (Hanssen, Ames, Snowden, Manning, Teixeira) are N≈5 with massive hindsight contamination and no matched controls. The *bias-correction* half is scorable — z-normalized calibration against resolved forecasts is exactly C-TRADECRAFT-7 and C-TRADECRAFT-8, done at cohort level — but the CI half is not, and fusing a scorable capability with an unscorable one to borrow its credibility is the specific failure this catalog should refuse to commit.
- **Benefit categories hit:** Claimed: (a) and (c). **Actually delivered: (a) partially and only for the cohort-level bias-correction half, which is already fully covered by C-TRADECRAFT-7 and C-TRADECRAFT-8 without any of this candidate's costs.** The CI half delivers a false-positive queue and calls it a benefit. Net contribution to the catalog: negative.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — **IARPA SCITE**, **DARPA ADAMS** (Anomaly Detection at Multiple Scales, UNVERIFIED as to current status), **DITMAC**, the **NITTF**, and DCSA's continuous-vetting program. Commercial — **Everfox** (formerly Forcepoint Federal), **Exabeam**, **Securonix**, **DTEX Systems** (all UNVERIFIED as to current federal vehicles). **This is a crowded, well-funded, incumbent-occupied market where the platform has no differentiated kernel and a badly mismatched architecture. Competing here would be competing on someone else's terms with a worse product.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** *Technical:* base-rate collapse (quantified above); feature drift; trivial gaming by anyone who knows the features; and the specific pathology that **dissent from the analytic line is simultaneously an ICD 203 virtue and a naive anomaly feature**, so a well-tuned detector would preferentially flag the analysts the Alternatives standard exists to protect. *Ethical/legal red lines — this candidate is on the wrong side of several:* it surveils cleared US persons; it chills protected dissent and interferes with the Analytic Ombuds channel; and it puts adverse clearance consequences behind an unvalidated statistic. **The structural enforcement that keeps the rest of this catalog safe is precisely what this candidate would have to remove:** every other candidate's record types carry *no natural-person identifier field*, so person-level output is not a policy choice but a type error. Building this means adding that field — and once it exists, it is available to every other candidate in the catalog. **That is the concrete reason to refuse it: it is not merely a weak candidate, it is the one candidate whose implementation weakens all the others.**
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** **TRL 2 now. Recommended TRL in 12 months: 2.** Do not advance this. If a customer asks for it, offer C-TRADECRAFT-7 and C-TRADECRAFT-8 at cohort level instead and say plainly why the individual-level version is not scorable.

**Positioning against IARPA REASON:** REASON is the closest funded government effort to C-TRADECRAFT-4 and should be framed as a transition partner, not a competitor. **Claims discipline for the white paper:** every accuracy number in this catalog that comes from the prototype is `synthetic_oracle` regime only. Real-world forecasting skill is not yet measured. The catalog's credibility rests on saying that first, in the same sentence as the +0.44 Brier skill figure — not in a footnote.

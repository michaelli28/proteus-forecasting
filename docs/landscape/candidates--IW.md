- **US persons.** E.O. 12333 §2.3 and its DoD implementation (DoDD 5240.01 / DoD Manual 5240.01) limit collection, retention and dissemination of US-person information and require least-intrusive means; CIA has no domestic internal-security function. Every candidate here is scoped to **foreign audiences, foreign actors, foreign populations**, with the US-person filter enforced in the domain adapter (a hard allowlist of population/corpus geographies) and not in a prompt. No candidate here emits a moderation request; the intervention-effectiveness candidate emits **cost-effectiveness estimates over intervention classes**, not target lists. ### C-IW-1 — Dark Figure: how much of the adversary network are we not seeing, and when does it adapt
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Estimates what fraction of a covert influence network your detectors are actually catching, and simulates how the operator re-tools once you tighten the screws — so an analyst stops reporting "we removed 900 accounts" and starts reporting "we removed 900 of an estimated 3,100–4,400, and the survivors will re-provision in 11–19 days."
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Of the Doppelgänger-affiliated asset base active against German-language audiences as of 1 Feb 2025, what share remains operational 90 days after the 15 Feb takedown wave, and what is the 80% band on the total asset population we never observed?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA Open Source Enterprise / DA covert-influence targeting cell; equally NSA/CYBERCOM foreign-influence analysts and FBI FITF's foreign side. **Today:** an analyst reads a Meta Quarterly Adversarial Threat Report, a Google TAG bulletin and a Graphika PDF, hand-merges the indicator lists in Excel, and reports the union as "the network." There is no estimator of what all three missed, and no model of operator adaptation — the coverage number is literally never computed. ([Meta Q2/Q3 2025](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-Q3-2025-Adversarial-threat-report/), [Google TAG on DRAGONBRIDGE](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/google-disrupted-dragonbridge-activity-q1-2024/))
- **What the N simulants are:** N operator-tradecraft profiles. State variables: asset inventory size, account age-at-activation distribution, posting-time jitter σ, asset reuse ratio ρ across narratives, shared-infrastructure overlap (registrar/ASN/TLS fingerprint), re-provisioning latency after burn, budget per asset. **riskTolerance** = willingness to run assets with tight, detectable coordination fingerprints (low jitter, heavy reuse) in exchange for reach. **escalationPropensity** = re-provisioning aggressiveness after exposure — burn-and-rebuild vs. go quiet. Variation is meaningful: these two knobs move the detection-survival curve by an order of magnitude in the same detector configuration.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** Two coupled pieces. (1) **Stackelberg detection game** — defender commits to a threshold vector τ over coordination features (temporal-synchrony z-score, co-posting Jaccard, infrastructure overlap); the operator best-responds by choosing (σ, ρ) to minimize P(detect) subject to a reach penalty R(σ,ρ); solved by deterministic grid + fixed-point iteration over the bilevel problem, seeded PRNG only for asset-graph draws. (2) **Capture–recapture / multi-list estimation** — Lincoln-Petersen and Chao's heterogeneity-corrected M_th estimator across three quasi-independent detector lists (platform takedown, external researcher report, infrastructure pivot) to produce the dark-figure band. Outcome bands over "% of asset base operational at day 90" and "total-population multiplier."
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over competing explanations of a coverage gap (adversary shrank / adversary adapted / detector drifted / lists are not independent), diagnosticity-weighted. Key-assumptions check on the list-independence assumption — the single assumption that, if violated, invalidates the whole estimate; falsifier required. Devil's advocate argues the network was always this small. Premortem on "we declared 85% coverage and the operation ran uninterrupted." Red team with standing to force a rerun under correlated-list priors. Rubric axes: evasion realism, list-independence defensibility, estimator sensitivity to heterogeneity, operational actionability of the τ recommendation.
- **The config pack:** `actors/doppelganger-2025.yaml`, `actors/spamouflage-2024.yaml` (tradecraft priors); `detectors/coordination-features.yaml` (τ grid, feature definitions); `world/asset-graph.yaml` (SBM parameters for asset↔narrative bipartite graph); `rubric/coverage-estimation.yaml`; `adapters/takedown-lists.ts` (Meta/Google/Graphika/DSA-DB ingest → three-list matrix); `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when list overlap < k or when only one list exists).
- **Data required:** **Unclassified and sufficient for a full demo.** Meta Quarterly Adversarial Threat Reports, Google TAG bulletins, OpenAI threat reports, EU DSA Transparency Database Research API (statement-of-reasons records, 6-month rolling window), Graphika/EU DisinfoLab/VIGINUM published indicator sets, DNS/WHOIS/certificate-transparency pivots. **Genuinely classified:** SIGINT confirming the operator's true inventory (the only way to ground-truth the dark figure outside synthetic worlds) and any tasking/funding chain. ([DSA Research API](https://transparency.dsa.ec.europa.eu/page/research-api?lang=en))
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** Three-tier. (a) **Synthetic closed world** — generate an asset population of known size K, run three imperfect detectors with configurable dependence, score the estimator's coverage of K (this is the honest primary oracle: ground truth did not exist before the run). (b) **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff** — Doppelgänger against the German federal election of 23 Feb 2025: cut the corpus at 1 Feb 2025, predict post-takedown survival, resolve against the Viginum May 2025 Storm-1516 report and subsequent Meta/EU disclosures. (c) **Live post-cutoff** — resolvable against the next Meta adversarial threat report's disclosed asset counts for a named operation. Scorable. ([Viginum/Storm-1516 coverage](https://www.cyfluence-research.org/post/storm-1516-viginum-report-insights), [Correctiv on German election ops](https://correctiv.org/en/fact-checking-en/2025/01/24/disinformation-operation-russian-meddling-in-german-election-campaign-exposed/))
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** replaces an uncomputed number with a banded estimate carrying a confidence field — accuracy gain is the difference between "unknown" and "quantified." **(c)** directly reconciles source discrepancies: the three-list matrix *is* a discrepancy reconciler, and disagreement between Meta's and Graphika's asset counts becomes an input, not an embarrassment. **(d)** the Excel merge of indicator lists is exactly the repetitive task automated.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government program: **DARPA INCAS** (detect/characterize/track influence campaigns with quantified confidence — but INCAS is sensemaking of messaging, not population-size estimation of the asset base). Closest commercial: **Graphika** (network mapping) and **Alethea** (asset attribution); neither publishes a dark-figure estimator — this is the gap. ([INCAS](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/influence-campaign-awareness-and-sensemaking))
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically it fails when the three lists are strongly dependent (all three derive from the same seed indicator set), which biases the population estimate downward — the abstention gate must fire on measured list dependence, and this is the single most likely silent failure. It also fails if the operator's re-provisioning is human-discretionary rather than rate-driven. **Red lines:** asset graphs must be foreign-operated; the adapter refuses ingestion of any list containing accounts geolocated to US persons, and the output is population-level only — no individual account is scored, named, or ranked, so nothing here is a targeting product. A US government customer **can** buy this.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 / 6. The capture-recapture and Stackelberg pieces are textbook; the work is the adapter and the synthetic-world validation.

### C-IW-2 — Belief Drift: forecasting narrative adoption in a named foreign population
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Forecasts what share of a specific foreign population will hold a specific measurable belief by a specific date, given an adversary campaign's observed tempo — and gives the answer as a probability band against a real survey question that will actually be asked.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "What share of Moldovan Russian-speaking respondents will agree that 'the EU accession process threatens Moldova's sovereignty' in the next IRI/Barometer wave, and what is the 80% band?"
- **Mission and user:** DIA/CIA regional analysts writing on Moldova, Georgia, the Sahel, Taiwan; State's European and East Asian bureaus; EUCOM/AFRICOM J2. **Today:** the analyst reads the last survey wave, reads a Doublethink Lab or EUvsDisinfo narrative report, and writes "we assess that PRC/Russian narratives are likely gaining traction" — a directional claim with no number, no band, and no falsifier. Nothing in that workflow is scored when the next survey wave lands.
- **What the N simulants are:** N population/exposure models. State variables per segment: segment size, prior belief distribution over the resolvable quantity, bounded-confidence threshold ε (how far a message can sit from your view and still move you), stubbornness weight λ_i (Friedkin-Johnsen anchoring to initial opinion), homophily coefficient, media-diet mix (state TV / Telegram / TikTok / diaspora channel), elite-cue coefficient, and campaign exposure rate. **riskTolerance** = adversary's willingness to push extreme claims that buy reach but trigger backfire and rejection outside ε. **escalationPropensity** = volume/tempo ramp toward the resolution date. Meaningful variation: ε and the elite-cue coefficient alone swing terminal adoption by 20+ points.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Opinion dynamics on a network** — Deffuant–Weisbuch bounded-confidence updates with Friedkin-Johnsen stubbornness anchors, run over a stochastic block model calibrated to segment sizes and cross-segment tie density, with an SEIZ-style (Susceptible-Exposed-Infected-skeptic) exposure layer driving encounter rates from observed posting volume. Fully deterministic given seed: the PRNG draws the network and encounter order, the update rule is closed-form. Output is a distribution over "% of segment above belief threshold at date D," binned into the framer's mutually exclusive outcome bands.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over competing drivers (adversary campaign / domestic elite cues / economic shock / measurement artifact in the survey instrument). Key-assumptions check on the survey-item-to-model-quantity mapping — every assumption carries a required falsifier ("if the next wave changes question wording, this forecast is void"). Devil's advocate: the population already believed this and the campaign is following opinion, not leading it. Premortem on "we forecast 12-point drift and the wave showed 1 point." Rubric axes: instrument-fidelity, exposure-estimate credibility, backfire modeling, segment-boundary defensibility.
- **The config pack:** `populations/md-ru-speakers.yaml`, `populations/tw-general.yaml` (segments, priors, ε, media diet); `actors/kremlin-moldova-2025.yaml`; `instruments/iri-moldova-q17.yaml` (the exact survey item and its response scale — this is what makes the forecast resolvable); `rubric/belief-drift.yaml`; `adapters/survey-waves.ts`; `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when no scheduled instrument exists within the horizon).
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo is fully possible.** Survey series with published schedules: Eurobarometer, Afrobarometer, Arab Barometer, Latinobarómetro, IRI/NDI Balkan and Caucasus waves, Reuters Institute Digital News Report, and the OSI-Sofia Media Literacy Index for population-level resilience priors. Campaign-tempo inputs: GDELT 2.0, Media Cloud, EUvsDisinfo, Doublethink Lab, EEAS FIMI reports. **Genuinely classified:** the adversary's own audience-research and internal targeting priorities (which segments they *believe* are movable). ([Media Literacy Index 2026 methodology](https://osis.bg/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Media-Literacy-Index-2026.pdf), [EEAS 4th FIMI report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-annual-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en))
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with hard as-of cutoff** is the primary oracle and it is strong here because survey series are dated: cut at wave t, forecast wave t+1, score Brier and calibration against the published result. Named candidate episodes: Moldova's 2024 EU referendum cycle; Taiwan's 13 Jan 2024 presidential election with Doublethink Lab's contemporaneous narrative tracking; Germany Feb 2025. **Live post-cutoff resolution** against the next scheduled wave of a named instrument — the cleanest live oracle in this whole catalog. Scorable, and the leakage guard (as-of retrieval + runtime assertion on evidence dates) is exactly what makes the backtest credible. ([Doublethink Lab 2024 Taiwan observation](https://medium.com/doublethinklab/2024-taiwan-elections-foreign-influence-observation-preliminary-statement-caeeccb5b88e))
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(b)** time-to-analysis and prediction accuracy — converts a weeks-long qualitative assessment into a banded forecast with a scored track record. **(c)** when two sources disagree about whether a narrative is spreading, the model turns the disagreement into diagnosticity weights rather than a hedge. **(a)** the ICD 203 likelihood/confidence split stops the classic collapse of "likely" and "we're not sure" into one word.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government program: **DARPA SocialSim** (high-fidelity simulation of information spread and evolution; concluded after 4+ years) and **DARPA INCAS** for the sensemaking half. Closest commercial: **Blackbird.AI** and **PeakMetrics** (narrative tracking — they measure spread, they do not forecast belief). ([SocialSim](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/computational-simulation-of-online-social-behavior))
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Fails when the survey instrument changes wording or sampling frame between waves (silent, and fatal — hence the required falsifier). Fails on elite-cue-dominated environments where one leader's statement swamps diffusion. Bounded-confidence models are notoriously easy to overfit to a single episode; the ensemble-diversity and inter-run-stability metrics are the guard. **Red lines:** populations are foreign-only, enforced by an allowlist in `adapters/survey-waves.ts` — no US population config ships, and the model outputs aggregate shares only, never individual-level susceptibility. The obvious misuse (use the forecast to select a persuasion target) is blocked because the artifact contains no message content, only numeric positions. A US government customer **can** buy this for foreign populations.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 / 6.

### C-IW-3 — Resilience Playbook: what a counter-campaign dollar actually buys
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Given a fixed budget and a menu of defensive options — prebunking ads, media-literacy programming, provenance labeling, delisting, public attribution — it estimates how many points of narrative adoption each portfolio prevents in a named foreign country, with the cost attached.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "With $2M and 120 days before the vote, which portfolio of interventions produces the largest expected reduction in adoption of narrative N among the target segment in Country C, and what is the probability that the best portfolio beats doing nothing by more than 5 percentage points?"
- **Mission and user:** State (public diplomacy, post-R/FIMI residual), USAID successors' information-integrity programming, allied partners (Sweden's MPF, Taiwan, Baltic ministries) via foreign-assistance channels, and the COCOM information-operations planner assessing partner-nation programs. **Today:** portfolio choice is made by anecdote and vendor pitch. Nobody computes the marginal point-of-adoption per dollar, because nobody has a model that takes an intervention effect size as input.
- **What the N simulants are:** N (defender posture × adversary counter-adaptation) pairs. Defender state: budget, intervention mix vector, latency-to-act, attribution posture (silent / private notify / public naming). Adversary state carries the same knobs as C-IW-2: **riskTolerance** = claim extremity under a labeling regime (labels punish falsifiable claims, so high-risk operators lose more), **escalationPropensity** = re-seeding rate after delisting. What varies is the *interaction*: an intervention that works against a low-escalation operator can be net-negative against a high-escalation one that treats takedowns as free credibility.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Intervention-augmented cascade + constrained portfolio optimization.** Base layer is an independent-cascade / linear-threshold diffusion on the C-IW-2 network. Interventions are operators on that process with empirically-anchored parameters: prebunking raises the adoption threshold by a sampled effect drawn from the published RCT distribution (the Jigsaw/Cambridge YouTube field study measured roughly **5–10 percentage points** in manipulation-technique recognition; an Instagram study reported a much larger 21-point effect — the spread between those two is itself modeled as parameter uncertainty); labeling multiplies share-through probability; downranking multiplies edge activation; takedown removes nodes with a re-provisioning delay; provenance/C2PA deployment shifts the credibility weight of unsigned media. Then a **submodular greedy / integer-knapsack** solve over portfolios subject to budget B, with the diffusion re-run per candidate portfolio. Outcome bands over "adoption points prevented per $100k." ([Cambridge/Jigsaw field study](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/inoculateexperiment), [Traberg/Roozenbeek/van der Linden review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00027162221087936))
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "why did adoption fall" (our intervention / adversary self-limited / news cycle / measurement). Key-assumptions check with mandatory falsifiers on every transported effect size — the central intellectual risk is assuming a lab/US-sample RCT effect transports to a Sahelian Telegram audience. Devil's advocate argues the whole portfolio is dominated by doing nothing. Premortem on backfire: public attribution amplifying the narrative it names. Red team with standing to force a rerun with effect sizes shrunk to the low end of the published CI. Rubric axes: effect-size transportability, cost-model realism, backfire modeling, counterfactual credibility.
- **The config pack:** `interventions/catalog.yaml` (each intervention: unit cost, latency, measured effect distribution with citation, decay half-life); `budgets/scenario.yaml`; `populations/*.yaml` (shared with C-IW-2); `actors/*.yaml`; `rubric/intervention-portfolio.yaml`; `adapters/rct-effects.ts` (loads effect sizes with their source and sample provenance — an intervention with no cited measurement cannot be scored, only abstained on); `thresholds/backfire.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo possible and, unusually, the effect sizes are public.** Published prebunking/inoculation RCTs; EU DSA Transparency Database for observed moderation volumes and action types; platform transparency reports for downranking scale; C2PA/Content Credentials adoption data; OSI-Sofia Media Literacy Index and Reuters Institute news-avoidance data as population priors. **Genuinely classified:** nothing required — which is a selling point. **Restricted (not classified):** platform-internal experiment results.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** (a) **Synthetic oracle** for the optimizer (does greedy find the known-optimal portfolio in a closed world). (b) **Historical backtest** against natural experiments with as-of cutoffs: Taiwan's pre-2024-election prebunking mobilization, the EU's Doppelgänger domain-seizure and sanction actions, Google's DRAGONBRIDGE disruption waves (Google reported disrupting **over 10,000 instances in Q1 2024** and **over 50,000 in 2022**, giving a dosed time series against observed reach). (c) **Live post-cutoff:** commit a portfolio forecast before an allied partner's programmed campaign and resolve against its own measurement plan. Scorable, with the honest caveat that intervention effects are the noisiest quantity in this catalog. ([Google TAG 2024](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/google-disrupted-dragonbridge-activity-q1-2024/), [Google TAG 2022](https://blog.google/threat-analysis-group/over-50000-instances-of-dragonbridge-activity-disrupted-in-2022/))
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(d)** cost reduction is the explicit output unit — points prevented per dollar. **(b)** replaces a months-long inter-agency argument about programming with a scored comparison. **(c)** the required-falsifier discipline surfaces exactly where two vendors' claimed effect sizes disagree.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **State GEC / R/FIMI** (now closed — its counter-messaging portfolio decisions were exactly this problem, made without this tool) and the **EEAS FIMI Toolbox / FIMI Deterrence Playbook**, which explicitly frames "raising costs" but does not compute them. Closest commercial/philanthropic: **Google Jigsaw** prebunking (the effect-size source, not a planning tool). ([EEAS 4th report](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-annual-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en))
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Effect-size transportability is the killer; a plausible-looking portfolio built on a US undergraduate sample can be worthless in Mali, and the model will not know unless the config forces population-match metadata. Backfire is under-modeled in the literature generally. **Red lines:** this is the candidate closest to the *Murthy* fault line — it must never emit a recommendation to a platform about specific content, and must never be instantiated against a US population. Structural enforcement: the intervention catalog contains no "request removal of item X" operator at all — only class-level rate parameters — and the population allowlist excludes US geographies. Smith-Mundt applies to any actual messaging that follows; the tool produces no messages. A US government customer **can** buy the analysis; the customer **cannot** lawfully use it to shape domestic US information flows, and the config makes that instantiation unrepresentable.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 2 / 5.

### C-IW-4 — Adversary Campaign COA Prediction, conditioned on our own posture
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Predicts which of several concrete information-campaign courses of action a named adversary will actually run in the next 90 days — and how that choice changes if we publicly attribute, quietly notify platforms, sanction, or do nothing.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "In the 90 days before Country C's next national vote, which DISARM technique cluster will dominate the observed GRU-linked activity — (i) fabricated-media impersonation of legitimate outlets, (ii) hack-and-leak, (iii) paid local proxies, (iv) AI-assisted volume flooding, (v) no significant activity — and how do those five probabilities shift under each of our four response postures?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA DA adversary-decision analysts and the NIC; DIA and COCOM J2/J39 planners; this is the candidate that most literally answers the solicitation's "inform and validate strategic decision-making, tactical execution, and adaptive behavior using adversary decision-making processes." **Today:** COA prediction is a whiteboard exercise plus a red-cell memo; the coupling to blue posture is asserted narratively and never scored.
- **What the N simulants are:** N adversary-planner profiles. State: resource endowment (assets, budget, contractor bench), doctrinal preference weights over DISARM tactic families, discount rate over time-to-effect, belief about blue detection capability, exposure-cost sensitivity (sanctions, indictment, expulsion), and a memory of prior blue actions. **riskTolerance** = the weight on detection hazard inside the utility function — a low-risk-tolerance planner routes around techniques with high attribution likelihood. **escalationPropensity** = probability of climbing to higher-rung techniques (forged official documents, hack-and-leak, incitement-adjacent content) after a blue action. These two knobs are the model; the candidate is strong precisely because the platform's existing knobs are the natural parameters here.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Quantal-response / bounded-rational planner over a DISARM TTP directed graph.** Each COA is a path through the DISARM tactic-technique DAG with an associated cost c(path), expected reach r(path), and detection hazard h(path | blue posture). The planner selects COA k with probability ∝ exp(λ · U_k), U_k = r_k − θ·c_k − φ·h_k·(exposure cost). Blue posture is a vector that shifts h and the political payoff term. Deterministic given seed and λ; Monte Carlo only over parameter uncertainty. Outcome bands over "probability technique cluster k is the dominant observed pattern in window W" — resolvable by counting DISARM-coded technique observations in post-hoc reporting. ([DISARM framework](https://www.debunk.org/disarm-framework), [DISARM as a disinformation ATT&CK analogue](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362200075_DISARM_a_Framework_for_Analysis_of_Disinformation_Campaigns))
- **What the judge panel is:** The full panel, and this is where the panel earns its keep. ACH ranks the five COAs by weighted inconsistency using diagnosticity weights over observed indicators (infrastructure staging, persona seeding, contractor hiring signals). Key-assumptions check on the adversary's assumed objective function — the classic mirror-imaging failure. Devil's advocate runs the "they will do the boring thing they always do" case, which the record supports: Microsoft's MTAC observed that many actors **pivoted back from generative AI to simple digital manipulation and mislabeled content**. Premortem on a surprise sixth COA. Red team with standing to force a bounded rerun under a different objective function. Rubric axes: adversary-model fidelity, indicator diagnosticity, blue-posture coupling realism, surprise coverage. ([MTAC Aug 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/security-insider/threat-landscape/iran-steps-into-us-election-2024-with-cyber-enabled-influence-operations))
- **The config pack:** `actors/gru-storm1516.yaml`, `actors/prc-spamouflage.yaml`, `actors/irgc-storm2035.yaml` (endowment, doctrine weights, exposure sensitivity, observed history); `ttp/disarm-graph.yaml` (the DAG with cost/reach/hazard per technique); `postures/blue.yaml` (four response postures); `rubric/coa-prediction.yaml`; `adapters/disarm-coding.ts` (map incident reports to technique codes); `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when fewer than k independent indicator classes are observed).
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo possible** using DISARM-coded incident corpora: EEAS FIMI threat reports (which publish incident-level TTP analysis), EU DisinfoLab's Doppelgänger hub, VIGINUM reports, Microsoft MTAC election reports, Meta/Google/OpenAI takedown reports, Recorded Future and Clemson Media Forensics Hub publications. **Genuinely classified:** the adversary's internal approval chain and resourcing decisions — HUMINT/SIGINT on who signs off on escalation, which is exactly the input that would move this from a good forecast to a decisive one. The candidate is honest about that: the unclassified version forecasts behavior, the classified version forecasts intent.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff** across a rich set of resolved episodes, each with published post-hoc TTP coding: Germany (23 Feb 2025), Romania's annulled 2024 presidential round, Moldova 2024–2025, Poland and Czechia 2025, Côte d'Ivoire 2025, Ghana's Dec 2024 presidential (where OpenAI disrupted covert activity), Taiwan (Jan 2024). Cut the corpus 60–90 days before each vote; score Brier and coverage@N against the coded observations. **Live post-cutoff** on the next scheduled national vote inside the horizon (specific 2026–27 election dates: **UNVERIFIED**, must be pinned per instance). Scorable, with a real risk that post-hoc TTP coding is itself noisy — Krippendorff alpha across independent coders is the guard and the platform already computes it.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(b)** the headline: time-to-analysis on a COA memo drops from weeks to a run, and the prediction is scored. **(a)** the ICD 203 separation of likelihood and confidence prevents the standard COA-memo failure of presenting a 55/45 call in the register of certainty. **(c)** where MTAC, EEAS and a vendor disagree on what an actor is doing, ACH diagnosticity turns the disagreement into evidence weight.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **DARPA INCAS** (characterize and track campaigns) and the **EEAS FIMI Situational Awareness Hub**; neither publishes a predictive COA model. Closest commercial: **Recorded Future** and **Nisos** (threat intelligence with TTP framing, retrospective). The forward-looking, blue-posture-conditioned COA distribution is the white space.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Mirror-imaging is the dominant technical failure: a utility function written by a Western analyst predicts a Western planner. Second failure: the DISARM DAG is a taxonomy, not a physics, and technique costs are guesses — the model's rank ordering can be right while its probabilities are badly calibrated (which the ECE/reliability-diagram metrics will expose, and should be reported prominently). **Red lines:** the actor set is foreign state and state-proxy actors only; no individual is modeled, and the output describes technique clusters, never people. Because the artifact is a probability vector over technique classes with no content, it cannot be repurposed into a targeting or messaging product. A US government customer **can** buy this. The adjacent thing they **cannot** buy from this platform is the mirror-image capability — planning *our* influence COAs — which would sit under Title 50 covert action and is structurally unreachable since the kernel emits no content.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 / 6. Highest customer pull in the catalog.

### C-IW-5 — Did the campaign actually move anything: dose-response against a real-world outcome
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Estimates how much of a change in a real-world outcome — protest counts, turnout, vaccination uptake, currency confidence, recruitment — is attributable to an adversary information campaign rather than to everything else that was happening, and reports the honest answer including "we cannot tell."
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Of the 9-point drop in measles vaccination coverage in Region R between Q1 and Q4, how many points are attributable to the identified anti-vaccine campaign, with an 80% credible interval — and is zero inside that interval?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA DA and NIC analysts asked the "so what" question by a policymaker; DIA assessing whether an adversary's campaign is worth a response; State assessing whether a partner's information environment is degrading. **Today:** the honest answer is "we assess the campaign contributed," a sentence that has survived decades precisely because it is unfalsifiable. The manual process being displaced is a literature-and-anecdote memo with no counterfactual.
- **What the N simulants are:** N causal-world hypotheses — and I will be honest that this candidate stretches the platform's two-knob frame. State variables: assumed effect size prior, confounder set, lag structure, dose operationalization (volume / reach / breakout-scale category), donor-pool composition for the synthetic control. The knobs enter through the dose path: **riskTolerance** governs claim extremity, which maps to a higher-variance dose with backfire; **escalationPropensity** governs the dose ramp. But the primary variation across runs is *causal structure*, not actor psychology. That is a genuine mismatch with the platform's actor-centric framing and should be stated in the proposal rather than papered over.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Bayesian structural time series with synthetic control.** State-space model: local level + seasonal component + regression on a donor pool of comparable untreated units; Kalman filter/smoother for the counterfactual; the estimand is cumulative deviation of observed from counterfactual over the campaign window. Deterministic given seed (PRNG only for posterior draws). Dose enters as a time-varying regressor built from campaign-volume telemetry. A forward mode simulates the counterfactual under continued vs. halted campaign. Outcome bands over "attributable change in Y, in the unit of Y."
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over rival causes (campaign / domestic political shock / economic shock / supply disruption / measurement change). Key-assumptions check on donor-pool exogeneity — the one assumption that, if the donors were also treated, silently destroys the estimate; falsifier required. Devil's advocate makes the reverse-causation case (the campaign followed the sentiment it claims to have caused — the same critique that sank many 2016-era impact claims). Premortem on "we attributed 9 points and a health-system audit later explained 8 of them." Red team can force a rerun with a placebo treatment date. Rubric axes: donor-pool validity, dose-measure validity, placebo-test performance, honest width of the interval.
- **The config pack:** `outcomes/measles-coverage.yaml`, `outcomes/acled-protest-counts.yaml`, `outcomes/fx-confidence.yaml` (each: series source, unit, seasonality, donor pool); `dose/campaign-volume.yaml`; `rubric/causal-attribution.yaml`; `adapters/acled.ts`, `adapters/gdelt.ts`; `thresholds/abstention.yaml` — and this is the candidate where the abstention gate matters most: INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE should be the modal verdict when the donor pool is thin or the campaign window overlaps a major confounder.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo possible.** Outcome series: ACLED (protest/violence events, 200+ countries, real-time, API, free for researchers), WHO/UNICEF immunization estimates, national electoral commissions, IMF/central-bank FX and reserve series, V-Dem and Freedom House for institutional covariates. Dose series: GDELT 2.0, Media Cloud, EUvsDisinfo, platform takedown volumes. **Genuinely classified:** almost nothing — another selling point. ([ACLED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Conflict_Location_and_Event_Data), [ACLED on HDX](https://data.humdata.org/organization/acled))
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** (a) **Synthetic oracle is the strongest tier here** — inject a known effect of known size into a simulated outcome series with realistic confounding and score recovery; false-positive rate on null injections is the headline metric, and the platform's measured abstention behavior (80/80 on a provably-unanswerable arm) is the right precedent. (b) **Historical backtest** against episodes with an independently-established answer: the documented near-zero engagement of Spamouflage/DRAGONBRIDGE despite enormous volume is a superb null case — a system that attributes real-world effect to Spamouflage is broken, and Graphika, Google and Clemson have all documented the null. (c) **Live post-cutoff** on a named campaign and a named outcome series with a published release date. Scorable, and the null cases are what make it credible. ([Spamouflage engagement failure — Graphika](https://www.graphika.com/reports/spamouflage-dragon-goes-to-america), [Clemson 5-year Spam](https://open.clemson.edu/mfh_ci_reports/7/), [Brookings Breakout Scale](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Nimmo_influence_operations_PDF.pdf))
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(c)** the strongest QA candidate in the catalog — it is a machine for finding out that a widely-repeated claim of campaign impact is not supported, which is precisely "identify discrepancies between sources and reconcile differences in analytic confidence." **(a)** accuracy: replaces an unfalsifiable sentence with an interval that can be wrong. **(b)** compresses a counterfactual analysis that today takes an academic quarter.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **IARPA's forecasting lineage** (Open Source Indicators, Mercury, HFC) is adjacent but forecasts events rather than attributing effects; **UNVERIFIED** whether any current IC program does campaign→outcome causal attribution. Closest commercial: brand-lift / marketing-mix modeling vendors (Nielsen, Kantar) doing structurally identical math for advertising, and **Graphika**'s qualitative impact assessment. The Breakout Scale is the closest published *rubric*, and it deliberately avoids measuring belief change because Nimmo judged it impractical for operational researchers — this candidate is the argument that it is now practical for a narrow class of outcomes with published series.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Confounding is the whole game and it will sometimes lose; the honest failure mode is a confident attribution that a later audit reverses. Dose mismeasurement (counting posts as reach) biases toward false positives. Donor-pool contamination is silent. **Red lines:** outcomes must be foreign; the model consumes aggregate published series only, never individual-level behavioral data, so no US-persons exposure arises. There is a real ethical trap adjacent to this candidate — an impact estimator is exactly the measurement layer an *offensive* campaign would want — and the structural answer is that the kernel takes an observed dose series as input and cannot generate one, and emits no content.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 / 5.

### C-IW-6 — Attribution Support: probability over actor sets, with the diagnosticity shown
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Turns attribution from a house judgment into an auditable probability over competing candidate actors, showing exactly which indicators moved the number and how much the answer changes if one indicator is wrong.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Given the observed infrastructure, linguistic, temporal and behavioral indicators of campaign X, what is P(actor = A) vs P(actor = B) vs P(actor = commercial contractor working for either) vs P(unattributable), and which single indicator, if reversed, would flip the ranking?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA/FBI counterintelligence attribution cells; NSA/CYBERCOM foreign-influence attribution; the interagency body that has to decide whether a public naming is defensible. **Today:** attribution is a narrative memo with a confidence adjective; the sensitivity of the judgment to any one indicator is not computed, and when a partner service disagrees there is no shared arithmetic to argue over.
- **What the N simulants are:** N candidate-actor hypotheses, each a tradecraft profile. State: infrastructure-reuse propensity, registrar/hosting preferences, work-rhythm signature (timezone-consistent posting), language-model usage patterns, contractor-vs-service organizational structure, historical technique repertoire, willingness to leave false flags. **riskTolerance** = OPSEC sloppiness (a high-tolerance actor reuses infrastructure and gets caught); **escalationPropensity** = operational tempo. **Honest weakness:** the variation here is across *hypotheses*, not across dynamical trajectories, so this candidate uses less of the simulation machinery than any other except the BAD one. It uses the adjudication and calibration layers fully.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Bayesian evidence update with explicit likelihood ratios**, plus **Chao capture-recapture on shared infrastructure**. Each indicator i contributes LR_i = P(i | actor A)/P(i | actor B), with indicator reliability drawn from Beta priors; Monte Carlo over reliability gives the posterior band; a deterministic one-at-a-time sensitivity sweep produces the "which indicator flips it" answer. Infrastructure overlap between the campaign and known actor-attributed campaigns is estimated with the same multi-list estimator as C-IW-1. Outcome bands over posterior P(actor set). This is Bayes plus structured sensitivity analysis — **it is a kernel, but a thin one**, and the proposal should say so rather than dress it up as a simulation.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH is the natural fit and here it is the product, not a wrapper: Heuer's algorithm ranking actor hypotheses by weighted inconsistency is literally the tradecraft standard for attribution. Key-assumptions check on indicator independence (correlated indicators double-count and inflate confidence — the single most common attribution error). Devil's advocate runs the false-flag hypothesis with standing. Premortem on a public naming that a partner service publicly contradicts. Red team can force a rerun with the two strongest indicators removed. Rubric axes: indicator independence, false-flag treatment, LR defensibility, sensitivity transparency.
- **The config pack:** `hypotheses/actor-sets.yaml`; `indicators/catalog.yaml` (each indicator: definition, LR priors per actor, reliability prior, source); `rubric/attribution.yaml`; `adapters/infra-pivots.ts` (WHOIS/CT/ASN); `adapters/stylometry.ts` (interface only — see red lines); `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain unless ≥k independent indicator families are present — the correct default answer for most campaigns is "unattributable on this evidence").
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo possible** on retrospective campaigns: published indicator sets from Meta, Google TAG, Microsoft MTAC, VIGINUM, EU DisinfoLab's Doppelgänger hub, Clemson's Media Forensics Hub (which first identified Storm-1516 in Dec 2023, later named by Microsoft's MTAC — a clean two-source attribution episode). **Genuinely classified:** the indicators that actually decide hard cases — SIGINT on tasking, financial flows, HUMINT. The unclassified version is a credible demo and an honest floor, not the operational article.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** (a) **Synthetic** — generate campaigns from a known actor generator with configurable false-flag rate; score posterior calibration and, critically, the false-attribution rate at each confidence level. (b) **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff** on campaigns later attributed by an independent authority: Storm-1516 (Clemson Dec 2023 → MTAC naming → VIGINUM May 2025), Storm-2035 (Microsoft Aug 2024 → OpenAI account takedown Aug 2024, an independent second list), Endless Mayfly (attributed by Meta in 2025 to Iran's IUVM). Cut evidence at the first-report date, score against the later authoritative naming. (c) SME panel for the hard residue. Scorable. ([Meta Q2/Q3 2025 on Endless Mayfly/IUVM](https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q2-Q3-2025-Adversarial-threat-report/), [OpenAI on Storm-2035](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-covert-iranian-influence-operation/))
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(c)** the flagship QA use: when two services disagree on attribution, this produces the diagnosticity table that localizes the disagreement to a specific indicator. **(a)** accuracy via measured false-attribution rate at confidence level — a number the community currently does not have. **(d)** the sensitivity sweep that an analyst does by hand for three indicators is done for forty.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **IARPA HIATUS** (human-interpretable authorship attribution with explainable AI — TA2 is literally interpretable attribution) and **DARPA SemaFor**'s attribution task area for media. Closest commercial: **Nisos**, **Recorded Future**, **Alethea**. This candidate should be positioned as the *adjudication layer above* HIATUS-class attribution engines, not a competitor to them. ([HIATUS](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hiatus), [SemaFor](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/semantic-forensics))
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Correlated indicators inflating confidence is the dominant silent failure; a false-flag-capable adversary is the adversarial failure, and the devil's-advocate judge is the only defense. **Red lines — the sharpest in the catalog:** authorship attribution applied to a US person is a First Amendment anonymity problem and, for CIA, outside mission. Structural enforcement: `adapters/stylometry.ts` operates only on documents whose corpus manifest is tagged foreign-origin, and the hypothesis space contains **organizations and states, never natural persons** — the config schema has no person type, so an individual-attribution instance is unrepresentable. A US government customer **can** buy the organizational version; the individual-author version against US persons is one they cannot lawfully deploy domestically and should not be offered.

### C-IW-8 — Synthetic Media Surge: verification capacity, adjudication latency, and the liar's dividend
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Models a verification cell as a queue — how many disputed images, audio clips and videos arrive, how long each takes to adjudicate, how far behind the cell falls during a surge, and how much authentic material gets wrongly doubted while the queue is backed up.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "During the 10 days around Event E, at an arrival rate of λ disputed items/day with our current 6-analyst forensic cell, what is the median and 90th-percentile time-to-adjudication for a priority item, what share of items are never adjudicated, and what is the resulting share of *authentic* items publicly doubted (the liar's dividend)?"
- **Mission and user:** The media-forensics cell inside an all-source shop; DIA/NGA imagery verification; State's embassy public-affairs sections during a crisis; allied partners (Taiwan, Baltic states) planning election-period surge staffing. **Today:** staffing a verification cell for an election is done by gut and last-cycle's headcount. When the cell saturates, the failure is invisible — items simply age out unadjudicated and nobody counts them.
- **What the N simulants are:** N surge profiles. State: generation rate by modality (image / audio / video / text-with-media), targeting concentration (one head of state vs. dispersed), quality distribution (which determines forensic service time), base rate of authentic-but-disputed items, and platform propagation speed. **riskTolerance** = the operator's willingness to fake high-salience, high-verification-attention targets — heads of state, which draw fast forensic response and blowback — versus low-salience local targets that get no attention. **escalationPropensity** = the ramp rate as the event approaches. Both knobs move the queue's arrival process, which is what the kernel consumes.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Queueing plus a trust ODE.** (1) M/G/c priority queue: arrivals from the surge profile, service times drawn from a measured forensic-analysis time distribution per modality, c servers with priority classes and an abandonment (age-out) process; compute waiting-time distribution, utilization, and abandonment rate — closed-form where the M/M/c approximation holds, discrete-event simulation with seeded PRNG otherwise. (2) A two-compartment trust ODE tracking the share of authentic items publicly doubted as a function of adjudication latency and detector false-positive rate. Detector accuracy is not assumed: it is a parameter drawn from the measured **cross-dataset generalization gap** — models trained on FaceForensics++/Celeb-DF lose on the order of 10–15% and in some transfers up to ~50% of discriminative power in the wild, and transformer architectures degrade less (~11%) than CNNs (>15%). Key-assumptions check on the transported detector accuracy — assuming benchmark AUC in the field is the assumption that kills this analysis, and it needs a hard falsifier. Devil's advocate: the queue is irrelevant because the audience has already moved on before any adjudication lands. Premortem on "we sized the cell correctly and still lost, because the liar's dividend does not depend on adjudication at all." Rubric axes: service-time realism, detector-accuracy transportability, triage-policy realism, decision-relevance of the latency target.
- **The config pack:** `surges/election-window.yaml`, `surges/crisis-72h.yaml`; `cell/staffing.yaml` (servers, shifts, priority rules, age-out policy); `detectors/accuracy.yaml` (per-modality accuracy distributions with the benchmark→field degradation factor and its citation); `rubric/verification-capacity.yaml`; `adapters/deepfake-benchmarks.ts`; `thresholds/abstention.yaml`.

([CISA/NSA content-credentials advisory](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jan/29/2003634788/-1/-1/0/CSI-CONTENT-CREDENTIALS.PDF), [C2PA 2.2 spec](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/_attachments/C2PA_Specification.pdf), [DeepfakeBench](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/0e735e4b4f07de483cbe250130992726-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf))
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** (a) **Synthetic oracle** for the queue — a discrete-event simulator validated against closed-form M/M/c results is a hard-verifiable component, unusual in this catalog. (b) **Historical backtest**: reconstruct the arrival stream around a documented surge — the AI-generated deepfake wave targeting German politicians before the 23 Feb 2025 federal election, or the Storm-1679 Paris Olympics campaign — and score predicted vs. observed fact-check publication latency using the timestamped output of AFP/Reuters/EDMO. (c) **Live post-cutoff** during a partner's election window, scoring predicted latency percentiles against the cell's own ticket log. Scorable; the liar's-dividend compartment is the weakest link and should be reported at lower confidence than the queue — the platform's weakest-link confidence aggregation does this automatically.

- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **DARPA SemaFor** (and its MediFor predecessor) — detection/attribution/characterization of falsified media, now extended through a CRADA with UL Research Institutes' Digital Safety Research Institute; SemaFor builds detectors, it does not model the *operational capacity* of the humans consuming them. Closest commercial: **Reality Defender**, **Hive AI**, **Truepic** (detection and provenance vendors; DoD contract specifics **UNVERIFIED**). The capacity-planning layer above the detectors is unoccupied. ([SemaFor](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/semantic-forensics), [DARPA 2025 deepfake update](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/furthering-deepfake-defenses))

### C-IW-9 — Retrieval Poisoning: forecasting how much of the IC's own AI answer surface an adversary owns
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Forecasts how much adversary-laundered content will end up cited in your own AI assistants' answers on a given topic six months from now, given the adversary's publishing rate and your index's refresh and delisting behavior — a defensive model of the customer's own analytic infrastructure.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "For topic T (e.g., NATO force posture in the Baltics), what share of retrieval-augmented answers produced by our analytic assistant will cite at least one laundered domain at month 6, under (i) current index policy, (ii) a source-allowlist policy, (iii) an active delisting policy — and how many additional domains must the adversary stand up to defeat each?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA's Open Source Enterprise and whoever owns the IC's LLM deployments (the office fielding analytic assistants); ODNI's analytic standards function; the same problem applies to every COCOM standing up a RAG assistant. **Today:** nobody computes this. Index composition is managed as an IT concern, and the adversary's publishing rate is treated as a content problem, not an infrastructure attack on the analytic pipeline.
- **What the N simulants are:** N laundering-network profiles. State: publishing rate (articles/day), domain count, per-domain authority, cross-linking density, topical breadth, language coverage, SEO investment, and re-provisioning rate after delisting. **riskTolerance** = the fraction of provably-false versus merely-slanted content — provably-false content is delisted faster, so a high-risk-tolerance operator trades durability for punch. **escalationPropensity** = rate of standing up new domains after enforcement. The empirical anchor is concrete: the Pravda network reportedly published **3.6 million articles in 2024** across roughly **150 sites covering 49 countries**, and an audit of 10 leading chatbots found they repeated its false narratives about **33% of the time**, with 7 of 10 citing Pravda articles directly.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Corpus-share dynamics plus retrieval sampling.** (1) A Pólya-urn / replicator ODE on per-topic corpus share: laundered share s(t) grows with publishing rate and cross-linking-driven authority, shrinks with crawler refresh lag and a delisting hazard. (2) A retrieval layer: given share s and an authority-weighted ranking function, compute P(at least one laundered document in top-k) for realistic k, then P(cite) through a citation-propensity parameter fit to observed audit data. (3) A defender-policy layer applying allowlist/delisting operators. Deterministic given seed. Outcome bands over "% of answers on topic T citing ≥1 laundered source at month M," which is directly comparable to a NewsGuard-style audit number.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over why a laundered citation appeared (corpus share / SEO authority / topical vacuum where no authoritative source exists / retrieval-ranker quirk) — the topical-vacuum hypothesis is the important one, because it implies the fix is publishing authoritative content, not delisting. Key-assumptions check on the retrieval-ranker model, with a falsifier. Devil's advocate: the model overstates risk because analysts do not act on chatbot citations. Premortem on a finished intelligence product carrying a laundered claim through the assistant. Red team can force a rerun against an adversary that shifts to non-Pravda infrastructure. Rubric axes: corpus-share estimation validity, ranker fidelity, policy-operator realism, downstream-harm plausibility.
- **The config pack:** `networks/pravda-2025.yaml` (domains, rate, languages, cross-linking); `index/policy.yaml` (crawl refresh, allowlist, delisting hazard, k); `topics/*.yaml` (per-topic authoritative-source density); `rubric/retrieval-integrity.yaml`; `adapters/corpus-share.ts` (Common Crawl / index snapshot ingest); `thresholds/abstention.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo is possible and unusually direct** — you can literally run the audit. Common Crawl snapshots for corpus share; NewsGuard's published methodology and Misinformation Fingerprints (commercial); the American Sunlight Project's "LLM grooming" analysis; VIGINUM, DFRLab, Recorded Future and EDMO reporting on the Pravda network; and any public chatbot as the retrieval surface under test. **Genuinely classified:** the composition and ranking behavior of the IC's own internal index — which is why the real version must be run inside the customer's enclave. This is the candidate whose *classified* instantiation is most obviously necessary and most obviously buildable. ([NewsGuard special report, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/moscow-based-global-news-network-infected-western-artificial-intelligence-russian-propaganda), [Axios coverage](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/06/exclusive-russian-disinfo-floods-ai-chatbots-study-finds))
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** (a) **Synthetic closed corpus** — build a corpus with a known poison ratio, run a real retriever, score predicted vs. observed citation rate; ground truth did not exist before the run. (b) **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff** — this one is unusually clean: NewsGuard's July 2024 audit found ~32% repetition of Dougan-seeded narratives and the March 2025 audit found 33.55% repetition (with 18.22% non-response and 48.22% debunk) across 450 responses. Fit on the earlier audit, predict the later one, score the error. (c) **Live post-cutoff** — commit a forecast for a named topic and resolve against a re-run audit at the horizon date. Scorable, with a genuine measurement caveat: model providers change retrieval behavior between audits, which is a confound the model does not observe.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(c)** this is analytic quality assurance applied to the analytic tooling itself — the most defensible QA story available, because it protects the customer's own AI adoption. **(a)** accuracy of the assistant, measured. **(d)** replaces manual, ad-hoc source-hygiene review with a forecast that says which topics need authoritative content published before they get poisoned.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: none found that forecasts retrieval poisoning; **IARPA REASON** is adjacent (it evaluates evidence and reasoning quality in draft analytic reports and could consume this system's output as a source-quality prior), and **DARPA SemaFor** covers synthetic text but not corpus-share dynamics. This is a genuine program gap, and gaps are what a solicitation response wants to name. Closest commercial: **NewsGuard** (measures the outcome, does not forecast it) and the **American Sunlight Project** (named the tactic). ([REASON](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/reason))
- **Failure modes and red lines:** The retrieval-ranker model is a black-box approximation of a system the customer may not control, so the forecast can be right about corpus share and wrong about citation rate. Adversary infrastructure shifts (from web domains to Telegram to Wikipedia edits to model-training-data poisoning proper) route around the model entirely. **Red lines:** analyzes foreign state-linked publishing infrastructure and the customer's own index; touches no persons and no US-person speech. The obvious misuse — using the same model to plan *our* corpus seeding — is Title 50 territory and structurally unreachable, since the kernel emits share dynamics, never content. A US government customer **can** buy this, and this candidate has the shortest path to an internal-enclave pilot.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 2 / 5.

### C-IW-10 — [BAD IDEA — DO NOT PROPOSE] Domestic narrative-threat monitoring and radicalization-funnel scoring
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Ingests domestic US social media, scores communities and individuals on a radicalization-funnel model, and forecasts which will produce violent actors — included here as the catalog's floor, because it is the candidate a customer might ask for and the one that must be refused.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Which US online communities will produce a violent extremist actor in the next 12 months, and what is each individual's radicalization score?" This is a well-formed question with a resolvable quantity, which is exactly what makes it dangerous — technical well-formedness is not sufficient.
- **Mission and user:** Nominally DHS I&A, FBI domestic terrorism, DHS CP3. For the actual buyer in this solicitation — CIA — this is outside mission entirely: CIA has no internal-security or domestic law-enforcement function. **Today:** the manual process is human-analyst tip-and-lead triage under specific legal predication, which is the correct process and should not be displaced by a scoring model.
- **What the N simulants are:** Nominally, individual susceptibility profiles with grievance, network embeddedness and exposure variables, and the two knobs mapped to willingness to act and escalation speed. **This is the disqualifying detail: the simulants would be models of real people.** Every other candidate in this catalog varies actors that are states, organizations, aggregate populations, or queues. This one varies persons, and the platform's structural guarantee — synthetic populations, opaque numeric positions, no content — does not save a design whose unit of analysis is an individual US person.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** A funnel/hazard model over stages (exposure → engagement → identity fusion → mobilization) with survival analysis for time-to-mobilization. The math is implementable. **The math is also useless at this base rate**, which is the second disqualifying fact: START's PIRUS dataset codes roughly **3,500 individuals across 1948–2022** in a US population of ~330 million. That is a base rate on the order of 10⁻⁵ per year. At an implausibly good 99% specificity, a nationwide screen produces on the order of millions of false positives per true positive. No calibration layer, no ACH panel, and no abstention gate rescues an estimator on the wrong side of arithmetic this stark. ([PIRUS](https://www.start.umd.edu/data-tools/profiles-individual-radicalization-united-states-pirus))
- **What the judge panel is:** Irrelevant. A panel that adjudicates between hypotheses about which Americans will become terrorists is a panel that should not be convened. If the model were built, the honest premortem judge would return the same finding as this entry.
- **The config pack:** Not to be built. If a customer requests it, the correct response is to offer the foreign-audience instantiation of C-IW-2 (belief drift in a named foreign population) and decline the domestic one.
- **Data required:** Bulk domestic social media, which for a DoD or IC customer implicates E.O. 12333 §2.3 and DoDD 5240.01 least-intrusive-means and retention limits, the Privacy Act, and — for the CIA specifically — the statutory bar on internal-security functions. No credible unclassified demo exists that is not itself the harm.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **UNSCORABLE in the only sense that matters.** A synthetic oracle can be built, and it would prove nothing, because the failure mode is not miscalibration on synthetic data — it is the false-positive burden on real people at a real base rate. A historical backtest on PIRUS would score AUC on a curated, already-adjudicated set, which massively overstates field performance. Any claimed accuracy number here would be evidence-class inflation of exactly the kind this platform exists to prevent.
- **Benefit categories hit:** None defensibly. A claim of **(b)** time-to-analysis would be a claim that a screening model can triage faster than predicated human review — true and irrelevant, since the constraint is legal predication, not throughput.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **DHS CP3** and the FBI's domestic terrorism program (operating under predication rules this would bypass); the **State GEC/R-FIMI** closure is the political precedent — it was shut down explicitly on the argument that it "spent millions of dollars to actively silence and censor the voices of Americans." Closest commercial: vendors marketing domestic narrative-threat monitoring, several of which have drawn sustained public controversy (**UNVERIFIED** as to specific companies and outcomes). ([State Dept statement](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2025/09/united-states-champions-free-expression-ceases-censorship-frameworks))
- **Failure modes and red lines:** **This is the candidate a US government customer legally cannot buy, and the reason is not one rule but four stacked:** (1) E.O. 12333 / DoDD 5240.01 US-persons collection and retention limits; (2) the CIA's statutory lack of an internal-security function; (3) the First Amendment problem of a government system scoring protected speech and association, aggravated by the post-*Murthy* environment in which the permissible-communication line remains undrawn; (4) the Privacy Act. The technical red line is the base rate. **Its value to this catalog is as the floor:** it establishes that the other nine are not merely tolerable but were selected against a boundary the authors can articulate. Naming it explicitly is a credibility asset in a proposal to this customer.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** Not applicable — should not advance. Recorded as TRL 0 by choice, not by capability.

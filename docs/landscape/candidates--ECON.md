### C-TECHECON-1 — Diversion Pathway Forecaster ("where does the next controlled tool leak through?")

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Given a new export-control rule, it simulates how a determined buyer would re-route the restricted goods through third countries and shell intermediaries, then predicts — as a numeric share, with a band — how much gets through each pathway over the next four quarters, so enforcement can be aimed at the node that actually matters instead of the node that made the news.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"After the [date] rule expanding controls on advanced-node semiconductor manufacturing equipment, what share of PRC-destined controlled HS 8486.20 / 8542.31 volume will transit via each of {UAE, Türkiye, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, Armenia, Hong Kong} in the four quarters after entry-into-force, and which single intermediary jurisdiction, if closed, removes the largest share?"* Resolvable against mirror-statistics volume shares (percentage of PRC-attributable inflow by transit jurisdiction) with bands like `[0–5%] [5–12%] [12–25%] [25–45%] [>45%]`.
- **Mission and user:** BIS Office of Export Enforcement's analytic support cell and its IC partners (CIA/DO&DI counter-proliferation, DIA, Treasury OFAC's Office of Global Targeting) — plus the FinCEN/BIS joint alert drafters. **Today:** an analyst pulls Comtrade and a commercial trade-record subscription by hand, eyeballs a year-over-year import spike in one HS code for one country, then hand-builds a corporate-ownership graph in Sayari or Kharon to find the consignee. This is retrospective and one-country-at-a-time; the [December 2024 Senate PSI report found BIS failed to prevent thousands of advanced chips reaching PRC military and intelligence end-users through third-country transshipment](https://globalinvestigationsreview.com/guide/the-guide-sanctions/seventh-edition/article/spotlighting-us-export-controls-amid-bis-crackdown-semiconductors-in-china) — the failure mode is exactly "we saw it after it happened." [BIS began adding address-only Entity List entries in June 2024 specifically to disrupt shell-company diversion](https://sayari.com/resources/blog/2026-export-control-priorities/), i.e. the incumbent response is enumerate-after-the-fact.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *procurement network archetype* pursuing the same end-user demand. State variables per run: `demandUnits` (units of controlled item sought per quarter), `routeSet` (ordered list of transit jurisdictions with per-hop latency and cost multiplier), `intermediaryChurnRate` (how fast the buyer spins up replacement front companies after a designation), `paymentRail` (correspondent-dollar / RMB-CIPS / barter / crypto), `endUseCoverStory` (declared civil end-use category), `seizureMemory` (Bayesian belief about which routes are being watched). **riskTolerance** = willingness to accept per-shipment interdiction probability before abandoning a route (0.02 for a state-linked entity that must not be caught vs 0.35 for a commercial gray-market broker who treats seizures as a cost of goods). **escalationPropensity** = willingness to move from legal-gray (re-export by a genuinely independent distributor) to overtly illegal (falsified end-user certificates, mislabeled HS codes, dual-invoicing). Meaningful variation: yes — a low-risk/low-escalation profile concentrates on Singapore and Malaysia legal re-export and stalls when the FDPR bites; a high-risk/high-escalation profile fragments across Armenia/Kyrgyzstan micro-consignments and is nearly unstoppable at the border but leaves a loud mirror-statistics signature.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Stackelberg game on a capacitated network-flow graph.** Defender (enforcement) allocates a fixed inspection/designation budget across nodes and edges; evader solves a min-cost multi-commodity flow where each edge carries `(freightCost, latency, detectionHazard(enforcementAllocation))` and the objective is expected delivered units minus expected seizure loss weighted by `riskTolerance`. Solved by an inner LP/successive-shortest-path with an outer best-response iteration to a fixed point. A seeded PRNG generates shipment arrival times (Poisson thinning) and detection realizations; the routing optimization itself is exact, so same seed ⇒ identical flow allocation. Layered on top: a **Bayesian belief update** where the evader updates edge-hazard beliefs from observed seizures, producing the realistic "enforcement whack-a-mole" dynamic where designations displace rather than reduce flow. Output quantity = delivered-share vector over jurisdictions.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over the competing pathway hypotheses, with evidence items = observed customs anomalies, designation events, corporate-registration bursts; diagnosticity is high for evidence that separates "route displacement" from "genuine demand growth." **Key-assumptions check** judge scores the elasticity assumption (does the buyer actually have a substitutable route?) and the fungibility assumption (are units interchangeable, or is the tool serial-tracked?). **Devil's advocate** argues the null: the volume spike is legitimate third-country demand growth, not diversion — this judge exists because that null is right often enough to matter. **Premortem** on "we designated the wrong node and flow moved to an unmonitored jurisdiction within one quarter." **Red team** with standing to force a bounded rerun if the sim assumed a route that is physically impossible (no bonded-warehouse capacity, no air-cargo lane for the tool's crate dimensions). Rubric axes: pathway plausibility, evidentiary grounding, displacement realism, enforcement-actionability, cost realism.
- **The config pack:** `actors/procurement-archetypes.yaml` (6 archetypes: state SOE proxy, PLA-affiliated research institute purchasing agent, commercial gray broker, distributor-with-plausible-deniability, academic front, hybrid smuggler), `domain/trade-graph-adapter.ts` (HS-code → commodity mapping, jurisdiction latency/cost matrix, bonded-warehouse capacities), `rubric/diversion-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when mirror-statistics coverage for the target HS code drops below a coverage floor or when the partner country's reporting lag exceeds the forecast horizon), `judges/panel-diversion.yaml`, `oracle/backtest-episodes.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified and sufficient for a credible demo.** [UN Comtrade](https://comtrade.un.org/) bilateral flows and mirror statistics — the [Harvard Growth Lab has published a reconciliation methodology for exactly these discrepancies](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-06488-2), and [Global Financial Integrity's mirror-analysis method](https://gfintegrity.org/reports/methodology/trade-misinvoicing-mirror-analysis/) is the accepted baseline; Eurostat COMEXT; national customs microdata (Indian and Turkish import records resold through Panjiva/S&P, ImportGenius, Trade Data Monitor); corporate registries (OpenCorporates, Sayari's 250+ jurisdictions of connected public records — [Sayari holds a $7.8M CBP supply-chain contract](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/sayari-labs)); BIS Entity List / Denied Persons / Unverified List; SEMI equipment billings; shipping manifests. **Genuinely needs classified access:** end-user identity confirmation, SIGINT on procurement tasking, and the fact of which routes are under active watch (which is also the input that makes the defender side of the game real). The demo runs entirely unclassified on the 2022–2024 episode.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with a hard as-of cutoff — the strongest oracle in this catalog.** As-of 2022-10-06 (day before the October 7, 2022 controls), forecast 2023 Q1–Q4 import shares for Türkiye, UAE, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Hong Kong, Malaysia in the controlled HS codes; score against realized Comtrade. Repeat as-of the September 2024 and December 2024 rule dates. Second backtest arm: Russia-facing microelectronics diversion post-February 2022, where the [Türkiye/UAE/Armenia/Kazakhstan pattern is documented](https://sayari.com/resources/blog/2026-export-control-priorities/). Plus **live post-cutoff resolution:** the H200 regime gives clean near-term resolvable quantities — [BIS's January 13, 2026 licensing policy caps China-destined H200-class exports at under 50% of total U.S. sales with an estimated 900,000 H200-equivalent ceiling and a 25% export fee](https://www.iaps.ai/research/bis-licensing-policy-for-h200s), while [Commerce's Jeffrey Kessler told Congress on July 14, 2026 that shipments had been "very few" against roughly $10B in approved licenses](https://introl.com/blog/bis-h200-china-export-policy-ai-overwatch-act-2026) — the gap between licensed and shipped is itself a scorable band. Also synthetic closed worlds for the abstention arm.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — replaces "spike = diversion" heuristics with an explicit displacement model that separates route substitution from demand growth. **(b)** time-to-analysis — a pathway assessment that takes an analyst two weeks of trade-record pulls becomes a run; and the product is *forward*, not retrospective. **(c)** QA — Comtrade reporter/partner disagreement is the canonical source discrepancy; the system reconciles it explicitly and reports which side it trusted and why, and confidence aggregates by weakest link so a low-coverage jurisdiction caps the whole assessment. **(d)** cost — the quarterly "watch-list refresh" is the repetitive task being automated.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — BIS Entity List and the address-only-entry practice; the Senate PSI semiconductor-diversion investigation; Treasury/BIS/FinCEN joint alerts on evasion typologies; DHS/HSI Operation Chokepoint-style interdiction. Commercial — [Sayari](https://sayari.com/), [Kharon](https://www.kharon.com/solutions/industries/public-sector) (which [Treasury selected for its risk-analytics platform](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kharon-data-and-technology-selected-by-us-department-of-the-treasury-302137436.html)), [C4ADS](https://c4ads.org/), Altana, Exiger, Interos ([on the $919M GSA SCRIPTS vehicle](https://www.interos.ai/blog/interos-ai-chosen-for-government-wide-919-million-gsa-scripts-contract)). **The gap:** every one of these is a *graph illumination and screening* product — they tell you what the network **is**. None of them **simulate the adversary's next move** and none publish a Brier score. That is the whole wedge.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — Comtrade reporting lag (6–18 months for some partners) can exceed the forecast horizon, making the oracle arrive too late; the abstention gate must fire on this and it is the single most important threshold in the config. Mirror-statistic noise from legitimate re-export hubs (Singapore, Netherlands) produces false positives. Route-cost matrices are the softest input and the sim is sensitive to them. Legal — this candidate deals in *entities and shipments*, not persons; structural enforcement: the domain adapter's schema admits only legal-entity and jurisdiction nodes, natural-person fields are absent from the type, so a person-level graph is not expressible in config; and a runtime assertion rejects any evidence reference to a US-person-attributed record. No targeting-package output: the product's only action recommendation type is `regulatory` (designation, license condition, outreach), enforced by an enum.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6. The kernel is standard network-flow plus a best-response loop and is buildable now; the backtest data is public today. TRL 6 requires a real as-of corpus pipeline with the date filter enforced in code (the platform already asserts this) and one clean scored backtest.

### C-TECHECON-2 — Sanctions Package Pre-Mortem (second-order effects *before* imposition)

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Before a sanctions package is signed, it runs the package through a model of the world economy and an adversary who gets to respond, and returns a banded forecast of what it does to the target's revenue, what it does to allied and US firms, and how likely retaliation is — so the policy conversation happens with numbers and named assumptions instead of adjectives.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"If we designate the top 8 PRC refiners and 3 trading houses handling Iranian crude on [date], what is the distribution of (i) Iranian seaborne crude export volume in the following 90 days, (ii) Brent price delta, (iii) US-refiner feedstock cost exposure, and (iv) probability that Beijing imposes a countermeasure touching a US-listed critical input within 120 days?"* Each of the four is a separate banded quantity; (i) resolves against Kpler/Vortexa loadings, (ii) against settlement prices, (iv) against MOFCOM announcements.
- **Mission and user:** Treasury TFI (OFAC Office of Global Targeting + the Office of Economic Sanctions Policy), State's Office of Economic Sanctions Policy and Implementation, NSC directorate staff, and their IC support (CIA's economic analysis line, DIA). **Today:** GAO found that [agencies do assess sanctions impacts on targets but that the assessments are largely qualitative and the evidence base on effectiveness is thin](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-145.pdf); [Treasury's own 2021 Sanctions Review committed to "tailoring sanctions to mitigate unintended economic and political impacts"](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Treasury-2021-sanctions-review.pdf) without shipping a modeling capability to do it. The manual process is: an action officer writes a two-page impact memo, an interagency call surfaces objections, and blowback is discovered afterwards — the Rusal 2018 aluminum-price episode being the canonical case of a designation that had to be partially unwound.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *target-and-third-party response regime*. State variables: `evasionCapacity` (share of blocked flow re-routable within 90 days), `substituteBuyerSet` (which non-aligned buyers absorb displaced volume, at what discount), `financialWorkaroundShare` (fraction settled outside dollar rails), `domesticAbsorptionCapacity` (can the target's fisc eat the revenue hit), `alliedComplianceRate` (over-compliance vs under-compliance by EU/UK/Japan/Korea banks), `retaliationInstrument` (rare-earth licensing, antitrust probe, procurement exclusion, none). **riskTolerance** = the target regime's willingness to accept fiscal pain before conceding; **escalationPropensity** = probability weight on responding with a *new* instrument class rather than absorbing. Meaningful variation: yes — the same package produces a 40% revenue hit under a low-evasion/low-escalation profile and a 9% hit plus a rare-earth counter-designation under high/high.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Multi-regional input-output (Leontief) cascade with rationing, coupled to a two-player escalation game.** Layer 1: the designation removes a set of (country, sector) supply links; propagate with a Ghosh/Leontief mixed model plus an inventory buffer and a CES substitution elasticity per input pair, iterating to a new equilibrium — this is the established method ([shock propagation through IO networks](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/how-demand-shocks-propagate-through-input-output-network-cases-global-financial); [counterfactual sanctions experiments calibrated to the OECD World Input–Output Table](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12128)). Layer 2: a finite-horizon two-player game where each side chooses from an instrument menu with a cost/benefit payoff computed *by layer 1*, solved by backward induction to a subgame-perfect path; `escalationPropensity` shifts the payoff weight on reputational/domestic-audience terms. Seeded PRNG only for the rationing tie-breaks and the discrete instrument draws. Same seed ⇒ identical equilibrium.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "package works / package is evaded / package backfires on us / package triggers a disproportionate counter"; diagnosticity is highest on evidence that separates *evasion* from *absorption*. **Key-assumptions check** on the substitution-elasticity table — the single most load-bearing and most contested input — with each assumption carrying a required falsifier ("if Indian refiner import discounts on the sanctioned grade narrow below $4/bbl within 45 days, the evasion-capacity assumption is wrong"). **Devil's advocate** argues the package is symbolically satisfying and economically inert. **Premortem** on the Rusal failure mode: the designation hits a commodity where the target holds a global share large enough that the price shock hurts us more than them. **Red team** with standing to force a rerun when the allied-compliance assumption is asserted without a named precedent. Rubric axes: economic mechanism validity, evasion realism, ally-collateral honesty, retaliation plausibility, decision-usefulness.
- **The config pack:** `actors/target-response-profiles.yaml`, `actors/third-party-profiles.yaml` (India, Türkiye, UAE, Brazil as swing buyers), `domain/mrio-adapter.ts` (OECD ICIO or GTAP table loader, HS↔sector concordance, elasticity table with provenance per cell), `rubric/policy-support-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when the target sector's IO coefficients are older than N years or the target country is absent from the IO table — a real and common condition for Iran, DPRK), `judges/panel-policy.yaml`, `oracle/sanctions-backtests.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo is fully credible.** OECD Inter-Country Input-Output tables; GTAP; UN Comtrade; IMF DOTS; [the Global Sanctions Data Base R4, 1,547 sanction cases 1950–2023 coded by type, objective and success](https://www.globalsanctionsdatabase.com/); Kpler/Vortexa or ClipperData for oil flows; commodity settlement prices; SWIFT RMB Tracker; national statistics. **Needs classified access:** the target regime's actual fiscal reserves and elite-consumption sensitivity, the pre-decisional package contents themselves, and allied government private positions. Note the demo can run on a *public* historical package with no classification issue at all.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff, on packages with clean numeric outcomes.** Named episodes: April 2018 Rusal/EN+ designation (as-of 2018-04-05 → forecast 60-day LME aluminum price band and probability of a US license/rollback — the model that says "backfire, high probability of partial unwind" wins); February 2022 CBR asset freeze (→ 90-day RUB/USD band, import-volume band); December 2022 G7 oil price cap (→ Urals-Brent discount band); May 2020 Huawei FDPR (→ Huawei handset unit-share band at 12 months); January 10, 2025 OFAC package on Sovcomflot/Gazprom Neft (→ Indian refiner Russian-crude import band at 90 days); 2010 China–Japan rare-earth episode (→ price band). **Live post-cutoff:** [the October 2025 PRC controls on five additional REEs and the extraterritorial content rule are suspended until November 10, 2026, and the gallium/germanium/antimony ban suspension runs to November 27, 2026](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/china-suspends-export-controls-certain-critical-minerals-related-items.html) — "does the suspension lapse, extend, or partially extend, and what happens to NdPr and Ge prices in the 60 days after" is a resolvable live question available right now. Scoring: Brier on the discrete bands, plus Murphy decomposition to separate resolution from calibration, plus explicit skill-vs-baseline where the baseline is "no change from current level."
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — the ally-collateral estimate is currently produced by intuition. **(b)** time — a package pre-mortem that takes an interagency week compresses to a run plus a review. **(c)** QA — this is the strongest QA candidate in the catalog: OECD IO tables, Comtrade, and commercial flow trackers routinely disagree on the same quantity, and the calibration layer's z-score normalization plus explicit source-discrepancy reporting is exactly the "reconcile differences and state overall analytic confidence" benefit the customer named. **(d)** cost — the repetitive per-designation impact memo.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — Treasury's internal impact assessments ([GAO-20-145](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-145.pdf)); State's ["Measuring Smartness: Understanding the Economic Impact of Targeted Sanctions"](https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Measuring-Smartness-Understanding-the-Economic-Impact-of-Targeted-Sanctions-1.pdf); the Atlantic Council [Global Sanctions Dashboard](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/programs/geoeconomics-center/global-sanctions-dashboard/) (descriptive, not predictive). Commercial/academic — Oxford Economics and IHS/S&P scenario macro models; GTAP consortium; Castellum.AI and Kharon on the compliance side. **The gap:** none couples an IO cascade to an adversary who *responds*, and none is scored.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — IO tables are years stale and coarse; elasticity values are the model's soft underbelly and small changes swing outcomes, so the key-assumptions judge and the sensitivity report are not optional. Sanctioned economies are underrepresented in the tables (Iran, DPRK, Russia post-2022) — the abstention gate must fire, and measuring its false-abstention rate here is a selling point rather than a caveat. Red line: this is *policy support*, and the output must never read as advocacy for a specific designation of a named natural person. Structural enforcement: the product schema's recommendation type is an enum over `{instrument_class, sector, jurisdiction}` with no person field, and the leakage assertion rejects any evidence reference post-dating the as-of date so a backtest cannot cheat.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6–7. The MRIO cascade is textbook; the escalation game is small; the historical episodes have public numeric outcomes. This is the candidate most likely to produce a credible scored result inside a year.

### C-TECHECON-3 — Time-to-Capability: banded arrival dates for a named technical milestone

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** For a specific technology in a specific country — say, sustained volume production of 5nm-class logic without EUV lithography — it simulates the physical and industrial process that has to succeed and returns a probability distribution over *when*, rather than a sentence saying "in the coming years."
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"By what date does the PRC sustain ≥30,000 wafer starts per month of 5nm-class logic at ≥50% die yield without EUV?"* Bands: `[before 2027-06] [2027-06 to 2028-06] [2028-06 to 2030-01] [2030-01 to 2032-01] [not before 2032]`. Resolvable against SEMI/TrendForce capacity reporting plus TechInsights teardown-confirmed node and yield estimates.
- **Mission and user:** CIA's science-and-technology analytic line (the WINPAC-lineage mission), DIA's Directorate for Science and Technology, NASIC, and the NIC officer who owns the S&T section of a National Intelligence Estimate. **Today:** an S&T analyst reads teardown reports, SEMI billings, Chinese procurement notices and Weibo-sourced job postings, and writes a judgment with a hedged time phrase. There is no explicit yield model, no explicit tool-availability constraint, and no way to say what evidence would change the date. The dispersion in public estimates is the tell: [SMIC's ≤7nm output was reported at roughly 45,000 wafer starts/month in late 2025 with projections to 60,000 in 2026 and 80,000 in 2027](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/smic-ai-chip-strategy-2026-inside-chinas-5nm-power-play/), while [Huawei Ascend 910C yields have been reported between 20% and 40%](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/smic-ai-chip-strategy-2026-inside-chinas-5nm-power-play/) — reconciling those into a date is exactly the unautomated step.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *industrial-execution profile* of the same program. State variables: `toolInventory` (per-tool-class counts of DUV immersion, etch, deposition, metrology — with an inflow constrained by export-control regime and diversion success), `multipatterningPasses` (SAQP vs SADP choice, driving mask count and defect exposure), `defectDensityD0` (per-layer, declining on a learning curve), `processMaturityTime`, `hbmSupplyUnits` (an independent binding constraint — [reporting identifies HBM as the bottleneck on the Ascend ramp](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)), `talentStock`, `subsidyDisbursement`. **riskTolerance** = willingness to run production at economically unviable yield for strategic reasons (a state program will ship at 20% yield; a commercial fab will not). **escalationPropensity** = willingness to pursue illicit tool acquisition and prohibited service/spares channels, which raises tool inflow but adds a seizure hazard and a maintenance-degradation term. Meaningful variation: strongly yes — the arrival date spans years across these profiles, and the spread is the product.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Stock-and-flow system dynamics coupled to a physical yield model and a Wright's-law learning curve.** Yield per layer = Poisson defect model `Y = exp(−D0·A·passes)` with `D0(t)` following a learning curve in cumulative wafer starts, `D0(t) = D0_0 · (cumWafers)^(−b)`; capacity accumulation is a difference equation over tool inventory with maintenance-degradation on uncertified tools; good-die output = capacity × yield × (1 − HBM binding constraint). The milestone date is the first time index where the output threshold is met and held for k periods. Seeded PRNG for the tool-acquisition success draws and for defect realizations. **Bayesian belief update layer:** each observed public datapoint (a teardown, a SEMI billing number, a procurement notice) updates the posterior over `D0_0` and `b`, so the forecast is re-scored as evidence arrives — and the *pace of updating* is itself a reportable metric.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "on-track / tool-constrained / yield-constrained / HBM-constrained / claimed-but-not-sustained." A dedicated **key-assumptions judge** scores the learning-rate assumption `b` against historical analogues (TSMC's and Intel's published defect-density curves), with a required falsifier per assumption. **Devil's advocate** argues the demonstration-vs-production distinction — that a teardown of one part proves a lab result, not sustained volume — which is the single most common analytic error in this domain. **Premortem** on "we called it constrained and they shipped," and its mirror, "we believed a state-media capacity number." **Red team** may force a rerun if any input traces to a single uncorroborated Chinese-state or vendor-marketing source. Rubric axes: physical plausibility, evidence quality per input, sustained-vs-demonstration discipline, sensitivity transparency, band width justification.
- **The config pack:** `actors/industrial-execution-profiles.yaml`, `domain/semiconductor-kernel-adapter.ts` (tool-class taxonomy, layer counts by node, patterning schemes, HBM stack requirements per accelerator), `rubric/tech-timeline-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when no independent teardown exists for the claimed node — a hard, checkable condition), `judges/panel-s-and-t.yaml`, `oracle/milestone-backtests.yaml`. The *domain adapter is the swappable part*: replacing it with `hypersonics-kernel-adapter.ts` (scramjet test cadence, thermal-protection material qualification), `quantum-kernel-adapter.ts` (physical→logical qubit ratios, error-correction threshold), or `enrichment-kernel-adapter.ts` (centrifuge cascade SWU accumulation) yields the same product for a different technology with no new code — which is the platform's core claim.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo is credible and strong.** SEMI equipment billings; customs HS 8486 import records for tool inflow; TechInsights/SemiAnalysis teardown reporting; Chinese government procurement and tender platforms; CNIPA and USPTO/EPO patent filings and their velocity; arXiv/OpenAlex publication counts by affiliation; corporate filings; hiring postings. **Needs classified access:** actual fab-floor yield telemetry, program intent, and covert tool-acquisition channels. The unclassified version is publishable and demoable end to end.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff — clean and available.** As-of 2022-09-01, forecast the date band for "SMIC ships a 7nm-class part in a consumer device"; ground truth is the Huawei Mate 60 Pro / Kirin 9000s teardown of August–September 2023. As-of 2023-01-01, forecast the ≤7nm wafer-starts/month level at end-2025; ground truth ~45,000 as reported. As-of 2024-06-01, forecast "volume production of a 5nm-class node without EUV"; ground truth is the [independently reported December 2025 teardown confirmation of SMIC N+3](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/smic-ai-chip-strategy-2026-inside-chinas-5nm-power-play/). Historical non-China arms: Soviet enrichment and thermonuclear milestone dates, Indian and Pakistani nuclear tests, Chinese J-20 first flight — all with public as-of-dated US assessments to score against, which additionally gives a **human baseline** for the skill score. **Live post-cutoff:** SMIC 2026 and 2027 capacity numbers, Ascend production units, HBM domestic qualification. Plus synthetic closed worlds for the abstention arm.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — replaces hedged prose with a scored distribution and an explicit physical mechanism. **(b)** time and prediction accuracy — the strongest fit to the customer's stated "reduce time to analyze complex phenomena and increase accuracy of predictions." **(c)** QA — vendor claims, state media, teardowns and customs data disagree systematically; the panel adjudicates the disagreement and the confidence field reports what the weakest link was. **(d)** cost — the recurring quarterly refresh of a capability timeline.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — the NIE S&T process itself; DIA's science-and-technology assessments; [IARPA's ForeST and Hybrid Forecasting Competition line of work](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hfc) on technology forecasting; [CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/) as the leading unclassified analog. Commercial — SemiAnalysis, TechInsights, TrendForce, Epoch AI (for compute trends). **The gap:** all of the above publish *point estimates with narrative*, none publishes a calibrated distribution with a Brier score against its own past calls.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — the learning-rate parameter dominates and is weakly identified from public data; the model will be over-confident if the Bayesian prior is too tight, which is precisely what the sharpness and ECE metrics are for. State-media capacity claims are an adversarial input and a deliberate deception channel — the red-team judge exists for this. Deep uncertainty about whether a *demonstrated* part reflects a *sustainable* process is irreducible and should widen bands rather than be modeled away. Red line: none of the usual ones apply cleanly here (no US persons, no targeting) — the relevant boundary is that a capability-timeline product must not be phrased as a recommendation for kinetic or covert preemption; structural enforcement is that the product schema has no `courseOfAction` field at all, only `indicators`, `assumptions`, and `bands`.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6. The yield and learning-curve math is settled science; the work is the domain adapter and the as-of corpus.

### C-TECHECON-4 — Critical-Mineral Chokepoint Coercion Simulator

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It plays out what happens if a supplier country weaponizes a mineral it dominates — how far the price moves, how fast buyers substitute, how long the national stockpile lasts, and which US defense programs slip — as banded numbers over a 180-day horizon.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"If the PRC allows the October 2025 controls to lapse back into force on November 10, 2026 and enforces the extraterritorial content rule, what is the 180-day distribution of (i) NdPr oxide ex-China price, (ii) months-of-supply remaining in the National Defense Stockpile for germanium, and (iii) number of named DoD programs breaching a schedule milestone attributable to material shortfall?"* Bands per quantity; (i) resolves against Asian Metal / Fastmarkets assessments, (ii) against DLA reporting, (iii) against program schedule data.
- **Mission and user:** DLA Strategic Materials (the National Defense Stockpile manager), OUSD(A&S) Industrial Base Policy, DIA's economic/industrial analysis line, and the NSC critical-minerals lead. **Today:** [DoD selects materials for the NDS based on a "material shortfall" analysis under a base-case scenario of a four-year military conflict combined with an attack on the homeland](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47833) — a single deterministic scenario, refreshed on a multi-year cycle, that does not model an adversary choosing *when and how* to squeeze. The manual process is a requirements study plus commodity-analyst judgment.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *coercion posture* by the dominant supplier. State variables: `controlScope` (element list × end-use restrictions × extraterritorial content threshold), `licenseApprovalRate` (the actual lever — [China's April 2025 licensing regime was never suspended, only the October 2025 additions were](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/china-suspends-export-controls-certain-critical-minerals-related-items.html)), `enforcementIntensity` on downstream content rules, `durationCommitment`, `carveOutSet` (which buyers get quiet approvals), `ownEconomyPainTolerance` (the supplier's domestic producers lose revenue too). **riskTolerance** = tolerance for the supplier's own export-revenue and reputational loss; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to widen from a narrow element list to the extraterritorial content rule that hits third countries. Meaningful variation: yes, and it is the analytically interesting part — the difference between "narrow, brief, carve-outs for autos" and "broad, extraterritorial, indefinite" is the difference between a price blip and the [$6.5 trillion of annual non-China economic activity the IEA has estimated to be at risk under full reimposition](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/china-suspends-export-controls-certain-critical-minerals-related-items.html) (figure as reported by counsel summarizing IEA — **UNVERIFIED** against the IEA original).
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Partial-equilibrium commodity market clearing with inventory dynamics and a substitution-lag pipeline.** A difference-equation system: supply = permitted exports + ex-China mine/refine capacity (with a commissioning lag pipeline), demand = downstream sector consumption with short-run price elasticity ε_s and a *substitution hazard* per sector (probability per period that a qualified substitute displaces demand, with qualification lead times of 12–48 months for defense-grade applications), inventory = commercial stocks + NDS, price clears the residual. A **queueing/inventory sub-model** tracks NDS drawdown against defense program consumption rates and returns months-of-supply and the first program to hit stock-out. Seeded PRNG for capacity-commissioning slippage and for licensing-approval realizations. Deterministic given seed and profile.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "brief signaling squeeze / sustained targeted squeeze / broad extraterritorial squeeze / no action," with diagnosticity concentrated on licensing-approval-rate evidence rather than announcement text (announcements are cheap; approvals are the real signal — a good judge should learn to weight them accordingly). **Key-assumptions check** on substitution lead times, which is where this model most easily lies to itself. **Devil's advocate** argues the supplier is deterred by its own revenue loss and by accelerating buyer diversification — the "coercion is self-defeating" case, which has real empirical support from the 2010 Japan episode. **Premortem** on "we stockpiled the wrong element." **Red team** may force a rerun when a substitution assumption lacks a named qualified alternative material. Rubric axes: market-mechanism validity, substitution realism, stockpile-arithmetic correctness, coercion-logic plausibility, actionability for a stockpile decision.
- **The config pack:** `actors/supplier-coercion-postures.yaml`, `domain/minerals-adapter.ts` (element × application × substitute matrix with qualification lead times, ex-China capacity pipeline, NDS inventory schema), `rubric/chokepoint-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when ex-China capacity data for the element is older than N quarters, or when no public price assessment exists for the specific oxide/metal form — true for several elements and a real abstention trigger), `judges/panel-minerals.yaml`, `oracle/mineral-episodes.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo credible.** USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries; Comtrade and Chinese customs; Asian Metal / Fastmarkets / Argus price assessments (commercial); [CRS R47833 on the NDS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47833); DLA Strategic Materials annual reporting; company filings for ex-China projects (MP Materials, Lynas, Iluka); [FY2026 NDAA covered-materials provisions adding gallium, germanium and molybdenum with restrictions effective December 18, 2027](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/fy2026-ndaa-sourcing-restrictions-critical-minerals-advanced-batteries.html). **Needs classified access:** actual per-program material consumption rates and classified program schedules; real NDS holdings at fidelity. The unclassified demo can use published proxies and say so.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff.** Episode 1: 2010 China–Japan rare-earth restriction — as-of 2010-09-01, forecast the 12-month NdPr price band and the 36-month price band (the correct answer includes the *collapse* after the spike, which naive models miss and which is the discriminating test). Episode 2: August 2023 gallium/germanium licensing — as-of 2023-07-01, forecast the 180-day Ge price band and export-volume band. Episode 3: April 2025 licensing regime — as-of 2025-03-01, forecast automotive production disruptions. Episode 4: December 2024 US-directed ban. **Live post-cutoff, available now:** the [November 10, 2026 and November 27, 2026 suspension expiries](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/china-suspends-export-controls-certain-critical-minerals-related-items.html) are date-certain resolution events sitting three months out — this is the cleanest live-scoring opportunity in the entire catalog and should be the demo's headline. Baseline for skill scoring: random-walk price forecast.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — replaces a single deterministic shortfall scenario with a distribution over adversary postures. **(b)** time — stockpile requirement studies run on multi-year cycles; this runs on demand. **(c)** QA — price assessments, customs volumes and company guidance disagree persistently, and the reconciliation plus confidence reporting is the deliverable. **(d)** cost — the periodic requirements-study refresh.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — the NDS requirements process (DLA/OUSD); [the Ex-Im Bank's reported $12B strategic stockpiling initiative launched February 2026](https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2026/3/6/gci-critical-mineral-stockpiling-presents-opportunities) (**UNVERIFIED** — single trade-press source); DPA Title III awards; USGS assessments. Commercial — Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, Project Blue, Fastmarkets, Wood Mackenzie; SFA Oxford for PGMs. **The gap:** commercial analysts model *markets*; none model a *coercing state choosing a posture*, and none score themselves.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — price elasticities for thin, opaque markets are badly identified; ex-China capacity announcements are systematically optimistic and the model must discount them or it will forecast relief that never arrives; the 2010 episode shows spike-then-collapse dynamics that a naive supply-shock model gets exactly backwards. Red line: none involving persons; the boundary here is *market-moving information* — a US government product forecasting specific commodity prices creates an insider-information and market-manipulation hazard if it leaks. Structural enforcement: price outputs are emitted as coarse bands only (the band schema forbids point estimates), and the config carries a `distributionRestriction` field that the product renderer enforces.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6–7. The market model is simple, the data is public, and a date-certain live resolution event exists inside the demo window.

### C-TECHECON-5 — Interdiction Portfolio Chooser for Sanctioned Oil Logistics (shadow fleet)

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It compares three or four candidate enforcement actions — designate 100 more tankers, sanction two refiners, pressure a flag registry, board ships in a chokepoint — and returns, for each, a banded forecast of how much sanctioned oil revenue actually drops over 90 days and how fast the fleet adapts around it.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"Comparing four enforcement options, what is the 90-day distribution of Russian seaborne crude export revenue, and what is the median time-to-adaptation (days until displaced volume returns to within 10% of baseline) for each option?"* Revenue bands in $B; adaptation-time bands in days. Resolves against Kpler/Vortexa loadings × assessed realized price.
- **Mission and user:** Treasury OFAC's Russia/Iran units and the Office of Foreign Assets Control's maritime task force, State's Bureau of Energy Resources, ONI, DIA, and the EU/UK counterparts in a coordinated action. **Today:** analysts track vessels one at a time in Windward or Kpler, designate in batches, and learn the adaptation rate after the fact. The adaptation is fast and documented: [Windward has tracked nearly 70 dark-fleet tankers broadcasting Russia as their new flag since May 2025, 40 of them since boardings and detentions began in December](https://windward.ai/blog/enforcement-shock-accelerates-russias-dark-fleet-reflagging), and the shadow fleet [roughly tripled from ~600 vessels since 2022](https://windward.ai/knowledge-base/illuminating-russias-shadow-fleet/). Nobody is forecasting the *next* adaptation before the action.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *fleet-adaptation regime*. State variables: `hullPool` (counts by sanctioned/gray/clean status, age, ice class), `reflaggingRate` (registries willing to accept, with capacity limits), `insuranceChannel` (Western P&I vs non-Western vs none), `stsCapacity` (ship-to-ship transfer zones and their throughput), `aisManipulationMode` (off / spoofed / identity-swapped), `buyerCompliancePosture` per destination refinery, `freightRatePremium` (endogenous — scarcity of willing hulls raises it and directly taxes the exporter). **riskTolerance** = the operator's tolerance for detention/seizure probability per voyage; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to move from gray techniques (flag-hopping) to overt ones (falsified port clearance, forged bills of lading, sailing uninsured through a strait). Meaningful variation: strongly yes — the same designation package costs the exporter 3% or 22% of revenue depending on hull-pool slack and registry willingness.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Agent-based maritime logistics with a bipartite cargo–hull matching market and endogenous freight pricing.** Each period: cargoes are generated at loading ports (seeded Poisson), hulls have a state machine (`laden → transit → STS → discharge → ballast`) with deterministic transit times from a port-distance matrix, and a matching step assigns cargoes to hulls by a deterministic auction where the clearing freight rate rises as available compliant-enough hulls become scarce. Enforcement actions remove hulls from the pool (designation), remove destinations (refiner sanctions), or remove registries (flag pressure), and the operator's response is a rule-based policy indexed by `riskTolerance`/`escalationPropensity`. Revenue = delivered barrels × (benchmark − discount(freightRate, buyerRisk)). The [typical evasion cycle — Russian port load with AIS off, dark transit, STS to a clean tanker, delivery on a non-Russian bill of lading — reportedly takes 21–45 days](https://oxint.io/briefs/shadow-fleet/), which sets the model's clock. Same seed ⇒ byte-identical vessel histories.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "capacity-constrained / insurance-constrained / buyer-constrained / unconstrained" — i.e. which binding constraint actually determines the outcome, since each implies a different enforcement lever. **Key-assumptions check** on hull-pool slack and registry willingness. **Devil's advocate** argues the enforcement action displaces rather than reduces, and that the price discount reflects buyer bargaining power rather than enforcement pressure. **Premortem** on the two real failure modes: a global oil-price spike caused by our own action (the politically fatal outcome) and a maritime environmental incident caused by pushing cargo onto uninsured hulls. **Red team** may force a rerun if the sim assumes a registry response with no precedent. Rubric axes: logistics realism, constraint identification, revenue-arithmetic validity, adaptation-rate plausibility, second-order-harm honesty.
- **The config pack:** `actors/fleet-operator-profiles.yaml`, `domain/maritime-adapter.ts` (port distance matrix, STS zones, registry roster with acceptance policy, P&I club roster, refinery capacity and crude-slate constraints), `rubric/interdiction-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when AIS coverage for the relevant sea area falls below a floor or dark-period share exceeds a ceiling — a genuine, checkable data-quality gate), `judges/panel-maritime.yaml`, `oracle/interdiction-episodes.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo credible with commercial data.** Terrestrial and satellite AIS (Spire, Kpler, MarineTraffic); Equasis for flag/ownership/class; IMO numbers; P&I club membership listings; OFAC SDN vessel entries and EU/UK equivalents; port-call records; [Kpler-reported figures such as 510 vessels loading sanctioned Russian oil in 2025, 59% of them sanctioned](https://theboard.world/articles/geopolitics/dark-fleet-tankers-shadow-fleet-sanctions-evasion/); refinery crude-slate data. **Needs classified access:** overhead imagery for confirming dark STS events, and intelligence on beneficial ownership behind registries. Note that [detection reliably requires remote sensing, not AIS alone](https://windward.ai/blog/what-is-the-dark-fleet/) — so the unclassified demo must state that its dark-period estimates are lower bounds, and the abstention gate is what keeps that honest.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff — several clean episodes.** As-of 2024-10-01, forecast the 90-day effect of the October 2024 tanker designations. As-of 2025-01-09, forecast the effect of the January 10, 2025 OFAC package on Sovcomflot and Gazprom Neft, scoring against realized Indian refiner import volumes and Urals discounts. As-of 2025-12-01, forecast the reflagging response to the boarding/seizure campaign — ground truth is [Windward's tracked ~70 Russia-reflagged hulls](https://windward.ai/blog/enforcement-shock-accelerates-russias-dark-fleet-reflagging). Iran arm: the 2018–2019 maximum-pressure export decline. **Live post-cutoff:** each new designation tranche is a scheduled scoring event. Baseline: "designation removes the designated hulls' volume permanently" — the naive assumption the model should beat.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — replaces "designations are good" with a comparative estimate per option. **(b)** time — option comparison before an action rather than damage assessment after. **(c)** QA — AIS, Kpler, Vortexa and customs data disagree on the same tanker's cargo routinely; explicit reconciliation with a reported confidence is the value. **(d)** cost — the recurring "who should we designate next" list-building.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — OFAC maritime designations; the G7 price-cap attestation regime; the EU shadow-fleet listings; ONI's merchant-shipping analysis. Commercial — [Windward](https://windward.ai/), Kpler, Vortexa, Lloyd's List Intelligence, Pole Star, Starboard, Synmax. **The gap:** these are *detection and screening* systems; not one of them answers "if we do X, what happens to revenue in 90 days," and none is scored on a forecast.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — AIS dark periods are exactly where the truth lives, and the model's estimate of dark-period volume is its weakest link; hull-pool slack is hard to observe and dominates the answer; freight-rate endogeneity is easy to get wrong in the direction that flatters enforcement. Red line: this system tracks *vessels and companies*, not crews. Structural enforcement: the entity schema admits IMO numbers, company registrations and flag states only — there is no crew or master field, so a person-level product is not expressible; and the recommendation enum is limited to `{designate_vessel, designate_entity, registry_demarche, port_state_control, insurance_demarche}` with no interdiction-at-sea targeting output.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6. Agent-based maritime logistics is mature engineering; the data is commercially available; the backtest episodes are dated and public.

### C-TECHECON-6 — Control-Backfire Counterfactual ("did our own policy accelerate their indigenization?")

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It answers the question every export-control debate turns on but nobody scores — whether a restriction *slowed* the adversary or *accelerated* their homegrown replacement — by simulating the world in which we had not imposed it and comparing.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"Relative to a no-control counterfactual, what is the change in the PRC's domestic share of semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment installed base at the end of 2028, attributable to the October 2022 through December 2024 US control regime?"* Bands over a signed delta: `[≤ −10pp (controls suppressed indigenization)] [−10 to −2pp] [−2 to +2pp (no effect)] [+2 to +10pp] [≥ +10pp (controls accelerated it)]`. Resolves against SEMI equipment billings by origin plus Chinese customs import share.
- **Mission and user:** ODNI/NIC and the CIA analytic line that supports the interagency export-control policy process; BIS policy (not enforcement); the NSC technology directorate. **Today:** this is argued in op-eds and think-tank papers with no counterfactual discipline at all, and the two camps cite the same facts. [IARPA's FOCUS program was built precisely to give analysts "practical, accurate, evidence-based techniques for counterfactual forecasting"](https://www.iarpa.gov/index.php/research-programs/focus) and evaluated its methods by having people predict outcomes in complex simulations — which is exactly the shape of this candidate, and a direct lineage argument to make to the customer.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *indigenization-response regime*. State variables: `subsidyElasticity` (RMB of state capital → capacity, with a misallocation loss term), `absorptiveCapacity` (engineer stock, prior process knowledge — this is where the *talent-flow* variable enters, and visa/immigration policy is a config knob here), `demandCertainty` (does the control guarantee a captive domestic market, which is the core backfire mechanism), `foreignSupplierRetaliation` (do restricted suppliers lose the revenue that funded their own R&D — the "we cut our own legs" channel), `technicalDifficultyFloor` (some steps do not yield to money — EUV being the standing example). **riskTolerance** = the state's willingness to fund ventures with a high probability of total loss; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to mandate domestic-content purchasing that forces adoption of inferior domestic tools. Meaningful variation: yes, and the sign of the answer flips across profiles, which is the point.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **A synthetic-control / difference-in-differences estimator wrapped around an endogenous-response system-dynamics model.** Two coupled pieces: (1) a *statistical* counterfactual — a synthetic control constructed from donor-pool sectors and countries not subject to the control, giving an empirical estimate of the untreated path with a placebo-based inference procedure; and (2) a *structural* counterfactual — the SD model of capacity accumulation from C-TECHECON-3 run twice, once with the control-induced demand-certainty and subsidy shocks and once without, with the difference reported. The two are deliberately *not* pooled; disagreement between the statistical and structural counterfactual is itself a reported diagnostic and a trigger for the abstention gate. Deterministic given seed; the PRNG drives only the placebo permutations and the misallocation draws.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "controls slowed them / controls had no measurable effect / controls accelerated indigenization / effect is real but confounded by pre-existing policy (Made in China 2025, Big Fund)." Diagnosticity is highest on evidence dated *before* the control (pre-trend evidence), which is what separates a real treatment effect from a continuation. **Key-assumptions check** on the donor pool and the parallel-trends assumption — the assumption that kills synthetic control when it is wrong. **Devil's advocate** takes the position that the counterfactual is unknowable and the honest answer is INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE; because the abstention gate runs before scoring, this judge can actually win, which is unusual and is a demonstrable selling point. **Premortem** on "we declared backfire, policy was loosened, and the adversary got both the tools and the subsidy." **Red team** may force a rerun when the pre-trend is visibly non-parallel. Rubric axes: counterfactual identification strength, pre-trend validity, confounder treatment, structural/statistical agreement, honest uncertainty.
- **The config pack:** `actors/indigenization-response-profiles.yaml`, `domain/counterfactual-adapter.ts` (donor-pool definition, treatment-date registry, outcome-series loaders), `rubric/counterfactual-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when pre-trend divergence exceeds a threshold, when the donor pool is smaller than k, or when the structural and statistical counterfactuals disagree in sign by more than a set margin — three independent, checkable gates), `judges/panel-counterfactual.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Fully unclassified; this is the most publishable candidate.** SEMI equipment billings by ship-to region and by supplier origin; Chinese customs HS 8486 imports; CNIPA patent filings by assignee and technology class; SMIC/Hua Hong/Naura/AMEC financial filings; Big Fund disbursement disclosures; OpenAlex/Web of Science publication counts; [CSET's Chinese Talent Program Tracker](https://chinatalenttracker.cset.tech/) and its [work on AI talent flows and visa policy](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/strengthening-the-u-s-ai-workforce/) for the absorptive-capacity input. **Needs classified access:** essentially none for the core estimate — which makes this the ideal public demonstrator.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **This is the hardest oracle in the catalog and must be stated plainly: the true counterfactual is unobservable, so the primary scoring is on *predictions the counterfactual model implies*, not on the counterfactual itself.** Three legitimate arms: (1) **Synthetic closed worlds** where a simulated economy's untreated path is generated by the platform and therefore genuinely known — this is the only place the counterfactual estimate itself can be scored, and it is exactly what [FOCUS did by evaluating counterfactual methods inside complex simulations](https://www.iarpa.gov/index.php/research-programs/focus). (2) **Historical backtest on the model's forward implications:** as-of 2022-10-06, the model implies a 2025 domestic-SME-share number; score that against realized SEMI data. As-of 2019-05-16 (Huawei Entity List), it implies HiSilicon design-share and Kirin re-emergence timing; score against reality. (3) **Analogue episodes with known outcomes:** COCOM-era Soviet machine-tool restrictions, the 1990s crypto export regime, Japan's 2019 restrictions on photoresist/HF exports to Korea (a clean, well-documented case where the restriction is widely assessed to have accelerated Korean indigenization — a labeled positive for the backfire class). It is *not* UNSCORABLE, but its scoring is indirect, and the proposal must say so in the same breath as the pitch.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — puts a signed, banded number on the single most contested claim in technology policy. **(c)** QA — the structural-vs-statistical disagreement diagnostic *is* a source-discrepancy reconciliation mechanism, and the weakest-link confidence rule is what stops the product from over-claiming. **(b)** partially — this is a deliberative-cycle product, not a fast-turn one. **(d)** weakly.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — [IARPA FOCUS](https://www.iarpa.gov/index.php/research-programs/focus) (the direct ancestor); the export-control policy reviews run by BIS and the interagency. Academic/commercial — CSET, RAND, Rhodium Group, Peterson Institute all publish qualitative versions of this argument. **The gap:** nobody publishes a *scored, banded, falsifiable* backfire estimate with a stated counterfactual method.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — synthetic control is fragile to donor-pool selection and to anticipation effects (firms stockpiled tools ahead of announced rules, contaminating the pre-period); confounding with Made in China 2025 and Big Fund disbursements that predate the controls is severe. The honest outcome for many configurations is abstention, and if the system does not abstain often here, that is evidence it is broken. Red line: a "controls backfired" finding is politically loaded and will be quoted selectively; structural enforcement is provenance-on-every-claim plus the required-falsifier field on every key assumption, so a quoted conclusion carries its own refutation conditions, and the evaluation-regime tag on the report makes it impossible to present a synthetic-oracle result as a real-world one.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 / 5. Lower than its neighbors, honestly: the estimator is standard but the identification problem is real, and the fastest credible path is the synthetic-world arm plus the Japan–Korea 2019 analogue.

### C-TECHECON-7 — Technology Proliferation Diffusion: who fields what, when

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It treats the spread of a military-relevant technology like the spread of a contagion across a network of supplier relationships and industrial capabilities, and predicts which countries or armed groups field it next and in what order.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"Of 40 candidate states and non-state armed groups, which will field a domestically produced one-way attack drone with ≥1,000 km range by 31 December 2029, and in what order?"* Two resolvable quantities: a per-actor probability (scored by Brier) and a total count band `[0–3] [4–7] [8–12] [13–20] [>20]`, plus rank-order accuracy (Spearman) against realized first-use dates.
- **Mission and user:** DIA's Missile and Space Intelligence Center and NASIC, State/ISN (nonproliferation policy and the MTCR delegation), the COCOM J2s who need to know what shows up in their AOR, and the CIA counter-proliferation mission. **Today:** proliferation assessments are built country-by-country from procurement reporting and combat observations, and the "who's next" question is answered by analogy. There is no diffusion model, so the assessment is systematically late — the technology appears, then the assessment updates.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *diffusion regime*. State variables per candidate actor: `supplierTies` (edges to states already fielding, weighted by transfer history from SIPRI), `absorptiveCapacity` (machine-tool stock, electronics assembly base, engineering graduates), `demandPressure` (active conflict, adversary air-defense density, cost-per-effect logic), `financingAccess`, `denialExposure` (MTCR/sanctions coverage), plus network-level `transferPermissiveness` and `componentAvailability` (COTS microelectronics availability — the actual enabler). **riskTolerance** = the supplier's willingness to transfer despite regime exposure; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to transfer *production capability* rather than finished units, which is the step-change (the Iran→Russia Shahed/Geran transfer is the canonical example, and [Russian domestic production is now assessed around 3,000 Shahed-class units per month with 2026 plans of 7.3 million FPV drones per Ukrainian military intelligence](https://drone-warfare.com/research/shahed-136/)). Meaningful variation: yes — the adopter set and ordering change substantially with `transferPermissiveness`.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Network-cascade diffusion with a survival/hazard model — the epidemiological family.** Adoption hazard for actor *i* at time *t*: `h_i(t) = λ_0(t) · exp(β₁·networkExposure_i(t) + β₂·absorptiveCapacity_i + β₃·demandPressure_i − β₄·denialExposure_i)`, where `networkExposure` is a weighted count of adopting neighbors (i.e. a genuine contagion term, not just covariates), integrated to survival probabilities; the cascade is computed on the transfer graph so that each adoption updates neighbors' exposure. Coefficients are fit on a *historical* diffusion (armed UAV adoption 2011–2023) and applied out-of-sample. Seeded PRNG for adoption realizations; the hazard computation is exact. Same seed ⇒ identical adopter sequence.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "supplier-driven diffusion / indigenous-development diffusion / component-availability-driven diffusion / demand-driven diffusion" — each implies a different interdiction lever (control the supplier, control the component, or accept it). **Key-assumptions check** on whether "fielding" means demonstrated, produced, or employed — a definitional assumption that silently drives the answer and must carry a falsifier. **Devil's advocate** argues the technology is already commoditized and the diffusion question is moot (for small drones this is arguably true, which is a useful thing for the panel to be able to conclude). **Premortem** on missing a non-state adopter entirely because the network model has no edge for a non-state actor. **Red team** may force a rerun when an adoption is predicted for an actor with no plausible acquisition pathway. Rubric axes: mechanism identification, network-data quality, definitional discipline, non-state coverage, denial-lever actionability.
- **The config pack:** `actors/diffusion-regimes.yaml`, `domain/proliferation-adapter.ts` (transfer graph loader, capability-definition schema with explicit fielding criteria, denial-regime coverage map), `rubric/proliferation-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when the transfer graph has fewer than k observed edges for the technology class, or when the fielding criterion cannot be operationalized against an observable), `judges/panel-prolif.yaml`, `oracle/diffusion-backtests.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo credible.** SIPRI Arms Transfers Database (the transfer graph's backbone); the Bard/New America and Drone Wars UK armed-UAV adopter datasets; UN Panel of Experts reports (DPRK, Libya, Yemen) for component sourcing; Conflict Armament Research field documentation of recovered components (this is genuinely excellent, unclassified, physical-evidence-grade data); UN Comtrade for dual-use component flows; ACLED for demand pressure; UNIDO/World Bank manufacturing indices for absorptive capacity; patent and publication data. **Needs classified access:** covert transfer channels, and pre-fielding program intelligence. The unclassified demo on historical armed-UAV diffusion is fully self-contained.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff — the best-specified oracle here.** Fit the hazard model on adopters through 2015, hold out 2016–2023, and score per-actor Brier plus rank-order correlation against realized first-confirmed-use dates from the public adopter datasets. Second arm: ballistic-missile proliferation 1960–2000 with a 1980 cutoff. Third arm: the Iran→Russia→(next) Shahed-class production transfer, with as-of 2022-08-01 forecasts scored against the documented Alabuga localization. **Live post-cutoff:** "which actors field a ≥1,000 km one-way attack drone in 2027" is directly resolvable from open reporting. Coverage@N and tail-recall are the metrics that matter most here — the mission cost of missing a low-probability adopter is asymmetric, and tail recall is already in the platform's metrics package.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — a fitted contagion model beats analogical reasoning, and can be shown to. **(b)** time and prediction — turns a reactive assessment into a ranked watch list with dates. **(c)** QA — SIPRI, UN panels, CAR field data and press reporting disagree on adoption dates constantly; the panel reconciles and reports which source drove the call. **(d)** cost — the recurring proliferation watch-list refresh.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — [MTCR](https://mtcr.info/) and the Wassenaar/NSG regimes; DIA/MSIC and NASIC assessments; UN Panels of Experts. Academic — the arms-diffusion literature (Horowitz's adoption-capacity theory is the closest theoretical ancestor); [Conflict Armament Research](https://www.conflictarm.com/). Commercial — Janes, Armada, no real predictive product. **The gap:** the theory exists in academia and the data exists in the open, and nobody has wired them into a scored forecasting product for the IC.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — the "fielding" definition is where this model dies if the config is sloppy; the transfer graph is sparse and biased toward reported transfers; non-state actors are systematically under-edged. Contagion coefficients fit on one technology generation may not transfer to another. Red line: this system forecasts *state and organizational* adoption, never individual scientists or procurement officers, and must not be usable as a targeting aid. Structural enforcement: the actor schema's node type is an enum over `{state, armed_group, state_owned_enterprise}` — natural persons are not representable — and the product schema emits `indicators` and `denial_levers` only, with no location, no facility coordinates, and no individual attribution fields.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6. Survival models on network exposure are well understood; the datasets exist; the backtest is straightforward.

### C-TECHECON-8 — Economic-Coercion Instrument Chooser (two-sided: adversary's move, and ours)

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Given a dispute, it predicts which economic weapon the adversary reaches for, against which target, at what intensity — and run in mirror mode, it does the same for our own instrument menu including sanctions, export controls, tariffs, and investment blocks.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"If [ally] proceeds with [action] in the next 180 days, what is the probability distribution over Beijing's response instrument — {no action, informal customs slowdown, targeted import ban, tourism/education restriction, rare-earth licensing pressure, antitrust/regulatory probe, procurement exclusion, formal tariff} — and what is the banded 12-month trade loss to the ally's affected sector?"* Instrument choice is a categorical distribution (scored by log score and Brier); trade loss is banded (% of affected-sector export value).
- **Mission and user:** CIA and DIA analysts supporting allied-consultation policy; State EB and the regional bureaus; the NSC when an ally is deciding whether to take a step that will be punished; and in mirror mode, the interagency choosing a US instrument (this is where CFIUS and outbound-investment screening enter — as instruments in the menu, not as a separate product). **Today:** analysts reason by precedent — "they did this to Australia over barley, to Korea over THAAD, to Lithuania over the Taiwan office, to Norway over the Nobel" — which is exactly a small-N nearest-neighbor lookup done in a human head, with no calibration and no probability.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *coercer decision-calculus profile*. State variables: `targetVulnerability` (export concentration to the coercer, substitutability of the good, ally's domestic political tolerance), `coercerSelfHarm` (own import dependence on the same good), `deniability` (informal customs measures are deniable; formal tariffs are not), `signalingAudience` (domestic vs third-party deterrent demonstration), `precedentWeight` (how much the coercer's own past cases constrain the choice), `institutionalExposure` (WTO challenge risk). **riskTolerance** = tolerance for economic self-harm and reputational cost; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to move up the instrument ladder from deniable to formal. Meaningful variation: yes — the instrument distribution shifts materially, and the *deniability* dimension is what most human analysts under-weight.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Discrete-choice utility maximization over an instrument menu, embedded in a two-stage signaling game.** Stage 1: the coercer computes, for each instrument *k*, a utility `U_k = α·targetPain_k − β·selfHarm_k + γ·signalValue_k − δ·institutionalCost_k`, where `targetPain_k` and `selfHarm_k` are *computed* from bilateral trade data by a small partial-equilibrium sub-model (concentration ratio × elasticity × substitution lag), not assumed; a softmax over `U` with temperature indexed by `riskTolerance` yields the instrument distribution. Stage 2: the target's compliance decision updates the coercer's continuation value, giving escalate/hold/withdraw dynamics over the horizon. Deterministic given seed (the PRNG draws the instrument realization and the target's political-tolerance shock). Coefficients α–δ are *fit on the historical coercion case set*, not hand-set — which is what makes this scorable rather than a story generator.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over the instrument classes directly (the hypothesis set and the answer set coincide here, which makes diagnosticity weighting unusually clean). **Key-assumptions check** on the target's substitution options — the assumption that determined whether the Australian barley and wine cases hurt (they found other markets for barley; wine was devastated). **Devil's advocate** argues for the null instrument: coercion is announced and never materially executed, which is a real and frequent outcome. **Premortem** on "we advised an ally that the response would be mild and it was severe" — asymmetric-cost error. **Red team** may force a rerun when the assessed target vulnerability rests on a single trade statistic. Rubric axes: instrument-menu completeness, vulnerability computation validity, deniability reasoning, precedent handling, ally-actionability.
- **The config pack:** `actors/coercer-profiles.yaml` (PRC, Russia, Türkiye, Saudi, plus a US mirror profile), `domain/coercion-adapter.ts` (instrument menu with observable signatures, bilateral trade loaders, sector-concentration calculators), `rubric/coercion-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when bilateral trade data for the affected sector is unavailable at the needed HS granularity, or when fewer than k historical cases exist for the coercer profile), `judges/panel-coercion.yaml`, `oracle/coercion-cases.yaml`. **Mirror config** `actors/us-instrument-profiles.yaml` swaps the menu to `{OFAC designation, BIS rule, Section 301 tariff, CFIUS block, outbound-investment prohibition, entity-list addition, no action}` for US decision support — same kernel, different config, which is the platform's config-not-code claim demonstrated on one candidate.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo credible and the training set exists.** [The Global Sanctions Data Base R4 — 1,547 cases, 1950–2023, coded by type, objective and success](https://www.globalsanctionsdatabase.com/) is the fitting corpus; UN Comtrade and IMF DOTS for bilateral concentration; WTO dispute filings; national customs clearance-time data (the observable signature of an informal slowdown); the documented China coercion case set (Norway 2010, Japan 2010, Philippines 2012, Korea 2016–17, Australia 2020, Lithuania 2021). **Needs classified access:** leadership intent and internal deliberation. The unclassified fit-and-score is complete without it, which makes this a strong demo.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff on a labeled case set — the cleanest fit-and-holdout in the catalog.** Fit on GSDB cases through 2015; hold out 2016–2023 and score instrument-class prediction (log score, Brier, ECE with a reliability diagram) and trade-loss band accuracy. Named holdout episodes: Korea/THAAD 2016 (tourism and retail measures — did the model pick the deniable instrument?), Lithuania 2021 (customs de-listing, an unusually creative instrument — a good test of menu completeness), Australia 2020 (multi-instrument, staged escalation — tests the stage-2 dynamics), Japan 2010 REE (tests cross-lens consistency with C-TECHECON-4). **Live post-cutoff:** any ongoing dispute with a 180-day horizon. Baseline for skill: the empirical base rate of each instrument class in the GSDB — a genuinely hard baseline to beat, which is the right kind of test.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — a fitted discrete-choice model with a published ECE against an analyst's precedent-recall. **(b)** time — a same-day answer for an ally consultation. **(c)** QA — GSDB coding, press reporting and customs data disagree on whether a measure was coercion at all; explicit adjudication with a confidence field. **(d)** cost — the repetitive "what will they do if we do X" tasking.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — State/EB and Treasury policy analysis; the [Atlantic Council Global Sanctions Dashboard](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/programs/geoeconomics-center/global-sanctions-dashboard/); allied efforts including the EU Anti-Coercion Instrument. Academic — the GSDB team (Felbermayr, Kirilakha, Syropoulos, Yotov et al.) and the Hufbauer–Schott–Elliott sanctions-effectiveness tradition; Rhodium and MERICS coercion case studies. **The gap:** the case data is coded and public and nobody has turned it into a calibrated forward predictor.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — small N (a few hundred usable coercion cases) risks overfitting; instrument menus evolve and a novel instrument (like the Lithuania customs de-listing) is by construction outside the menu, so menu-completeness is a named failure mode and the abstention gate should fire on "no menu item fits the observed signature." Coding "success" is contested in the source data itself. Red line: the mirror mode produces recommendations about *US instrument choice*, which edges toward policy advocacy; structural enforcement is that the mirror config's product schema emits `instrumentEffectEstimate` per instrument with no ranking field and no `recommended: true` — the system estimates effects and refuses to pick, by type.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 / 6–7. This is the fastest-to-credible candidate: labeled training data, a small kernel, a hard baseline, and a holdout that can be scored this quarter.

### C-TECHECON-9 — Financial-Rail Escape Threshold ("when does the chokepoint stop being a chokepoint?")

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It forecasts how much of an adversary's cross-border money movement can escape dollar-clearing chokepoints, and — the part that matters — identifies the tipping point at which enough counterparties have joined an alternative rail that adoption becomes self-sustaining.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"By 31 December 2028, what share of PRC–Gulf and PRC–Russia goods trade will be settled outside dollar correspondent clearing, and what is the probability that mBridge or a successor reaches a participant count and volume sufficient for self-sustaining growth?"* Bands on settlement share; a probability on the threshold-crossing event, resolvable against SWIFT RMB Tracker shares, CIPS published volumes, and central-bank disclosures.
- **Mission and user:** Treasury's Office of Terrorist Financing and Financial Intelligence and its IC support; the CIA and DIA economic lines; the Federal Reserve's international affairs staff as a consumer. **Today:** analysts track headline share numbers and argue about significance. The numbers are volatile and easy to misread in both directions — [the renminbi's share of world payment volume reportedly fell from 4.33% in February 2025 to 2.74% by February 2026](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/06/24/china-dollar-dedollarization-yuan-renminbi-brics-finance-banks-sanctions/) while [mBridge is simultaneously being moved from pilot toward commercial operation](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/06/15/chinas-mbridge-platform-aims-to-combat-us-dollar-dominance/) — meaning a "share is falling, threat is receding" read and a "capability is maturing, threat is growing" read are both defensible from the same month's data. That is exactly the condition a competing-hypotheses adjudicator is for.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is a distinct *adoption regime*. State variables: `participantSet` (which banks and central banks are on the rail — [mBridge is backed by central banks in China, Hong Kong, Thailand and the UAE after the BIS transferred management to participants in 2024](https://www.vtmarkets.com/en-asia/live-updates/chinas-mbridge-cbdc-network-challenges-swift-with-cheaper-cross-border-settlement-alongside-cips/)), `liquidityDepth` per currency pair, `settlementCostDelta` vs correspondent banking, `secondarySanctionsRisk` perceived by each candidate joiner, `tradeInvoicingShare` by corridor, `capitalControlFriction` (the structural reason RMB settlement stalls even when politically favored). **riskTolerance** = a joining bank's tolerance for US secondary-sanctions exposure; **escalationPropensity** = a state's willingness to *mandate* settlement-rail use for its own trade rather than let commercial preference decide. Meaningful variation: yes — mandated-adoption profiles cross the threshold years earlier than commercial-preference profiles.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **A network-externality threshold model (coordination game with critical mass) coupled to a payment-routing flow model.** Each candidate participant *j* adopts when `benefit_j(n) > cost_j`, where `benefit_j(n)` rises with the number of adopted counterparties weighted by *j*'s trade exposure to them — a Granovetter/Schelling threshold cascade on the trade-weighted counterparty graph, with `cost_j` including a secondary-sanctions expected-loss term scaled by `riskTolerance`. Iterating the cascade to a fixed point yields either a small stable adopter set or a tip to near-universal adoption within the corridor — and the model's real output is the *location of the tipping point in participant-count space*, which is a decision-relevant number nobody currently produces. A flow sub-model then routes actual corridor trade values across rails subject to liquidity-depth capacity constraints, yielding the settlement-share quantity. Seeded PRNG for the adoption-order tie-breaks. Deterministic cascade given seed.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over "structural stall (capital controls dominate) / slow linear substitution / corridor-specific tip with no global effect / systemic tip." Diagnosticity is highest on *corridor-level* evidence, because aggregate share numbers are nearly non-diagnostic between these hypotheses — a nice demonstration of what diagnosticity weighting actually buys. **Key-assumptions check** on the secondary-sanctions deterrence coefficient, which is the US's actual policy lever and the most uncertain parameter. **Devil's advocate** argues the entire question is a category error — that [mBridge has attracted only four members since ~2022 and the project became toxic after the sanctions-circumvention controversy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/06/24/china-dollar-dedollarization-yuan-renminbi-brics-finance-banks-sanctions/), so the threat is rhetorical. **Premortem** on both directions: crying wolf and being blindsided. **Red team** may force a rerun when a corridor's invoicing-share input comes from a single national statistical source. Rubric axes: threshold-model validity, corridor-level grounding, deterrence-coefficient justification, aggregate-vs-corridor discipline, policy-lever identification.
- **The config pack:** `actors/adoption-regimes.yaml`, `domain/payments-adapter.ts` (corridor definitions, counterparty graph from BIS locational banking statistics, rail roster with capability attributes, liquidity-depth loaders), `rubric/payments-rubric.yaml`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when corridor-level settlement-currency data is unavailable — which is frequently true and is the honest state of this question), `judges/panel-financial.yaml`, `oracle/payments-backtests.yaml`.
- **Data required:** **Unclassified demo credible but thinner than others.** SWIFT RMB Tracker; CIPS published participant lists and volumes; BIS locational and consolidated banking statistics; BIS Triennial Survey; IMF COFER for reserve composition; central-bank swap-line announcements; customs invoicing-currency data where published (a real gap for several corridors); on-chain analytics (Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic) for the crypto rail. **Needs classified access:** actual transaction-level flows, bank internal risk decisions, and the intent behind state mandates. **The unclassified demo is possible but must abstain more often than the others, and that should be shown, not hidden — it is the best available live demonstration of the abstention gate doing real work.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoff.** Episode 1: Iran's 2012 SWIFT disconnection — as-of 2012-02-01, forecast the 24-month share of Iranian trade settled via non-dollar mechanisms (the ground truth includes the gold-for-gas Türkiye channel, a genuine test of whether the model finds unanticipated rails). Episode 2: Russia post-February 2022 — as-of 2022-03-01, forecast the 24-month RMB share of Russian trade settlement; the realized shift is large, well documented, and a model that predicts a small shift fails cleanly. Episode 3: the 2015 launch of CIPS — as-of launch, forecast 5-year volume. **Live post-cutoff:** mBridge participant count at end-2027; RMB payment share; CIPS volume. Baseline: linear extrapolation of current share — which is a *strong* baseline here and the model may not beat it, which the metrics package will say out loud rather than obscure.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(a)** accuracy — replaces headline-share reading with an explicit tipping-point model. **(c)** QA — this is the domain where sources most flagrantly disagree (SWIFT share, CIPS volume, and trade-invoicing share measure different things and are routinely conflated in reporting); explicit reconciliation with the weakest-link confidence rule is the core value. **(b)** moderate. **(d)** moderate.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government — Treasury TFI analysis; Fed international affairs; [the BIS's original mBridge stewardship and its 2024 handover under US pressure](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/06/24/china-dollar-dedollarization-yuan-renminbi-brics-finance-banks-sanctions/). Think tank — [Atlantic Council's Dollar Dominance Monitor and CBDC Tracker](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/programs/geoeconomics-center/); Peterson Institute. Commercial — Chainalysis and TRM on the crypto slice; no vendor owns the rail-adoption forecasting question. **The gap:** everyone tracks the level; nobody models the threshold.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — corridor-level invoicing data is the binding constraint and is often absent, so the honest output is frequently abstention; threshold models are notoriously sensitive to the assumed benefit function and can produce spurious sharp tips; the crypto rail's on-chain data is high-volume and low-relevance for state-scale settlement and will mislead a naive model. Red line: financial-flow analysis abuts US persons and financial-privacy law directly. Structural enforcement: the domain adapter ingests only *aggregate, institution-level and corridor-level* series — the input schema has no transaction, account, or individual-payer field, so transaction-level analysis is not expressible in config; and the source allowlist in the domain adapter rejects any dataset flagged as containing US-person financial records at ingest, before the run starts.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 / 5. Lower than most: the kernel is easy, the *data* is the problem, and the abstention rate in the unclassified configuration will be high.

### C-TECHECON-10 — **BAD IDEA (labeled):** Adversary GDP Nowcast from Satellite and Shipping

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** A continuously updated estimate of an adversary economy's real output, built from nighttime lights, port throughput, industrial thermal signatures and freight movement, sold as a forecasting product.
- **The analyst question it answers:** *"What was PRC/Russian/Iranian real GDP growth this quarter, ahead of and independent of the official statistical release?"* — a measurement quantity, resolvable against later official and reconstructed estimates.
- **Mission and user:** CIA and DIA economic analysts, Treasury, the Fed. **Today:** they use official statistics adjusted by judgment, plus commercial satellite-derived indices. This part is real: [nighttime-lights data is genuinely used to evaluate sanctions impacts precisely because conventional data from targeted countries is unavailable or untrustworthy](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecin.13183).
- **What the N simulants are:** **This is the disqualifying flaw and it must be stated first.** Essentially nothing varies run to run. A nowcast is an estimator, not a contested future; there is no adversary making a decision, no branching, no mutually exclusive world-states to fan out over. Forced into the platform's shape, the "N simulants" degenerate into N random-seed perturbations of the same regression — which produces an ensemble-diversity metric near zero and a bogus confidence interval that reflects estimator noise rather than genuine hypothesis divergence. **The candidate is weak on exactly the axis the platform exists to serve.** The riskTolerance and escalationPropensity knobs have no referent at all here — there is no actor whose risk tolerance is being modeled.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Effectively NO KERNEL in the platform's sense.** What it computes is a panel regression or a state-space/Kalman nowcast mapping observable proxies to output — a *statistical estimator*, not a simulation of a mechanism. There is no dynamics, no interaction, no decision. Calling a Kalman filter a "deterministic kernel" is technically true and substantively dishonest, and a technically literate government evaluator will say so.
- **What the judge panel is:** The panel adds nearly nothing. ACH over "the economy grew X vs Y" is not a set of competing *explanations* with differing evidential support; it is a confidence interval. Devil's advocate and premortem have no purchase on a point estimate. A key-assumptions check on the luminosity–output elasticity is genuinely useful — but that is one judge's worth of value, and does not need a panel, a fan-out, or an adjudication layer.
- **The config pack:** `domain/nowcast-adapter.ts`, a proxy roster, and a calibration table. Notably short — which is itself a tell that the platform is not adding anything.
- **Data required:** VIIRS/DMSP nighttime lights; Sentinel-2 and commercial optical; AIS port throughput; thermal anomaly detection; freight rail; NO₂ column density as an industrial-activity proxy. All unclassified; a demo is trivially possible. That easiness is part of the trap.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** Scorable in principle (against revised official data or independent reconstructions), so not UNSCORABLE — **but the scoring exposes the problem rather than solving it.** The literature already shows the incumbent proxies are badly behaved: [DMSP nighttime-lights data suffers from blurring and bottom-coding such that the estimated impact of closing the Kaesong Industrial Zone was a precisely estimated 50% luminosity decline with better data but small and imprecise with DMSP](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecin.13183), meaning published sanctions-impact estimates built on this proxy are biased. The platform's contribution would be to inherit that bias with extra steps.
- **Benefit categories hit:** **(d)** only, weakly — automating a data pull. It does not hit (b) meaningfully because commercial nowcasts already exist at speed, and it barely touches (c) because there is only one source class to reconcile.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Commercial incumbents are strong, cheap and entrenched: SpaceKnow, Orbital Insight (and its successors), Kpler, Descartes Labs, Planet, plus the entire quant-hedge-fund alternative-data industry, which spends far more on this than any IC program will. Government: NGA's commercial GEOINT programs and the Fed/IMF nowcasting literature. **We would be entering a crowded, well-capitalized market with a differentiator we do not have.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical — proxy-to-output elasticity drift (LED lighting conversion alone breaks the nighttime-lights relationship over time), cloud cover, sensor changeover discontinuities, and structural breaks precisely during the shocks you care about. Strategically — it invites the evaluator to judge us on measurement accuracy against specialists, on their turf, instead of on adjudicated forecasting, which is where we are actually differentiated. Red line: none serious. **Recommendation: do not propose this as a candidate solution. If satellite-derived economic activity is wanted, it belongs as an *evidence source* feeding C-TECHECON-2, -4 and -6, not as a product.**
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 5 / 6 — and the high TRL is exactly the problem: it is high because the capability is commoditized, which means it is not a reason to fund us.

## Cross-cutting notes for the writer

**Strongest four, ranked by evidence-per-dollar in the next 6 months:** C-TECHECON-8 (labeled training data in GSDB, small kernel, hard baseline, scorable this quarter) > C-TECHECON-2 (public packages with clean numeric outcomes, and it is the candidate that most directly serves benefit category (c)) > C-TECHECON-4 (a **date-certain live resolution event on 10 and 27 November 2026** sits inside the proposal window) > C-TECHECON-1 (best backtest data, most crowded incumbent field).

**The differentiator to state in every candidate:** the named incumbents — Sayari, Kharon, C4ADS, Altana, Interos, Govini, Windward, Kpler — are **illumination and screening** products. They answer *what is the network*. Not one of them answers *what happens next if we do X*, and **not one of them publishes a Brier score against its own past calls.** The platform's abstention gate, evaluation-regime tag, and separated likelihood/confidence fields are the specific things none of them have.

**Weakest oracles, flag honestly:** C-TECHECON-6 (counterfactual is unobservable; only the synthetic-world arm scores the counterfactual itself), C-TECHECON-9 (corridor-level data gaps force frequent abstention). Both are worth including *because* they demonstrate the abstention gate doing real work — but only if the proposal says so plainly rather than burying it.

**Not covered, deliberately, and why:** a standalone CFIUS product — the scoring oracle is weak (mitigation-agreement outcomes are largely non-public) and it collapses into a Bayesian belief network with a thin kernel; it is folded into C-TECHECON-8's mirror config as one instrument among several. Talent/visa flows are folded into C-TECHECON-3 and -6 as an absorptive-capacity input rather than a standalone candidate, because the policy-to-capability lag is long relative to any scorable horizon.

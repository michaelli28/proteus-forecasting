# Candidate catalog — Lens: Cyber operations, critical infrastructure, and cyber-physical escalation

**Lens key: `CYBER`.** Evidence base current as of 2026-08-10. Every factual claim carries a URL in the source list; assertions I could not verify this session are marked **UNVERIFIED** inline. Where I am reasoning rather than citing, I mark **INFERRED**.

A note that shapes the whole catalog: the customer's worked example (Iran hacking US water infrastructure) stopped being hypothetical on 2026-07-26, when a coordinated campaign disrupted operational technology at 30+ Minnesota water utilities — Braham's well and treatment plant were shut down, Plymouth disconnected cellular-connected equipment at two water towers and multiple wastewater lift stations, and CISA advisory **AA26-097A** (issued 2026-04-07, updated 2026-07-22, four days before the attacks) had already expanded observed PLC targeting from Rockwell to Schneider Electric and Siemens and documented project-file exfiltration for the first time (CONFIRMED via Tenable; the CISA advisory page itself returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch, so the advisory's exact text is **UNVERIFIED** — secondary sourcing only). Federal officials had **not** officially attributed the Minnesota campaign as of that reporting; the pattern aligns with CyberAv3ngers/IRGC-CEC. Candidates below are built to be resolvable against events of exactly that class.

### C-CYBER-1 — Activation Clock: pre-positioned access as an exercise decision, not a detection problem
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Treats an adversary's hidden footholds inside US infrastructure as a portfolio of perishable options and forecasts *when and against what* they would be spent, rather than trying to find them.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Conditional on a Taiwan blockade beginning in month M, what share of PRC pre-positioned accesses in US critical infrastructure is expended in the first 30 days, and what is the probability that at least one produces publicly-visible physical disruption in the Transportation or Water sectors?" Resolvable against: count of distinct US CI entities with adversary-attributed *disruptive* (not espionage) effect, per CISA advisories and EuRepoC coding, in a 30-day window.
- **Mission and user:** DIA/China analytic cadre and NSA/CYBERCOM J2 supporting INDOPACOM J39 contingency planning; ODNI NIC for the strategic estimate. Today the process is a hand-built scenario matrix in a Word document, argued through in a red-cell session, refreshed episodically. There is no reproducible artifact and no way to say what changed a judgment between two versions.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one PRC leadership decision profile. State variables: assessed size and diversity of the access inventory (by sector and by ICS Cyber Kill Chain stage); assessed detection hazard per access per month (Dragos assesses fewer than 10% of OT networks worldwide have network visibility — the hazard is genuinely low and that is the crux); assessed US resolve; assessed marginal military value of a delay effect versus a signaling effect; belief about whether the US already knows about the access. Knobs: **riskTolerance** = willingness to absorb US retaliation and the loss of the espionage value of a burned access; **escalationPropensity** = probability of selecting effects with kinetic-adjacent consequence (water, rail, pipeline) over reversible IT effects. Divergence is real: a low-risk/low-escalation profile holds everything and produces zero observable effects, a high/high profile burns 40% of the inventory in week one. These generate genuinely different observable futures.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** Game-theoretic **optimal stopping / real-options** with a competing-risks hazard layer. Each access is an option with a stochastic decay (detection hazard λ per month, patch/refresh hazard, and a "burned by a peer operation" hazard), an exercise payoff that is a function of the crisis phase, and an exercise cost (retaliation draw + permanent loss of collection). The kernel solves the value function backward over the crisis timeline with a seeded PRNG driving the hazard draws and the retaliation draw. Output is an exercise schedule per sector and a distribution over "accesses spent by day D." Calibration anchors: VOLTZITE's documented progression to Stage 2 (manipulating engineering workstations to dump configuration files and alarm data to learn what would trigger a process stop), SYLVANITE's observed access hand-off to VOLTZITE "within days," and KAMACITE's March–July 2025 US reconnaissance of Altivar VFDs, Smart HMIs, Accuenergy AXM modules and Sierra Wireless Airlink gateways — that is control-loop mapping, and it is the last step before exercise.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over the exercise-schedule hypotheses, weighted by diagnosticity of each indicator (an indicator that all profiles produce — e.g. continued scanning — gets near-zero weight; one only the high-escalation profile produces — e.g. wiper staging on an engineering workstation — dominates). Key-assumptions check judge scores the four load-bearing assumptions (detection hazard, inventory size, retaliation credibility, doctrinal preference for delay-over-punishment) each with a required falsifier. Devil's advocate judge argues the null: access is held indefinitely as deterrent signaling and never exercised. Premortem judge writes the 2027 retrospective "we predicted a burn and nothing happened." Red team may force a bounded rerun if the inventory-size prior is not traceable to a cited holding. Rubric axes: evidentiary grounding, discriminating power, sensitivity to the detection-hazard parameter, operational actionability, ICD 203 compliance.
- **The config pack:** `actors/prc-decision-profiles.yaml` (7 profiles), `kernel/option-exercise.ts` params (hazard priors by sector, retaliation draw distribution), `judges/panel-activation.yaml`, `rubric/coa-prediction.yaml`, `adapters/domain-ics-killchain.ts` (maps effects to ICS Cyber Kill Chain stage and ATT&CK for ICS technique IDs), `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (abstain when inventory-size prior width exceeds a bound).
- **Data required:** Unclassified and sufficient for a credible demo: CISA AA24-038A (Volt Typhoon, five years of persistence in communications, energy, transportation, water/wastewater), Dragos 2026 Year in Review threat-group profiles, MITRE ATT&CK for ICS, EuRepoC's 3,416-incident global dataset for base rates on state-attributed disruptive effects. Genuinely classified: actual counts and locations of known adversary accesses, SIGINT on tasking, and the intelligence behind any assessed decision threshold. **A credible unclassified demo is possible** — the demo forecasts the *shape* of the burn, and the classified instance swaps one config file (the inventory prior).
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** Three regimes, never pooled. (1) *Synthetic oracle*: closed worlds where an inventory and hazard are drawn and the optimal schedule is computable in closed form — measures whether the LLM-plus-kernel recovers the right schedule, and whether the abstention gate fires when inventory is unspecified. (2) *Historical backtest with as-of cutoff*: Russia's January 2022 position in Ukraine, cutoff 2022-01-15 — predict the observed burn sequence (WhisperGate, HermeticWiper, the Viasat modem event, the April 2022 Industroyer2 attempt); a second backtest on Iran–Israel June 2025, where PYROXENE/BAUXITE deployed custom wipers against Israeli targets, testing "rapid activation of pre-positioned access." (3) *Live post-cutoff*: "Will a US government body publicly attribute a *disruptive* OT effect against a US critical-infrastructure entity to a PRC-nexus actor before 2027-06-30?" — resolves against CISA advisories and DOJ/Treasury actions.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (b) time-to-analysis — a contingency estimate that today takes a red cell weeks becomes a same-day run with an auditable artifact hash; (c) analytic confidence — the confidence field is separated from likelihood and aggregates by weakest link, so a wide inventory prior visibly caps confidence instead of being averaged away; (a) accuracy — Brier skill is measured against an explicit base rate of state-attributed disruptive effects rather than asserted.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government program: **DARPA CASTLE** (Cyber Agents for Security Testing and Learning Environments) — but CASTLE trains *defenders* with reinforcement learning against APT tooling in emulated networks; it does not model the adversary's political exercise decision. Also adjacent: **IARPA REASON**, which improves the evidence and reasoning inside a draft analytic report but does not simulate. Closest commercial: **Dragos WorldView** threat-group intelligence (descriptive, not predictive) and **Mandiant/Google Threat Intelligence** actor profiles. Nobody sells the exercise decision as a modeled quantity — this is the gap. **INFERRED.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical failure is parameter dominance — the whole output is a smooth function of the assumed detection hazard and inventory size, and if the analyst can move the answer by moving one unciteable prior, the system is a laundering machine for a guess. Structural mitigation: a sensitivity report is mandatory output, and the abstention gate fires when any single parameter's elasticity exceeds a threshold. Second failure: modeling an adversary as a unitary rational actor when PRC cyber is a contractor ecosystem with its own incentives. Legal/ethical: no US-persons data enters this pipeline at all — the model's objects are adversary decision states and sector-level effects, never named individuals or accounts. The system produces *warning*, not target packages; the domain adapter has no field for a US-side offensive option, structurally.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 now (kernel family proven, no domain instance built) / 5–6 in 12 months (backtested on two historical episodes, one live question resolved).

### C-CYBER-2 — Hydra: cyber-physical consequence twin for water and wastewater
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Runs an actual hydraulic simulation of a real water utility under a real attacker's real playbook, and answers how many people lose safe water, for how long, before anyone has to guess.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "If the actor set behind the July 2026 Minnesota campaign repeats it against the metro system serving [City], what is the probability that a boil-water advisory affecting more than 100,000 residents is issued within 72 hours, and which three control points dominate that probability?" Resolvable against: issued boil-water advisories, population affected, and duration — all publicly reported quantities.
- **Mission and user:** EPA Office of Water as the designated Sector Risk Management Agency, the CISA regional cybersecurity advisor who has to triage which of 1,062 large drinking-water systems to visit, and WaterISAC's analytic staff. Today: EPA's own Inspector General ran a *passive external scan* of 1,062 systems serving 193 million people and found 97 systems serving 26.6 million with critical or high vulnerabilities — and then discovered EPA has no cybersecurity incident reporting system for water systems to notify it through. Meanwhile EPA's May 2024 enforcement alert found over 70% of inspected systems out of compliance with AWIA §2013 risk-and-resilience assessment requirements. The manual process being displaced is a consultant-run tabletop exercise plus a spreadsheet risk register, per utility, at roughly one utility per multi-week engagement.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one attacker profile crossed with one plant-state assumption. State variables: which control points are reachable (internet-exposed HMI on VNC 5900–5910 versus authenticated engineering workstation versus vendor remote access); attacker's process knowledge (none / device-level / control-loop-level — the distinction Dragos flags as the 2025 shift); operator detection latency and reversion-to-manual capability; chemical dosing setpoint manipulation versus pump/valve manipulation versus loss-of-view; time of day and demand pattern. Knobs: **riskTolerance** = willingness to cause a visible, attributable public-health effect versus a deniable nuisance; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to escalate from single-site to coordinated multi-site (the Minnesota signature) and from reversible setpoint change to alarm suppression.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** A genuine physical-process kernel, not a scoring heuristic. **WNTR** (Water Network Tool for Resilience) — the EPA/Sandia Python package, EPANET-2.2-compatible — run in **pressure-dependent demand (PDD)** mode so that when pressure falls below the required threshold, delivered demand falls with it according to the standard pressure-demand relationship, and goes to zero below minimum pressure. That is the difference between "the attacker changed a setpoint" and "37,000 people had no water at the tap for nine hours," and it is arithmetic, not judgment. Water-quality effects (chlorine residual decay, contaminant transport) run through the EpanetSimulator's water-quality engine. WNTR's `scenario` subpackage supplies fragility/survival curves for component failure. Given the same seed and the same INP network file, the artifact hash is byte-identical. The LLM's job is to translate an attacker playbook into a set of control actions and to interpret the hydrograph; the hydraulics are solved by Newton–Raphson or Todini's global gradient algorithm.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over consequence bands (no advisory / localized advisory / >100k advisory / multi-day outage) weighted by diagnosticity. A dedicated **key-assumptions check** judge on the three assumptions that dominate every water model: tank starting level, operator reversion time, and whether the SCADA-to-PLC path is actually reachable — each requires a falsifier naming a document or scan result that would refute it. Devil's advocate argues the hacktivist null: the actor produces a screen recording for Telegram and no physical effect, which is exactly what CISA documented pro-Russia groups doing (they "cannot reliably estimate the true impact of their actions"). Premortem judge writes the post-incident review. Red team may force a rerun if the network model's control logic is not sourced. Rubric axes: physical plausibility, evidentiary grounding of the access path, consequence-band discrimination, operator-actionability of the named control points, provenance completeness.
- **The config pack:** `networks/<utility>.inp` (EPANET network, or EPA's public Net3 for demo), `actors/bauxite-cyberavengers.yaml` and `actors/pro-russia-hacktivist.yaml` (TTPs sourced to AA23-335A and AA25-343A with ATT&CK for ICS IDs: T0836 modify parameter, T0831 manipulation of control, T0878 alarm suppression, T0829 loss of view, T0816 device restart/shutdown), `judges/panel-consequence.yaml`, `rubric/cyber-physical.yaml`, `adapters/domain-water.ts` (maps control actions onto WNTR controls), `thresholds/abstention.yaml`. **A sector-wide defensive-allocation variant and a "does this advisory move sector risk" variant ship as different config on this same kernel** — swap the single-utility network for a population of utilities and the attacker for a remediation-diffusion process; that is the "everything is config" claim doing real work.
- **Data required:** Unclassified: EPA/Sandia WNTR and EPANET including the public Net3 example network; CISA AA23-335A (IRGC-affiliated actors, Unitronics Vision PLCs, TCP/20256, default credentials, Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa); AA26-097A as reported (Rockwell MicroLogix 1100/1400, CVE-2021-22681 authentication bypass with no vendor patch, added to CISA KEV March 2026, Studio 5000 Logix Designer abuse); AA25-343A (VNC-based intrusions by CARR, Z-Pentest, NoName057(16), Sector16); EPA OIG report 25-N-0004; WaterISAC advisories; MITRE EMB3D for device-level threat properties. Classified: adversary intent and tasking; specific utility network models are usually not classified but are protected as sensitive infrastructure information. **A credible unclassified demo is not just possible, it is the strongest demo in this catalog** — Net3 plus AA23-335A plus AA26-097A, end to end, on a laptop.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** *Historical backtest with hard as-of cutoff* is available and cheap here, which is rare. Episodes: (1) Aliquippa, PA booster station, November 2023 — cutoff at AA23-335A publication, predict effect band (answer: no public-health effect, manual operation); (2) Muleshoe, TX, January 2024 — tank overflow; (3) the Minnesota campaign, 2026-07-26/27, cutoff at the 2026-07-22 AA26-097A update — predict the number of affected utilities' effect bands (answer: 30+ utilities, disabled controls and plant shutdown at Braham, precautionary disconnection at Plymouth, and per reporting "drinking water quality was not affected and no boil-water advisories were issued in the state" — a *low* consequence band despite high access, which is exactly the discrimination a hedging model fails). Sharpness is the metric that matters: a model that says "somewhere between nothing and catastrophe" is useless and the sharpness metric catches it. *Live post-cutoff*: resolvable monthly against WaterISAC incident reporting and state primacy-agency advisories. Plus the *synthetic oracle*: closed-world networks where the true consequence is computed by the same hydraulics but hidden from the reasoning path.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — replaces a qualitative "high/medium/low" risk register with a population-hours-without-water distribution; (b) time-to-analysis — a consultant tabletop per utility becomes a config file per utility, so 1,062 systems become tractable; (d) cost — the AWIA §2013 risk-and-resilience assessment is a repeating statutory obligation for every community water system serving >3,300 people, and its cyber component is exactly the repetitive simulation task this automates; (c) QA — reconciles the discrepancy between a vendor's "critical CVSS 9.8" and the utility's actual hydraulic exposure, which is the single most common source of misplaced confidence in this sector.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **INL's Consequence-driven Cyber-informed Engineering (CCE)** — four phases (consequence prioritization, system-of-systems analysis, consequence-based targeting, mitigations and protections). CCE is the right methodology and is entirely human-executed at roughly one engagement per organization; this candidate is CCE Phase 1 and Phase 3 rendered as a repeatable simulation. Also: EPA's Water Sector Cybersecurity Evaluation Program and free Water Cybersecurity Assessment Tool (checklists, not simulation); **MITRE Caldera for OT** (adversary emulation over BACnet, DNP3, Modbus, IEC 61850-MMS, Profinet, mapped to ATT&CK for ICS — emulation on real gear, not consequence forecasting). Closest commercial: **Dragos Platform** (detection and threat intel, not consequence simulation), **Claroty/Nozomi** (asset visibility), **SimSpace** (military-grade cyber ranges founded by USCYBERCOM and MIT Lincoln Laboratory alumni, used by USCYBERCOM and the FBI — high-fidelity *network* emulation, not process hydraulics).
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical: garbage network model in, confident hydrograph out — the INP file's control logic is often wrong or stale, and the kernel will happily simulate a fiction. Structural mitigation: provenance is required on the network model itself and an unsupported-assertion count is reported as a metric; the abstention gate fires when control logic is unsourced. Second: over-fitting to internet-exposed HMI paths because those are the documented ones, and missing the vendor-remote-access path that has no public advisory. Legal/ethical: this system computes how to break a water system, so the target-detail output (CCE Phase 3 "Target Details") is the sensitive artifact. Structural enforcement: the domain adapter emits consequence bands and named control points *by function* (e.g. "chlorine dosing setpoint at the primary treatment train") and the run configuration carries a classification tag that gates whether device-level target detail is rendered at all; the product template has no field for exploit code or specific credentials. No US-persons data is involved.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now (WNTR is mature and free; the coupling layer and the actor configs are the new work) / **7 in 12 months** — this is the fieldable one, because the physics already exists, the advisories already exist, and the backtest episodes already happened.

### C-CYBER-3 — Rung: the response-option tree for a US decision-maker after a cyber effect
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** After an adversary hits US infrastructure, this forecasts what each available US response actually does to the odds of things getting worse, instead of arguing about it in a room.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Following [a named adversary cyber effect on US critical infrastructure], for each of six candidate US responses — public attribution, indictment, OFAC designation, allied joint statement, a proportionate cyber response, a kinetic response — what is the probability of adversary counter-escalation to a *higher* effect band within 90 days, and which response minimizes it?" Resolvable against: the observed subsequent incident intensity in the same dyad, using EuRepoC's coded intensity and political-response variables.
- **Mission and user:** NSC cyber directorate and State's cyber bureau preparing an options memo; CYBERCOM J5; ODNI supporting the deputies. Today: an options memo built from analogy and expert intuition, where the escalation estimate is a sentence like "we assess Tehran would likely respond in kind" with no attached number and no record of what would have changed the judgment.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one adversary leadership model in one dyad. State variables: adversary's assessed audience cost of doing nothing; belief about US attribution confidence; belief about whether the effect was intended to be deniable; domestic-political calendar; capability inventory for a proportional reply; third-party (proxy) tasking availability. Knobs: **riskTolerance** = willingness to accept economic and diplomatic cost; **escalationPropensity** = preference for climbing a rung versus lateral/horizontal response (hitting a different sector or a third country at the same intensity). Horizontal escalation is the state most human panels miss and it must be a distinct model output.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** A **Markov decision process over an escalation ladder with an incomplete-information signaling layer** — states are (effect band, attribution state, audience-cost state), the defender's action set is the six responses, the adversary's transition probabilities depend on the profile's knobs, and the kernel computes the 90-day distribution over terminal states by exact forward propagation (no sampling needed for small state spaces; seeded PRNG for the larger ones). Priors on transitions are *calibrated to data, not asserted*: Schneider's wargame corpus supplies human-decision base rates — 115 teams and 580 players in the cyber-nuclear game (finding: cyber *vulnerability* produced restraint, while confidence in a cyber *exploit* drove aggressive counterforce), and the multi-year crisis series with 500+ players found cyber operations "primarily used to shape narratives as a complement to diplomacy prior to war and then as a support to military operations after war has escalated." The Hoover Wargaming Archive hosts this material publicly for replication. EuRepoC's political-response-type coding supplies the observed frequency of each US/allied response class given incident intensity.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH across the six response options treated as competing hypotheses about *which minimizes escalation*, with diagnosticity weighting that heavily discounts indicators every option shares. A **key-assumptions check** on the four assumptions that carry the model: adversary's audience cost, adversary's attribution belief, availability of a deniable proxy, and whether the dyad is in a conflict-intensity regime where cyber is a substitute versus a support (Schneider's central finding is that this regime matters more than the capability). Devil's advocate argues the "cyber fizzle" null — no response changes anything because cyber effects don't drive escalation. Premortem. Red team with standing to force a rerun when the dyad's base rate is drawn from fewer than five coded EuRepoC incidents. Rubric axes: transition-prior sourcing, sensitivity to audience-cost assumption, horizontal-escalation coverage, decision-relevance, ICD 203 separation of likelihood and confidence.
- **The config pack:** `dyads/us-iran.yaml`, `dyads/us-prc.yaml`, `dyads/us-rus.yaml` (each carrying coded historical incidents and observed responses), `actors/leadership-profiles.yaml`, `kernel/escalation-mdp.ts` params, `judges/panel-escalation.yaml`, `rubric/response-options.yaml`, `adapters/domain-eurepoc.ts` (ingests the 60-variable EuRepoC schema), `thresholds/abstention.yaml`.
- **Data required:** Unclassified and unusually good: **EuRepoC** Global Dataset v1.3 — 3,416 incidents from 2000–2024, 60 coded variables per incident including attribution basis, attribution type, political response type and date, cyber intensity, and HIIK offline-conflict intensity for the dyad; the Hoover Institution Wargaming Archive; CFR Cyber Operations Tracker and CSIS Significant Cyber Incidents for cross-checking (**UNVERIFIED** this session — named from memory). Classified: adversary leadership intent, private diplomatic signaling, and the actual US response menu including options that are never publicly visible. **A credible unclassified demo is possible** and is genuinely strong, because EuRepoC's political-response coding gives you a labeled outcome variable.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** *Historical backtest with as-of cutoff* on coded dyadic episodes: Iran–Albania 2022 (Homeland Justice; Albania severed diplomatic relations — a response class the model must be able to predict), the US–Russia response to CARR's water-sector claims culminating in OFAC designation, the US–Iran response to CyberAv3ngers culminating in the February 2024 Treasury designation of six IRGC-CEC officials, and the US–PRC response to Salt Typhoon telecom intrusions. The scored quantity is the 90-day forward intensity in the dyad plus the realized US response class. *Synthetic oracle* for the abstention gate: constructed dyads where the escalation answer is provably indeterminate. *SME panel* as a secondary, clearly labeled regime — never pooled with the backtest. This is the candidate most at risk of being scored against a tiny N; the coverage@N and Krippendorff alpha metrics are load-bearing here and low dyad-N must trigger abstention rather than a confident number.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (b) prediction accuracy — attaches a measured, calibration-tested probability to a claim that is today made in prose; (c) analytic confidence and source reconciliation — where the wargame evidence (players show restraint) and the incident record (states do respond, mostly with legal and economic instruments) disagree, the contradiction is surfaced and counted as a metric rather than smoothed; (a) efficiency for the options-memo cycle.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **IARPA REASON** (strengthens reasoning in a draft report, does not simulate escalation); the **RAND Forecasting Initiative / INFER**, which runs crowdsourced, Brier-scored forecasting on decomposed policy questions for government stakeholders using Cultivate Labs' platform — the closest thing to a scoring oracle already in government service, and a natural integration rather than a competitor. Older adjacent DARPA work on gray-zone competition modeling (COMPASS) — **UNVERIFIED**, named from memory. Closest commercial: Good Judgment Inc. and Metaculus for scored forecasting (**UNVERIFIED**); no commercial product forecasts cyber escalation as a decision tree with measured calibration.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical: N is small and non-stationary — the dyadic response record has few incidents per dyad and adversary doctrine changes, so the transition priors are the weakest link and the confidence-by-weakest-link rule will (correctly) cap most outputs at moderate confidence. If it doesn't, the aggregation is broken. Second: the wargame calibration data are from US and allied players and may not transfer to adversary decision-makers — this is an assumption that must appear in the key-assumptions block with a falsifier, every run. Legal/ethical: the output is a *US decision-support* product about response options, which brushes against covert-action planning. Structural enforcement: the product schema's response-option enumeration is a closed `as const` union of publicly-acknowledged instrument classes; there is no free-text field in which an option can be invented, and adding one is a config change with an audit record. No US-persons data.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 now / 5 in 12 months. Honest constraint: the data thinness caps this below the water candidate no matter how good the engineering.

### C-CYBER-4 — Ledger: the ransomware ecosystem as an economy, and what disruption levers actually do to it
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Models ransomware as a market with suppliers, middlemen and price-sensitive customers, and forecasts what a specific sanction, takedown or payment ban does to victim counts and revenue two quarters out.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "If OFAC designates bulletproof-hosting provider X in Q1, what is the probability that quarterly claimed-victim counts on leak sites fall by more than 15% by the end of Q3, and what is the expected change in total on-chain ransom receipts?" Resolvable monthly against eCrime.ch / ransomware.live leak-site counts and annually against Chainalysis on-chain totals.
- **Mission and user:** Treasury/OFAC's Office of Intelligence and Analysis choosing between designation targets; FBI Cyber Division and the NSC's ransomware policy staff; the Ransomware Task Force's government participants. Today: a designation package is argued on the *legal* sufficiency of the nexus and the *anticipated* effect is asserted qualitatively. Nobody produces a scored forward estimate, and nobody grades the last one.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one ecosystem-structure hypothesis. State variables: number and concentration of active RaaS brands (119 groups tracked by Dragos in 2025, up 49% from 80 in 2024 — a decentralizing market); affiliate mobility between brands; initial-access-broker inventory and price; victim-side payment propensity by sector and revenue band; laundering throughput and its cost; law-enforcement pressure. Knobs: **riskTolerance** = affiliate willingness to operate under sanctions exposure and to hit high-attention targets (hospitals, water); **escalationPropensity** = shift toward data-theft-only extortion and toward OT-adjacent targets when encryption stops paying.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Agent-based market model with a system-dynamics revenue layer.** Agents: RaaS operators, affiliates, IABs, victims. Each tick: IAB inventory replenishes (Poisson), affiliates buy access at a clearing price, attacks convert to payments with a sector-and-size-dependent probability, revenue splits flow, entry/exit is governed by expected profit. The market has a measurable, published lead-lag structure to calibrate against: Chainalysis found spikes in IAB payments precede increases in ransomware payments and US leak-site posts by roughly 30 days — that is a testable dynamic the kernel must reproduce before it is allowed to forecast. Deterministic given seed; the LLM configures the sector mix and the policy shock, the math runs the market.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over outcome bands for the two-quarter delta (victims up / flat / down >15%), with a **diagnosticity** step that is unusually sharp here because different hypotheses about market structure make genuinely different predictions about *median* versus *total* payments. Key-assumptions check on payment propensity, affiliate mobility, and substitution (do displaced affiliates leave or rebrand). Devil's advocate argues displacement-not-reduction: the 2024–2025 record is that total payments fell 8% to $820M while claimed attacks rose 50% and the median payment rose 368% from $12,738 to $59,556 — a market that reorganizes rather than shrinks. Premortem. Red team. Rubric axes: reproduction of the known lead-lag, out-of-sample fit, substitution reasoning, policy-actionability, provenance.
- **The config pack:** `market/ransomware-2026.yaml` (brand list, concentration, affiliate mobility priors), `victims/sector-payment-propensity.yaml`, `interventions/ofac-designation.yaml`, `interventions/payment-ban.yaml`, `interventions/takedown.yaml`, `judges/panel-economy.yaml`, `rubric/policy-effect.yaml`, `adapters/domain-onchain.ts`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml`.
- **Data required:** Entirely unclassified and unusually rich: Chainalysis Crypto Crime Report series (2026 edition: $820M in 2025, −8% YoY from a revised $892M for 2024; payment rate 28%, an all-time low, versus 62.8% in 2024 and 78.9% in 2022; IABs received at least $14M on-chain in 2025); eCrime.ch leak-site victim counts (+50% YoY, most active year on record); Dragos Year in Review for the industrial slice (3,300+ industrial organizations impacted, manufacturing over two-thirds of victims); OFAC designation records; Coveware quarterly payment-rate reporting (**UNVERIFIED** directly — cited via Chainalysis as aligned). Classified: pre-designation intelligence on target entities and non-public disruption operations. **A credible unclassified demo is possible and is arguably the single most defensible one in the catalog**, because the resolution data are published monthly by third parties.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** *Historical backtest with as-of cutoff* on real interventions with published before/after: LockBit's disruption (Operation Cronos, February 2024), the ALPHV/BlackCat exit (March 2024), the Hive takedown (January 2023), and Australia's mandatory ransom-payment reporting regime (2025) (**UNVERIFIED** — dates from memory). For each, cut off at the day before, predict the two-quarter claimed-victim and payment-rate deltas, score with Brier against the realized band and with Murphy decomposition to separate resolution from calibration. *Live post-cutoff resolution* is available continuously and cheaply — leak-site counts publish monthly; this candidate can be running a live scored forecast within weeks of build. ### C-CYBER-5 — Q-Model: adjudicating competing attributions under deliberate deception
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** When two credible reports blame two different countries for the same attack, this weighs the evidence formally, says how confident anyone should be, and names the single piece of missing evidence that would settle it.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Given the current evidence set for [incident], what is the posterior probability over the candidate responsible actors, what is the probability that the leading candidate is the product of a deliberate false-flag, and what specific additional evidence would most change the ranking?" Resolvable against: the eventually-settled attribution as recorded by indictment, sanctions designation, or EuRepoC's settled-attribution coding.
- **Mission and user:** The all-source attribution cell — CIA's cyber analytic line, NSA, and the FBI case agent who has to decide whether the evidence supports an indictment. Also Treasury before a designation. Today: attribution is adjudicated in a meeting where competing technical reports are compared narratively, and the confidence statement that emerges ("moderate confidence") is not traceable to which pieces of evidence carried it.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one *attribution hypothesis* held by a coherent adversary-or-analyst model. State variables: which evidence classes are present (infrastructure reuse, code overlap, operational timing, language artifacts, victimology, TTP overlap, self-attribution); the assumed prior probability that each evidence class was *deliberately planted*; the assumed capability of each candidate actor to plant it; the assumed sharing/contracting relationships that make a shared tool non-diagnostic. Knobs: **riskTolerance** = the modeled actor's willingness to accept exposure of a real capability in order to plant a convincing false flag; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to attribute *to* a third party in a way that is intended to cause a real-world response against that third party. Note that in this candidate the divergence is over *evidence-generating processes*, which is why the kernel is Bayesian rather than a simulation of physical effects.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Bayesian belief update over an evidence graph** with explicit deception terms. For each evidence item e and candidate actor a, the kernel holds P(e | a, honest) and P(e | a′, a′ planting evidence pointing at a) — the second term is what makes this different from every ACH spreadsheet ever built. Posterior by exact enumeration over the actor set (small), with the *diagnosticity* of each evidence item computed as its contribution to the log-odds separation between the top two hypotheses. Deterministic, seeded, and the value-of-information calculation — "which unobserved evidence item most reduces posterior entropy" — is the actionable output. This is Heuer's ACH made numerically honest; the adjudication panel's ACH judge and the kernel are computing the same object at two levels of abstraction, which is a design feature: the panel's ranking-by-weighted-inconsistency should agree with the kernel's posterior ranking, and a disagreement is itself a reportable metric.
- **What the judge panel is:** This is the candidate where the panel *is* half the product. ACH judge (diagnosticity-weighted, ranks by weighted inconsistency ascending). Key-assumptions check judge on the deception priors — each requires a falsifier. **Devil's advocate judge is mandatory and adversarial by construction**: it must argue for the second-ranked actor using only the evidence in hand. Premortem judge writes "we attributed to X and the indictment named Y." Red team judge has standing to force a bounded rerun when the deception prior for any evidence class is unsourced. Additional judge specific to this candidate: a **source-independence judge** that scores whether two "independent" reports actually share an upstream telemetry source — the single most common way attribution confidence is inflated. Rubric axes: evidence-item provenance, deception-prior sourcing, source independence, discriminating power, calibration of the stated confidence against the posterior spread.
- **The config pack:** `evidence/schema-mictic.yaml` (evidence classes; MICTIC-style decomposition — **UNVERIFIED**, framework named from memory), `actors/candidate-sets/<region>.yaml`, `priors/deception.yaml` (per actor, per evidence class, each with a citation), `judges/panel-attribution.yaml`, `rubric/attribution.yaml`, `adapters/domain-eurepoc-attribution.ts`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml` (this is the candidate where INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE should fire most often, and its false-abstention rate is the headline metric).
- **Data required:** Unclassified: EuRepoC's dedicated **attribution dataset**, which unpacks every attribution claim per incident with attribution basis (media / receiver / IT-security community / attacker confirms / contested / third-party / receiver government / international organization / EU institution), attribution type (self-attribution, media report, technical report, political statement, domestic legal action → indictment, sanctions, arrests, lawsuit), attributing actor, and the temporal sequence of technical versus political attribution — this is a ready-made labeled corpus for attribution adjudication and I know of no better one. Plus vendor technical reports, DOJ indictments, OFAC designations, and the BfV's four-part analysis of the i-SOON leak for ground truth on contractor-ecosystem attribution difficulty ("the attribution of individual cyber operations within this highly complex system is [made harder]"). Classified: SIGINT and HUMINT that in practice settles most real attributions — meaning the unclassified instance will be *worse* than the classified one, and must say so. **A credible unclassified demo is possible** and is the honest one: it demonstrates the machinery and the abstention behavior, not superior accuracy.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** *Historical backtest with as-of cutoff at the first public technical report*, scored against the eventually-settled attribution. Candidate episodes with hard ground truth: Olympic Destroyer (2018, a documented deliberate false-flag — the model must abstain or correctly discount the planted Lazarus artifacts); CyberAv3ngers → Treasury designation of six IRGC-CEC officials, February 2024; Cyber Army of Russia Reborn → OFAC designation, 2024, establishing the Sandworm nexus; i-SOON → DOJ indictments and the BfV analysis; the Minnesota 2026 campaign, which is *currently unattributed* and therefore a live post-cutoff question. The critical metric is not accuracy but **false-abstention rate versus true-abstention rate** — a system that never says "insufficient evidence" on attribution is dangerous, and one that always says it is useless. Contradiction precision/recall is also directly meaningful here.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (c) **This is the quality-assurance candidate.** It is precisely "improve quality assurance — identify discrepancies between sources and reconcile differences in overall analytic confidence," implemented as a numerical object rather than a process aspiration. (a) accuracy; (b) time — attribution adjudication meetings are expensive in senior analyst hours.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: **IARPA REASON** is the nearest match in intent — it automatically identifies contrary evidence and reasoning weaknesses in draft analytic reports, explicitly detecting confirmation bias (a hypothesis with only favoring evidence), satisficing bias (only one hypothesis analyzed), and absence-of-evidence bias (too many assumptions). REASON is a *writing-time checker*; this candidate is a *simulation-and-adjudication* engine that produces the judgment. IARPA also ran SoURCE CODE on malware authorship attribution (**UNVERIFIED**). Closest commercial: **Mandiant/Google Threat Intelligence** and **Recorded Future** produce attribution assessments with confidence language but no published posterior, no deception term, and no measured abstention rate.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical: the deception priors are the whole ballgame and are close to unknowable; a system that lets an analyst set P(planted) by feel will produce laundered intuition with a decimal point. Structural mitigation: deception priors must be citable to a documented case, the sensitivity of the posterior to each prior is a mandatory output, and the abstention gate is tuned aggressively. Second: source non-independence between vendor reports collapses apparent corroboration. Legal/ethical: **this is the highest-risk candidate in the catalog.** Attribution to a *person* is the boundary — the system attributes to *actor sets* (state, service, unit, contractor firm) and its schema has no field for a natural person's identity, so producing an individual-level attribution requires changing the type, not the prompt. Second boundary: attribution products can support sanctions and indictments against real entities, so provenance completeness on every claim is not a nicety, it is the thing that makes the product admissible into that chain. Third: no US-persons data — the candidate actor sets are foreign; a domestic-nexus incident routes out of this pipeline entirely.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now (the platform's ACH and calibration layers are the largest existing asset) / 6 in 12 months.

Devil's advocate argues the absorption null — neighbors absorb the volume with no mortality effect, which is what the market-level evidence actually shows. Premortem. Red team may force a rerun when the regional adjacency graph is not sourced. - **The config pack:** `regions/<hrr>.yaml` (facility list, beds, ICU, adjacency, market share), `disruption/profiles.yaml`, `effects/dose-response.yaml` (each coefficient carrying its citation and its confidence interval), `judges/panel-health.yaml`, `rubric/regional-consequence.yaml`, `adapters/domain-health.ts`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml`.

- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** *Historical backtest with as-of cutoff* on named episodes where regional data exist: the May 2021 San Diego attack that is the Dameff study's own subject (cutoff at day 1, predict the adjacent-ED metrics that the study later measured — this is a genuine held-out test because the outcome was published after the fact); Change Healthcare (February 2024); Ascension (May 2024); CommonSpirit (October 2022) (**UNVERIFIED** — the last three named from memory; each needs its published regional-data availability confirmed). *Live post-cutoff*: hospital ransomware events occur monthly and OCR reporting resolves them. *Synthetic oracle* for the queueing layer.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — replaces "significant patient impact" with an excess-mortality distribution; (b) time-to-analysis — the regional-disaster determination becomes a same-hour product; (c) confidence — the tension between the strong *attacked-facility* mortality effect and the null *neighboring-facility* mortality effect is a genuine source-level discrepancy that a naive system would average into mush; (d) cost.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: HHS ASPR's regional healthcare coalition planning and HC3 advisories (qualitative); CISA's Regional Resiliency Assessment Program (**UNVERIFIED**). Closest commercial: **Censinet** and health-sector GRC tools for risk registers (**UNVERIFIED**); cyber-catastrophe modelers for insured loss (**UNVERIFIED**). No product forecasts regional clinical consequence.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical: transferring Medicare-claims-derived effect sizes from 2016–2021 attacks to a 2026 attack on a different payer mix and a different EHR-downtime posture is the central validity threat, and the honest handling is a wide confidence band plus abstention when the region's characteristics fall outside the calibration population. Second: duration is the dominant driver and is unknowable on day one, so the day-one product must be a conditional family of curves, not a point. Legal/ethical: **this candidate touches US persons' health data and is the one place where a hard structural line is required.** The pipeline consumes only aggregate, already-public facility-level statistics and published coefficients; the domain adapter has no ingestion path for patient-level records and the product schema has no field below facility-day granularity. It forecasts consequence to inform *response*, and the config carries no targeting-relevant output; a request to run it as a targeting aid would require a different domain adapter, which is an auditable change.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now / **6–7 in 12 months** — the effect sizes are published, the data are public, and the backtest subject is a peer-reviewed paper.

### C-CYBER-9 — Treasury Line: DPRK revenue-generating cyber operations as a resource-allocation decision
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Models Pyongyang's hacking as a business with revenue targets and a money-laundering bottleneck, and forecasts how much it will steal next year, from what, and where the choke point is.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Over the next four quarters, what is the probability that DPRK-attributed cryptocurrency theft exceeds $2.0 billion, what share comes from third-party service-provider compromise versus direct exchange compromise, and what is the expected effect on that total of designating the top three off-ramp intermediaries?" Resolvable against Chainalysis annual attributions and MSMT reporting.
- **Mission and user:** Treasury's Office of Terrorism and Financial Intelligence and OFAC targeting; State's DPRK sanctions team supporting the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team; the IC's DPRK analytic line, where the question "does the cyber revenue line relax the sanctions constraint on the missile program" is a first-order strategic question, not a cyber question.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one hypothesis about how the Reconnaissance General Bureau allocates a fixed cyber workforce across revenue lines. State variables: personnel allocation across crypto theft, IT-worker fraud and espionage; target-class selection (exchange versus custodian versus third-party asset provider); expected yield and variance per class; laundering throughput and cost by corridor; seizure hazard; the regime's revenue requirement given alternative earnings. Knobs: **riskTolerance** = willingness to accept exposure, seizure and designation of laundering partners; **escalationPropensity** = willingness to move up the target-size distribution toward a Bybit-class single event and to compromise supply-chain intermediaries rather than end targets.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Constrained portfolio allocation over a flow network with a capacity bottleneck.** The attacker allocates effort across target classes to maximize expected *laundered-to-fiat* revenue — not gross theft — subject to laundering-corridor capacities, per-corridor cost, and a seizure hazard per corridor. The kernel solves the allocation (convex program) and then simulates realization with a seeded PRNG over yield draws and seizure draws. The critical structural insight the kernel encodes: **the binding constraint is the off-ramp, not the intrusion**, which is why designating an off-ramp is a different intervention from designating a hacker. Deterministic given seed.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over allocation hypotheses. Key-assumptions check on laundering capacity, seizure hazard, and the regime's revenue requirement. Devil's advocate argues that revenue is opportunity-driven rather than target-driven — that the 2025 total was dominated by one lucky event and is not a plan. That argument is strong and must be scored fairly: the February 2025 Bybit theft of roughly $1.5 billion accounted for "the vast majority" of 2025 proceeds, and all of it was cashed out to fiat or hard currency by September 2025. Premortem. Red team. Rubric axes: distinguishing plan from opportunity, laundering-capacity sourcing, sensitivity to a single-event tail, sanctions-actionability, provenance.
- **The config pack:** `actors/rgb-organization.yaml` (110 Research Institute and its subordinate APTs: Temp.Hermit, Citrine Sleet, TraderTraitor, CryptoCore), `revenue/target-classes.yaml`, `laundering/corridors.yaml` (with capacities and designated-status flags), `interventions/ofac-offramp.yaml`, `judges/panel-dprk.yaml`, `rubric/revenue-forecast.yaml`, `adapters/domain-onchain.ts`, `thresholds/abstention.yaml`.
- **Data required:** Unclassified and remarkably specific: the **MSMT report of 2025-10-22** on DPRK violation and evasion of UN sanctions through cyber and IT-worker activities — at least $1,191,554,000 stolen in 2024, $1,645,780,000 from January–September 2025, $2,837,334,000 across the reporting period; crypto heists as approximately one-third of DPRK total foreign-currency revenue in 2024; laundering through facilitators in China, Russia, Argentina, Cambodia, Vietnam and the UAE; the pattern of compromising *third-party providers* rather than the exchanges themselves in the DMM Bitcoin, WazirX and Bybit cases; IT workers earning $3,500–$10,000 monthly with top performers up to $100,000, with 1,000–1,500 in China and 150–300 in Russia. Plus Chainalysis's independent estimate of $2.02 billion stolen in 2025, a 51% year-over-year increase. Classified: the internal tasking and the actual quota structure. **A credible unclassified demo is possible and would be unusually convincing**, because the ground truth is published annually by two independent bodies.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** *Historical backtest with as-of cutoff*: cut off at 2024-12-31 with only the 2024 figure ($1.19B) and the then-known corridor structure, and forecast the 2025 total and its composition. The realized answer — roughly $2.0B, dominated by one $1.5B event against a third-party provider, fully cashed out within seven months — is a hard test that punishes both a naive trend model and a hedging model. Repeat with 2023→2024. *Live post-cutoff*: forecast the 2026 total ahead of the next MSMT and Chainalysis reports, which resolve on a known schedule. Baselines for Brier skill: prior-year persistence and a log-linear trend. Because the distribution is heavy-tailed and one event dominates, **tail recall is the metric that matters here**, not mean accuracy, and reporting mean-only would be a failure of the metrics package.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (b) prediction accuracy on a quantity that directly feeds the WMD-financing estimate; (a) accuracy — the "strategic function" question (does cyber revenue materially relax the sanctions constraint) becomes a modeled fraction rather than an adjective; (c) reconciliation — MSMT's $1.645B (Jan–Sep 2025) and Chainalysis's $2.02B (full-year 2025) are different measurements of overlapping quantities and the system must present them as such rather than picking one; (d) cost.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government: the **Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team**, established after the UN Panel of Experts lapsed, which produces exactly the descriptive reporting this would forecast against — a natural customer, not a competitor. Closest commercial: **Chainalysis** (which contributed the blockchain intelligence to the MSMT report and offers Hexagate screening) and **TRM Labs** — measurement and tracing, not forward allocation modeling.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technical: the distribution is dominated by rare enormous events, so any model scored on mean error will look fine while being useless, and any model that forecasts the mean will miss the thing that matters. The heavy tail must be modeled explicitly and the sharpness metric must be read alongside tail recall. Second: attribution of thefts to DPRK is itself uncertain and revised upward over time — the same data-vintage problem as the ransomware candidate. Legal/ethical: this pipeline touches financial data about real entities and can feed designations; provenance completeness on every claim is what makes it usable in that chain. No US-persons data; the schema's actor objects are foreign state organs and foreign facilitators, and there is no field for a natural person below the level of an already-designated individual.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now / 6 in 12 months.

### C-CYBER-10 — **BAD IDEA (labeled): "Warning Board" — name the next US critical-infrastructure victim, 30 days out**
- **Pitch (1 sentence):** A dashboard that each morning names the specific US critical-infrastructure organizations most likely to be successfully attacked in the next 30 days, ranked with a probability.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Which named US critical-infrastructure entities will suffer a successful adversary intrusion in the next 30 days?" **This is the question every customer asks for first, and it is the reason this candidate is in the catalog as the floor.**
- **Mission and user:** A CISA or sector watch floor, or an IC warning cell. What they do today: consume advisories, KEV, and vendor telemetry, and prioritize by exposure. That process is imperfect but *honest about being a prioritization rather than a prediction*.
- **What the N simulants are:** Nominally, attacker target-selection profiles varying over sector preference, opportunism versus strategic targeting, and exposure sensitivity. **In practice almost nothing meaningfully varies at the entity level**: every profile ends up ranking by internet exposure and known-vulnerable software, because that is the only signal available, so all N runs converge to approximately the same list. By the brief's own test — "if nothing meaningfully varies, this candidate is weak" — **this candidate is weak, and I am saying so.**
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **NO KERNEL.** There is no defensible dynamical model that maps an entity's observable exposure to a 30-day intrusion probability, because the two quantities are separated by adversary intent, which is unobserved and adaptive. What would actually get built is a ranking heuristic over exposure features dressed as a simulation. Naming this honestly: **NO KERNEL is disqualifying**, and no amount of ensemble machinery repairs it.
- **What the judge panel is:** Whatever panel you configure will produce well-formed ACH tables over hypotheses that no evidence can discriminate between, because the diagnosticity of every available indicator with respect to "will entity E be hit" is approximately zero. The panel would give the output the *appearance* of rigor, which is worse than having no panel — this is the failure mode where the adjudication layer actively launders a guess.
- **The config pack:** `entities/critical-infrastructure.yaml`, `features/exposure.yaml`, `judges/panel-warning.yaml`, `rubric/warning.yaml`. The config pack is easy to write, which is part of the trap.
- **Data required:** Shodan/Censys-class exposure data, KEV, sector rosters. Nothing classified is needed, which makes it easy to demo — also part of the trap. A demo would look impressive and mean nothing.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **UNSCORABLE — disqualifying for this customer.** Three independent reasons, each sufficient on its own. (1) *Censored labels*: you cannot distinguish "not attacked" from "attacked and not detected," and Dragos estimates fewer than 10% of OT networks worldwide have the network visibility to detect this activity at all — the negative class is fiction. (2) *Label lag*: intrusion-to-public-disclosure runs months to years (Volt Typhoon actors maintained access "for at least five years" before the advisory), so a 30-day forecast cannot be resolved on a 30-day horizon, and by the time it can be, the world has changed. (3) *Base rate*: the per-entity 30-day probability is small enough that a model predicting "no" for everyone achieves excellent Brier score, so Brier *skill against the base rate* — the platform's own headline metric — will be near zero or negative for any honest model, and any model that shows positive skill here should be assumed to be leaking. There is no oracle, synthetic or historical or live, that fixes this.
- **Benefit categories hit:** Claims all four; **delivers none.** The apparent (b) benefit — faster warning — is negative value if the warning is uncalibrated, because it redirects finite defensive resources on noise.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Security-ratings vendors (**Bitsight**, **SecurityScorecard**) sell exactly this shape of product as an exposure *score*, and are careful — mostly — not to call it an intrusion probability (**UNVERIFIED** — named from memory). The instructive precedent is **EPSS**, which is disciplined about what it claims: FIRST's own guidance is that "if there is evidence that a vulnerability is being exploited, then that information should supersede anything EPSS has to say," and independent analysis finds EPSS behaves as a trailing indicator of observed exploitation rather than a leading predictor — and EPSS is forecasting a *vulnerability-level* quantity with far more signal than an entity-level one.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** It fails by being *convincingly wrong*: well-calibrated-looking probabilities on an unmeasurable quantity, which is precisely the failure the platform's abstention gate and evaluation-regime tagging exist to prevent — so shipping this would falsify the product's own thesis. There is also a real ethical hazard: a ranked list of named US organizations with attack probabilities is a targeting-useful artifact if it leaks, and it invites the system to reason about named US entities, which is the one place the US-persons and homeland-targeting boundaries are thinnest. **Recommended disposition: refuse this framing when the customer asks for it, and offer C-CYBER-6 (which forecasts a resolvable *product-family* quantity) and C-CYBER-2 (which forecasts a resolvable *consequence* quantity) as the two things that can actually be scored.** Being able to say no to this question, with the measurement argument attached, is a competitive differentiator against every vendor that says yes.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 2 now / 2 in 12 months — it does not advance, because the blocker is epistemic, not engineering.

## Cross-cutting notes

**Which are fieldable first, on evidence rather than preference:** C-CYBER-2 (water) has a mature free physics kernel, three dated backtest episodes including one from three weeks ago, and a fully unclassified demo. C-CYBER-4 (ransomware economy) and C-CYBER-6 (exploit pipeline) have *continuously publishing* oracles, which means a real Brier track record within a quarter rather than a year. Those three would be the fieldable-concept slate; the rest are strong but slower to score.

**Where the platform's specific design choices earn their keep in this lens:** the abstention gate matters most in C-CYBER-5 (attribution) and C-CYBER-10 (which it should refuse outright); confidence-by-weakest-link matters most in C-CYBER-3, where thin dyadic data must cap confidence rather than be averaged away; the evaluation-regime tag matters most in C-CYBER-4 and C-CYBER-9, where the resolution data are *revised upward over time* and a system that silently re-scores against a moving target is dishonest; the as-of leakage guard matters most in every historical backtest here, because these episodes are heavily written about and a model that has read the 2026 Dragos Year in Review cannot be allowed to "predict" KAMACITE's March–July 2025 scanning.

**The single most important honest caveat for this buyer:** every candidate above forecasts a quantity that is *observed through a detection filter*. Fewer than 10% of OT networks worldwide have the visibility to detect the activity being modeled. That is not a caveat to bury — it is a structural property that the kernels must model as thinning and that the products must state, because a system that reports "no attacks predicted" when it means "no *detected* attacks predicted" is the exact failure that got the water sector to July 2026.

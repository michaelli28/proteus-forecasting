# C-IW-9 — Retrieval Poisoning of the IC's Own AI Answer Surface

*Concept of operations. One of six deep dives. Scored M4/A4/W5.*

---

## 1. In Plain English

**What this does:** It measures, and then forecasts, how much of an intelligence agency's own AI question-answering tool an adversary can hijack by flooding the internet (or any feed the tool reads) with planted articles. It answers one question: *if an analyst asks our AI about Topic X, what are the odds the answer repeats something an adversary planted?*

**Why it matters:** CIA already fields a generative-AI tool over open-source data, used by thousands of analysts across all 18 intelligence agencies. A Moscow network has been publishing millions of articles a year with the apparent goal of getting into exactly that kind of tool. Nobody currently measures how far in they have gotten.

**What is new:** Every other capability in this package asks the customer to trust a prediction about the outside world. This one makes a claim about a machine the customer owns — and the customer can check it by running the audit. It also ships the defensive half: which fixes to the search index actually cut the hijack rate, how much each costs, and what each one breaks.

**The unusual part:** the classified version is the *easier* one. What is being modeled — the contents and ranking behavior of the customer's own index — exists only inside the enclave. That inverts the normal problem, where the unclassified demo is a shadow of a classified capability we can never show.

## 2. The Analytic Question and Its Resolvable Quantity

### 2.1 The question, stated so it can be scored

> For analyst-facing AI answers on topic cell *c*, generated by retriever *R* at depth *k* over index snapshot *S*, during the 90 days following as-of date *D* — what share of answers will contain at least one claim whose provenance chain includes an adversary-planted document?

### 2.2 The measures

| | Definition | Resolves without | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACR** — Answer Capture Rate | answers containing ≥1 claim traceable to a planted document ÷ answers generated | — | The thing the customer cares about |
| **TKO** — Top-*k* Occupancy | mean fraction of the top-*k* retrieved set drawn from the planted set | running the generator | The **causal** variable, and the tier that clears accreditation first |
| **WCC** — Worst-Cell Capture | max over topic cells of ACR | — | The number that keeps the product from lying (see §14.2) |

TKO is elevated to a first-class measure because of a result the panel will already know: PoisonedRAG achieved 97% attack success on Natural Questions by injecting **5 texts per target question into a database of 2,681,468** — a poisoning rate of **0.0002%** ([Zou et al., USENIX Security '25, pp. 3827–3844](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/zou)). **CONFIRMED, from the published paper.** The consequence for kernel design is decisive and is stated here rather than buried in §3: **global corpus share is the wrong state variable.** A model built on "what fraction of the corpus is poisoned" is structurally incapable of reproducing a 0.0002% → 97% result. The quantity that carries the causality is *query-conditional occupancy of the retrieved set*, and the kernel is built around it.

### 2.3 The partition

Structural MECE over ACR, pre-registered before any run:

`[0, 0.02)` · `[0.02, 0.10)` · `[0.10, 0.25)` · `[0.25, 0.50)` · `[0.50, 1.00]`

Plus `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` as a first-class verdict — the platform's abstention gate fires **before** scoring, and its true/false abstention rates are measured, not assumed (measured on the shipped configuration: 80/80 true abstention on a provably unanswerable arm; 7% false abstention at the shipped threshold, from a 6-arm threshold sweep).

Band edges are chosen to be decision-relevant, not round: 2% is roughly "below the noise floor of a 450-response audit"; 25% is "the analyst will encounter it within four questions"; 50% is "the surface is more captured than not."

### 2.4 The resolution event

- **Unclassified:** the next dated NewsGuard AI False Claims Monitor / Pravda-network audit. NewsGuard has moved to a **quarterly** cadence — Quarterly AI False Claim Monitor editions for **January 2026** and **May 2026** are both listed ([NewsGuard AI False Claims Monitor](https://www.newsguardtech.com/ai-false-claims-monitor/)). This satisfies the strongest structural selection criterion in the whole catalog: **the resolution source is a third party's published artifact on a known schedule.**
- **Classified:** a scheduled quarterly index-hygiene audit producing a signed memo (§10).

### 2.5 As-of discipline

The platform's leakage guard is in code, in three layers (`engine/src/corpus/as-of.ts:1-13`): retrieval cannot return a post-as-of document; tests assert unreachability through every accessor; and `assertNoLeakage()` throws a `LeakageError` carrying the offending document IDs and dates if a *finished* product cites a post-as-of document.

**This pack adds a requirement the platform does not currently meet.** The forecast is conditional on an index snapshot *and a retriever version*. Both must be in the run identity, or two runs over different indices share a run id. That is architecture gap #1 in a new costume — `PanelPlan` carries an LLM-generated `systemPrompt` per adjudicator that is never persisted and never hashed, and `runId = computeRunId(identity)` where identity has no panel field. **C-IW-9 cannot ship correctly until gap #1 is fixed.** That is stated as a dependency, not a caveat, and it is the same fix the reproducibility capability in Part B needs (§B.6).

## 3. The Kernel

### 3.1 Architecture class — stated honestly

This is a **consequence twin with a simulated adversary-supply front-end.** It is *not* simulate-and-adjudicate of adversary decision-making. We do not model whether Moscow decides to run the campaign; we model what happens to a retrieval index if it does, at a given intensity, against a given ranker. The customer's own worked example (Iran deciding whether to hit US water infrastructure) *is* simulate-and-adjudicate; this one is not, and framing it as one would be a claim the code does not support.

The consequence-twin framing is also the source of the capability's unusual strength: **the back half of the model is not modeled at all — it is executed.** The retriever is real. The generator is real. Only the corpus composition is simulated. That is why the synthetic oracle gives exact ground truth (§7.1) rather than a proxy.

### 3.2 Named model families

| Stage | Family | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Supply** | Pólya-urn reinforcement + exogenous injection (compartmental flow on *documents*, not people) | Reproduces superlinear growth endogenously — the network's own laundering loop, not a fitted trend |
| **B. Ranking** | Plackett–Luce scores + **simulated order statistics** over the score mixture | Turns corpus share into top-*k* occupancy through a mechanism; isolates the ranking confound into named parameters |
| **C. Generation** | Parameterized dose-response *g(m,k)* | **The defensive interventions are shape changes to this one function** |

Stage A uses reinforcement rather than a growth rate because the observed dynamics are reinforcing: the Pravda network does not author original content, it **aggregates** from state media and pro-Kremlin influencers "through a broad set of seemingly independent websites," acting as "a central hub for disinformation laundering" ([NewsGuard, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/moscow-based-global-news-network-infected-western-artificial-intelligence-russian-propaganda)). A model with an exogenous growth rate assumes its own conclusion; a reinforcement model has to earn it — the same reasoning the platform's existing bounded-confidence belief kernel gives for choosing Hegselmann–Krause (`engine/src/sim/adapters/influence-ops/dynamics.ts:1-9`).

Stage B **simulates** the order statistic rather than approximating it in closed form. Sampling scores and taking the top *k* is cheap given a seeded PRNG, and it removes an approximation a technical panel would otherwise spend the meeting on.

### 3.3 State variables

Per topic cell *c*, per tick *t*:

```
Nc[c]       clean document count
Np[c]       planted document count (originals)
Dp[c]       surviving near-duplicates = (1 - rho) * s * Np[c]
mu_c, sig_c clean relevance-score distribution for cell c
delta       RETRIEVAL-OPTIMIZATION GAIN: mu_p = mu_c + delta
s           syndication multiplier (near-duplicate copies per original)
rho         index near-duplicate collapse rate         [defense]
tau         ranker sharpness (Plackett-Luce temperature) [confound]
w[class]    source-class prior weight, log-additive     [defense + confound]
k           retrieval depth
j           per-source cap in top-k                      [defense]
g(m,k)      generation dose-response by aggregation rule [defense]
theta       abstention gate on top-k source diversity    [defense]
C(t)        cumulative defensive spend
```

`delta` is the load-bearing parameter and the one the whole design turns on. It is the difference between "the adversary published a lot" and "the adversary published things engineered to be retrieved for the questions that matter." NewsGuard attributes the network's effectiveness "largely" to SEO techniques boosting content visibility ([TechCrunch, 7 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/07/russian-propoganda-is-reportely-influencing-ai-chatbot-results/)) — that is `delta > 0` in ordinary language. **ASSUMED**, not confirmed: no public dataset measures the network's embedding-space similarity gain directly; the oracle (§7.1) measures it under our control instead.

### 3.4 Dynamics, per tick

```
Nc[c] += Poisson(lambda_organic[c])
Np[c] += Poisson(lambda_adv * budget(t))                    # injection
Np[c] += Poisson(kappa * Nc[c] * poisonShare[c])            # laundering reinforcement
Dp[c]  = (1 - rho) * s * Np[c]                              # syndication net of dedup

scores_clean  ~ Normal(mu_c, sig_c)      x (Nc[c])
scores_poison ~ Normal(mu_c + delta, sig_c) x (Np[c] + Dp[c])
z_i = score_i + log w[class_i]
topK  = argsort(z + Gumbel(0, 1/tau))[:k]                   # Plackett-Luce sampling
m     = |{i in topK : planted(i)}|, capped by j per source
capture ~ Bernoulli( g(m, k) )   unless diversity(topK) < theta -> ABSTAIN
```

### 3.5 The dose-response function is the product

```
concat (vanilla RAG):       g = 1 - (1 - p1)^m          p1 in [0.6, 0.9]
isolate-then-aggregate:     g = P(planted keywords survive threshold mu = min(alpha*n, beta))
abstention gate:            answer withheld; enters the false-abstention denominator
```

Vanilla concatenation is steep in *m* because a single planted passage often suffices — that is the whole content of PoisonedRAG's 5-passage result. Isolate-then-aggregate flattens it into a threshold, which is what makes RobustRAG's certifiable robustness possible ([Xiang et al., arXiv:2405.15556](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2405.15556)). **A defense is a change to the shape of one function.** That is the config-not-code argument landing on a concrete example.

### 3.6 What varies across the ensemble

`delta`, `s`, `lambda_adv`, `tau`, per-cell `(mu_c, sig_c)`, the adversary budget schedule, and the analyst query mix. 32 runs per question, matching the shape of the platform's measured 80q × 32-run batch.

### 3.7 The two knobs, and where they do not reach

`riskTolerance` → detectability of the injection: high tolerance means duplicate-heavy, high-`delta` optimization that leaves a fingerprint (a near-duplicate cluster with anomalously tight embedding spread). `escalationPropensity` → `lambda_adv` and campaign start tick.

This is one of the domains where the two knobs fit *well* — better than four of the six mission lenses that had to re-bind or bypass them. But the fit is not complete: the pack re-bounds `riskTolerance` to span **operational-security preference**, because in this domain the adversary's central trade is "get retrieved" vs. "stay unattributed," and those pull in opposite directions on the same knob. The re-binding goes in `mapping.ts` with documented `MAPPING_BOUNDS` and a test asserting against them — the pattern the influence-ops adapter already uses (`engine/src/sim/adapters/influence-ops/mapping.ts:29-35`), so "what does riskTolerance=70 actually mean here" has an answer a reviewer can check.

## 4. The Panel

Five techniques. Each is given the one question it is actually good at, not a generic "review this."

| Technique | What it adjudicates | Rubric |
|---|---|---|
| **ACH** (Heuer diagnosticity weighting, ranked by weighted inconsistency ascending) | Which capture band survives the evidence | Evidence items: index statistics, duplicate-cluster growth, source-class score drift, probe results. **The diagnosticity weighting is the confound machinery** — evidence that discriminates "corpus share grew" from "the ranker changed" gets weight; evidence consistent with both gets none |
| **Key assumptions check** — every assumption carries a REQUIRED falsifier | The model's load-bearing assumptions | e.g. *"The probed retriever is the production retriever."* Falsifier: **probe-vs-production score rank correlation < 0.90 on a held-out query set.** An assumption without a runnable falsifier is rejected by the schema |
| **Devil's advocate** | The benign explanation | Argues that observed concentration is wire-service syndication and legitimate aggregation, not adversarial planting. This is the strongest counter-hypothesis and it must be argued by someone whose job is to win |
| **Premortem** | Being right and useless | *"90 days on, the audit says 2% and an analyst was still captured. How?"* The forced answer is worst-cell vs. mean — which is why the premortem's output is a **required WCC field on the product**, not advice |
| **Red team**, bounded rerun authority | Adaptive adversary | May request **at most one** rerun with a bounded increase to `delta` (adversary adapts to the disclosed defense). The request and its bound are recorded in the artifact |

Confidence is aggregated by **weakest link**, never by mean. Likelihood (ICD 203 terms) and confidence are separate fields; there is structurally no function of type `(Judgment) => number` in the codebase.

**Two panel realities this pack must design around, both measured:**

1. **Adjudicators refuse influence-operations framing.** Measured live on the existing influence-ops task: 5/5 refusals, and later 2 of 3 declined. This is a live-measured guardrail, not a hypothetical. **Design consequence:** the pack is framed in the panel rubric as a **defensive audit of a system the customer owns**, with the fictional-entity constraint (§13) visible in the adjudicator's context. If the panel still refuses, that is a reportable finding about the pack, not a bug to prompt around.

2. **k-of-N panel loss is currently a boolean, not a grade.** `available: results.length > 0` → `level: adjudicationAvailable ? "high" : "moderate"`. One surviving adjudicator of eight scores identically to a full panel. Given (1), partial panel loss is not an edge case here — **it is the expected operating condition for this domain.** This pack cannot report honest confidence until architecture gap #2 is fixed. Stated as a dependency, alongside gap #1.

### 5.2 New code — the harness (the larger build, outside `sim/`)

`sim/` is under a hard purity constraint: `Math.random` and `Date.now` are banned there and a test scans the source to enforce it. A real retriever cannot live under that constraint, so it lives in a separate harness:

- `harness/retriever/` — BM25 (Lucene/Tantivy), a dense retriever (E5/BGE/Contriever class), a cross-encoder reranker, and a hybrid; each pinned by version hash
- `harness/planted/` — the planted-document generator and **the fictional-entity validator** (§13). This is the red-line enforcement point and gets the most test coverage in the pack
- `harness/probe/` — probe-query scheduling with the split-seed discipline (§14.6)

Honest sizing: the harness is a larger engineering effort than the adapter and is the pack's real schedule risk.

### 5.3 Config only — no code changes

- Framer question templates and the band partition (§2.3)
- Actor-profile grids over `riskTolerance` × `escalationPropensity`
- Corpus snapshot manifests and as-of dates
- Baseline definitions (§7.4)
- **The defense-arm catalog** — six arms, each a `DefenseConfig`-shaped record with `kind`, `startTick`, `intensity`, `costPerTick`, `coverage`. This is a direct reuse of the influence-ops defense schema (`config.ts:72-82`), which is the config-not-code claim doing real work
- Panel plan — *with the caveat that it is currently generated and unpersisted (gap #1)*

### 5.4 Zero changes

Framer, ACH with Heuer weighting, abstention gate, calibration (z-score normalization, outlier detection, weakest-link aggregation), the full metrics package, BLUF/I&W/key-assumptions product assembly, provenance accounting, artifact canonicalization and store, and the as-of leakage guard.

**The honest sentence for the white paper:** *one new domain adapter and a retrieval harness; nothing downstream of the adapter changes.* Not "it's all config."

## 7. The Oracle and the Evaluation Plan

Every report carries an `EvaluationRegime` tag (`synthetic_oracle | historical_backtest | live_post_cutoff`) and the metrics package provides **no function that pools them** (`engine/src/metrics/index.ts`). Three regimes, three different strengths of claim.

### 7.1 `synthetic_oracle` — the strongest oracle in the catalog

Run a **real retriever** over a corpus with a **known planted ratio**. Ground truth on capture is not observed, it is **constructed**: we know exactly which documents were planted, we can read the returned ranked list, and we can read the generated answer's provenance chain. There is no estimation step between the world and the label.

Design points that make it hold up:

- **Fictional entities.** Planted documents assert facts about invented countries, companies and people. This kills two problems at once: the red line (no real false claim is ever authored), and parametric contamination (the generator cannot "know better" from pretraining, so we measure retrieval capture rather than the model's prior).
- **Planted documents are generated to a numeric similarity target**, not to a narrative. `delta` is a dial, and the harness verifies the achieved similarity gain rather than trusting the generator.
- **Three retriever configurations minimum** (sparse, dense, hybrid+reranker). A defense ranking that does not survive across all three is not a product (§8, W6 falsifier).

**Pre-registered oracle questions** (80 questions × 32 runs, matching the platform's measured batch shape): *"At planted ratio r in topic cell T, retriever R, defense D, retrieval depth k=10 — which ACR band?"*

### 7.2 `historical_backtest` — real, and honestly thin

**Fit on:** NewsGuard's audit published **6 March 2025**. 10 chatbots (ChatGPT-4o, You.com Smart Assistant, Grok, Pi, le Chat, Copilot, Meta AI, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) × 15 false narratives × 3 prompt styles (Innocent / Leading / Malign) = **450 responses**. Results: **33.55% repeated the false claim, 18.22% non-response, 48.22% debunk.** Provenance detail available for fitting: **56 of 450 responses carried direct links** to Pravda-network stories, citing **92 distinct articles**, with two models each referencing **27**. Network state at that date: **150 domains, 49 countries, 3.6M articles in 2024, 207 provably false claims.**

**Predict:** NewsGuard's **January 2026** Pravda retest. **Five** false narratives tested; chatbots repeated them as fact in **half of the cases**. Network state: **370 sites, roughly 6 million articles in 2025.** Source: NewsGuard's Chine Labbé, quoted in [France 24, 3 June 2026](https://www.france24.com/en/technology/20260603-ai-chatbot-responses-polluted-pro-russian-disinformation); corroborated independently at ~360 sites and "50 percent, up from 33 percent in March 2025" by [The Integrity Project, 9 June 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/the-integrity-project_misinformation-pravda-disinformation-activity-7470136978269876224-eurD).

> **UNVERIFIED:** I could not retrieve NewsGuard's January 2026 Pravda report page directly (the AI-monitor URL returned not-found through both fetch tools). The **number of chatbots tested and the prompt-style structure of the January 2026 retest are not stated in any source I could reach**, so the response count N behind "half of the cases" is unknown. The 33.55% / 450 figures for March 2025 are from NewsGuard's own report text and are **CONFIRMED**.

**The honest statement of what this is:** two dated points on the poison-attributable series. Fitting a multi-parameter kernel on one point and predicting one point is **a single held-out prediction, not a backtest, and it cannot establish skill.** Anyone who reports a Brier skill score from N=1 is reporting noise.

**What makes it more than N=1:** the broader AI False Claims Monitor is a ~24-month **model × month panel** — 10–11 models across roughly fourteen monthly audits plus three quarterly ones, de-anonymized by model from September 2025 — giving on the order of 150+ cells. But that series measures **all** false claims, not poison-attributable ones, so it is a noisy proxy for our quantity and is used to estimate the *confound* (§7.3), not the effect. That division of labor is the design, and it should be stated as such rather than blurred.

### 7.3 The confound a judge named — and why it is not fatal

**The objection:** providers change ranking and refusal behavior between audits, so a 33% → 50% change is not purely corpus-share growth.

**The objection is correct, it is large, and it is measurable in the customer's own evidence base:**

- Non-response rate across the same monitor went from **31% (August 2024) to 0% (August 2025)** while the false-claim rate went **18% → 35%**. NewsGuard names the mechanism itself: "As chatbots adopted real-time web searches, they moved away from declining to answer questions" ([August 2025 monitor](https://www.newsguardtech.com/ai-monitor/august-2025-ai-false-claim-monitor/)). That is a pure **provider-side** shift — a change in `tau` and in refusal policy, with no corpus-share component.
- A single-provider shift with no corresponding corpus jump: across seven audits over 18 months, Claude cited Russian state-affiliated sources about **4%** of the time; in the most recent audit that "nearly quadrupled to **15%**" ([Matt Skibinski, NewsGuard, 5 May 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattskibinski_anthropics-ai-chatbot-is-leaning-more-on-activity-7457438070544510976-fpja)). **UNVERIFIED** which audit edition this refers to and whether it is the January or May 2026 quarterly.
- Cross-sectional dispersion at a single date is enormous: in the January 2026 quarterly, Mistral gave false information on **50%** of prompts (15/30) and the next-worst on **46.67%** (14/30), while **Claude was correct on every prompt** ([NewsGuard, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/newsguard-technologies_in-our-first-quarterly-audit-of-the-year-activity-7432486681372573696-ERNo)). Identical corpus, five-fold spread in outcome. **That spread is the ranking parameter, visible.**

**Four-part handling, in order of strength:**

1. **Parameterize, do not wave.** `tau` (ranker sharpness) and `w[class]` (source priors) are explicit kernel parameters. The confound is not an unmodeled residual; it is two named variables.
2. **Identify them off a poison-free control series.** Non-response rates and non-Pravda false-claim rates from the same monitor give an independent read on provider behavior that carries no corpus-share signal.
3. **Identify within provider-month cells.** Corpus share is common across providers at a date; provider behavior is not. The between-provider variance at fixed corpus share estimates `tau`-drift; the within-provider change across dates at fixed `tau` estimates the share effect. **Honest expectation: with two poison-attributable dates this is not point-identified. The correct output is a bound on the decomposition, not a point estimate** — and §8 W10 pre-commits to reporting the bound.
4. **The decisive answer: the confound is a property of the unclassified proxy, not of the product.** In the enclave we own the retriever and hold it fixed across audits. `tau` and `w` become *known constants* rather than nuisance parameters. **This is a second, independent reason the classified instantiation is the easy one**, and it belongs in the bid: the demo's hardest methodological problem does not exist in the deployment.

### 7.4 Baselines to beat — named, and deliberately hard

| Baseline | Why it is the right adversary |
|---|---|
| **Corpus-share proportionality** — ACR = planted share of the topic corpus | **The primary baseline.** The entire capability exists to beat it. If the kernel does not, delete the ranking stage |
| **Persistence** — last audit's rate | The one a skeptical reviewer will fit in five minutes |
| **Base rate** — the monitor's all-claims false rate for that month | The platform's default; too easy here, reported for continuity only |
| **Two-feature logistic** on (domain count, article count) | The obvious regression. If a two-feature GLM matches the simulation, the simulation is ornament |

Beating base rate is not evidence for this capability. **Beating corpus-share proportionality is.**

### 7.5 The honest headline metric for this question class

- **Synthetic oracle:** `brierSkillVsCorpusShareBaseline`, reported alongside ECE **with bin occupancy** and sharpness — because a system that states the base rate everywhere is perfectly calibrated and worthless, which is why the metrics package refuses to report calibration without sharpness. Plus a **negative control**: a deliberately misspecified arm must score *worse* than baseline. The platform has demonstrated this pattern before (−0.4561 with coverage@1 of 0.123 on its misspecified arm) and the pack repeats it here.
- **Backtest / live:** **coverage@1 on the pre-registered bands, with the interval width reported**, and an explicit written statement that N is too small for a skill claim.
- **The number the customer actually buys:** **worst-cell capture (WCC) at production *k***. Never the mean alone. §14.2 explains why reporting the mean alone would make the product actively misleading.

## 8. The 90-Day Unclassified Demonstration

Twelve weeks. Each week names what is shown, what number comes out, and what would falsify it.

| Wk | Build | Number out | Falsifier |

### 9.2 What the customer must expose — tiered, deliberately

**Tier 1 — index statistics, no content.** Per-source-class document counts over time, near-duplicate cluster sizes, ingestion rate by feed. *No text leaves the enclave and no text is read by our code.*

**Tier 2 — query-log schema, not queries.** Counts per topic cell, production retrieval depth *k*, reranker on/off, aggregation rule.

**Tier 3 — a retrieval-only shim.** Run N synthetic probe queries through the **real** retriever; receive only (a) ranked document IDs, (b) source-class labels, (c) similarity scores. **No document text.**

**Tier 4 — planted documents in a mirror index.** A shadow partition where synthetic fictional-entity documents can be inserted and capture measured. *This is what upgrades the forecast to exact ground truth.*

**Tier 5 — the generator.** Answer-level capture rather than retrieval-level.

**What we never need, at any tier:** the content of any document, the identity of any analyst, the text of any query, or any classified analytic judgment.

### 9.3 The accreditation obstacle, named precisely

The obstacle is **not** classifying our code. It is the **write path at tier 4.** Tiers 1–3 are read-only telemetry against an already-accredited system and can plausibly ride an existing read-only authorization. Tier 4 writes to an accredited system and requires a separate shadow partition, a data-integrity control guaranteeing planted documents cannot reach production retrieval, and an AO decision. **That is the single long-pole item in the whole program.**

**The design principle that follows, and it is the reason this capability is the shortest path to a first contract:** the pack is built so that **tier 3 alone produces a real, defensible number** — a retrieval-level capture forecast with a stated interval. The pilot is therefore not blocked on the tier-4 decision; tier 4 upgrades it from forecast to measurement. A capability that can only start after an AO signs a write authorization is a capability that starts in eighteen months.

### 9.4 The IARPA REASON contracting pattern applies, and is cheaper here

Performers build and are tested on unclassified data; an independent cleared T&E team evaluates on classified material in parallel; performers never receive classified access and refine against unclassified summary-level feedback. REASON is a 42-month program with Phase 2 running Oct 2025–Mar 2027 and a Government Purpose Rights floor.

**Deterministic seeded byte-identical artifacts make that pattern cheaper than it has ever been**, and this capability is where the argument is sharpest: the cleared T&E team runs *our exact artifact* inside the enclave and gets a hash. If the hash matches, the uncleared team's configuration and the cleared team's execution are provably the same object. The feedback channel back across the boundary can then be a **band, an interval and a hash** — three unclassified numbers — rather than a narrative summary that has to be reviewed for spillage.

## 10. Buyer, Workflow Displacement, and the Analyst-Hour Denominator

### 10.1 The buyer is not the analyst

The buyer is the **owner of the AI answer surface**, and there are three named signature blocks:

1. **The Authorizing Official** who signs the system's authorization and its continuous-monitoring reports. Dated, signed, recurring — and **currently carrying no evidence at all for the claim "our retrieval corpus is not captured."** This is the strongest of the three.

3. **The Open Source Enterprise's source-vetting / ingest decision** for a new feed. ICS 206-01 (2 Dec 2024) governs citation and reference specifically for publicly available information, commercially available information, and OSINT — the corpus class under attack.

### 10.2 The relationship to CIA's own reported tooling

This is not a hypothetical surface. **OSIRIS** is a CIA Open Source Enterprise generative-AI tool that summarizes open-source data and lets analysts ask follow-up questions in plain English; it is used by **thousands of analysts across the 18 U.S. intelligence agencies**, and it runs on **unclassified, publicly or commercially available data**. OSE Director Randy Nixon described the value as analysts being able to "start chatting and asking questions of the machines to give you answers, **also sourced**." OSIRIS received the inaugural IC OSINT Innovation of the Year Award.

Three consequences:

- **The corpus OSIRIS reads is precisely the corpus the Pravda network floods.** The network published 3.6M articles in 2024 across 150 domains in 49 countries and dozens of languages, growing to ~370 sites and roughly 6M articles in 2025 — targeting, by NewsGuard's and ASP's shared assessment, the retrieved data of AI systems rather than human readers. ASP named the tactic **"LLM grooming."**
- **The customer has already stated the threat in its own words.** CIA's first CTO, Nand Mulchandani, on generative AI in 2024: *"adversaries could steal and poison them."* ([AP, 23 May 2024](https://apnews.com/article/us-intelligence-services-ai-models-9471e8c5703306eb29f6c971b6923187))
- **The IC OSINT Strategy 2024–2026** calls OSINT "the INT of first resort" and makes tradecraft standards and source vetting a named priority.

The language-asymmetry finding sharpens the worst-cell argument considerably: France 24, replicating a Nordis test, found Copilot correctly debunking a pro-Russian claim in English and French while **repeating it as true in Finnish, Danish and Slovenian**. Labbé: *"in regions where propaganda is more pervasive, and the fact-checking ecosystem is weaker, the outcomes are significantly worse."* An aggregate English-weighted capture rate systematically understates exposure in exactly the languages an IC OSINT surface most needs.

### 10.3 The analyst-hour denominator — honestly weak, and stated as such

**The honest answer is that this capability displaces no analyst hours today, because nobody is performing the task.** The displacement is not labor; it is **an unanswered question on a recurring accreditation checklist**.

The nearest existing manual activity is source vetting and spot-checking of AI outputs, where an audit replaces sampling with a census. Any hour figure attached to that is **ASSUMED**, and the platform's own code will not let us launder it into a claim: the metrics package's operations module **requires a measured manual baseline as an argument** before it will compute a speedup, precisely so that a speedup cannot be reported without one. If the pilot wants a speedup number, the pilot must first time the manual baseline. That is a design constraint we ship with, and it is worth saying out loud to a panel that has been shown a lot of unfalsifiable efficiency arithmetic.

**The real return is two things:** an avoided incident (a captured claim entering a coordinated product), and **schedule** — evidence that already exists when the authorization package is assembled.

## 11. Benefit-Category Mapping

**(a) Efficiency and accuracy.** *Mechanism:* replaces sampling with a census over the retrieval surface. A source-validation analyst spot-checks; the audit probes every topic cell at production depth and returns per-cell occupancy. Accuracy gain is specific: the audit measures **top-*k* occupancy**, the quantity that actually causes capture, rather than the corpus-share proxy that a manual review would reach for and that the PoisonedRAG result shows to be four orders of magnitude off.

**(b) Reduced time to analyze, better prediction.** *Mechanism:* the 90-day forecast gives the index owner a window to intervene *before* the next audit resolves — dedup, source caps, quarantine. And the abstention gate acts at answer time: a captured answer is withheld rather than corrected after it has entered a product. Prediction accuracy is claimed **only in the synthetic-oracle regime**, where it is exactly measurable, and explicitly **not** claimed for the open web, where N=1 (§7.2).

**(c) Quality assurance — identify discrepancies between sources, reconcile analytic confidence.** *Mechanism:* **the strongest fit of the four.** Capture rate *is* a source-base discrepancy measure. The platform's contradiction precision/recall and provenance-completeness metrics attach to it directly, and the output maps onto ICD 203's first tradecraft standard and ICD 206's source descriptors without translation. The confidence side matters too: confidence is aggregated by **weakest link**, so a single captured source class in the evidence base cannot be averaged away by nine clean ones — which is precisely the reconciliation behavior the solicitation asks for.

**(d) Cost reduction by automating repetitive analysis and simulation.** *Mechanism:* the probe-and-audit loop is definitionally repetitive, and re-auditing at a new as-of date is a **config change with a new seed**, not a re-analysis. Measured platform throughput: 15,832 simulations in 305 s. The determinism means a re-audit that produces the same hash proves nothing changed — a null result that costs 305 seconds instead of an analyst week.

## 13. Red Lines — Structural Enforcement, Not Policy Notes

**The red line:** this must never become a guide to poisoning someone else's index.

The platform has a precedent for enforcing a scope boundary in the type system rather than in a prompt, and the exact language is in the code. From `engine/src/sim/adapters/influence-ops/config.ts:1-8`:

> *"SCOPE BOUNDARY, enforced structurally: narratives are opaque identifiers with numeric positions. There is no text field anywhere in this model, so no code path can emit persuasive content — there is no content in it to emit. The population is synthetic; nothing here touches a real platform or person."*

That is the standard C-IW-9 must meet. Five enforcement points, each a code property with a test, not a paragraph in a policy annex:

1. **No real claim is ever authored.** Planted documents assert facts about **invented entities**, and the harness ships a validator that rejects any planted document whose named entities intersect a real-entity gazetteer. The validator's **false-negative rate must be zero on a red-team set, and week 2 of the demo is a hard stop if it is not** (§8). This is not only a red line — it is better science, because fictional entities remove parametric contamination from the oracle.

2. **Documents are generated to a numeric target, not a narrative.** The generator's objective is a **similarity gain `delta`**, verified by measurement. The pack's internal representation of a "planted claim" is an integer ID and a score. Following the influence-ops precedent: there is no persuasive-content field, so there is no content to emit.

3. **No write path to any index the customer does not own.** The pack ships **no crawler, no publisher, no account creation, no submission path of any kind.** The single write path in the entire pack targets a mirror index named in config, gated behind a field that must be explicitly set, and **that field is part of the artifact hash** — so any run that wrote is permanently distinguishable from any run that did not.

4. **The output schema cannot carry an attack plan.** The product has no field capable of holding a target domain, a target platform, or a publication schedule. `readQuantity` returns a scalar in [0,1]; the artifact carries IDs, scores, bands and hashes.

5. **The information-asymmetry check, run honestly.** *Does an adversary learn anything from this work?* The general principle — occupancy beats share — is already public in PoisonedRAG and cannot be un-published. **The genuinely sensitive output is the customer's own index composition**, which is why the enclave artifact is classified at that level and why every unclassified demo runs on public corpora with fictional planted content only. The asymmetry is favorable: we publish the defense ranking, we never publish an index profile.

**One further structural fact, reported rather than engineered around:** the adjudication panel has already declined influence-operations framing under live measurement (5/5 refusals; later 2 of 3 declined). The pack presents itself to the panel as a defensive audit of a customer-owned system, with the fictional-entity constraint visible in the adjudicator's context. **If the panel still refuses, that refusal is reported as a finding about the pack** — a model-level guardrail firing is evidence about the work, not an obstacle to route around.

## 14. Failure Modes

### 14.1 The ranking stage does not fit the customer's retriever
Production retrieval is a hybrid of BM25, dense embeddings, a cross-encoder reranker and undisclosed business rules. Tier-3 exposure calibrates the score distributions directly, which is the mitigation. **Honest worst case:** if the customer's ranker is a black box with undisclosed rules, Stage B degenerates from a mechanism into a curve fit, and the capability's central claim — that it beats corpus-share proportionality *because it models ranking* — becomes unfalsifiable. **The tripwire is the week-9 test:** if the kernel and the share-only baseline land in the same band, the ranking stage gets deleted.

### 14.2 Query-conditionality defeats the aggregate — the misleading-state failure
**This is the most dangerous failure mode and it is a product-honesty failure, not a technical one.** Capture rate is not one number; it is a distribution over topic cells, and *the adversary selects the cell*. An index with 3% mean capture can have 90% capture on the four questions that matter. A dashboard reporting "3% — healthy" beside a captured topic cell is exactly the class of defect that must be flagged first: a static reassuring indicator next to an unhealthy reality.

**Structural mitigation:** the product schema makes **worst-cell capture a required field**, and the mean is not renderable without it. The premortem adjudicator's job is to enforce this (§4). A mark renders only when it can differ from its neighbours — a per-cell capture readout differentiates; a single green aggregate does not.

### 14.3 Classified ground truth is only as good as the write permission
Without tier 4, the enclave number is a **forecast** validated against synthetic probes, not a **measurement**. The pack is designed to produce a defensible tier-3 number precisely so this is a degradation rather than a blocker — but the tier-3 number must be labelled as a forecast every time it is reported, and the schema enforces that by carrying the exposure tier in the artifact.

### 14.4 The unclassified backtest has N≈1
Two poison-attributable audit dates. **Not skill, and it must never be reported as skill.** The mitigation is regime separation, which the platform enforces in code: the report carries `regime: historical_backtest`, and no function pools it with the synthetic-oracle numbers.

### 14.5 Provider and ranker drift swamps corpus share on the open web
Documented, quantified, and bounded rather than solved (§7.3). Absent in the enclave.

### 14.6 The audit becomes a target — and this one collides with the platform's own reproducibility story
If an adversary learns the probe schedule, injections can be timed around it. The obvious mitigation is randomized probe timing seeded from the run seed. **But the seed is in the artifact, so anyone holding the artifact can reconstruct the schedule.** Determinism and unpredictability are in genuine tension here, and pretending otherwise would be a defect a red team would find in an afternoon.

**Resolution — a split seed.** The run carries two seeds: a **published structural seed** (corpus construction, ensemble parameters, everything needed to recompute the analysis) and a **held probe-timing seed** that never enters the published artifact. The artifact hashes a **commitment** to the held seed — `H(salt ‖ probeSeed)` — rather than the seed itself. Anyone can verify after the fact that the probe schedule was fixed in advance and not chosen to flatter the result; nobody can predict it from the artifact. **This costs the pack the property that a third party can recompute the probe schedule, and that loss should be stated in the bid rather than discovered by an evaluator.**

### 14.7 The honest worst case
The oracle works, the defenses rank cleanly and transferably, and the customer declines tier 4 — leaving a well-calibrated retrieval-level forecast that nobody can convert into a measured capture rate on the system that matters. In that world the capability is still worth its cost, because the defense ranking transfers and the index-hygiene interventions are actionable without ground truth. **But it would be a forecasting product, not the verification product this concept is sold as, and that reclassification must be reported, not absorbed.**

# Part B — Reproducibility-as-the-Product: An Audit Trail for AI-Assisted Analysis

*The adjacent capability both judges called the highest-win-probability item missing from the entire catalog.*

## B.1 In Plain English

**What this does:** It makes every machine-assisted judgment re-checkable. Hand someone the record of a judgment and a laptop with the network switched off; they run one command and get the identical answer, plus a list of exactly which pieces of evidence produced it.

**Why it matters:** As more agencies put AI into analysis, "can you show your work" stops being a courtesy and becomes a condition of shipping. Right now, most AI-assisted analysis cannot answer it — the evidence the machine read was never saved.

**What is new:** the record is produced *as a by-product of running*, not reconstructed afterwards by a compliance team. And it is honest about its own limits: the arithmetic can be re-run exactly, the language-model steps cannot, so they are recorded and signed instead of pretended to be repeatable.

## B.2 Why it wins where forecasting capabilities struggle

Every forecasting capability in this package asks the buyer to accept an accuracy claim that will not resolve for months. **This one has no accuracy claim to be wrong about.** The oracle is *mechanical*: "does recomputation produce the same hash" is decidable in seconds, on the evaluator's own hardware, with no ground truth to wait for and no baseline to argue about.

This is also the correct strategic response to the ForecastBench correction. As of 2026-07-16, ForecastBench operators reported several systems statistically indistinguishable from superforecaster accuracy, with one outranking the superforecaster median on market questions. **Raw forecasting skill is commoditizing.** The durable differentiators are measurement, abstention, provenance and calibration — and this capability is the pure play on provenance.

## B.3 The buyer already exists, with a standing budget line

**ICD 203, Section D.2:** *"The IC Analytic Standards are the foundational assessment criteria for a regular program of review of IC analytic products. **Each IC element shall maintain a program of product evaluation** using the IC Analytic Standards as the core elements for assessment criteria."*

That is a mandated, recurring, funded review function — a buyer who is already obligated to do this work and currently has no instrument for doing it on AI-assisted products.

**ICD 206 is stronger still, because it already binds and it predates the problem.** From the directive (22 Jan 2015):

- **D.5:** *"If citing a source that is dynamic (e.g., from an Internet posting), ephemeral..., or not subject to an IC element's policy for systematic storage for analyst access..., **a record of the source shall be preserved for retention** by the IC element producing the covered analytic product **for at least one year**."* For products with a specified time horizon, retention runs to the time horizon or five years, whichever is shorter.
- **D.6:** *"The covered analytic product with all appropriate sourcing information **shall be retained as a document of record**."*

**The consequence, stated plainly: an LLM-assisted analytic product whose retrieval set was never persisted cannot satisfy ICD 206 D.5 for dynamic sources.** Web pages are the paradigm case of a dynamic source. A RAG pipeline that retrieves a page, uses it, and keeps no record has produced a product that is out of compliance with a directive signed in 2015 — and there is no way to fix it after the fact, because the page has changed.

Third signature block: the **AO's authorization and continuous-monitoring package**.

## B.4 The schedule argument — and its honest label

CIA's new Acquisition Framework, announced **9 February 2026** by Director Ratcliffe and led by chief procurement executive **Efstathia Fragogiannis** (previously director of contracts and senior procurement executive at DARPA, joined November 2025), is built around centralized vendor vetting and a **streamlined IT authorization process**, explicitly to *"significantly reduce the time between when CIA defines a mission requirement and when it receives operating authority,"* with clear pathways to *"rapidly onboard breakthrough technology prototypes."*

Authorization evidence is the long pole in that timeline. A system that emits, per run, a byte-identical artifact with a hashed evidence manifest supplies audit evidence **by construction** rather than through after-the-fact instrumentation.

> **ASSUMED, not confirmed.** I have no evidence of any authorization decision turning on artifact determinism. This is a hypothesis with a falsifier: *if a pilot's authorization timeline is not measurably shortened relative to a comparable non-deterministic system, the schedule argument fails and the capability reverts to a quality argument only.* It should be bid that way — as a testable claim, not an assertion.

## B.5 The intellectual core: a two-class ledger

**Any vendor claiming full reproducibility of an LLM pipeline is lying, and a technical evaluation panel will know it.** The honest product draws the line explicitly:

| Class | Contents | Guarantee | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RECOMPUTABLE** | Numeric kernel, seeded PRNG (sfc32), scoring, calibration, all metrics, artifact canonicalization | **Same seed ⇒ byte-identical artifact hash.** No wall clock: `Math.random` and `Date.now` are banned in `sim/` and a test scans the source to enforce it | Re-run offline; compare hashes |
| **ATTESTED** | Every LLM call — framer, panel adjudicators, red team | Exact request, exact response, model identifier, and a content hash, **replayable but not recomputable** | Compare recorded artifacts; measure divergence on re-run |

**The measurable quantities — and one of them is a number nobody publishes:**

1. **Third-party re-derivation rate** — fraction of shipped judgments a third party reproduces bit-for-bit from the artifact alone, on independent hardware, offline. Target 1.00 on the recomputable class.
2. **Provenance completeness** — share of claims with a resolvable evidence reference. Already a platform metric; unsupported assertions are **counted, not blocked**.
3. **ICD 206 SRC sufficiency** — share of source reference citations carrying all required elements (originator, unambiguous identifier, title, date, page, classification of extract, overall classification, source descriptor, portion marking).
4. **Replay divergence** — re-run the attested class 30 days later against the same model identifier: **what fraction of judgments change band?** This is measurable, it is the honest headline for this capability class, and **no vendor publishes it.** Publishing it — including when it is uncomfortable — is the differentiator.

## B.6 What blocks it today — and it is not research

**Architecture gap #1 is a direct, disqualifying contradiction of this capability's core claim.** `PanelPlan` carries an LLM-generated `systemPrompt` per adjudicator. It is never persisted and never hashed. `runId = computeRunId(identity)`, and identity has no panel field. **Therefore two runs judged by different generated rubrics share a run id.**

An evaluator's very first adversarial test of a reproducibility product will be: *"produce two runs with the same run id and different judgments."* **Today they can.** Until the rubric is in the identity, this capability cannot be sold, and any claim made about it would be an evidence-class violation.

**Architecture gap #2 is the second blocker, and it is the misleading-state failure again.** `available: results.length > 0` → `level: adjudicationAvailable ? "high" : "moderate"`. An audit trail that records "adjudication available: true" when one of eight judges survived is not an audit trail — it is a green light over a degraded panel. Partial panel loss is the normal operating condition. The fix is a graded k-of-N field carrying which adjudicators ran, which declined, and which failed, with confidence derated accordingly.

Both fixes are ordinary engineering. Neither is research. **Both are prerequisites, and the honest bid says so before an evaluator finds them.**

## B.7 The demo — five moves, one command each

1. **The hash.** Hand the evaluator an artifact and a container. `--network none`. One command. It prints the hash in the paper. *(Measured today: same seed ⇒ identical artifact hash, by unit test and CLI.)*
2. **The tripwire.** Change one evidence document's date by one day. Re-run. The hash changes, `assertNoLeakage()` throws, and the `LeakageError` names the offending document and its date. Evidence integrity is not a checkbox; it is a thrown exception.
3. **The provenance query.** Take a shipped judgment. Ask *"which evidence produced this?"* The artifact answers per claim, with the ACH diagnosticity weights that ranked the hypotheses and the abstention gate's margin at the decision point.
4. **The uncomfortable number.** Re-run the attested class 30 days later against the same model identifier. Report **replay divergence**, including when it is nonzero. This move is what separates the product from a compliance veneer.
5. **The ledger.** Show which fields are recomputable and which are attested, and state that no vendor can make the attested class recomputable.

**The demo's own falsifier, stated up front:** *if the evaluator can produce two runs with the same run id and materially different judgments, the capability has failed.* That is precisely gap #1, which is why gap #1 is the highest-priority fix in the codebase.

**Verification status, stated up front.** The shared web-search budget for this session was exhausted (200/200) before I could issue a single query, and `cia.gov`, `dni.gov` and `iarpa.gov` all returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch. One fetch succeeded: [EPA's water-sector cybersecurity page](https://www.epa.gov/waterresilience/epa-cybersecurity-water-sector), which describes *recommendations*, *requestable* assessments and technical assistance — voluntary framing throughout, no mandatory regulatory lever. That single confirmation is load-bearing below (C-CYBER-2). **Everything else in this adjudication is professional judgment about institutional behavior, not a citation check.** Where I contradict a factual claim in the catalog I mark it CONFIRMED / ASSUMED / UNVERIFIED explicitly.

**Scoring scale (mission reality only — not novelty, not method, not scorability):**

- **5** — A named office with signature authority buys it, drops it into an existing workflow, and defends it in a tradecraft review. Data and authority in hand.
- **4** — Real buyer, real workflow, one solvable obstacle (accreditation, a data buy, a coordination fight).
- **3** — Plausible buyer, but the output has to fight into an existing product line, or the decision point is soft.
- **2** — Analysts find it interesting; nobody signs a requirement. Duplicates an owned product line, or solves a problem the office doesn't experience as a problem.
- **1** — Unbuyable. No user, no authority, or the act of building it damages the rest of the portfolio.

## 2. Top 8 — why, from mission reality

**C-ADV-5 — Technoeconomic Retaliation.** This is the only candidate in the catalog where the customer wrote the requirement into the solicitation and the receiving office has both the data and the pen. A BIS rule package goes to interagency with a retaliation annex; that annex has a due date, a drafter, and a signature block, and today it is one person reasoning from the last two episodes while a different office estimates the price consequence on a different timeline, if at all. That is a *named, dated, recurring artifact produced badly* — the single strongest form of mission reality there is. The data is entirely public, the resolution is a MOFCOM announcement, and the office can act on the answer because it owns the rule. It also passes the "would this survive tradecraft review" test cleanly: a named material, a band, an assumption with a falsifier about substitution elasticity. My only reservation is that the answer arrives into a policy process that has already largely decided, which is why it must be positioned as *produced with the rule package, not after it* — the catalog says this and it is the right pitch.

**C-GEO-3 — Coercion Tempo Ladder.** A watch officer at INDOPACOM J2 maintains a daily tally from Taiwan MND releases and writes "sustained elevated pressure." That sentence has been in the product for four years and nobody can score it. This candidate displaces an actual daily manual task, resolves publicly every day, and answers the question the command genuinely asks — not "how bad is it" but "does the announced quarantine happen." Critically, the honest handling of the zero-base-rate rung (abstain rather than emit a confident number) is the thing that keeps the command's confidence when the answer is uncomfortable. The calibration loop can start next week on data anyone can check, which means the vendor can show a real track record before the contract's first option year. That is worth more than any TRL number.

**C-GEO-8 — Contradiction Ledger.** This is the candidate a mission-center chief buys first, and it is not close. It maps word-for-word onto the customer's stated benefit (c). It lands on a review desk that already exists and is already overloaded. It displaces a task — a senior reviewer catching contradictions from memory across 340 reports — that provably does not scale and that everyone knows does not scale. And, decisively, **it makes claims about the source base rather than about the world**, which means no analyst has to accept a machine's judgment about Iran to accept its output. The adoption barrier for every other candidate in this catalog is "why should I believe your model"; this one's barrier is only "is your claim-identity resolution any good," which is a testable engineering question. The band-flip sensitivity output ("which contradiction, if resolved, moves the BLUF") is exactly the finding a reviewer needs and cannot generate.

**C-TECHECON-2 — Sanctions Package Pre-Mortem.** Treasury has an institutional memory named Rusal: a designation whose second-order effects had to be partially unwound. That memory is what converts an interesting capability into a felt need. The decision point is a dated package memo with a signature; the current input is a two-page qualitative impact assessment; GAO has publicly said the assessments are largely qualitative. The office has the authority to act on the answer because the office issues the designation. The catalog's ally-collateral estimate is the part that will sell — not because it is the most rigorous, but because the ally-collateral surprise is the failure mode that generates a phone call from a deputy secretary. One caution: the honest abstention rate on Iran and DPRK will be high because those economies are missing from the IO tables. Lead with that; a Treasury analyst will respect it.

**C-TECHECON-4 — Critical-Mineral Chokepoint.** The rarest thing in this catalog: a decision point attached to appropriated money. DLA Strategic Materials buys stockpile. Today the requirement rests on a single deterministic conflict scenario refreshed on a multi-year cycle, with no model of an adversary choosing *when* to squeeze. A distribution over supplier postures, with months-of-supply and first-program-to-stock-out as outputs, feeds a purchase decision directly. Add that a date-certain resolution event sits inside the proposal window and you have the only candidate that can be pre-registered and scored before award. The red line the catalog names — emitting coarse price bands only, never point estimates, to avoid a market-moving-information hazard — is correct and should be foregrounded, because a Treasury or Commerce lawyer will raise it in the first meeting.

**C-TRADECRAFT-2 — Effective-N.** Ask any IC reviewer what went wrong on Iraq and you will hear one word before you finish the question. Circular reporting is the community's founding trauma, and yet nobody runs an independence check, because doing it by hand across fourteen documents is tedious rather than hard. The output — *"you believed fourteen sources; you have 2.3; your posterior odds fall from 61:1 to 4:1"* — is comprehensible in one sentence to a branch chief, a DNI briefer, and a congressional staffer, and it changes a field (confidence) that is signed and audited. It runs on entirely open data, demonstrates in minutes, and has labeled positives that are almost comically easy to verify. My reservations are two and both are manageable: false-circularity from good translation and wire-service phrasing will erode trust fast, so the false-circularity rate must be published alongside detection rate; and it must be pipeline-enforced upstream of any reconciliation product, not documented as a dependency in a README.

**C-TRADECRAFT-4 — Tradecraft Auditor.** The rubric is the customer's own directive; the review is mandated and reported annually; the task is one that every branch chief does badly at 6pm. The Dutch-book detector is the genuinely new thing — nobody checks whether the seven estimative statements in a single document are mutually satisfiable, because holding seven propositions in working memory is beyond any reviewer, and the failure ("X is likely" beside "X or Y is unlikely") is invisible and embarrassing. It also directly implements the customer's benefit (d) at a defensible denominator: every product, every element, every year. Two hard warnings. First, a false contradiction alarm will destroy analyst trust faster than any other failure in this document — the extracted proposition set must be shown back for confirmation before infeasibility is declared, and the parse-error rate must be published. Second, the moment product scores can be aggregated to a named author, this stops being a tradecraft tool and becomes a personnel instrument, and adoption dies. The catalog's structural answer (no author field in the record type) is right; make it a demonstrable failing test, not a paragraph.

**C-TRADECRAFT-6 — Indicator Lab.** Indicator lists grow monotonically and never shrink, because no watch chief will remove an indicator and own the risk. That is a real, universal, unaddressed organizational pathology, and the deletion output is the only thing in this catalog that solves it — a measured likelihood ratio with a confidence interval gives the chief cover he cannot otherwise obtain. The buyer owns the artifact outright (watch centers maintain their own lists; no cross-directorate boundary to cross), the data is public and continuously resolving, and the two outputs a chief actually cares about — false-alarm rate and mean lead time — are stated as numbers for the first time. The rare-but-decisive indicator is the failure mode that will be raised in the first briefing; the answer must be a distinct third category ("insufficient evidence to rate"), never a low LR, and that is exactly what the platform's first-class abstention verdict provides.

## 3. Bottom 8 — the disqualifying flaw

- **C-IW-3 — Resilience Playbook.** Every buyer it names — State public diplomacy post-R/FIMI, USAID successors — is a demolished or dying budget line; the catalog says so and then proposes it anyway. *This is the finding: a candidate nobody labeled bad scored as low as the ones everybody did.*
- **C-GEO-6 — Water-food stress pathway.** It concedes it will lose to ACLED CAST and the WPS tool, which are free and better-resourced, and its primary data input's institutional home may no longer exist (UNVERIFIED — must be checked before this appears anywhere).
- **C-ADV-10 — Leader Digital Twin.** No kernel, no oracle, and it requires a behavioral model of a named living individual — the one thing the actor schema exists to refuse.
- **C-IW-10 — Domestic radicalization scoring.** Four stacked legal bars plus a base rate that guarantees roughly a thousand false positives per true one; for the buyer that issued this solicitation it is outside statutory mission entirely.
- **C-GEO-10 — Global Instability Heat Map.** A 0–100 construct with no resolvable referent, in a lane the customer has funded since the 1990s; it will be recognized and dismissed in thirty seconds.
- **C-TECHECON-10 — Adversary GDP Nowcast.** A Kalman filter is not a simulation of a decision, and entering a market where quant funds outspend the entire IC means being judged on measurement accuracy by specialists on their turf.
- **C-CYBER-10 — Warning Board.** The negative class is fiction (fewer than a tenth of OT networks can detect the activity), labels lag by months to years, and any positive Brier skill on this quantity should be assumed to be leakage.
- **C-TRADECRAFT-10 — Bias / insider-risk scorer.** Building it means adding the natural-person identifier field whose absence is the structural legal argument protecting every other candidate — it is not merely weak, it weakens the portfolio.

## 4. Cross-cutting observations

**a) The catalog confuses "an analyst would find this interesting" with "an office will buy it."** The single reliable discriminator across all 60 is whether the output lands on a *dated artifact with a signature block*: a BIS rule annex, an OFAC designation memo, a stockpile purchase, an ICD 203 review, a watchlist, a NAC demarche, a deployment plan's cyber annex. Candidates that name one score 4–5. Candidates that emit a standalone probability for "senior policymakers" score 2–3, because a standalone probability has no home in IC production — there is no serialized product type it can be published as, and no one is empowered to act on it.

**b) The coordination problem appears in zero of sixty candidates, and it is the largest adoption risk in the document.** IC finished intelligence is *coordinated*, not authored. Nobody in this catalog says who represents the machine's judgment in a coordination meeting, who takes the dissent footnote when NGA disagrees with the kernel, or what a run artifact's status is when three agencies concur and one does not. Note which candidates dodge it entirely: the ones making claims about the *source base* rather than the *world* (C-TRADECRAFT-1, -2, -4; C-GEO-8). Those are the four that will actually field, and it is not a coincidence — they never enter coordination at all.

**c) The tradecraft lens has the highest mission reality and the lowest glamour, and the proposal is probably about to get this backwards.** Four of my top eight are QA candidates. They displace mandated, repetitive, universally disliked tasks and produce no judgment anyone has to defend in front of a deputy. The adversary-simulation candidates win the demo and lose the deployment. My recommendation: lead the bid with C-ADV-1 as the *demo* (the customer's own scenario, unclassified, spectacular) and structure the *program* around the tradecraft candidates, because those are what still get used in year three.

**d) Nobody costed the analyst-hours claim, and a branch chief will ask in the first meeting.** Every candidate asserts time savings; none states a denominator — how many people, how many hours a week, on which named product. For most candidates the honest answer is "one analyst, a few hours a quarter," which does not fund a program. The arithmetic only works on recurring obligations: ICD 203 review (every product, every element, annually mandated), the AWIA §2013 risk-and-resilience cycle, the daily watch tally, the KEV/EPSS triage loop, the quarterly designation-impact memo. Lead with those denominators or the cost claim dies in the first hour.

**e) The abstention gate is undersold as a technical metric when it is a career-protection argument.** "Measured false-abstention rate on a benchmark" is a graph. "It tells you when not to answer" is a chief's problem — being on the record with a confident wrong call is the thing that ends analytic careers, and no incumbent offers it. Reframe. Likewise, likelihood/confidence separation is presented as architectural virtue when it is really a *mandated compliance check nobody performs*.

**f) TRL is inflated across the board by counting the platform instead of the domain pack.** Almost every candidate claims TRL 4 on the strength of the existing kernel and 663 tests. That is the platform's TRL. The adapter, the corpus, the actor configs and the cost/likelihood tables are TRL 2–3 everywhere without exception. A government evaluator will draw this line for you if you don't. Ship two columns.

**g) Every historical backtest in the catalog implicitly re-grades the customer's own analysts.** Niger 2023, Ukraine 2022, Pokhran-II 1998, Aliquippa — the IC made calls at the time and some of them are documented. If the model beats them, the proposal is saying "your people were wrong," in a room containing people who were there. Backtest against *public* baselines (persistence, base rate, INFER/Metaculus crowd) and never against the community's own historical calls in the bid.

**h) The US-domestic consequence candidates are good products for the wrong buyer.** C-CYBER-2 (US water), C-CYBER-7 (US ports), C-CYBER-8 (US hospitals), and the US-receiving branch of C-GEO-7 are strong for CISA, TRANSCOM, HHS and DHS, and out of mission for the office that issued this solicitation. Present them as adjacent-buyer expansion in a separate annex, not as core capability — mixing them in signals you don't know whose building you're in. Related and confirmed this session: **EPA's water-sector cyber lever is advisory, not regulatory**, so C-CYBER-2's consequence number lands on a municipal utility with no mandate and no money, through an agency with no compulsion authority. The AWIA §2013 recurring-assessment argument is the only durable business case there.

**i) The user labels are named at the wrong altitude, and the catalog admits several are unverified.** "CIA Directorate of Analysis Iran-focused cyber/ICS analysts" describes people, not a requirement owner. The requirement owner is a program manager with a budget line — an analytic modernization office, a mission-center resource manager, a CIO. Zero of those appear in sixty candidates. And since the catalog itself flags specific CIA mission-center and directorate names as UNVERIFIED, **remove them entirely** — nothing marks an outsider faster than getting a customer's internal org chart slightly wrong in writing.

**j) The ICD 203 audit will fail on Sourcing and Argumentation, not on Uncertainty.** Every candidate handles likelihood/confidence well. Two gaps nobody addresses: (1) an auditor will demand a sourcing statement on every model *parameter*, not just every evidence item — no candidate treats parameters as sourced claims, and several (C-CYBER-1's inventory prior, C-ADV-8's rung-skip, C-TRADECRAFT-3's observable costs) have parameters with no possible source; (2) Standard 6 wants an *argument*, not a number, and a run artifact is not an argument. Every candidate needs a prose reasoning chain traceable to the kernel's sensitivity output, or it fails a review it should pass.

**k) The red-line story is a type-system argument, which is the right argument, and it has never been tested against an accreditor.** "The schema has no natural-person field, so the run fails at config load" is genuinely stronger than a prompt instruction and is the best legal argument in the document. But no security or privacy reviewer has seen it. One artifact — a schema diff plus a failing test showing the config load rejection — is worth more in the bid than every TRL number combined.

**l) Six deliberately-bad candidates is padding, not discipline.** Every lens produced exactly one, all with the same structure (no kernel / unscorable / person-level). One or two, argued hard, reads as judgment. Six reads as a template. Keep C-TRADECRAFT-10 (because it is the one whose construction damages the others) and C-CYBER-10 (because refusing the question the customer will actually ask is the differentiator), and fold the rest into a single paragraph.

## 5. What is missing entirely

From the mission-reality axis, these are the candidates nobody generated and should have. Several are more buyable than most of what is here.

1. **The standing-product refresh engine.** The largest analyst-hour sink in any office is not novel analysis — it is the recurring serialized product, where 80% of the text is unchanged and someone re-reads everything to find the 20%. "These three things changed since your last edition, and here is what they do to your standing judgment" would be bought instantly by every office with a serialized line. It has a clean oracle (did the judgment actually need to change?), it is the purest benefit-(d) case available, and its denominator is enormous. Its absence from a catalog claiming to automate repetitive analysis is the most conspicuous gap in the document.

2. **Coordination-comment adjudication.** A draft goes out and comes back with forty comments from six agencies, many mutually contradictory, and someone spends days reconciling them and drafting the dissent footnotes. This is C-GEO-8's exact math pointed at a corpus of comments instead of sources, and it lands on a named, mandated, universally-hated process with a hard deadline. It is arguably a better product than C-GEO-8 itself.

3. **Assumption-drift monitoring across the live product line.** ICD 203 requires key assumptions; the catalog rightly requires a falsifier on each. Nobody anywhere monitors whether a falsifier has *fired*. A standing watch over the assumption set of every live product, alerting when a falsifier condition is met, is directly actionable, trivially scorable, uses machinery already in the platform, and converts the falsifier field from a compliance box into an operating capability.

4. **Requirement-to-question decomposition as a product.** A policymaker asks "what is Iran's strategy?" Someone has to turn that into resolvable sub-questions, and that translation is where most analytic effort is wasted and most estimative failure originates. The Framer stage already does this. Nobody proposed it as a standalone capability, even though it is the platform's own first stage and the thing analysts complain about most.

5. **Denied-area estimation where the measurement filter is the modeled object.** Six lenses independently note that their data undercount closed societies — DPRK, Iran internals, Eritrea, Turkmenistan — and then proceed anyway. Nobody proposed the candidate that models "what would we observe if X were true, given what we can observe at all." That is the hardest problem on the hardest targets and precisely where a mission center most wants help; the catalog's own thinning models (C-CYBER-6, C-IW-1) are the right machinery pointed at the wrong question.

6. **Crisis-surge operations.** Every candidate assumes deliberate-cycle timing. Real mission centers fail during the first 72 hours of a crisis, and the failure mode is throughput, not accuracy — contradictions unnoticed, standing assumptions silently invalidated, the same question answered three ways by three shifts. A capability explicitly built for surge tempo would be bought on the strength of the last crisis alone, by a chief who remembers it.

7. **Releasability and tearline generation.** Not one candidate in sixty mentions REL TO, tearlines, or partner-releasable output modes, despite a large fraction of real analytic exchange being Five Eyes and NATO. A system that generates a defensible partner-releasable version from a classified run — with provenance preserved and the withheld basis auditable — is worth more than several of the forecasting candidates put together, and it is a natural fit for a type system that already tracks provenance per claim.

8. **Deception-aware collection tasking as the product, not as two components.** C-TRADECRAFT-3 (which observables are cheap to fake) and C-TRADECRAFT-5 (which observable most reduces uncertainty) are proposed as a judge and a dependency. Composed, they answer the question a collection manager actually has — *which observable is both diagnostic and hard for the adversary to fabricate* — and that is a better product than either alone.

9. **The consumer-facing view.** Everything here serves the analyst or the reviewer. The policymaker's real complaint is that they cannot tell what changed since last time, what is genuinely contested, and what would change the answer. A "what would have to be true" overlay on an existing product is smaller, cheaper, and more likely to generate the pull that funds everything else.

10. **Visible in-house competition as an adoption mechanism.** C-TRADECRAFT-8 measures the machine against forecasters in a tournament. The missing organizational version: the machine posts to the office's *own* question board, before the analysts, on the office's real questions, and the office watches. Trust in a mission center is not won by a Brier score in a report; it is won by being visibly right in front of the people who bet against you. That is a deployment design, not a research design, and no candidate in this catalog has one.

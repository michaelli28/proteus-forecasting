# IARPA Program Archaeology: Forecasting, Anticipatory Intelligence, and Analytic Tradecraft

**Scope:** every IARPA program bearing on forecasting, anticipatory intelligence, reasoning/argumentation, crowdsourcing, bias mitigation, and open-source analysis, from SCIL (2008) through the Emerging Technology Accelerator releases of 29 April 2026. Assembled from IARPA program pages, program BAAs, performer publications, and independent T&E documentation.

**Verification convention used throughout:**
- **CONFIRMED** — read this session from a cited primary source (IARPA page, BAA, peer-reviewed paper, performer report).
- **UNVERIFIED** — could not be established from an open source; stated as unknown rather than dropped or asserted.
- Evidence class for every measured claim is named: *rubric score by human raters*, *proper score against resolved real-world events*, *proper score against a simulation with known ground truth*, or *self-reported by performer*.

## 0. Orientation: one program manager owns almost all of this

Dr. **Steven Rieber** joined IARPA in 2014 and has led **REASON, ACE, CREATE, FOCUS, MOSAIC, and HFC** — and now BENGAL. His stated research focus is "forecasting and rational judgment and decision-making." [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/who-we-are/program-managers]

This is the single most operationally useful fact in this report. The entire IARPA line of effort on judgment, forecasting, argumentation, and analytic quality — 2010 to today — runs through one desk. ACE's founding PM was **Jason Gaverick Matheny**, later IARPA Director [CONFIRMED — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregative_Contingent_Estimation_Program]; Rieber inherited and extended the portfolio. Most other program pages now list only "Main Office" rather than a named PM [CONFIRMED — observed across iarpa.gov/research-programs/*].

IARPA's own methodological doctrine is also worth quoting, because it defines the evidentiary bar this customer applies: IARPA "holds back roughly one-quarter of a program's budget for an independent testing and evaluation (T&E) team that confirms the results reported by the performers," and program managers are "encouraged to abandon metrics when it is shown that uncertainty cannot be rigorously evaluated by the original metric." [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/images/pdfs/GSS.pdf]

## 2. The programs that matter most, in depth

### 2.1 ACE — the foundational result, and its structural blind spot

**What was built.** A four-season forecasting tournament (2011–2015), five university-led research teams plus an IARPA control group, ~100–150 questions per year, all forecasts scored by daily Brier score against resolved real-world outcomes. IARPA's stated bar: beat the control crowd's combined forecast by **20% in year 1, rising to 50% by year 4** [CONFIRMED — Tetlock & Gardner, *Superforecasting*, p.69, quoted at https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project-an-accompanying-blog-post/; corroborated contemporaneously by a performer institution: "20 percent increase... Years two, three and four will aim for 35-, 50-, and 50+ percentage point increases" — https://www.socsci.uci.edu/newsevents/news/2011/2011-08-08-you-be-the-expert.php].

**What was measured, and the headline.** The Good Judgment Project beat the control by **60% in Year 1 and 78% in Year 2** — i.e., it cleared the four-year target in year two — and beat rival university teams by 30–70%. The simple median of superforecasters' judgments was **35–72% more accurate than any other research team's aggregate**, and virtually indistinguishable from GJP's best algorithm running on 1,200 additional forecasters. [CONFIRMED — https://goodjudgment.com/resources/the-superforecasters-track-record/the-first-championship-season/; https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/]

**Mechanism decomposition** — this is the part that matters for us, because it says *why* it worked:

- **Training, teaming, tracking.** A ~45-minute probability-training module improved Brier scores by 6–11% and the effect persisted across all four years. Teams beat crowd-belief forecasters, who beat independents. Placing the top 2% of Year 1 into elite teams produced forecasters who "outperformed all other groups by a wide margin," with no regression to the mean. Critically, **both calibration and resolution improved**, and the superforecaster edge was **largely resolution** — appropriate decisiveness, not hedging. [CONFIRMED — Mellers et al., *Psychological Science* 25(5):1106–1115, https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2014---psychological-strategies-for-winning-a-tournament.pdf]
- **The aggregator.** Three ingredients: temporal subsetting (use only the most recent *k*% of forecasts — *k* = 20% for sub-elite teams, 53–73% for superpolls), performance weighting (the most accurate forecaster received **~13–16× the weight of the median forecaster**), and **extremization** (a = 1.5 for sub-elite, 1.32 for elite — less extremization for better crowds). [CONFIRMED — Atanasov, Witkowski, Mellers & Tetlock, *International Journal of Forecasting* 41(2):580–595, preprint at https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Crowd-Prediction-Systems.pdf]
- **Composition beats algorithm.** Elite forecasters improved aggregate accuracy by **21%** over sub-elite; the choice of system (market vs. poll) was **statistically tied**; and "the simple unweighted linear opinion pool of small, elite crowds outperformed the full aggregation algorithm of large, sub-elite crowds, yielding **15% lower Brier scores**." [CONFIRMED — same source]
- **What aggregation actually fixes.** The Bias–Information–Noise decomposition: "Simple averaging works almost entirely by reducing noise, whereas more complex techniques, like prediction markets and Bayesian aggregators, work via all three pathways: better signal extraction as well as noise and bias reduction." [CONFIRMED — Satopää, Salikhov, Tetlock & Mellers, https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/SSRN-id3781405.pdf]
- **An independent replication of the aggregation lever.** Draper Lab's ACE team, on 64 year-1 questions, went from mean Brier **0.40 (unweighted linear opinion pool) to 0.30 (heuristic weighted aggregation) — a 28% improvement**, using only confidence, cognitive-style score, and update frequency. [CONFIRMED — http://www.cliftonforlines.com/papers/2012_Forlines_HIFA.pdf]

**Why it ended.** Program concluded on schedule in 2015 [CONFIRMED — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregative_Contingent_Estimation_Program]. It did not end because it failed.

**The gap it left open.** Three, and they are the load-bearing ones for our positioning:

1. **Questions were curated to be answerable.** ACE questions "were carefully chosen so that a reasonable answer would be somewhere between 10% and 90%." [CONFIRMED — https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project-an-accompanying-blog-post/] The tournament, by construction, never contained a question the evidence could not support. **The abstention problem was designed out of the benchmark.**
2. **Proper scoring rules make silence impossible.** Brier and log scores require a number on every question, every day. There is no ACE-lineage system in which "INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE" is an emittable verdict, let alone a scored one.
3. **It required a standing crowd.** The result is a property of hundreds-to-thousands of trained humans updating over months. It does not survive being asked a new question at 0300 with no crowd standing by.

### 2.2 HFC — the machine-augmentation ceiling, quantified

**What was built.** SAGE (Synergistic Anticipation of Geopolitical Events) at USC ISI, plus a Raytheon BBN system, evaluated by MITRE using **randomized controlled trials against a control platform with a separate sample of 538 forecasters**. [CONFIRMED — Benjamin et al., *AI Magazine* 44:112–128, https://cs.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/forecasting-aimag23.pdf]

**What IARPA claims.** "Beat the state of the art for human-only forecast systems with a **10 percent increase in accuracy over baseline**"; "Conducted foundational research on human trust of machine models." Program "completed nearly three years of research in May 2020." [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hfc]

**What the performers measured (RCT-B, 1,085 users, 398 questions, 8 months):**

- Best official SAGE method vs. control: **Cohen's *d* = 0.126**, Brier score difference **0.0333**.
- Backcast decomposition of that 0.0333: applying SAGE's human-aggregation algorithm to the *control's* human forecasts recovers 0.01 (~**31%** of the gap); applying it to control + SAGE forecasters recovers 0.032 (~**97%**); adding machine models at aggregation rounds out the last 3%.
- "The distinguishing feature of the best-performing human aggregation model was that it **extremized aggregate forecasts less**, especially at the start of the season."
- Forecasters who could see model projections produced aggregates with **6% better Brier scores**; injecting model estimates at the aggregation stage gave **2–3%**.
- **Only skilled forecasters benefited** from model access. Best condition: skilled forecasters with data charts *and* model predictions, zBrier −0.618 vs. −0.545 control.
- The real value of the machine side was **robustness to sparsity**: "including model forecasts insulated the aggregate forecast against the negative effects of sparse human judgments that would occur when scaling to large numbers of questions."
- A question-recommender that assigned forecasters where they were most needed produced **up to a 7% relative Brier reduction**.

[All CONFIRMED — https://cs.stanford.edu/~jure/pubs/forecasting-aimag23.pdf]

**The gap it left open.** The "machine" half of the hybrid was statistical/time-series modeling on historical data. The paper is explicit about its limits: machine methods "are ill-suited to forecast rare or idiosyncratic events for which such data may not exist, or when the underlying context has changed in ways not reflected by the historical data." **HFC never built a mechanism model.** It never simulated an actor's decision process; it extrapolated series. And after three years and an RCT, the machine contribution beyond better human aggregation was ~3% of a 0.033 Brier gap. The scalability finding — machines let you cover more questions with fewer humans — is the strongest thing HFC established, and it is a resource argument, not an accuracy argument.

### 2.3 GFC and GFC2 — the "nobody won" result

GFC1 ran Feb–Sep 2018 in parallel with HFC on a largely overlapping question set, 165 questions, 17 teams, $200,000. The baseline was explicitly "a method based on the state-of-the-art from the IARPA ACE Program": a weighted average over recent forecasters, weighted by historical accuracy, update frequency, and training completion, then extremized. Scoring was Net Brier Points against that baseline. JHU/APL's "DigitalDelphi" won overall and the elections sub-challenge. [CONFIRMED — https://www.herox.com/IARPAGFChallenge; https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/181108-johns-hopkins-apl-wins-iarpa-geopolitical-forecasting-challenge]

**The result IARPA published that nobody cites:** "**Ultimate Forecaster: no winner.**" And: "We are pleased to announce a new tier of 'Second Chance' Prizes that may be awarded in the event that **fewer than 5 methods qualify** for Overall Prizes... We recognize that we set an extremely high bar." [CONFIRMED — https://www.herox.com/IARPAGFChallenge]

GFC2 ran May 2019 – Jan 2020, 304 resolved questions, $250,000, winners *cassandre*, *datscilly*, *michalbod*, *Ckar*, *EagleWings*. [CONFIRMED — https://www.herox.com/IARPAGFChallenge2]

**The gap it left open — and the most under-exploited IARPA dataset in existence.** The GFC2 corpus was later mined by Karvetski, Meinel, Maxwell, Lu, Mellers & Tetlock: **399 questions, 485 forecasters, 27,507 written rationales**. The mean accuracy difference from the consensus across all 485 forecasters was M = −.01, confirming the ACE-style consensus is a genuinely hard benchmark. But the *text* of the rationale predicted accuracy: **use of a comparison class** (outside-view / base-rate reasoning) and **integrative and dialectical complexity** each correlated with accuracy more strongly than word count, with comparison-class use at r = .32 in the GJP corpus. [CONFIRMED — https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/SSRN-id3779404.pdf]

Nobody turned that into a product. IARPA proved that *the structure of the justification predicts the accuracy of the judgment* and then measured it with an NLP classifier applied post hoc. A system that makes comparison classes, competing hypotheses, and falsifiers **structurally required outputs rather than latent textual properties** is the obvious next move and has never been funded as such.

### 2.4 CREATE — the strongest evidence that structured adjudication moves reasoning quality, with a hard caveat

**What was built.** Four competing platforms plus two Google-Docs control systems (Conclude for individuals, Concur for teams), all integrated into a common evaluation environment run by **JHU/APL and Good Judgment Inc.** Volunteers received problems and chose a system. [CONFIRMED — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.01541]

- **SWARM** (Melbourne) — "Contending Analyses": emergent teams of 5–50 develop rival takes, refine, and vote.
- **BARD** (Monash + UCL + Birkbeck + Strathclyde) — causal Bayesian networks built by small groups via a **Real-Time Delphi** protocol: private answer first, then reveal, then revise. Anonymity and moderated discussion by design, "to avoid common pitfalls of group deliberation, e.g., overweighting the first opinion expressed."
- **Co-Arg** (George Mason, Tecuci) — Wigmorean evidence networks with Baconian probabilities, on a scale explicitly "a refinement of the scale provided in **Intelligence Community Directive 203**."
- **TRACE** (Syracuse) — a flexible toolkit whose report template maps to ICD 203 sections: information evaluation, judgment, justification, assumptions, alternative hypotheses, information gaps.

**The program's own metric.** "CREATE... had specified at the outset its own performance goals for **'Quality of Reasoning'** to be achieved by the end of each of its three Phases: Cohen's *d* (pooled) of at least **0.25 (small), 0.5 (medium), and 1.0 (large)** respectively." For calibration, a meta-analysis of argument mapping found a full university semester bought ~0.6 *d*. [CONFIRMED — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01054/full]

**Results:**

- **BARD, individuals:** SoloBARD (N=29) vs. controls with generic training and Microsoft Office (N=30): **Glass' Δ 0.8 and 1.6** on the two scoring measures.
- **BARD, teams:** 25 teams of 6–9 (N=198) vs. 58 individual controls using Google Suite plus the best available pen-and-paper probability techniques, three problems over 5 weeks, blind external raters against problem-specific rubrics: **Glass' Δ 1.4–2.2, "greatly exceeding CREATE's initial target."** [CONFIRMED — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9290058/]
- **SWARM:** "improved the quality of analysis by **over one standard deviation**," and Melbourne states it "was the only performer team to be selected for Phase Two." [CONFIRMED as a claim — https://huntlab.science.unimelb.edu.au/home/swarm/] **Note a tension:** Monash's own account says IARPA judged *d* ≥ 0.25 sufficient "to warrant further funding and development (Phase 2)" for BARD. These two performer accounts of who continued are not obviously reconcilable from open sources — **UNVERIFIED which teams continued past Phase 1.**
- **Operational proof point:** SWARM coordinated ~100 researchers from eight Australian universities over three weeks to produce "Roadmap to Recovery," delivered to Australia's health minister and chief medical officer in April 2020. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/create]

**The caveat that we must carry, not hide.** Syracuse's TRACE experiment (N=356 valid participants, three conditions) compared unaided reasoning, an ordered ACH-style technique they call Evidence Alignment Assessment, and flexible TRACE. Result: **TRACE β = 0.61, p < 0.001 vs. control; EAA β = 0.10, p < 0.05.** "The more flexible analytic technique, TRACE, significantly outperforms the structured EAA, and **EAA only modestly outperforms the control condition of unaided reasoning**." [CONFIRMED — https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/272733/1/272733.pdf, published in *Intelligence and National Security* 36(2):279–298]

That is a peer-reviewed, IARPA-funded finding that a rigid, ordered, ACH-shaped procedure barely beats doing nothing. Our adjudication panel uses ACH. We should present ACH as *one diagnosticity-weighting mechanism inside a larger panel*, be prepared to show its ablation contribution separately, and cite Stromer-Galley ourselves before a reviewer does.

**Why it ended.** "The CREATE program ended in February 2020." [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/create]

**The gap it left open.** CREATE measured **reasoning quality only** — rubric scores by blind human raters against problem-specific marking rubrics. It never measured whether CREATE-produced judgments were more *accurate* about the world. And every result required humans working for hours to weeks. There is no CREATE artifact that produces an ICD-203-conformant product without a team of people and a facilitator.

### 2.5 REASON — the government's current, explicit statement of the quality problem

**Design.** Three task areas: TA1 find additional supporting *and contrary* evidence; TA2 identify reasoning strengths and weaknesses; TA3 produce comments that raise argumentation quality. Explicitly framed as "an automated grammar check, but for evidence and reasoning." Explicitly **not** to write the report or do the analysis. [CONFIRMED — https://arl.devcom.army.mil/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2023/04/W911NF-23-S-0007-REASON-BAA-Final-17-March-2023.pdf]

**The metrics — read these carefully, they are a specification we should target:**

| Metric | Phase 1 target | Phase 2 target |
|---|---|---|
| TA1 α-nDCG (evidence discovery) | > 0.25 | > 0.40 |
| TA2 Reasoning Explanation Quality (1–4 scale) | > 2.75 | > 3.5 |
| TA2 F1 on strengths/weaknesses | > 0.65 | > 0.80 |
| TA3 REASON Comment Quality (1–4) | > 2.75 | > 3.5 |
| TA3 **ΔReport Quality Score** | **> 1.5** | **> 3.0** |

**RQS is scored on six IC Analytic Tradecraft Standards — sourcing, uncertainty, assumptions, alternatives, logic, accuracy — each 1 (poor) to 4 (excellent), total range 6–24, compared to a control group.** Final exams use real cleared intelligence analysts in Phase 2. Systems "may only cite items in the corpus." [CONFIRMED — same BAA]

**Schedule:** 42 months. Phase 1 Oct 1 2023 – Sep 30 2025 (unclassified testing, with parallel classified retraining by T&E). Phase 2 Oct 1 2025 – Mar 31 2027 (classified data, cleared analysts). [CONFIRMED — same BAA]

**Performers: not published. UNVERIFIED.** Public Proposers' Day capability statements identify at least two hopefuls: George Mason's Learning Agents Center (Tecuci's "Cogent REASON," with explicit automated detection of confirmation bias, satisficing bias, and "absence of evidence bias") and the University of Dundee's Centre for Argument Technology (which discloses $2.5M in prior IARPA funding and a human–agent critique prototype). [CONFIRMED as capability statements — https://www.iarpa.gov/images/PropsersDayPDFs/REASON/33_Tecuci_GMU_IARPA.pdf; https://www.iarpa.gov/images/PropsersDayPDFs/REASON/ARGtech-Capability-Statement-v3_1.pdf]

**The gap it leaves open — and it is the widest one in the portfolio.** REASON is a **critic of a human's draft**. It does not generate hypotheses. It does not simulate. It does not produce a probability. It is **not scored on forecast accuracy at any point** — RQS is a rubric, not a Brier score. Rieber said so directly: "REASON doesn't aim to [produce reports] — because if we had automated production of analytic reports, that technology would have to be just about perfect." [CONFIRMED — https://defensescoop.com/2023/01/06/iarpa-to-develop-novel-ai-that-automatically-generates-tips-to-improve-intel-reports/]

That statement is our opening. The reason IARPA declined to automate production is that they had no way to *measure* whether an automated product was good enough. A system that emits a first-class abstention verdict, reports its own unsupported-assertion count, and refuses to pool evaluation regimes is precisely a proposal for how to make that measurable.

### 2.6 FOCUS — IARPA already accepted synthetic worlds as legitimate evaluation

FOCUS asked: how would history have differed if actor X had done Y on date Z? Because history cannot be re-run, "**FOCUS methods were evaluated by having hundreds of people make predictions about outcomes in complex simulations**," and performers made "numeric forecasts of the likelihood of a range of outcomes." Kairos Research's lead put the point plainly: "What's unique about the FOCUS program is that the use of simulated worlds allows researchers to **actually measure the accuracy** of counterfactual predictions." [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/focus; https://kairosresearch.com/2019/12/kairos-research-awarded-contract-to-improve-what-if-reasoning-in-intelligence-analysis/]

Performers: Charles River Analytics (RAMPAGE, with Georgia Tech, GTRI, Michigan Tech, U. Maryland — $4.5M), Raytheon BBN with Kairos, and the University of Pennsylvania. T&E: JHU/APL. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/focus; https://www.executivebiz.com/articles/iarpa-funds-charles-river-analytics-to-support-multi-path-reasoning-program]

**Public headline results: UNVERIFIED.** I could not locate published effect sizes for FOCUS Phase 2 or 3 in open sources.

**The gap.** FOCUS's methods were *cognitive procedures for humans*. Nothing automated was delivered. And the sibling literature contains the honest limitation: DARPA's Ground Truth program, running the same simulated-worlds design for social science methods, concluded that "there remains the obvious question of how to assess whether — and to what degree — a research method that performs well on a simulation test bed would perform equally as well on an associated real-world system." [CONFIRMED — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10588-021-09346-9] **That is the exact criticism our synthetic-oracle-only results will attract, from a document the customer's community wrote.** We should pre-empt it, not be surprised by it.

### 2.7 OSI / EMBERS — the provenance and scoring architecture the customer already accepted

EMBERS is the most product-shaped thing in the IARPA anticipatory-intelligence portfolio, and its *evaluation design* is worth copying wholesale.

- Operational 24×7 from Nov 2012, ~50 forecasts/day, 10 Latin American countries, forecasting the **date, city-level location, protesting population, and event type** with associated uncertainty. [CONFIRMED — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4276118/]
- Scored monthly by **MITRE** against a Gold Standard Report built by human analysts, using **precision, recall, lead time, and a 0–4 quality score** decomposed into population / event-type / date / location components, matched via bipartite matching, plus a separate confidence score assessing the imputed probabilities. [CONFIRMED — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00033]
- **Every message carries a GUID and timestamp; derived messages carry their parent's identifier; "when a warning is produced by the system, it is already connected to all of the data that produced it," and the chain is queryable in reverse.** There is a shipped **audit-trail UI** and an **ablation visualizer** letting an analyst remove a data source and watch which alerts survive. [CONFIRMED — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4276118/]
- Architecture: multiple models, each tuned for *high precision*, unioned and then fused/suppressed with a tunable knob to trade precision against recall per the analyst's objective.
- CNAS's summary of the outcome: EMBERS "achieved near-perfect accuracy in predicting occurrences, though it did not always categorize them correctly." [CONFIRMED — https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/getting-it-righter-faster]
- The team published its **misses** and an ablation study of what happens when Twitter and Facebook are removed. [CONFIRMED — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00033]

**The gap.** EMBERS answers "will there be a protest in Curitiba next Tuesday." It does not answer "what will Iran do about US water infrastructure and why." It is event detection at population scale, with no actor model, no competing-hypothesis structure, and no analytic product. It is the *plumbing* precedent, not the *reasoning* precedent.

### 2.8 SCITE — the sleeper program, and our closest procurement precedent

SCITE's second thrust, **Inference Enterprise Models**, was defined as "mathematical models that **forecast the accuracy of current and proposed automated systems** for detecting insider threats," and IARPA records as "a key accomplishment... the deployment of active indicators and IEMs by a USG trusted agent and an entity in the commercial sector." Ran March 2016 – December 2019. T&E: JHU/APL, MIT Lincoln Laboratory, and Los Alamos. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/scite]

This is the IC having already bought, evaluated, and *transitioned* the idea that **you should model and report the expected accuracy of your own analytic machinery before you trust its output**. Our metrics package — Brier skill vs. explicit baselines, ECE/MCE, coverage@N, contradiction precision/recall, unsupported-assertion rate — is the same idea applied to the analytic product rather than to a detector. SCITE is the strongest available answer to "why would they buy a system that grades itself?" Because they already did.

### 2.9 Sirius — the cheap-debiasing counterexample

Six target biases: confirmation, fundamental attribution error, bias blind spot, anchoring, representativeness, projection. Primes: Applied Research Associates, Boeing, Leidos, Raytheon BBN, SUNY Albany, University of Oklahoma. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/sirius]

Headline result (Leidos/ARA's *MISSING* games, with Morewedge et al.): a **single 30–60 minute intervention** produced bias reductions of **−31.94% and −46.25% immediately**, persisting at **−23.57% and −34.76% two-to-three months later**; videos gave −18.6%/−25.7% immediate and −19.2%/−20.1% at follow-up. Overall-bias Cohen's *d* for the game: 1.68 and 1.74 post-training, 1.11 and 1.16 at follow-up. Effects were **domain-general** — they transferred to problem formats not taught. Sample sizes were pre-declared to the sponsor, and "independent third-party analyses of the data were performed that confirmed the accuracy of our results." A separate MACBETH study ran 703 participants. [CONFIRMED — https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf]

**The counterweight.** A systematic review of retention and transfer found only four articles / six studies met inclusion criteria; only one examined transfer; conclusion: "there is **insufficient evidence** regarding the retention and transfer of bias mitigation interventions, to recommend them as useful tools to help policymakers make better decisions." [CONFIRMED — https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34638407/CxncBE/gerritsma-2019-retention.pdf]

**The gap.** Sirius debiased *the analyst*. Nobody debiased *the process*. There is no IARPA program that made devil's advocate, premortem, and key-assumptions-check into automatic, per-question, always-on machinery whose contribution is separately measured.

### 2.10 The rest, briefly

- **ICArUS** (Dec 2010 – Jun 2014) produced MITRE's "Octaloop" — a Bayesian-computational decomposition of sensemaking into eight discrete steps, built specifically because "researchers have described the cognitive challenges of sensemaking only informally using conceptual notions like framing and re-framing, which are **not sufficient to support T&E**." MITRE explicitly identified downstream uses "beyond ICArUS experiments... structured analytic techniques, training of critical thinking skills, and automated tools for improving the effectiveness of intelligence analysis." [CONFIRMED — https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA624918/index.html] Nobody built those tools. The normative Bayesian yardstick for sensemaking exists, unused, in the public domain.
- **ForeST / SciCast** (Jun 2013 – Jun 2015) built the world's largest S&T prediction market: 1,275 questions, 494 links, 11,000+ registered users. Brier 0.267 overall / 0.240 binary; **18–23% better than its own unweighted opinion pool, beating it ~7 times in 10**; predecessor DAGGRE was ~38% better than the ACE baseline Feb 2012–May 2013. The single strongest individual-level predictor of accuracy was **Actively Open-Minded Thinking (r = −0.32 with Brier)**. Paying for activity raised activity without hurting accuracy; paying for accuracy raised both. [CONFIRMED — https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/SciCast2015.pdf; https://c4i.gmu.edu/~pcosta/F15/data/fileserver/file/472076/filename/Paper_1570114025.pdf] **It shut down the moment funding lapsed:** "Our main IARPA funding has ended, and we were not able to finalize things with our (likely) new sponsor in time." [CONFIRMED — https://scicast.org/blog_subdomain/2015/06/]
- **Mercury** (Jan 2016 – Jan 2019 planned) tested whether classified foreign SIGINT could forecast military activity, civil unrest, and disease in Arabic-speaking MENA. The public Mercury Challenge required beating a **base-rate model floor** and scored lead time, precision, recall, F, and a 0–4 quality score. **Primes and results are not public — UNVERIFIED.** [CONFIRMED for design — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/mercury; https://github.com/planetmercury/mercury-challenge]
- **HIATUS** (2022–~2026) is the portfolio's best *design* precedent for a red team with standing: attribution teams and privacy teams are placed in direct competition against each other on sequestered data, and explainability is evaluated by a protocol co-developed with government partners. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/hiatus]
- **HAYSTAC** (2022–2026) sets an explicit two-sided operating point — detect 80% of anomalous activity while generating synthetic normal activity that is only 10% detectable — over a **microsimulation** of background human movement. This is IARPA specifying a false-positive/false-negative contract over synthetic ground truth. Structurally identical to what an abstention gate needs. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/haystac]
- **BENGAL** — the live one. An 18-month "super seedling," PM Rieber, T&E **Los Alamos**, primes Carnegie Mellon, Columbia, IBM, Penn, USC (IARPA's own kickoff post says **six** primes; the page lists five — minor discrepancy, UNVERIFIED which is current). Three domains: hallucination detection "while minimizing the impact on the LLM's capabilities to produce correct and/or plausible inferences"; safe information flow / sensitive-inference-by-aggregation; and **"working resiliently to improve poisoned sources — techniques to improve LLM reliability through evaluating the quality of sources, explainable techniques to infer source intentions, and extracting reliable information from biased or incomplete sources."** Deliverable: "turn-key prototype software which is independently validated by testing and evaluation partners." [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/bengal]

## 3. What IARPA has already proven, and what it never built

### 3.1 Proven — the eleven findings we should build on, not re-litigate

1. **Aggregating many independent judgments, weighted by demonstrated track record and recalibrated, beats individual experts and beats the naive crowd.** Replicated across ACE (60–78% over control), DAGGRE (38% over baseline), SciCast (18–23% over its own ULinOP), and Draper's independent aggregator (28% over ULinOP).
2. **Judgmental forecasting skill is real, measurable, and persistent.** Top 2% retained status ~70% year-over-year, no regression to the mean.
3. **Ensemble *composition* dominates ensemble *algorithm*.** Elite crowds beat sub-elite by 21%; a simple unweighted pool of a small elite crowd beat the full weighted-extremized algorithm on a large sub-elite crowd by 15%. Systems (market vs. poll) tie.
4. **Bias correction at the aggregate level is a first-class accuracy lever with a tunable optimum.** Extremization coefficients differ by pool sophistication (1.5 sub-elite vs. 1.32 elite); HFC's best aggregator extremized *less*; the BIN decomposition shows simple averaging only removes noise while better aggregators also extract information and remove bias.
5. **Both calibration and resolution are separately improvable, and resolution was the superforecaster edge.** Sharpness is not a nice-to-have metric; it is where the win came from.
6. **Machines added to a human crowd buy roughly 10% (IARPA's claim) / d = 0.126 (measured), and their real value is scalability under sparse coverage.**
7. **Structured multi-perspective adjudication moves reasoning quality by 1–2+ standard deviations against rubrics anchored to ICD 203.** BARD Δ 1.4–2.2; SWARM >1 SD; against a program bar of *d* ≥ 1.0 that IARPA itself called ambitious.
8. **Rigid, ordered SATs — including ACH-shaped ones — barely beat unaided reasoning; flexible process scaffolding beats both.** β 0.61 vs. β 0.10.
9. **Bias training works and persists 2–3 months at large effect sizes, but transfer to real decisions is unproven.**
10. **Simulated worlds with known ground truth are an accepted IC evaluation instrument for counterfactual and prediction accuracy** (FOCUS, HAYSTAC, and DARPA Ground Truth) — *with the known caveat that generalization from test bed to real system is unestablished.*
11. **You can model and report the expected accuracy of your own analytic machinery before fielding it, and the IC will transition it** (SCITE Inference Enterprise Models).

### 3.2 Never built — the twelve-item delta

**(1) Abstention as a measured, first-class output.** This is the largest single gap and the cleanest differentiator. ACE questions were pre-screened to fall in the 10–90% band. Proper scoring rules require a number on every question by construction. GFC's baseline was an extremized aggregate — the opposite of an abstention. **No IARPA program in this portfolio has ever measured a false-abstention rate or a true-abstention rate.** The only structural analogues are precision/recall over *alerts* (EMBERS, Mercury) and HAYSTAC's Pd/FAR contract — both about whether to *emit an event*, never about whether a *question is answerable from the available evidence*. An abstention gate that runs before scoring, with measured firing rates on a provably-unanswerable arm, is not an incremental improvement on the IARPA record. It is a category the record does not contain.

**(2) Reasoning quality and forecast accuracy measured on the same artifact.** ACE/HFC/GFC report Brier and never rubric-score the reasoning. CREATE/REASON rubric-score the reasoning (ICD 203) and never report a Brier score. **No IARPA program has ever reported both for the same product.** Our system emits one artifact that carries both a probability with skill scores and a provenance-audited, assumption-explicit product. That joint measurement is genuinely novel to this customer.

**(3) End-to-end automation.** Every reasoning-quality result requires humans for hours to weeks (BARD: 25 teams × 5 weeks). Every forecast-accuracy result requires a standing crowd of hundreds to thousands over months. **Nothing IARPA funded produces a calibrated, sourced, ICD-203-conformant product from a cold question with no humans in the loop.** Rieber has publicly explained why: they had no way to certify such a thing as safe.

**(4) Mechanism-level adversary simulation with a deterministic kernel.** HFC's machine half was time-series extrapolation, explicitly weak on rare, novel, or regime-changing events. ICArUS modeled the *analyst's* cognition; nothing modeled the *adversary's* decision process. **No IARPA program runs N mutually-exclusive actor profiles — parameterized on risk tolerance and escalation propensity — through a seeded numeric kernel to produce a distribution over outcome bands.** This is precisely the customer's stated worked example (Iran / US water infrastructure / adversary technoeconomic policy), and it is the one thing in the portfolio with no precedent at all.

**(5) Reproducibility as an engineering property.** IARPA reserves a quarter of program budget for independent T&E because reported results need confirming. Yet nothing in the analysis portfolio is *run-reproducible*: human crowds are irreproducible by construction, and LLM sampling is irreproducible by default. Same seed → byte-identical artifact hash is a property the T&E apparatus would immediately recognize as reducing its own cost. **No IARPA analysis program has ever offered it.**

**(6) Leakage control enforced in code.** REASON's closed-corpus rule ("Performer system responses may only cite items in the corpus") is the closest thing in the portfolio, and it is a *curation* control applied by the T&E team, not a runtime guard the system enforces on itself. **No public IARPA analysis program specifies a runtime assertion that throws if a finished product cites evidence post-dating its as-of date.** Every leakage guard in the IARPA record is procedural. Ours is executable.

**(7) A reported unsupported-assertion rate.** REASON's α-nDCG measures whether a system *finds* evidence. RQS scores *sourcing* 1–4 by human raters. **Nobody counts, per product, the fraction of claims with no provenance and reports it as a number on the product itself.** The difference between "a rater judged the sourcing a 3" and "this product contains 4 unsupported assertions out of 61 claims" is the difference between an evaluation artifact and a shipped metric.

**(8) Likelihood and confidence separated by construction.** ICD 203 requires them separate. CREATE and REASON both treat "uncertainty" as one rubric dimension scored by a human. **No IARPA system makes collapsing likelihood into confidence structurally impossible.** A type system in which no function of signature `(Judgment) => number` exists is an engineering answer to a policy requirement the customer already owns.

**(9) Evaluation-regime hygiene.** Each IARPA program lives in exactly one regime — ACE/HFC/GFC are live-post-cutoff; FOCUS and HAYSTAC are synthetic-ground-truth; CREATE is rubric-on-fixed-problems. There is consequently **no cross-program convention preventing anyone from pooling a synthetic-oracle result with a live one**, and the portfolio contains at least one place where it matters: FOCUS's simulated-world accuracy has no established mapping to real counterfactual accuracy, and DARPA's Ground Truth team said so in print. A mandatory regime tag that no aggregation function will pool is a governance artifact IARPA's own structure implies and never codified.

**(10) An automated adjudication panel with red-team standing.** CREATE tested contending analyses (SWARM) and Delphi consensus (BARD) **among humans**. Nobody automated devil's advocate, premortem, key-assumptions check, and a red team empowered to force a bounded rerun, as a per-question auto-designed panel.

**(11) Judge-severity normalization as product machinery.** CREATE and REASON both depend on human raters scoring rubrics; rater bias is handled as an *evaluation nuisance* by the T&E team. **Nobody turned z-scoring across judges, dimensional outlier detection, Krippendorff's alpha, and weakest-link confidence aggregation into runtime components of the analytic product itself.** Note specifically that weakest-link confidence contradicts the entire ACE lineage, which aggregates by weighted mean — and correctly so, because ACE aggregates *forecasts* while we aggregate *confidence in an argument*. That distinction is defensible and should be argued explicitly, not glossed.

**(12) Transition.** ACE ended in 2015 and the winning capability left government: Good Judgment Inc. was later re-imported as a *contractor* to serve as CREATE's T&E partner. SciCast shut down in June 2015 when funding lapsed. The NIC announced a crowdsourced platform in 2021. The live US capability in 2026 is **RAND's Forecasting Initiative** — formerly INFER, formerly CSET's Foretell, originally seed-funded by **Open Philanthropy**, not by an IC program of record — run by RAND with Cultivate Labs, and the cited model of success is the **UK's Cosmic Bazaar**. [CONFIRMED — https://perryworldhouse.upenn.edu/app/uploads/2025/02/PWH-2022-Forecasting-Report-June-2022.pdf; https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/rfi-blog/rand-program-update] **IARPA proved the science and did not field the system.** A Harvard assessment of IARPA reached the same structural conclusion from the inside: "Finding 1: Low-TRL Transitions Impede Long-Term Impact Tracking." [CONFIRMED — https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7530a4c1-7e19-47fa-847b-d39ecf005acb/download]

### 3.3 The delta stated in one paragraph

IARPA established, with independent T&E, that ensembling plus performance weighting plus calibration correction beats any individual analyst, and that structured multi-perspective adjudication raises ICD-203 reasoning quality by one to two standard deviations. It established both of those **with humans in the loop, on questions pre-screened to be answerable, in single evaluation regimes, with no reproducibility guarantee, no runtime leakage guard, no shipped provenance metric, and no measured path to saying "I don't know."** A config-driven simulate-and-adjudicate platform that runs the same ensemble-and-calibrate logic over a deterministic numeric kernel, gates on abstention before scoring, and reports its own regime-tagged metrics on every artifact is not a competitor to ACE or CREATE. It is the automation and the instrumentation of what they proved, aimed at the six failure modes their evaluation designs excluded by construction.

## 4. Strongest evidence FOR our approach

Ranked by how directly a program officer can use it.

1. **ACE's aggregation stack is our calibration layer, validated at IC scale.** Performance weighting, temporal recency, and recalibration/extremization *are* the ACE algorithm. We are not proposing a novel aggregation theory; we are proposing to run the government's own validated one over machine-generated ensemble members. [Atanasov et al. 2025; Mellers et al. 2014]
2. **Composition beats algorithm — which is the argument for divergent hypothesis framing.** Elite crowds beat larger sub-elite crowds by 21%, and a *simple* pool of a good ensemble beat a *sophisticated* pool of a mediocre one by 15%. This is the strongest available empirical warrant for our Framer producing N *mutually exclusive, deliberately divergent* hypotheses instead of N samples of one model. Ensemble diversity is not a vanity metric; ACE data says it is where the accuracy lives.
3. **The BIN decomposition justifies a calibration layer over a mean.** Simple averaging removes only noise. Sophisticated aggregation also removes bias and extracts information. Our z-score normalization and outlier detection are the machine analogue of what BIN says the good aggregators do. [Satopää et al.]
4. **Resolution, not calibration, was the superforecaster edge — so sharpness must be a reported metric.** Mellers 2014 is explicit that superforecaster accuracy "was in large part due to their greater resolution relative to all other groups." Our sharpness metric catching hedging is directly responsive to the strongest finding in the ACE corpus.
5. **CREATE proves the reasoning-quality ceiling is high and IARPA's own bar is calibrated.** BARD's Δ 1.4–2.2 against a program target of *d* ≥ 1.0, on blind-rated rubrics — from *causal models plus a structured multi-perspective social process*. Our panel is the automated form of exactly that architecture.
6. **REASON's metric table is the government's own written specification of the problem we solve.** ΔRQS on sourcing, uncertainty, assumptions, alternatives, logic, and accuracy — with targets of +1.5 and +3.0 on a 6–24 scale. This is a published, classified-evaluation-backed statement that the customer will pay four years and a cleared T&E team to move these six numbers.
7. **FOCUS and HAYSTAC make synthetic ground truth a familiar instrument, not an excuse.** IARPA has twice paid for evaluation against simulations with known ground truth precisely because reality could not supply the counterfactual. Our synthetic closed worlds are methodologically native to this customer.
8. **SCITE is the procurement precedent for a self-measuring system.** IEMs — forecasting the accuracy of a detection system before deployment — were built, evaluated by three national labs, and *transitioned to a USG trusted agent*.
9. **EMBERS is the precedent for shipped provenance.** Per-warning audit trails to source, an ablation visualizer, and a scoring regime built around lead time and a decomposed quality score. Our provenance-on-every-claim and artifact-hash reproducibility are the same instinct, executed with stronger guarantees.
10. **BENGAL is the live statement that our hardest sub-problems are unsolved and funded right now.** Hallucination detection with preserved inference capability, source-quality evaluation, and attribution preservation — PM Rieber, T&E Los Alamos, six primes, kickoff already held. Our unsupported-assertion metric and provenance-on-every-claim land in the middle of an active IARPA program's problem statement.
11. **"Ultimate Forecaster: no winner."** IARPA set a bar in 2018 that seventeen teams failed to clear, and had to introduce consolation prizes. The problem is open, and IARPA said so in public.
12. **Karvetski et al.: the shape of the justification predicts the accuracy of the judgment.** Comparison-class use and dialectical complexity beat word count as predictors, across 27,507 GFC2 rationales and four years of GJP data. Our product makes base rates, competing hypotheses, and required falsifiers **structural outputs** rather than latent text properties an NLP classifier has to go looking for afterwards.

## 5. Strongest evidence AGAINST needing us — state these before a reviewer does

1. **A live, funded US crowd-forecasting capability already exists and serves this customer set.** RAND's Forecasting Initiative (formerly INFER, formerly CSET Foretell), co-run with Cultivate Labs, "support[s] U.S. policymakers in governmental organizations such as the Intelligence Community, the Department of Defense, and the Department of State." 2024 alone: 32,565 forecasts, 124 questions, 66 countries, a paid Pro Forecaster cohort that "outperformed the crowd consistently," and six Pros embedded as adjuncts with RAND analysts. The UK's Cosmic Bazaar is fielded and cited as the model. [CONFIRMED — https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/about-infer; https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/rfi-blog/2024-review] **If the customer's question is "give me a calibrated probability on a resolvable geopolitical question," this is solved, staffed, and running.** Our differentiator cannot be the probability; it must be the mechanism, the abstention, the reproducibility, and the audit.
2. **REASON may deliver the quality-assurance benefit before we do.** If it hits ΔRQS > 3.0 on classified data with cleared analysts by March 2027, IARPA will have moved sourcing, uncertainty, assumptions, alternatives, logic, and accuracy inside the analyst's existing workflow — benefit category (c) — with no new system to adopt. We are proposing into an occupied lane and should say so.
3. **TRACE is a peer-reviewed warning shot at ACH.** An ordered, ACH-shaped technique beat unaided reasoning at β = 0.10 while flexible scaffolding beat it at β = 0.61. Our panel's ACH component must be presented as *one diagnosticity-weighting mechanism whose contribution is separately ablatable*, never as the source of the lift.
4. **Sirius says debiasing is cheap and already works.** A 60-minute game bought −32% to −46% bias reduction persisting three months, domain-general, independently verified. A reviewer who believes bias is a training problem will ask why the IC should buy a platform instead of an afternoon of training. The answer — that training decays, does not demonstrably transfer to live decisions (per the TNO review), and does not produce an auditable artifact — must be made, not assumed.
5. **Our headline results are self-scored against an oracle we built.** IARPA reserves ~25% of every program budget for independent T&E specifically because performer-reported results need confirming, and their Gold Standard Science document says so in plain language. 15,832 simulations against a synthetic oracle we authored is exactly the evidence class their process exists to distrust. **The correct move is to volunteer for their T&E model — propose JHU/APL or MITRE as the scorer, propose sequestered problems, propose a held-out arm we never see — not to argue around it.**
6. **Real-world forecasting skill is unmeasured, and every competing IARPA result is measured on live resolved events.** ACE, HFC, GFC1, GFC2, OSI/EMBERS, and Mercury were all scored against real outcomes by an independent T&E team. We have not been. That is the single largest evidentiary asymmetry between our claims and the government's existing record, and the first line of every run should keep saying so.
7. **Simulation-to-reality generalization is a known open problem in this customer's own literature.** DARPA's Ground Truth program — the closest methodological sibling to our synthetic closed worlds — concluded that whether a method performing well on a simulation test bed performs equally well on the associated real-world system "was out of scope" and "remains the obvious question." [CONFIRMED — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10588-021-09346-9]
8. **IARPA's 2026 posture is not pointed at us.** The five programs released 29 April 2026 under the Emerging Technology Accelerator — ARCADE, COSMIC, DECIPHER, LocUS, MOVES — are circuit design, commercial geospatial, coded language, geolocation, and clinical movement. None is forecasting, calibration, argumentation, or anticipatory intelligence. [CONFIRMED — https://www.iarpa.gov/newsroom/article/iarpa-releases-five-new-innovation-programs-to-enhance-national-security-capabilities] This cuts both ways: it is a *gap* (nobody is currently funding what we do) and a *risk signal* (the current DNI's stated ETA priorities are lean, agile, near-term capability delivery, not multi-year measurement science). Framing our proposal as a lean, config-driven, already-built prototype with rigorous performance assessment maps to the stated ETA framework far better than a four-year research program does.

## 6. Two structural observations worth carrying into the white paper

**The portfolio never asked the question we answer.** Read end to end, the IARPA analysis portfolio decomposes into three non-communicating stacks: *accuracy* (ACE, ForeST, HFC, GFC, OSI, Mercury — scored by Brier and lead time), *reasoning quality* (Sirius, ICArUS, CREATE, REASON — scored by rubric and effect size), and *trustworthiness of the machine* (TrojAI, HIATUS, BENGAL — scored by detection rates and adversarial competition). Nothing crosses. There is no artifact anywhere in twenty years of IARPA funding that carries a Brier skill score, an ICD-203 rubric score, and a provenance completeness count at the same time — because no program was ever chartered to produce one.

**The one thing the portfolio consistently failed at was keeping what it built.** SciCast shut down within weeks of funding lapsing. CREATE's four platforms ended in February 2020 with no known IC deployment. ACE's winning capability became a private company and returned as a subcontractor. The live US forecasting platform in 2026 traces to a philanthropic grant, not an IC program. IARPA's own internal lessons-learned name the mechanism: low-TRL transitions impede impact. **A working prototype with 663 tests, byte-reproducible artifacts, and everything expressed as config — where a new scenario is a config file, not new code — is an answer to IARPA's most durable institutional failure, and that argument is available to us for free.**

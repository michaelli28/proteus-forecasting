# Proteus dashboard — design brief

## Design Context

### Users

Government and agency evaluators reviewing a whitepaper submission — technical
evaluators, program managers, and national-security decision-makers. They read
on screen and print figures. They are smart but time-pressed and are NOT the
engineers who built this; the interface must be understandable at a glance
without domain jargon.

### Use cases

- Grasp, in one screen, what Proteus is: it forecasts national-security
  questions, red-teams the adversary's decisions, and ranks where systems are
  most exposed and which defenses are worth the money.
- Read the water-infrastructure worked example: biggest exposures, defense
  value-for-money, honest uncertainty.
- See that the method generalizes beyond one domain (cyber-physical, influence
  operations, insider recruitment, economic and geopolitical coercion).
- Support a formal whitepaper — credible, quotable, screenshot-clean.

### Brand personality

Institutional, plainspoken, rigorous. Calm and credible, never flashy. Three
words: **credible, plain, exact.** It should read like a well-designed federal
report, not a startup landing page or an AI demo.

### Aesthetic Direction

- Light, single theme. Near-white ground `#FBFCFD`, white cards, ink text.
- Navy `#1B2A4A` for structure, slate grays for secondary text and lines, a
  single restrained blue accent `#2563A8`. Semantic: green `#2E7D57` (good /
  robust), rust `#B4532A` (harm / warning).
- Fonts: **Spectral** (serif) for headings — institutional gravitas; **Public
  Sans** (the US federal typeface) for body, UI, and tabular data.
- Anti-references: the old dark forest-green + cream theme; cyan-on-dark AI
  dashboards; gradient text; glassmorphism; accent side-stripes; jargon.
- Motion: subtle Framer Motion — fade/rise on scroll, one-shot number count-ups,
  chart draw-in. 200–400ms, ease-out, no bounce. Respect reduced-motion.

### Design Principles

1. **Plain first.** Every heading and label is something a non-engineer
   understands. Technical terms get a one-line gloss or are cut.
2. **The exceptional state gets the ink.** A pill, color, or mark renders only
   when it differs from its neighbors — misleading "all green" surfaces are the
   first thing to remove.
3. **Deletion test.** If removing a badge, caption, or number loses the reader
   nothing, it does not render.
4. **Data carries the color.** 60% neutral surface, 30% slate structure, 10%
   accent. The accent is rare on purpose.
5. **Honest about maturity.** Live worked examples, modeled domains, and
   designed-but-unbuilt domains are labeled as what they are — never dressed up
   as more than they are.

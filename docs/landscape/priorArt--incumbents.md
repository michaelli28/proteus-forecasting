# Competitive Landscape: Who Already Sells Simulate-and-Adjudicate to the IC

**Scope:** commercial, FFRDC/UARC, and government-internal capabilities a CIA white paper on config-driven simulate-and-adjudicate would be scored against. Research window: web sources through August 2026. Every factual claim carries a URL. Claims I could not verify are labeled **UNVERIFIED**. Where I am reasoning rather than citing, I label it **INFERRED**.

---

## 1. Bottom line up front

1. **Nobody in the defense/IC vendor market sells a measured abstention rate.** I found zero vendors — primes, platforms, startups, or FFRDCs — advertising "insufficient evidence" as a first-class, *measured* output. The mechanism exists only in open-source RAG kernels ([attestq](https://vinayvobbili.github.io/posts/attestq-evidence-grounded-questionnaires/), [cautious-rag](https://github.com/alp-oz/cautious-rag), [SURE-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03534v1)) and one finance startup ([KnowYourCompany.ai](https://www.knowyourcompany.ai/blog/we-taught-our-ai-to-say-i-dont-know)). **This is the strongest differentiator in the deck.**

2. **"Measured Brier score" is NOT a differentiator — and claiming it as one is the fastest way to lose.** A NATO-adjacent vendor, [Logicon](https://logicon.ro/), publicly advertises **Brier 0.106 / AUC 0.95 on 480 resolved real-world predictions** with isotonic calibration and drift detection. [Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Superforecasters-A-Decade-of-Stochastic-Dominance.pdf) publishes 0.166 vs 0.259 Brier over 108 questions. The [RAND Forecasting Initiative](https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/faq-2022) and [Cultivate Labs](https://www.cultivatelabs.com/support/forecasts/running-a-pilot/assessing-accuracy) publish Brier scores *and* calibration curves on live policy questions. **All of them have real-world resolution; you have synthetic oracle only.** On this axis you are currently behind, not ahead.

3. **ICD 203 likelihood/confidence separation is real but shallow as a moat.** No major platform vendor ships it as enforced structure; it exists in [CIS CTI practice](https://www.cisecurity.org/ms-isac/services/words-of-estimative-probability-analytic-confidences-and-structured-analytic-techniques), [OpenCTI](https://docs.opencti.io/latest/usage/reliability-confidence/), and hobby tools ([Intel Workbench](https://solomonneas.dev/projects/intel-workbench), [Treadstone 71 IS2](https://www.treadstone71.com/is2)). It is a two-week feature for Palantir. Claim it as a *demonstration of tradecraft literacy*, not a moat.

4. **The closest single competitor is Johns Hopkins APL's GenWar Lab** — a UARC, not a company, already building **classified versions for DoD and the IC**, explicitly chasing "greater rigor and repeatability," physics-based traceable adjudication via AFSIM, adversary personas, and alternative-futures generation ([JHU APL, Oct 2025](https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/251030-genwar-lab)). They own the words "rigor," "repeatability," and "traceable."

5. **The market for "AI wargaming / adversary decision simulation" got crowded in the last 14 months.** New entrants since early 2025: [Anadyr Horizon](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260508450055/en/Anadyr-Horizon-Launches-Red-Horizon-the-First-Decision-Infrastructure-for-Strategic-Crisis-Simulation) (Red Horizon, May 2026), [Smack Technologies](https://smacktechnologies.com/journal/smack-announces-32m-in-funding-to-build-the-first-frontier-ai-lab-for-national-security) ($32M, deep-RL synthetic warfare), [Worldscape](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/worldscape-raises-10-million-seed-extension-to-accelerate-ai-native-mission-engineering-302844185.html) ($10M ext., Aug 2026), [Agon](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/first-look-agon-emerges-from-stealth-with-30m-in-funding/) ($30M), [DecisionAdvantage.ai](https://www.decisionadvantage.ai/defense), [ScenarioAI](https://scenarioai.tech/). The category label is now noise; the metrics are the only signal.

6. **The single biggest business risk is not a competitor — it is the Anthropic ban.** On 27 Feb 2026 the President ordered federal agencies to stop using Anthropic technology and SecDef Hegseth designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk under 10 U.S.C. §3252, stating "no contractor, supplier, or partner that does business with the United States military may conduct any commercial activity with Anthropic" ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-orders-federal-agencies-to-stop-using-anthropic-tech-over-ai-safety-dispute), [Mayer Brown client alert](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/pentagon-designates-anthropic-a-supply-chain-risk-what-government-contractors-need-to-know), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/president-trump-orders-federal-agencies-to-stop-using-anthropic-after-pentagon-dispute/)). Anthropic filed suit 9 March 2026; status unresolved as of this research. **If the prototype's LLM layer is Claude-shaped, the white paper must lead with model-agnosticism, not mention it in an appendix.**

7. **DoD/ODNI is currently buying exactly your metrics package as a separate product.** The DIU/ODNI **MYSTIC DEPOT** solicitation (11 March 2026, responses due 24 March) seeks "an evaluation harness and government-specific benchmarks that together enable rigorous, reproducible, vendor-agnostic assessment of any AI system," including "operational stress and network degradation in a controlled, reproducible environment," gaming-resistant benchmarks, and human-vs-AI-vs-team comparisons ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/03/11/ai-system-testing-dod-intelligence-agencies/), [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/03/12/pentagon-seeks-system-to-ensure-ai-models-work-as-planned/), [Inside Defense](https://insidedefense.com/insider/diu-seeks-evaluation-infrastructure-solutions-ai-capabilities)). This is both a validation of your framing and a second door.

## 3. Segment A — Platform incumbents

### Palantir (Gotham, Foundry, AIP, Maven)

**What they sell.** AIP is an LLM/agent layer bound to the Ontology — a "living map of data, logic and processes" — with four builder surfaces: AIP Logic (no-code LLM workflows over Ontology objects), AIP Chatbot/Agent Studio (multi-agent orchestration), Actions, and AIP Evals ([Palantir docs](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/aip/overview), [platform overview](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/platform-overview/aip-capabilities)).

**To whom, on what contract.**

- U.S. Army Enterprise Service Agreement, 31 July 2025: **up to $10B over 10 years**, consolidating 75 contracts (15 prime + 60 sub) into one vehicle ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/287506/u_s_army_awards_enterprise_service_agreement_to_enhance_military_readiness_and_drive_operational_efficiency), [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/08/army-consolidates-dozens-of-palantir-software-contracts-into-one-deal-worth-up-to-10-billion/), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-army-pools-contracts-into-up-10-billion-palantir-deal-2025-07-31/)).
- Maven Smart System designated a **program of record** in DepSecDef Feinberg's 9 March 2026 memo; oversight moved from NGA to CDAO within 30 days ([Reuters, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.reuters.com/technology/pentagon-adopt-palantir-ai-as-core-us-military-system-memo-says-2026-03-20/)). Prior Maven awards: $480M (2024), ~$100M follow-on, up to $795M 2025 modification ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/feature/2026/03/22/pentagon-expands-palantirs-role-ai-contract.html)).
- **Palantir–Anthropic–AWS:** Nov 2024, Palantir became the first industry partner to bring Claude into a classified (IL6, up to SECRET) environment ([Nasdaq/press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/anthropic-and-palantir-partner-bring-claude-ai-models-aws-us-government-intelligence)); April 2025 Anthropic joined Palantir FedStart for FedRAMP High / IL5 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250417172108/en/Anthropic-Joins-Palantirs-FedStart-Program-to-Deploy-Claude-Application)). **Status of that deployment after the Feb 2026 Anthropic ban is UNVERIFIED** — I found no source confirming whether the IL6 Claude deployment was terminated.

**Technical approach to evaluation — the part that matters to you.** AIP Evals is "specifically designed to help you deal with the non-deterministic nature of LLMs." It provides test cases, built-in **deterministic reference-less evaluators** (string length, regex, keyword, ROUGE), exact-match evaluators, **LLM-as-a-judge** evaluators, custom pro-code evaluators, per-metric pass thresholds, and the ability to "examine variance across multiple runs" by configuring N iterations per test case ([overview](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/aip-evals/overview/), [create-suite docs](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/aip-evals/create-suite/), [Palantir blog: Testing & Evaluating AI Systems](https://blog.palantir.com/from-prototype-to-production-engineering-responsible-ai-3-ea18818cd222), [Evaluating Generative AI: A Field Manual](https://blog.palantir.com/evaluating-generative-ai-a-field-manual-0cdaf574a9e1), [community guide](https://community.palantir.com/t/guide-writing-effective-evals/2856)).

**What Palantir does NOT do.** Across all AIP Evals documentation and Palantir's own three-part T&E blog series I found **no** probability scoring rule (Brier/log score), **no** calibration measurement (ECE/reliability diagram), **no** abstention path or coverage/risk tradeoff, **no** judge severity normalization (their guidance is "evaluate the evaluator" and "align to a golden set," not de-bias across a panel), **no** ICD 203 likelihood/confidence typing, and **no** artifact hashing or seeded determinism for the LLM-mediated run. Their explicit posture is pass/fail unit-testing of prompts, not epistemic calibration of judgments. **This is the single cleanest wedge against the incumbent.**

**Adversarial note.** Palantir has the Ontology, the ATOs, the vehicles, and 10 years of trust. If your metrics package is a good idea, the realistic outcome is Palantir shipping "AIP Calibration" as an AIP Evals module within a year. **INFERRED.** Your defensibility must be the measured benchmark and the leakage-guard architecture, not the feature list.

## 5. Segment C — Predictive warning

### Rhombus Power (Guardian) — the incumbent narrative you must displace
- **$200M five-year USAF IDIQ** for PPBE decision/analysis tool development, through Sept 2030, out of an SBIR Phase II ([The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2025/09/27/rhombus-power-snags-200-million-us-air-force-contract-for-ai-powered-strategic-decision-making-platform/), [OrangeSlices](https://orangeslices.ai/rhombus-power-lands-200m-us-air-force-ppbe-ai-decision-analysis-tool-development-idiq/)).
- Guardian: ML + signal detection + multi-domain fusion; also "Raven Sentry" (Taliban attack prediction, Afghanistan 2020) and "Artemis" ([Tectonic Defense profile, May 2025](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/meet-the-ai-helping-to-shape-us-intelligence/)).
- Their public claims are anecdotal, not scored. CEO Anshu Roy and Gen. (ret.) McChrystal wrote in Foreign Policy that Guardian told them "war was coming" by late October 2021 and that the system "can make aggression predictions in future hot spots around the globe **with specific levels of confidence**" ([AI Has Entered the Situation Room](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/06/19/ai-artificial-intelligence-national-security-foreign-policy-threats-prediction/); [What AI Can Tell Us About Putin's Next Steps](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/06/19/russia-ukraine-war-counteroffensive-ai-artificial-intelligence-gazprom-potok-putin-wagner/)).
- **The authoritative counter-evidence you should cite.** The Alan Turing Institute's CETaS study *The State of AI for Strategic Warning* assesses that AI warning tools "highlight broad hotspots of existing violence, which often remain static over time… **There is limited evidence that these tools can accurately predict novel outbreaks of violence**, particularly in areas that have been historically peaceful. In addition, **these models cannot get into the minds of leaders** who make the decisions that produce such outcomes" ([CETaS](https://cetas.turing.ac.uk/publications/state-ai-strategic-warning)).
- **What Rhombus does NOT do:** publish a Brier score, a calibration curve, a baseline comparison, a false-positive rate, or an abstention path. **CONFIRMED by absence across all sources found; UNVERIFIED whether classified evaluations exist.**

**Positioning consequence.** The CETaS critique — *cannot model leader decision-making, no evidence on novel events* — is the exact gap your actor-profile simulation claims to fill. Use CETaS as the third-party framing of the problem, then show the measurement.

### Logicon (Romania) — **the one vendor who already ships your headline metric**
Self-described "dual-use deep tech decision support module delivering probabilistic conflict forecasts (**Brier 0.106 / AUC 0.95 on 480 resolved predictions**), automated scenario preparation, and natural-language operational queries. Designed as a plug-in capability for MSS NATO and Allied digital warfighting platforms" ([logicon.ro](https://logicon.ro/)). Also advertises: every prediction carries probability + confidence interval + horizon + ranked drivers; **isotonic calibration (Pool-Adjacent-Violators)** validated offline on the same 480; walk-forward temporal validation; **Page-Hinkley drift detection**; audit trail for post-operation review; feeds IPB and COA development.

**Do not ignore this.** It is a small European vendor with an ensemble of logistic regression + decision stumps, not an LLM system, and it explicitly defers agent-based wargaming to "complementary MSS NATO modules." But **it publicly claims a measured Brier on real resolved events, which you do not.** A reviewer who finds this will ask why an unfunded Romanian module has real-world calibration and you have synthetic oracle only. **Have the answer ready.**

## 12. Where you are genuinely weaker than the field

State these before a reviewer does. Every one has a competitor who is ahead.

1. **No real-world forecast track record.** Logicon (480 resolved), Good Judgment (554+ resolved), RFI, and Metaculus all have real-world resolution. 15,832 synthetic-oracle simulations is an *engineering* result, not a *forecasting* result. Your monotonic Brier-skill-vs-evidence-quality curve (+0.44 / +0.42 / +0.11 / −0.46) is strong evidence that **the machinery responds correctly to evidence quality** — say exactly that, and no more.
2. **No accreditation, no vehicle, no ATO, no IL6 path.** Competitors have FedStart (Primer, Anthropic), JWICS ATO (Primer), IL2–IL7 and air-gap (Worldscape), $4B IDIQ ceilings (Two Six), a $10B EA (Palantir). Name your intended path (Palantir FedStart, Second Front Game Warden, or an SMX-style prime teaming) or the white paper reads as a science project.
3. **Model dependence in a hostile procurement climate.** Post-27-Feb-2026, any perceived Anthropic dependency is disqualifying for federal work. The market answer is already visible: Vannevar/SMX sell "model-agnostic agent orchestration"; CIA's OSIRIS is "not committing to any single model or tech vendor"; Edgerunner sells on-prem custom models. **Lead with model-agnosticism and show the swap.**
4. **Thin adversary modeling.** See table row 11.
5. **The IC has already tried and abandoned scored forecasting.** ICPM existed, was measured, lost to unclassified amateurs, and integration "fell flat due in large part to internal politics" ([FAS](https://fas.org/publication/collaborative-intelligence-harnessing-crowd-forecasting-for-national-security/)). A CIA reviewer over 45 will remember this. **Your adoption argument — why this time is different — is more important than your technical argument.** The honest answer is that the cost per forecast collapses when the forecaster is a machine, and the abstention gate removes the analyst's fear of being scored on unanswerable questions. Make that argument explicitly.
6. **RAND's published skepticism is the reviewer's likely prior.** "A dangerous mirage of knowledge, and there be dragons" ([RAND, 2025](https://www.rand.org/pubs/commentary/2025/04/should-i-use-ai-in-my-wargame.html)); RAND RR-A1722-4 finds AI is most useful in games "that already give a significant role to computational models during adjudication" — which happens to describe your deterministic kernel exactly. **Quote RAND's own criterion and show you meet it.**
7. **The escalation literature is a loaded gun pointed at LLM-driven wargaming.** Rivera et al. found all five tested off-the-shelf LLMs escalate, develop arms-race dynamics, and occasionally deploy nuclear weapons, with GPT-3.5's escalation score rising 256% in a neutral scenario ([arXiv 2401.03408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03408), [FAccT '24](https://facctconference.org/static/papers24/facct24-57.pdf)). Lamparth et al., with 214 national-security experts, found LLM-simulated teams "cannot account for human player characteristics, showing no significant difference even for extreme traits such as 'pacifist' or 'aggressive sociopath'" and produce dialogue of "farcical harmony" ([arXiv 2403.03407](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03407), [Hoover](https://www.hoover.org/research/human-vs-machine-behavioral-differences-between-expert-humans-and-language-models-wargame)). **Pre-empt: your architecture puts the LLM at setup and interpretation and the deterministic kernel at simulation — which is precisely the mitigation these papers imply. Cite them yourself. Nothing builds more credibility with an IC reviewer than citing the strongest argument against your own category.**

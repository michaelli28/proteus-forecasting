# Architecture-Fit Adjudication — 60 candidates

**Axis:** Is there a real process with state that evolves? A deterministic kernel with dynamics, or an LLM narrating? Does anything meaningfully vary across N runs? Is there a resolvable quantity bands can tile? Is there an oracle that makes a Brier score mean anything? Would abstention ever fire, measurably? Is the domain adapter genuinely config?

**Scoring:** 5 = the shape the platform was built for, with an oracle. 4 = strong, one structural defect. 3 = real kernel, compromised oracle or thin variation. 2 = kernel without a scorable headline. 1 = NO KERNEL and/or UNSCORABLE.

**Distribution:** 5s = 11 · 4s = 20 · 3s = 21 · 2s = 3 · 1s = 5.

## 2. Top 8

**1 — C-CYBER-2 (Hydra).** This is the only candidate in sixty where the deterministic kernel is not something we would write and then have to defend. WNTR is an EPA/Sandia solver, EPANET-2.2-compatible, with pressure-dependent demand and a documented numerical method — "the `EpanetSimulator` uses Todini's Global Gradient Algorithm… the `WNTRSimulator` uses a Newton-Raphson algorithm" ([WNTR docs](https://usepa.github.io/WNTR/hydraulics.html), confirmed this session). The LLM translates an attacker playbook into control actions and interprets a hydrograph; the physics is not negotiable and not ours to be wrong about. Three other axes are equally clean. The domain adapter is *literally* a config file — a new utility is a new `.inp`, which is the only place in the catalog where "a new scenario is a new config file, not new code" is true without qualification. The resolvable quantity (boil-water advisory, population affected, duration) is publicly reported. And the backtest set is unusually well designed: Aliquippa and Minnesota both resolve to *low* consequence despite high access, so a hedging model that emits "somewhere between nothing and catastrophe" fails on sharpness while scoring fine on Brier. A candidate whose historical episodes punish hedging is worth more than one whose episodes reward it.

**2 — C-IW-7 (Censorship Posture).** A discrete-time HMM over control states coupled to a competing-risks hazard is a real process with state that evolves, and — rarely — the two knobs map onto the modeled actor's genuine decision calculus rather than being renamed to fit. Both the predictor stream (OONI, IODA, NetBlocks) and the outcome stream (ACLED) are timestamped open data, which means the as-of date can be rolled forward day by day over a decade and the system can publish a 30-day forecast weekly and be graded weekly, forever, by anyone. That is the only continuously falsifiable public track record available in the catalog outside C-GEO-3. The abstention gate is the best-posed anywhere: probe-coverage collapse looks exactly like absence of censorship, so the gate must fire on a *measurable* data-coverage condition — which means its false-abstention rate is computable rather than asserted.

**3 — C-GEO-3 (Coercion Tempo Ladder).** Hawkes self-excitation is not a decorative choice; it is the correct empirical model for a self-exciting incursion series, and coupling it to a repeated ladder game with a commitment ratchet gives a decision layer on top of a fitted point process. What earns it a 5 on my axis is the baseline discipline: it names **"a plain Hawkes fit with no game layer"** as a model to beat. That is a pre-registered ablation of the platform's own contribution. Every other candidate compares itself to a base rate or persistence, which tests whether the *data* has signal; this one tests whether the *simulate-and-adjudicate architecture* has signal. If the bid can show a positive ΔCRPS from the decision layer on daily public Taiwan MND / CSIS data, that single number is worth more than the rest of the catalog's synthetic results combined. If it can't, we need to know before a customer finds out.

**4 — C-TRADECRAFT-9 (The Range).** Misclassified in the catalog as a peer product; it is infrastructure. It is the only place in sixty candidates where ground truth is exact by construction, and at least seven other candidates cannot measure a false-positive or false-abstention rate without it: C-TRADECRAFT-1 (conflict-type classification), -2 (seed-set recovery), -3 (deception detection), -6 (indicator LRs), C-IW-1 (dark-figure estimator), C-IW-6 / C-CYBER-5 (false-attribution rate at confidence level), C-GEO-8 (contradiction precision/recall). The architecturally important component is not the Karval world generator — it is the **observation model**: delay, censoring, source bias, quantization, plus a deception policy and a laundering operator. That layer should be a platform component that any kernel can be wrapped in, and the geopolitical world should be one instance of it. It also uses `riskTolerance`/`escalationPropensity` natively and unmodified, which no other high-scoring candidate does.

**5 — C-CYBER-4 (Ledger).** The best piece of kernel discipline in the entire catalog is buried in one clause: the market must reproduce a **published** lead-lag structure (initial-access-broker payments preceding ransomware payments and leak-site posts by roughly 30 days) *before it is allowed to forecast*. That is a falsification test on the kernel itself, separate from the forecast score, and nothing else in the catalog has one. Beyond that: an agent-based market with entry/exit and price clearing is a genuine process; the oracle publishes monthly; persistence is named as the baseline with the correct verdict attached ("if it can't beat persistence it has no business in a briefing"); and it is the only candidate that confronts an oracle which *moves* — on-chain attribution grows over time, so scores must be re-computed on data revision rather than silently kept.

**6 — C-TECHECON-3 (Time-to-Capability).** This is the catalog's strongest proof of the config-not-code claim, and it is the claim the customer will test hardest. Swap `semiconductor-kernel-adapter.ts` for a hypersonics, quantum or enrichment adapter and the Poisson-yield + Wright's-law + capacity-accumulation kernel is unchanged. The process is genuinely industrial — tool inventory flows in under a control regime, defect density falls on a learning curve, HBM binds independently — and the Bayesian re-scoring as each public datapoint (teardown, SEMI billing, procurement notice) arrives makes the *pace of belief updating* itself a reportable metric. It also has something almost nothing else has: a **human baseline**. Past US as-of-dated assessments of Soviet enrichment, Indian and Pakistani tests, and Chinese first flights are public, which lets the skill score be computed against the analysts we are proposing to help rather than against a base rate.

**7 — C-IW-2 (Belief Drift).** Bounded-confidence opinion dynamics with stubbornness anchors over a stochastic block model is a real process, well studied, deterministic given seed, and the divergence is large and legible — ε and the elite-cue coefficient alone swing terminal adoption by 20+ points. The decisive property, though, is that the resolvable quantity has a **scheduled resolution date**: a named item on a named survey wave that will be fielded whether we forecast it or not. Almost every other candidate resolves against an event that may or may not happen; this one resolves against a publication. That is what a calibration curve needs and what intelligence questions almost never provide. The abstention gate is correspondingly crisp — no scheduled instrument in the horizon, abstain — and it is checkable from a calendar.

**8 — C-TRADECRAFT-2 (Effective-N).** Easy to mistake for an estimator, but the independent-cascade fit over a timestamped provenance DAG is an actual diffusion process with an evolving adoption sequence, and the seed-set size k̂ is recovered by maximum likelihood rather than declared. On the oracle axis it is close to perfect on three tiers simultaneously: exact synthetic recovery (inject one origin, launder through a known topology, measure error in N̂_eff); cheap unambiguous labeled positives from outside the intelligence domain (the coati hoax, a fabricated middle name, the Niger forgeries); and live resolution in *days* against Wayback first-appearance timestamps. It also gets the failure direction right where most candidates get it wrong: over-declaring circularity suppresses real corroboration, which is not a safe error, so the false-circularity rate is a reported metric. Finally, it is a hard dependency — C-TRADECRAFT-1's posteriors are falsely tight without it, and that dependency should be enforced in the pipeline, not documented.

*Also scored 5 and ranked 9–11: **C-CYBER-6** (explicit thinning of a censored observation process, hard published baseline), **C-ADV-7** (best process/oracle pairing in the ADV lens, uncontested lane), **C-GEO-9** (five MECE bands and an unlimited supply of date-certain questions).*

## 4. Cross-cutting observations

### 4a. Duplicate clusters (explicit, by id)

- **Ordinal escalation ladder** — `C-ADV-2` · `C-ADV-8` · `C-GEO-3` · `C-CYBER-3`. One kernel family. C-ADV-8 admits it outright ("the same ladder CTMC as C-ADV-2, conditioned into tail states"). Four entries, one implementation, four different scorability outcomes.
- **Opinion / threshold dynamics on a network** — `C-IW-2` · `C-IW-3` · `C-ADV-7` · `C-GEO-2`. Deffuant–Weisbuch, Hegselmann–Krause, DeGroot and Granovetter are the same family; C-IW-3 explicitly reuses C-IW-2's `populations/*.yaml`, and C-ADV-7 differs from C-IW-2 only in whether the nodes are member states or population segments.
- **Multi-source contradiction reconciliation** — `C-GEO-8` · `C-TRADECRAFT-1`. The *same product* with different math (Dempster–Shafer conflict mass vs. Bayes-factor model selection). Both target benefit (c), both name IARPA REASON as the incumbent. These must be merged before the bid; shipping both invites the question of why one platform needs two answers to one problem.
- **Bayesian attribution / adjudication with a planted-evidence term** — `C-IW-6` · `C-CYBER-5` · `C-TRADECRAFT-3` · (inverted) `C-ADV-3`. C-IW-6 and C-CYBER-5 are near-identical: Bayesian likelihood ratios plus capture–recapture on shared infrastructure plus a false-flag devil's advocate.
- **Capture–recapture / "how much am I not seeing"** — `C-IW-1` · `C-IW-6` · `C-TRADECRAFT-2`. Different estimators, identical epistemic move.
- **Economic-coercion instrument choice** — `C-ADV-5` · `C-TECHECON-8` · (partly) `C-TECHECON-2`. C-ADV-5 is the PRC-specific instance of C-TECHECON-8's general model, with hand-set rather than fitted coefficients; the ECON version is strictly better methodologically and C-ADV-5 should be retired into it.
- **Capacity / production queue with a throughput-vs-risk decision layer** — `C-ADV-9` · `C-IW-8` · `C-CYBER-6` · `C-CYBER-7` · `C-CYBER-8` · `C-TECHECON-3`. Six candidates, one kernel. The single most reused kernel in the catalog, and nobody says so — which is a missed selling point, not just an editorial slip.
- **Indicator diagnosticity** — `C-ADV-6` · `C-TRADECRAFT-5` · `C-TRADECRAFT-6`. C-ADV-6's "mutual information I(observable; branch)" and C-TRADECRAFT-5's "expected reduction in Shannon entropy" are literally the same quantity.

### 4b. The catalog conflates three architectures and pitches all three as one

There are three distinct shapes here, all legitimate, and only one matches the pitch:

1. **Simulate-and-adjudicate proper** — a decision process with state evolving under a rule, fanned out over divergent decision profiles (C-ADV-1/2/4/7, C-IW-2/7, C-GEO-1/2/3/9, C-TECHECON-1/3/5/7/9, C-CYBER-1/6, C-TRADECRAFT-2/9).
2. **Estimate-and-adjudicate** — an estimator over a fixed evidence set, fanned out over *interpretive* hypotheses (C-IW-5/6, C-GEO-8, C-TECHECON-6, C-CYBER-5, C-TRADECRAFT-1/4/5/6/7).
3. **Consequence twin** — a physical or operational process with the adversary as a boundary condition, not a modeled chooser (C-CYBER-2/7/8, C-IW-8, C-GEO-7, C-ADV-9).

Classes 2 and 3 have systematically **better oracles** than class 1, because their quantities are constructible or physically measured. The bid should name the three shapes and claim each on its own terms. Dressing class 2 as class 1 — which several entries do — is the exact evidence-class inflation the platform exists to prevent.

### 4c. Ensemble diversity is decoupled from the oracle far more often than C-ADV-10

The catalog disqualifies C-ADV-10 because "a system whose diversity metric is decoupled from any oracle is producing decoration." The same defect, weaker, afflicts **C-ADV-8** (rung-skip has no data), **C-ADV-6** and **C-CYBER-3** (branch probabilities never resolve), **C-CYBER-1** (output is a smooth function of an unobservable inventory prior), **C-TECHECON-6** (the counterfactual is unobservable by definition).

**Recommended structural fix, absent everywhere:** every knob, prior and parameter in a config must carry an `estimator:` field naming how its value would be measured, and the abstention gate must fire when the ensemble's variance decomposition attributes the spread predominantly to a parameter with `estimator: none`. That converts a rhetorical objection into a runtime assertion — the same move the platform already makes with the post-dated-evidence guard.

### 4d. Counterfactual-branch products are structurally half-unscorable, and there are five

Any candidate whose output is "for each of our N options, what happens" can resolve at most the branch actually taken: **C-ADV-6**, **C-CYBER-3**, **C-IW-3**, **C-GEO-6**'s policy-conditional arm, **C-TECHECON-2** in part. The correct architecture rule — stated nowhere — is: score the realized branch as a forecast under `historical_backtest` or `live_post_cutoff`; score unrealized branches **only** under `synthetic_oracle`; and forbid any report from pooling the two. That is one more field on the evaluation-regime tag and it turns a weakness into a demonstration.

### 4e. Shared input/oracle contamination is a distinct failure class with no guard

Leakage guards stop the model reading the future. Nothing stops the *oracle* being generated by the same source as the input. This occurs in **C-GEO-2** (ACLED undercount biases predictor and outcome identically — the entry says it "can manufacture apparent skill"), **C-IW-1** (Meta's disclosed counts are both an input list and the resolution source), **C-ADV-9** (scored against an analyst consensus built from the same reporting), **C-CYBER-4 / C-CYBER-9** (revised-upward on-chain series), **C-CYBER-1 / C-CYBER-6** (everything observed through a detection filter).

**Recommended fix:** add a fourth field to the mandatory evaluation-regime tag — `oracle_independence: {independent | shares_source_with_input | analyst_consensus | revised_series}` — and have the metrics package **refuse to compute a skill score** against a `shares_source_with_input` oracle, the same way it currently refuses to pool regimes. C-CYBER-4 half-invents this already by proposing to record data vintage; generalize it.

### 4f. The base-rate trap catches far more than the two candidates it is named in

Any rare-event forecast over a short window has a devastating "predict no" baseline. That applies to **C-ADV-1** (confirmed US water OT manipulation per 90 days), **C-GEO-1** (coup in a 180-day window), **C-ADV-8**, **C-CYBER-1**. Brier skill vs. base rate will be near zero for an honest model on all of them. The right headline for this class is **tail recall and coverage@N, not Brier skill**, and the catalog says so only for C-ADV-8 and C-CYBER-9. Conversely, **sharpness** is the metric that separates the good candidates from the hedgers, because several backtests resolve to the null band (Aliquippa, Minnesota, Spamouflage, Iranian non-acceptance under maximum pressure) — those are the discriminating cases and sharpness is the only metric that notices.

### 4g. "Everything is config" is true in about five places and false everywhere else

Nearly every candidate ships at least one new `.ts` kernel *and* one new `.ts` adapter — the file extensions in the config packs give it away. The claim is genuinely demonstrable in: **C-CYBER-2** (a new utility is a new `.inp` against an unchanged solver), **C-TECHECON-3** (swap the domain adapter, keep the yield/learning kernel), **C-TECHECON-8**'s mirror config (same kernel, US instrument menu), **C-ADV-4**'s detainee-exchange variant, **C-IW-3** reusing **C-IW-2**'s populations. Claim it there, with those five as proof, and claim *kernel-family reuse* (§4a) everywhere else. Overclaiming this to a technically literate evaluator costs more than the claim is worth.

### 4h. The two knobs are not enough and four lenses admit it by renaming them

TRADECRAFT re-binds `riskTolerance`/`escalationPropensity` to `credulity`/`commitmentPropensity`; C-IW-5 says the variation is causal structure; C-GEO-8 says it is evidence-partition resampling; C-ADV-9 says it is exogenous parameters. This is fine and should be said plainly: the platform has a **generic two-knob actor profile** *plus* a **free-form ensemble dimension**, and only the class-1 candidates use the actor knobs. That is a strength (the fan-out generalizes past actor psychology) currently presented as an embarrassment.

## 5. Factual corrections and verifications

**Correction — C-ADV-2's simulator is inconsistent with its own kernel.** The entry specifies a "continuous-time **semi-Markov** jump process… forward-simulated by the **Gillespie algorithm**" with "a duration-dependent fatigue term" and "a transition intensity matrix Q(θ)". These cannot all hold. The standard Gillespie SSA assumes "each reaction is Markovian in time" and that "the random waiting time for some reaction is exponentially distributed"; non-exponential holding times require a generalized non-Markovian variant (Bratsun et al. 2005, Barrio et al. 2006, Cai 2007) ([Gillespie algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillespie_algorithm)). A duration-dependent intensity is also not a constant `Q`. This is repairable — use inversion sampling on the semi-Markov holding-time distributions or a next-reaction method — but as written the kernel description would not survive a technical evaluator, and it is the candidate whose whole case rests on the kernel being fitted properly.

**Correction — the TRADECRAFT lens misquotes the SWARM result.** It states "SWARM 22.0/32 vs 15.9/32" in both C-TRADECRAFT-4 and C-TRADECRAFT-9. The published figures are **21.4/32** (SWARM, weeks 2–4) vs 15.9/32 (normal methods, week 1), Cohen's *d* = 1.37 ([van Gelder et al., *JCEDM* 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1555343420926287)). Small, but this number is being used as the bar to clear and it should be right.

**Verified this session:** WNTR supports PDD and EPANET 2.2, with Todini's Global Gradient Algorithm (EpanetSimulator) and Newton–Raphson (WNTRSimulator) — [WNTR docs](https://usepa.github.io/WNTR/hydraulics.html), supporting C-CYBER-2's kernel claim. ICB v16 covers "1918–2021… 512 international crises, 1,131 crisis actors, and 1,000 crisis dyads" — [ICB data collections](https://sites.duke.edu/icbdata/data-collections/), matching C-ADV-2. The ICBe escalation paper analyzes 475 crises and defines escalation by "whether an action tends to shorten or extend the length of a crisis" — [Douglass et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03340), matching C-ADV-2. Neprash et al.: volume falls "17–24 percent during the initial attack week" and in-hospital mortality for already-admitted patients rises "34–38 percent" — [AEJ: Policy](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fpol.20240594), matching C-CYBER-8. ViEWS benchmark model names and CRPS/MIS/ab-log metrics — [ViEWS leaderboard](https://viewsforecasting.org/leaderboard/), matching C-GEO-10.

## 6. What is missing entirely, from the architecture axis

**a. A decision→consequence composition, which is the customer's own worked example.** C-ADV-1 forecasts *whether* Iran acts against US water; C-CYBER-2 forecasts *what happens hydraulically if they do*. They are two halves of one product and nobody chained them. A candidate that pipes a decision-model band distribution into a consequence twin — evaluation regimes kept separate, confidence aggregated by weakest link across the join — is the single most persuasive artifact this bid could build, uses two existing candidates as components, and answers the customer's named scenario end to end. Its absence is the largest gap in the catalog.

**b. A mandatory decision-layer ablation harness.** C-GEO-3 alone names its own kernel-minus-the-game as a baseline. This should be automatic for every candidate: run the kernel with the actor knobs frozen at their means and report ΔBrier / ΔCRPS. If the decision layer contributes nothing, the honest answer is to ship the statistical model. Offering this proactively to a PITF-literate evaluator is the most credible thing in the whole bid, because it is the question they will ask and no vendor volunteers it.

**c. A stylized-facts gate on every kernel.** C-CYBER-4 invents it once (reproduce the 30-day IAB lead-lag before you may forecast). Generalize it: every kernel config carries a `stylizedFacts:` block of published empirical regularities with citations and tolerances, and the runner refuses to emit a forecast if the kernel fails to reproduce them. That is a config-level, testable guard that separates "we fit a model" from "our model is consistent with what is already known," and nothing else in the catalog has it.

**d. The observation-corruption layer as a platform component, not a candidate.** C-TRADECRAFT-9 contains it (delay, censoring, bias, quantization, deception policy, laundering operator) but scopes it inside one geopolitical world generator. It should be a wrapper any kernel can be run through, so that every candidate gets a synthetic false-positive and false-abstention rate for free. Right now the catalog's abstention rates are measurable for one candidate and asserted for the rest.

**e. A commit-then-resolve register.** C-TRADECRAFT-7 invents hash-committed resolution criteria for itself. Every candidate with a "live post-cutoff" arm needs it, or those arms are not evidence-class (c) at all — an unregistered live forecast is a backtest with extra steps. This should be a platform service: append-only, timestamped, publicly hashed, one entry per pre-registered question.

**f. Portfolio-level coherence checking.** Several candidates forecast overlapping quantities — C-ADV-5 and C-TECHECON-8 both forecast PRC counter-instrument choice; C-TECHECON-4 and C-ADV-5 both forecast rare-earth controls; C-ADV-1 and C-CYBER-2 both concern Iran-nexus water effects. Nothing checks that the platform's own products agree. This is C-TRADECRAFT-4's Dutch-book detector applied across the portfolio instead of within one document, and it is a QA capability no incumbent could match, because no incumbent has machine-readable posteriors to check.

**g. A platform-wide null suite.** C-IW-5 has the right instinct (false-positive rate on null injections as the headline metric). Nobody proposes running *every* domain pack against a quiet period and reporting the false-alarm rate per pack. That is the number a warning customer actually wants and that no vendor publishes.

**h. Cost per resolved forecast.** "Cost per insight" is in the metrics package and not one candidate reports it. For at least three candidates (C-GEO-4, C-GEO-5, C-GEO-6) the honest verdict is "the regression is cheaper and as good," and publishing $/forecast and $/Brier-point alongside the skill numbers is precisely what makes the other numbers believable.

**i. The rerun-diff as the point of determinism.** Every candidate treats the product as a terminal artifact, but the process being displaced is a loop — the analyst disagrees with an assumption, changes it, and reruns. Nothing in the catalog exposes an "assumption override → rerun → what changed" diff. Byte-identical artifact hashes are currently a claim about reproducibility; they should be a claim about **attributable change**: *here is the single config line that moved the BLUF band, and here is the line that did not.* That is the operational payoff of a deterministic kernel and it is the one thing an LLM-narrating competitor structurally cannot offer.

**j. A config-staleness forecaster.** C-TECHECON-1 has a staleness abstention; C-TECHECON-5 has an I-O matrix that "lags by years"; C-GEO-1 has a roster-currency gate; C-ADV-2 ships a frozen hashed fit. Nothing generalizes this into a product that forecasts *when a frozen fit, matrix or roster stops being valid*. It would be a real capability, it protects every other product in the portfolio, and it is exactly the kind of self-auditing move that distinguishes this platform from the screening vendors.

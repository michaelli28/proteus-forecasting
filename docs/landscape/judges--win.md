# Win-Probability Adjudication — Federal Capture View

**Scoring axis:** probability this candidate wins a competitive award and survives a technical evaluation panel. Not technical merit. A brilliant candidate with an entrenched incumbent, no transition partner, no 90-day unclassified demo, or a legal tripwire scores low no matter how elegant the kernel.

**Scale:** 5 = I would bet the bid on it · 4 = strong, wins with the right partner · 3 = credible but contested or slow to prove · 2 = likely loses on incumbency or scorability · 1 = do not propose.

## 2. Top 8 — why each wins

**C-CYBER-2 — Hydra (water cyber-physical twin).** This is the one I would put first money on, and the reason is unglamorous: *the hard part is already built and it is free and it is the government's own*. WNTR/EPANET 2.2 is EPA/Sandia code implementing Wagner's pressure-demand relationship; I confirmed the mechanics directly against the [WNTR hydraulics documentation](https://usepa.github.io/WNTR/hydraulics.html) and the [EPANET 2.2 analysis algorithms](https://usepa.github.io/EPANET2.2/12_analysis_algorithms.html). A technical evaluation panel can verify the kernel themselves in an afternoon, which is worth more than any claim we make about it. The backtest episodes are dated, public, and one of them is three weeks old. Most importantly for capture: AWIA §2013 obliges every community water system over 3,300 people to redo a risk-and-resilience assessment on a statutory cycle, and EPA's own OIG documented >70% non-compliance plus the absence of any incident-reporting mechanism — that is a demand signal written into law, with a named SRMA (EPA Office of Water), a named regional triage function (CISA advisors), and a named methodological ancestor that is a partner rather than a competitor (INL's CCE, which is exactly this analysis executed by humans at one engagement per organization). One caveat to write into the proposal rather than discover in evaluation: WNTR's default `required_pressure` is 0.07 m, so a PDD run left at defaults behaves almost identically to a demand-driven one — the "37,000 people without water" number is a function of a config value, and that parameter belongs in the sensitivity report and the abstention gate, prominently.

**C-ADV-1 — Sub-Threshold Action Selector.** In capture, the single strongest position is being the answer to the question the customer wrote down. They named Iran and US water infrastructure. This candidate forecasts that exact quantity, on entirely unclassified evidence, with public dated resolution via CISA advisory publication. I verified the actor-alias claim against the primary source the CYBER lens could not fetch: [CISA AA26-097A](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-097a) (updated 22 July 2026) lists "Hydro Kitten, Storm-0784, APT Iran, Bauxite, Mr. Soul, Soldiers of Solomon, UNC5691, and the Shahid Kaveh Group," confirms the ≥75-device figure, and states that the authoring agencies "assess a group of Iranian-affiliated APT actors is conducting this activity to cause disruptive effects within the United States." The catalog's UNVERIFIED flags on that advisory can be lifted. Weakness to name up front: backtest n is three episodes and they are heavily represented in pretraining, so the historical number is an upper bound and only the live 90-day arm is real evidence.

**C-TRADECRAFT-4 — Tradecraft Auditor.** The most defensible position a vendor can occupy is scoring against the customer's own published rubric, and this does. It also has something almost nothing else in the catalog has: a *published bar*. The CREATE/SWARM evaluation used the IC Rating Scale and reported 22.0/32 versus 15.9/32 with d = 1.37 — a number a panel can check. The incumbency picture is sharper than the catalog states, and in a useful way. The [REASON BAA](https://arl.devcom.army.mil/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2023/04/W911NF-23-S-0007-REASON-BAA-Final-17-March-2023.pdf) confirms a 42-month program, Phase 1 Oct 2023–Sep 2025, Phase 2 Oct 2025–Mar 2027, whose TA3 final-exam metric is Report Quality Score built on **six** IC tradecraft standards (sourcing, uncertainty, assumptions, alternatives, logic, accuracy) with targets ΔRQS > 1.5 then > 3.0. So the rubric-scoring ground is occupied by a funded program with an independent cleared T&E team — but REASON's metrics contain nothing resembling a probabilistic-coherence check, nothing that calibrates its own raters, and no abstention concept. Pitch this as the REASON transition target and time it to Phase 2's March 2027 end.

**C-TRADECRAFT-9 — The Range.** The only candidate with no data dependency at all, which means no corpus clearance, no as-of leakage risk, and no possibility a model memorized the answer. Its buyer is the one growing fastest: an IC standing up LLM-based analytic tools across 18 agencies needs a memorization-proof evaluation harness and does not have one, because every historical case study is in every model's weights. There is also government precedent for the method that the catalog missed — IARPA's [FOCUS program](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/focus) evaluated its methods "by having hundreds of people make predictions about outcomes in complex simulations," which is exactly this design, already accepted as a legitimate evaluation regime by this customer's own research arm. And it is the acceptance-test harness for C-TRADECRAFT-1, -2, -3 and -6, which turns a catalog of separate bets into one program with an internal proof engine.

**C-GEO-3 — Coercion Tempo Ladder.** Best live oracle per dollar in the catalog: Taiwan MND publishes daily, CSIS ChinaPower curates it, and a monthly Brier score accrues from the day the config lands. That matters more than it sounds — the fastest way to lose a bid built on "we measure ourselves" is to have no measurement yet. INDOPACOM J2/J5 is a large, funded, motivated buyer, and the discrete quarantine rung — zero base rate, no training rows — is the cleanest place in the catalog to demonstrate that abstention is a first-class output rather than a hedge. Score it against a plain Hawkes fit with no game layer, publish the comparison, and let the delta be the argument.

**C-IW-9 — Retrieval Poisoning.** The sleeper. Every other candidate asks the customer to trust a forecast about the world; this one tells the customer something verifiable about *their own infrastructure*, and the verification is running the audit. NewsGuard's July 2024 and March 2025 audits give a genuine fit-then-predict backtest with published numbers. The classified instantiation is obviously necessary and obviously buildable, because the thing being modeled — the composition and ranking behavior of the customer's own index — lives inside the enclave and cannot be modeled anywhere else. That is the inverse of the usual classification barrier: here the classified version is the *easy* version, because it requires no adversary access, only the customer's own system. Shortest path to a first contract in the catalog.

**C-TRADECRAFT-2 — Effective-N.** In a briefing, this produces the one artifact nobody in the room has ever been handed: "you believe fourteen sources; you have 2.3; your posterior odds fall from 61:1 to 4:1." It lands on the institutional memory this customer has never finished paying for — a single fabricator supplying the bulk of an assessment — and it does it on GDELT, Common Crawl and Wayback timestamps with no classification, no persons, and no author node in the graph model. It also has clean low-stakes labeled positives (the coati hoax, the Guttenberg middle name) for demonstrating precision without touching an intelligence case. The dependency runs the right direction: it makes C-TRADECRAFT-1 and C-GEO-8 sound, so it is the correct first build in the tradecraft line.

**C-ADV-5 — Technoeconomic Retaliation.** The solicitation names technoeconomic policy and US response options explicitly, and this is the only candidate that both answers it and resolves in public. UN Comtrade, USGS, Federal Register, MOFCOM — no classified inputs required for a working instance. The incumbent picture is favorable in a specific way worth stating in the bid: Sayari, Kharon, C4ADS, Altana, Exiger and Interos are all *illumination and screening* products that answer "what is the network," and not one publishes a forward-scored prediction. BIS and Treasury are buyers with real budget and a recurring per-rule analytic workload that today gets rebuilt from scratch every time.

## 4. Cross-cutting observations

**The catalog's central honesty claim went stale in July 2026, and a technical panel will catch it.** Multiple lenses — most explicitly the GEO cross-cutting section — build their positioning on "LLMs lose to superforecasters, and we are honest about that": ForecastBench superforecasters at Brier 0.068 versus best LLM at 0.119. Those are 2024 numbers. The benchmark's own operators reported on [16 July 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity) that several systems are now statistically indistinguishable from superforecaster accuracy, that Cassi AI's system **outranks the superforecaster median on market questions** for the first time, and that 17 submissions rank above superforecasters on the preliminary leaderboard. The [tournament leaderboard](https://www.forecastbench.org/leaderboards/) puts the superforecaster median at Brier Index 69.2 with Cassi at 68.7 and Grok 4.20 at 68.0. Two consequences. First, delete the stale figures — quoting a two-year-old benchmark as current is exactly the error the platform's own metrics package exists to prevent, and a panel that notices will discount everything else. Second, this *strengthens* the strategic case: if raw forecasting skill is commoditizing, the durable differentiator is measurement, abstention, provenance and calibration — which is where the top 8 already sit — and it *further* weakens C-TRADECRAFT-8, since entering a public tournament is now table stakes rather than a claim.

**Rhombus Power is the wrong bogeyman, and the catalog cites it four times.** C-ADV-1, C-ADV-2, C-GEO-1 and C-GEO-3 all invoke Guardian as the adversary-forecasting standard to beat. The [$200M IDIQ awarded 26 September 2025](https://orangeslices.ai/rhombus-power-lands-200m-us-air-force-ppbe-ai-decision-analysis-tool-development-idiq/) (FA7014-25-D-0011, SBIR Phase III off open call AF192, through Sept 2030) is for **planning, programming, budgeting and execution decision-and-analysis tooling** — resource allocation, not adversary prediction. The earlier Guardian OTA (FA87262090007, $200M ceiling, Sept 2020) is complete with roughly $54M realized. Rhombus is entrenched in the *budget* decision-support lane and much thinner in the adversary-forecasting lane than the catalog fears. The unverified Ukraine-prediction claim should be cited once, as a vendor claim, in the context of "the marketing standard nobody has independently scored" — which is the actual differentiator — and not treated as competitive positioning.

**Nobody in the catalog answered "how do they buy it," and the answer changed six months ago.** Sixty candidates, zero mentions of a contract vehicle, an authority, or an accreditation path. In February 2026 CIA announced a [new Acquisition Framework](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/cia-launches-new-acquisition-framework-to-turbocharge-collaboration-with-private-sector/) under Director Ratcliffe with a new procurement chief (Efstathia Fragogiannis, DARPA alum, arrived November 2025), built around centralized vendor vetting, a **streamlined IT authorization process to reduce time from mission requirement to operating authority**, and explicit pathways to "rapidly onboard breakthrough technology prototypes." That framework targets precisely the bottleneck every candidate hand-waves as "the classified instance swaps one config file." A white paper that names DDI's Digital Gateway / Strategic Vendor Management on-ramp, In-Q-Tel for the early-stage path, and the prototype-onboarding pathway will read as written by someone who has done this before. One that does not will read as academic.

**The classification barrier has a solved contracting pattern the catalog never names.** REASON's structure is the template: performers build and are tested on unclassified data in Phase 1, an *independent cleared T&E team* retrains and evaluates on classified draft reports in parallel, and in Phase 2 performers **still do not get classified access** — they refine against unclassified examples using "actionable summary level unclassified feedback" from the cleared T&E team. Every candidate in this catalog that says "the classified version swaps one config file" should instead say "here is the cleared-T&E evaluation pattern IARPA already runs, and our deterministic, seeded, byte-identical artifact hashes make that pattern cheaper than it has ever been." Related and equally unaddressed: REASON requires **a minimum of Government Purpose Rights** so the government can modify and deploy on classified networks. That is the data-rights floor for anything in this space, and it needs to be priced into the bid rather than discovered at negotiation.

**Reproducibility is being treated as an engineering property when it is a procurement asset.** Deterministic kernels, seeded PRNG, no wall clock, byte-identical artifact hashes, 663 tests — the catalog presents these as evidence of rigor. They are also the direct answer to the question an accreditation reviewer and an IG will both ask about any AI-assisted judgment: *show me exactly how this was produced and re-derive it in front of me.* No LLM wrapper can answer that. Given that CIA's new framework explicitly targets the authorization timeline, "auditable by construction" is a schedule argument, not just a quality argument, and it is not made anywhere in sixty candidates.

**Duplicate clusters, by id.** The catalog presents sixty capabilities; it contains roughly a dozen kernels. That is a strength for the platform pitch and a liability for a document that reads as sixty distinct products.

- **Cluster A — Iran/water/OT, three layers of one product:** `C-ADV-1` (will they decide to act) → `C-CYBER-1` (when do they spend the access) → `C-CYBER-2` (what physically happens). These should be fused into one decision→activation→consequence chain. The fused product is far stronger than any of the three and is the single best demo available.
- **Cluster B — adversary economic instrument choice:** `C-ADV-5` and `C-TECHECON-8` are the same output type with different kernels (I-O cascade vs. discrete choice fit on GSDB); `C-TECHECON-2`, `C-TECHECON-4` and `C-GEO-5` overlap substantially. One candidate, two kernels, three domain packs.
- **Cluster C — source contradiction and analytic QA:** `C-GEO-8` and `C-TRADECRAFT-1` are the same product described twice; `C-TRADECRAFT-4` and `C-TRADECRAFT-2` sit on top of it. `C-TRADECRAFT-1` has the better kernel; `C-GEO-8` should be folded in.
- **Cluster D — escalation ladders:** `C-ADV-2`, `C-ADV-8` (which openly reuses C-ADV-2's CTMC), `C-GEO-3`, `C-CYBER-3`. Four candidates, one kernel family.
- **Cluster E — threshold cascade on a weighted network (largest cluster):** `C-ADV-7`, `C-IW-2`, `C-GEO-1`, `C-GEO-2`, `C-TECHECON-9`, partially `C-IW-1`. Six candidates, one kernel. This is the cluster that best proves "a new scenario is a config file, not new code" — say so explicitly rather than presenting six products.
- **Cluster F — multi-list / capture-recapture provenance:** `C-IW-1`, `C-IW-6`, `C-TRADECRAFT-2`. One estimator family.
- **Cluster G — queueing and production pipelines:** `C-ADV-9`, `C-TECHECON-3`, `C-CYBER-6`, `C-IW-8`.
- **Cluster H — value of an observation:** `C-ADV-6` (mutual information), `C-TRADECRAFT-5` (EVSI), `C-TRADECRAFT-6` (indicator likelihood ratio). The same object at three altitudes.
- **Cluster I — deception:** `C-CYBER-5` and `C-TRADECRAFT-3` compute the same likelihood ratio; C-TRADECRAFT-3 is the principled version (persuasion solved as optimization) and C-CYBER-5 the tractable one. Merge; ship the tractable one first.
- **Cluster J — counterfactual policy effect:** `C-IW-5` and `C-TECHECON-6` share the synthetic-control estimator.
- **Cluster K — the deliberate BAD ideas:** `C-ADV-10`, `C-IW-10`, `C-GEO-10`, `C-TECHECON-10`, `C-CYBER-10`, `C-TRADECRAFT-10`. Six floors making largely the same argument. Two is the right number in a proposal — one unscorable and one legally unbuyable. Six reads as padding and, worse, invites the evaluator to wonder how many of the other fifty-four are near the line.

**A structural pattern worth naming to the customer:** across all six lenses, the candidates that score well on my axis are the ones whose *resolution source is a third party's published artifact on a known schedule* — CISA advisories, MOFCOM announcements, Taiwan MND daily counts, EU sanctions package adoptions, KEV additions, monthly leak-site counts, IAEA quarterlies, survey waves, election dates, MSMT annual reports. That property, not kernel sophistication, is what separates a demo from a track record. It should be an explicit selection criterion stated in the white paper, because it is also the criterion that distinguishes this bid from every vendor who says yes to C-CYBER-10.

## 5. What is missing from the catalog entirely

**1. A capture and transition candidate.** Sixty technical candidates and no answer to how the customer buys, accredits, or fields any of them. Given CIA's February 2026 framework, the highest-leverage missing candidate is a *deployment* candidate: the platform running accredited inside the enclave, with the deterministic-artifact-hash chain positioned as an authorization accelerator. A technically weaker capability with a funded path beats a stronger one without.

**2. Reproducibility-as-the-product: an audit trail for AI-assisted analysis.** The single highest-win-probability candidate not in the catalog. As IC LLM adoption scales, the question "can you re-derive this judgment exactly, and show which evidence produced it" moves from nice-to-have to requirement, and it is the one thing this architecture answers structurally where no wrapper can. Nobody proposed it, and it is the asset the prototype actually already has.

**3. A cost-per-insight candidate — the only direct answer to benefit (d).** The solicitation names cost reduction through automating repetitive analysis. Every candidate treats (d) as a byproduct. Nobody proposed a measured before/after model of the analytic production line on named tasks with named hours. That is the benefit category a contracting officer finds easiest to defend and the catalog's weakest coverage.

**4. Coordination-outcome adjudication.** IC analytic confidence is not set by an analyst; it is negotiated in interagency coordination, where dissent gets absorbed and confidence gets rounded. "Reconcile differences in overall analytic confidence" almost certainly originates there. Nothing in sixty candidates models the coordination process itself — which dissent survives, which footnote gets written, where a confidence level was traded away. It would hit benefit (c) more literally than anything in the catalog.

**5. A dissent-and-override channel.** Everything here is a machine emitting a number. IC analytic tools die of non-adoption far more often than of inaccuracy, and nothing addresses how an analyst interrogates a run, disagrees with it, and has the disagreement recorded as a first-class typed object scored alongside the machine. That is both an adoption mechanism and a direct implementation of ICD 203's Alternatives standard.

**6. Adversarial evaluation of the platform itself.** Nearly every candidate documents a decoy-loop failure mode — the adversary learns what the model ranks first and feeds it. C-TRADECRAFT-3 comes closest but points outward at adversary deception of analysts, not at deception of the system. A standing red-team-the-model arm is technically necessary and, in a proposal, a strong differentiator against vendors who never mention the possibility.

**7. Sell the evaluation harness to a program that must prove improvement.** Several candidates note IARPA adjacency; none makes the obvious capture move of positioning as a program's *T&E* rather than as a competing performer. REASON, and its successors, must demonstrate ΔRQS against a control — that is a measurement problem this platform is unusually good at, it is a much easier first contract than selling into an analytic line, and it puts the vendor inside the program rather than outside it.

**8. Allied and FVEY as a market.** UK Cosmic Bazaar, Dstl, and allied partners appear once in passing and never as a candidate. An allied reference customer is often the fastest route to a scored public track record and substantially de-risks the US bid — particularly for the unclassified-native candidates (C-IW-7, C-GEO-3, C-TRADECRAFT-2, C-TRADECRAFT-9), which can be fielded with a partner service in months rather than after an accreditation cycle.

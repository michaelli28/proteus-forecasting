### C-ADV-1 — Sub-Threshold Action Selector: will Iran turn the water off?

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** Given a specific US or Israeli action against Iran, it runs hundreds of independent simulations of the IRGC cyber command deciding what to do about US water and energy control systems, and reports the probability that a disruptive attack of each severity happens in the next 90 days — plus the handful of things you'd see first if it were coming.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "If the US designates a new tranche of IRGC-CEC-linked entities on 1 September, what is the probability that an Iran-nexus actor causes a *confirmed manipulation-of-control* event at one or more US water or wastewater utilities within 90 days, in bands {0 utilities | 1–2 | 3–9 | ≥10}, and which observable moves first?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA Directorate of Analysis Iran-focused cyber/ICS analysts and the DHS CISA–WaterISAC warning cell; adjacent, CYBERCOM J2 and Treasury OFAC's targeting shop. **Today** this is a human writing a 3-page assessment after reading Dragos/Mandiant/Microsoft reporting, CISA advisories, and Persian-language IRGC media, with a hedged "we assess it is likely" and no number, no band, no falsifier, and no record of whether the last one was right. The manual step being displaced is specifically the *implicit* mental simulation of "how would Tehran weigh attribution risk against the need to show a response" — which today exists only as prose.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each run is one IRGC-CEC decision profile. State variables: `exposureStock` (count of internet-reachable PLCs of the targeted vendor class, decaying at a remediation rate ρ after each CISA advisory), `attributionBelief` (actor's own estimate of P(attributed | action)), `retaliationExpectation` (believed US response tier), `domesticSignalDebt` (how much visible retaliation the regime owes its own base after a humiliation), `accessInventory` (pre-positioned footholds, a depleting resource). Knobs: **riskTolerance** scales β_attribution — how heavily the actor discounts an option by its attribution-driven retaliation cost; **escalationPropensity** shifts the ordered-logit cutpoints so mass moves up the physical-effect ladder at identical utility. These are not cosmetic: at low riskTolerance the modal outcome is defacement-only, at high escalationPropensity the modal outcome crosses into unsafe-state manipulation, and the whole point is the shape of the distribution across profiles.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Mixed-logit discrete choice over an ordered action ladder, coupled to a competing-risks proportional-hazards timing model.** Action set A = {none, defacement/nuisance, data theft, HMI spoofing without physical effect, PLC logic change with recoverable physical effect, destructive/unsafe-state manipulation}. Utility U(a) = β_coerce·Coerce(a) − β_attrib·P(attrib|a)·Retal(a) + β_domestic·SignalValue(a) − β_cost·OpCost(a | accessInventory). Timing hazard λ(t) = λ₀(t)·exp(γ·TriggerIndex_t). Seeded PRNG draws the β mixing distribution and the hazard realization; same seed, byte-identical artifact. The exposure stock is a real observable (Shodan/Censys counts of the vendor class), not a fitted fudge factor.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over the outcome bands, diagnosticity-weighted, with the columns being the CISA/Dragos evidence items. **Deception Detection judge (MOM-POP)** scores whether the observed indicator pattern is better explained by a false-flag or a criminal actor wearing IRGC branding. **Quality-of-Information judge** scores each evidence item on source independence — critical here because vendor reporting cross-cites heavily and looks like corroboration when it is one source. **Devil's advocate** argues the null ("this is opportunistic scanning, not a decision"). **Premortem**: "it's February and three utilities went to manual operations — what did we miss in August?" **Red team** with standing to force a bounded rerun if exposureStock was measured pre-advisory. Rubric axes: diagnosticity, source independence, mechanism plausibility, indicator observability, cost-asymmetry of being wrong.
- **The config pack:** `actors/irgc-cec.actor.json`, `actors/hacktivist-cover.actor.json`, `actors/criminal-opportunist.actor.json` (the deception alternative must be a first-class simulant); `kernels/ot-action-ladder.kernel.ts` + `kernels/params/ics.yaml`; `bands/water-manipulation-90d.bands.json`; `rubric/subthreshold-cyber.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/ics-ot.adapter.ts` (parses CISA advisory IDs, MITRE ATT&CK for ICS technique IDs, Dragos threat-group aliases); `thresholds/abstain.yaml` (abstain when exposureStock is stale > 30 days or when no independent second source exists); `corpus/asof.manifest.json`.
- **Data required:** Fully unclassified and genuinely rich. CISA advisories including [AA24-038A on the Unitronics campaign and AA26-097A on the 2026 Minnesota water utility intrusions](https://www.tenable.com/blog/coordinated-cyberattack-on-minnesota-water-utilities-what-you-need-to-know); vendor reporting under the group's aliases — Storm-0784 (Microsoft), Bauxite (Dragos), UNC5691 (Mandiant), MITRE ATT&CK G1027 ([Tenable FAQ](https://www.tenable.com/blog/what-to-know-about-cyberav3ngers-the-irgc-linked-group-targeting-critical-infrastructure)); Treasury designations of six IRGC-CEC officials in February 2024 (same source); Shodan/Censys exposure counts; EPA/WaterISAC incident notices; Persian-language IRGC-affiliated media. Classified access adds SIGINT on tasking and HUMINT on IRGC-CEC internal debate — which sharpens the `domesticSignalDebt` and `retaliationExpectation` variables but is not required for a working instance. **A credible unclassified demo is possible today** and is the single best demo in this catalog because the customer named this exact scenario.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** Three-regime. (1) **Historical backtest with as-of cutoffs** on the real 2023–2026 campaign: cutoff at 1 Oct 2023 (predict the November 2023 [Aliquippa PLC compromise](https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/municipal-water-authority-of-aliquippa-hacked-iranian-backed-cyber-group/) and the ~75-device spread), cutoff at 1 Feb 2024 (post-designation, predict the quiet period), cutoff at 1 Jan 2026 (predict the [Minnesota cluster](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/07/29/iran-linked-cyberav3ngers-suspected-in-attacks-on-minnesota-water-systems/5280357)). Corpus retrieval is date-filtered in code with a runtime assertion that throws on any post-cutoff citation — necessary here because these events are heavily in model pretraining. (2) **Live post-cutoff resolution**: standing 90-day questions resolved by CISA advisory publication, which is a public dated artifact. (3) **Synthetic closed worlds** with a known attacker utility function, to measure the abstention gate's false-abstention rate. Brier, Brier skill vs. the base rate of "≥1 confirmed US water OT manipulation event per 90-day window 2021–2026," ECE, and tail recall on the ≥10 band.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — replaces "likely" with a calibrated band carrying a measured Brier skill; (b) time-to-analysis — the 3-day assessment cycle collapses to a run, and the I&W list is generated rather than brainstormed; (c) QA — the Quality-of-Information judge mechanically detects the circular-citation pattern in vendor reporting (three reports, one primary source) that human analysts routinely miscount as corroboration, and the confidence-by-weakest-link rule prevents a single-sourced link from inheriting the panel's mean confidence; (d) cost — the exposure-stock refresh and advisory ingestion are repetitive tasks currently done by hand each cycle.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Closest government program: CISA/EPA water sector I&W plus the DIU-brokered [Thunderforge](https://scale.com/blog/thunderforge-ai-for-american-defense) agentic planning work at INDOPACOM and EUCOM ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2025/03/05/diu-thunderforge-scale-ai-combatant-commands-indopacom-eucom/)) — Thunderforge is planning-side, not adversary-decision-forecast-side, so this is adjacent rather than duplicative. Closest commercial: Dragos (OT threat intelligence, no probabilistic decision model), Recorded Future and Mandiant (attribution and reporting, no bands), Rhombus Power's Guardian, whose company-claimed four-months-out call on the 2022 Russian invasion at 80% certainty ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/06/19/ai-artificial-intelligence-national-security-foreign-policy-threats-prediction/)) is the marketing standard to beat — note that claim is a **company/advisor assertion, not an independently scored result**.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: the exposure stock is the load-bearing variable and it is measured with a scanner, so a vendor-class misidentification silently rescales everything; and the historical backtest is contaminated by pretraining unless the date-filter assertion actually fires (it does, in code — but a single evidence item pulled from model weights rather than the corpus bypasses it, which is why unsupported-assertion rate is a reported metric rather than a blocked condition). Red lines: this system models an *organizational* decision process; the actor schema accepts only organizational and role-level actors, and the domain adapter hard-rejects any corpus document carrying a US-person tag, so a named US utility operator can never become a modeled decision node. It produces no target recommendations — the output type is a probability band over adversary action, and there is structurally no path from a run artifact to an effects recommendation.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now (kernel and platform validated in lab against synthetic oracle; this domain pack not yet built). 6 in 12 months — the backtest data is public and dated, so a real historical-backtest number is achievable, and live post-cutoff resolution starts accruing immediately.

### C-ADV-2 — Crisis Ladder: rung-transition forecasting backtested on a century of crises

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It treats a live international crisis as a piece moving up and down a numbered ladder of hostile actions, learns how fast real crises have moved between rungs over the last hundred years, and forecasts the probability of reaching any given rung within a stated window.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "As of today, what is the probability that the India–Pakistan crisis reaches rung ≥7 (sustained cross-border fires by regular forces) within 30 days, in bands {<10% | 10–25% | 25–50% | >50%}, and what is the expected time-to-first-de-escalatory-act?"
- **Mission and user:** ODNI National Intelligence Council warning staff and the CIA Operations Center's watch; adjacent, the State Department's Bureau of Intelligence and Research. **Today** the warning judgment is a Delphi-ish conversation among senior analysts, anchored on the most recent comparable crisis that someone in the room personally remembers — a sample of one to three, chosen by availability. The manual process being displaced is the analogical reasoning step: "this feels like 2019 Balakot" is currently unaudited, unweighted, and unrecorded.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is a dyad-level behavioral profile. State variables: `currentRung` (ordinal, from the ICBe action ontology), `reciprocityDebt` (unanswered hostile acts by each side), `crisisDuration` (fatigue term — long crises de-escalate differently than short ones), `thirdPartyPressure`, `audienceCommitment` (public statements that raise the cost of climbing down). Knobs: **riskTolerance** scales the *de-escalation* intensity (how costly a climb-down the actor will accept); **escalationPropensity** multiplies up-rung intensities relative to lateral and down-rung. Variation is substantial and structural — different profiles produce visibly different absorption behavior (some crises die at rung 3, some ratchet).
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Continuous-time semi-Markov jump process on an ordinal ladder**, forward-simulated by the Gillespie algorithm with a seeded PRNG. Transition intensity matrix Q(θ) with a dyadic reciprocity term and a duration-dependent fatigue term, estimated by maximum likelihood against real crisis event sequences. The ladder rungs are not invented — they come from the ICBe ontology's armed/unarmed × escalatory/de-escalatory partition (alert-status raises, mobilization, fortification, exercises on the escalatory side; the mirrored acts on the de-escalatory side), per [Douglass, Gartzke, Lindsay, Gannon and Scherer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03340), who additionally give a data-driven escalation measure: whether an action tends to shorten or extend crisis duration, fit across 475 crises.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over rung bands. **Structured Analogies judge** scores which historical crises the kernel's fitted regime most resembles and whether the analogy set is diverse or all drawn from one region/era. **Key Assumptions Check** on the ladder's ordinality (is "cyber disruption of a power grid" really below "artillery exchange"? — this is the assumption that breaks first). **Indicators/Signposts judge** emits the observable that most sharply separates the top two bands. **Devil's advocate** argues the crisis is already de-escalating and the model is reading noise. **Red team** may force a rerun with the ladder reordered — an ordinality stress test. Rubric axes: analogy-set diversity, ladder-ordering sensitivity, indicator observability, evidence sufficiency, calibration honesty.
- **The config pack:** `ladders/icbe-ordinal.ladder.json` and `ladders/cyber-inclusive.ladder.json` (two orderings, deliberately); `actors/<dyad>.profile.json`; `kernels/semi-markov-ladder.kernel.ts`; `fits/icb-v16.fit.json` (frozen MLE fit, hashed); `rubric/warning.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/polecat.adapter.ts`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml` (abstain when fewer than K historical analogues share the dyad's structural features); `bands/rung-by-T.bands.json`.
- **Data required:** Almost entirely unclassified, and unusually good. The [International Crisis Behavior project](https://cidcm.umd.edu/research/international-crisis-behavior-project) — v15 covers 496 crises and 1,100 crisis actors through 2019; v16 covers 1918–2021 with 512 crises, 1,131 actors and 1,000 dyads ([ICB data collections](https://sites.duke.edu/icbdata/data-collections/)). ICBe and ICBeLLM for within-crisis event sequences. For live state: [POLECAT](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/AJGVIT), the official successor to ICEWS, built on the PLOVER ontology with the NGEC coder, covering 2018-onward, updated hourly and posted weekly ([Halterman et al.](https://www.andrewhalterman.com/publication/plover-polecat-new-event-data/)); GDELT with CAMEO and the Goldstein −10..+10 scale as a cross-check, noting the published finding that GDELT has large coverage but poor redundancy and domain accuracy ([comparative analysis](https://doi.org/10.3390/data11070158)). Classified adds force-posture truth that media-coded events miss. **Unclassified demo: yes, and it is the cleanest backtest in the catalog.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Strict historical backtest**, and this is the candidate that can actually produce a defensible real-world number. Hold out crises by date: fit on 1918–1989, test on 1990–2021 (roughly 200 held-out crises), as-of cutoff at crisis onset + 7 days, forecast rung-at-day-30. Then a second, harder holdout by *region* to test transfer. Metrics: Brier and Brier skill vs. two explicit baselines (unconditional rung base rate; persistence — "it stays where it is"), Murphy decomposition to separate resolution from reliability, ECE with reliability diagrams, coverage@N on the ordinal bands, and tail recall on rungs ≥8. Live post-cutoff: standing questions on active crises resolved against POLECAT-coded events with a pre-registered coding rule. Evaluation-regime tag is `historical_backtest`, and no report pools it with the synthetic numbers.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — a warning judgment with a measured skill score against persistence, which is the baseline warning analysis actually has to beat and rarely tests itself against; (b) time-to-analysis — the analogy search over 512 crises is instant instead of anecdotal; (c) QA — POLECAT and GDELT disagreements on the same event become an explicit source-discrepancy finding with a reconciliation, rather than a silent choice of feed; (d) cost — the daily crisis-state update is mechanical.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: DARPA's ICEWS lineage and its POLECAT successor (above); IARPA's [FOCUS](https://www.iarpa.gov/index.php/research-programs/focus) program, aimed at improving analysts' ability to learn lessons from the past, is the closest program in intent. Commercial: Rhombus Power Guardian, Recorded Future's geopolitical modules, Predata (defunct), and the public forecasting platforms — [INFER](https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/faq-2022) at UMD's ARLIS with Cultivate Labs, and the RAND Forecasting Initiative that succeeded INFER and Georgetown CSET's Foretell ([RFI](https://www.randforecastinginitiative.org/)) — all of which score with Brier and are the right comparison class.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: the ladder's ordinality is a modeling assumption smuggled in as data, and cyber and space actions have no agreed rung — the model will confidently misplace them, which is why two ladder configs ship and disagreement between them is reported, not hidden. Media-coded event data undercounts closed societies, so the model is systematically blinder on DPRK than on India. Red lines: none acute — this is state-level dyadic data with no persons in it, enforced by an adapter that only admits actor codes at the state/organization level.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now. 6–7 in 12 months; this is the candidate where a published, independently checkable backtest number is realistically achievable within the period, and that number is the company's most valuable asset.

### C-ADV-3 — Belief Gap: modeling misperception as a measured quantity and picking the signal that closes it

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It estimates what the adversary currently believes about US resolve, measures how far that belief is from the truth, and then tests a menu of US signals to find which one most reliably corrects the belief — and which ones are most likely to be read as weakness.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "What does the PLA leadership currently believe about the probability of direct US military intervention in a Taiwan blockade scenario, and if the US executes signal S (a named carrier deployment, a specific arms sale, a specific statement), what is the probability the resulting posterior shift is in the *wrong* direction — i.e. read as weakness — in bands {<5% | 5–15% | 15–35% | >35%}?"
- **Mission and user:** NSC-supporting analysts at CIA's East Asia Mission Center and the Joint Staff J5's deterrence shop; adjacent, the former Global Engagement Center mission at State for the messaging-reception half. **Today** this is the "how will Beijing read it?" paragraph at the end of an options memo, written by one person, based on their read of Chinese commentary, with no distinction drawn between *what we intend to signal* and *what they will infer*. The displaced manual process is the entirely tacit inversion step — analysts reason forward from US intent, almost never backward from adversary priors.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is an adversary belief profile. State variables: `resolvePrior` (a Beta distribution over US willingness to intervene), `priorVariance` (how hedged the belief is), `signalHistoryDiscount` (how much past unfulfilled US warnings have devalued current ones — the crying-wolf stock), `costPerception` (their estimate of what the signal cost us — the whole informational content of a costly signal), `channelTrust` per source (official statement vs. deployment vs. leak). Knobs: **riskTolerance** maps to `priorVariance` — a risk-tolerant actor holds a tighter, more committed prior and is *harder* to move; **escalationPropensity** maps to the payoff asymmetry between conceding-when-strong and standing-firm-when-weak, which determines whether an ambiguous signal is resolved optimistically or pessimistically.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Bayesian belief update layered on a costly-signaling / cheap-talk game.** Posterior over resolve type via Bayes with a likelihood parameterized by signal cost (separating vs. pooling equilibrium determined by whether the signal cost exceeds the bluffing type's budget). Misperception is a *number*: KL(actor_belief ‖ ground_truth) and the signed resolve gap Δ = E[resolve_believed] − resolve_true. For each candidate signal s the kernel computes E[Δ_after | s], Var[Δ_after | s], and P(sign flip) — the misread probability. All closed-form or quadrature; seeded PRNG only for the type-draw and channel-noise realization.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over belief-state hypotheses (they believe we will intervene / won't / are undecided / are deliberately ambiguous). **Deception Detection judge** — because the adversary's *stated* belief is itself a signal and may be strategic misrepresentation; this judge scores whether observed rhetoric is better explained by genuine belief or by an attempt to manipulate our estimate of their belief. **Key Assumptions Check** on the mirror-imaging assumption, the single most common failure in this domain. **Devil's advocate** argues the adversary is not reasoning about resolve at all and is on a fixed timetable. **Premortem**: "the signal was sent, they escalated anyway — what did we get wrong about their prior?" Rubric axes: mirror-imaging risk, evidentiary basis for the prior, signal-cost estimation confidence, sign-flip sensitivity, falsifiability of the inferred belief.
- **The config pack:** `actors/pla-leadership.belief.json`, `actors/kremlin.belief.json`, `actors/irgc-supreme-leader-office.belief.json`; `signals/menu-taiwan.signals.yaml` (each signal carries an estimated cost and a channel); `kernels/nested-bayes-signaling.kernel.ts`; `rubric/misperception.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/prc-authoritative-media.adapter.ts` (People's Daily/PLA Daily commentary tiering — authoritative vs. nationalist noise, a real and non-trivial distinction); `thresholds/abstain.yaml` (abstain when no authoritative-tier source in the window); `bands/misread-probability.bands.json`.
- **Data required:** Unclassified: authoritative PRC media tiering, PLA Daily, Qiushi, Ministry of National Defense pressers, Chinese-language academic strategy journals, plus the doctrinal literature on systems confrontation and the 2020 *Science of Military Strategy*'s treatment of intelligentized warfare ([Georgetown SSR](https://gssr.georgetown.edu/the-forum/regions/asia/blinding-the-elephant-assessing-pla-systems-confrontation-and-the-fight-for-information-dominance/); [Army OE Enterprise](https://oe.t2com.army.mil/product/peoples-liberation-army-transitioning-from-informationized-to-intelligent-warfare-concepts/)). Genuinely needs classified: HUMINT and SIGINT on what leadership *actually* believes is the only direct evidence for the target quantity; unclassified work infers it from public commentary, which is exactly the strategic-misrepresentation problem the deception judge exists for. **Unclassified demo: yes but honestly caveated** — the demo shows the machinery and the historical backtest; the live version needs the classified channel.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest against the declassified record**, which is what makes this candidate scorable at all. Episodes where an adversary's real beliefs were later revealed by archives, memoirs, or captured documents: the Cuban Missile Crisis (Soviet estimate of US willingness to invade), Able Archer 83 (Soviet belief that the exercise was a real first-strike preparation), Kargil 1999 (Pakistani estimate of Indian and US responses), Iraq 2002–03 (Iraqi leadership's belief about invasion likelihood, documented in the Iraqi Perspectives Project), and Ukraine 2021–22 (Russian belief about Ukrainian and Western resolve). Scoring: as-of cutoff before the decision point, corpus restricted to what was publicly known then, and the model's inferred belief distribution scored against the later-revealed record with a log score and a directional-accuracy metric. Plus synthetic closed worlds where the true resolve parameter is set by construction, to measure calibration cleanly. Note honestly: n is small (perhaps 12–20 usable episodes), so confidence intervals on the skill estimate will be wide and the system must say so.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — makes misperception a reported number rather than an unquantified worry; (b) time-to-analysis and prediction — the signal menu is evaluated in one run instead of one-at-a-time in successive memos; (c) QA — this is the strongest QA candidate: the deception judge and the source-tiering adapter directly implement "identify discrepancies between sources and reconcile differences in analytic confidence," and the weakest-link confidence rule stops a well-evidenced kernel from laundering a poorly-evidenced prior into high confidence; (d) cost — modest.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: [IARPA REASON](https://www.iarpa.gov/images/PropsersDayPDFs/REASON/REASONTechnicalDescriptionfinal122222-1.pdf) is the closest in spirit — improving evidence and reasoning in draft analytic reports and helping evaluate alternative explanations, with Phase 1 running Oct 2023–Sep 2025 and Phase 2 Oct 2025–Mar 2027; REASON critiques drafts, this simulates beliefs, so they compose rather than compete. Commercial: nothing close. The academic incumbent is the costly-signaling literature itself; the nearest tooling is generic ACH software.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: the whole thing rests on an inferred prior, and if the prior is wrong the elegant Bayesian machinery propagates the error with false precision — which is why the abstention gate here is aggressive and the false-abstention rate must be measured, not assumed. Mirror-imaging is the named enemy and the Key Assumptions judge is the only structural defense. Red lines: modeling adversary *beliefs* to select US *signals* sits one step from influence operations; the structural boundary is that the output type is a probability band over adversary inference, and the signal menu is an input the customer supplies — the system never generates message content, and the domain adapter has no text-generation path to an external audience. It does not model US-person audiences at all; the adapter rejects any corpus with a domestic-audience tag.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 now (the kernel is standard but the belief-elicitation adapter does not exist). 5 in 12 months, limited by the small backtest episode count rather than by engineering.

### C-ADV-4 — Regime Survival Constraint: when domestic politics forecloses the deal

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It models the adversary leadership's domestic survival as a set of stocks that drain and refill — money, legitimacy, coercive capacity — and reports the probability that the leadership can politically afford to accept a given external deal by a given date.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "What is the probability that Iran's Supreme Leader authorizes acceptance of an agreement containing enrichment-cap term Y before 31 March 2027, in bands {<10% | 10–30% | 30–60% | >60%}, and what is the domestic condition under which that probability doubles?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA analysts supporting the negotiating team, State's Bureau of Intelligence and Research, and Treasury's sanctions-policy shop, who need to know whether the next tranche makes a deal more or less likely. **Today** the answer is a qualitative "hardliners are ascendant" narrative built from Persian media and expatriate commentary, with no mechanism connecting the sanctions instrument to the acceptance decision — so the recurring policy question "does more pressure help or hurt here?" is answered by prior, not by model.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is a regime-survival profile. State variables: `legitimacyStock` L, `coerciveCapacity` C, `economicBuffer` B (FX reserves plus discounted oil revenue), `protestIntensity` P, `elitCohesion` (winning-coalition defection risk), and `externalRallyValue` (how much domestic benefit an external confrontation currently buys). Knobs: **riskTolerance** sets τ, the survival-index threshold below which the leader's acceptance set opens — how degraded things must get before external concession becomes cheaper than domestic pain; **escalationPropensity** weights the rally-around-the-flag substitution, i.e. the leader's preference for buying legitimacy with external confrontation rather than external accommodation. Those two knobs produce genuinely opposed behaviors under identical pressure, which is exactly the analytic disagreement the customer wants resolved.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Coupled-ODE system dynamics with a selectorate decision layer.** dB/dt = revenue(exports, sanctions discount, price) − subsidies − repression cost; dP/dt = f(inflation, focal events, repression) − suppression(C); dL/dt = −g(P) + rally(externalConfrontation) − h(inflation). Survival index S = φ(L, C, B, eliteCohesion). Acceptance set opens when S < τ. Numerically integrated with fixed-step RK4 (deterministic), stochasticity entering only through seeded shock draws (oil price, focal event arrival as a Poisson process). Bands come from the ensemble of trajectories.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over acceptance-by-date bands. **Key Assumptions Check** on the sanctions-to-behavior transmission mechanism (the single most contested assumption in the entire sanctions literature — this judge's job is to make the assumption visible and require a falsifier). **Outside-In judge** scores exogenous factors the kernel omits (a succession event, a regional war). **Devil's advocate** argues the rally branch: pressure entrenches rather than softens. **Premortem** on the "deal signed, we said <10%" outcome. **Red team** may force a rerun with the oil-revenue estimate replaced by the low-end independent estimate. Rubric axes: transmission-mechanism evidence, parameter observability, exogenous-shock coverage, falsifier quality, sensitivity to the oil-revenue input.
- **The config pack:** `actors/iran-supreme-leader.regime.json`, `actors/irgc-economic-bloc.regime.json`, `actors/pragmatist-faction.regime.json`; `kernels/regime-survival-sd.kernel.ts` + `kernels/params/iran-2026.yaml`; `bands/deal-acceptance-by-date.bands.json`; `rubric/coercive-bargaining.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/sanctions-ofac.adapter.ts` (parses Federal Register and Treasury press releases into designation counts by sector); `adapters/acled-protest.adapter.ts`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml`. A near-identical config pack instantiates the **coercive-bargaining / detainee-exchange** variant — same kernel, different action set — which is a cheap second product from one code path.
- **Data required:** Unclassified and abundant. Treasury/OFAC designations including the February 2026 action against 30+ individuals, entities and vessels tied to Iran's shadow fleet and missile-production networks ([State Department release](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/sanctions-to-combat-illicit-traders-of-iranian-oil-and-the-shadow-fleet)) and the Treasury action against the Shamkhani-linked shipping network ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0562)); export-volume estimates from commercial trackers (Kpler, TankerTrackers, Vortexa) noting the reported floor of roughly 1 mbd that pressure has not broken through; ACLED protest event data; rial black-market rate series; IMF/World Bank macro; Iranian budget documents. Classified adds elite-cohesion and succession intelligence, which is where the model is genuinely blind. **Unclassified demo: yes.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoffs on named episodes:** JCPOA negotiation 2013–2015 (cutoff Jan 2013 — did acceptance probability rise as the model says it should have?), the Nov 2019 fuel-price protests (predict repression vs. concession), the 2018–19 maximum-pressure period (the model must predict *non*-acceptance, which is the harder and more informative test), the Sep 2022 protest wave, and the 2025–26 pressure campaign. **Live post-cutoff:** the acceptance question is date-resolvable and is exactly the kind of question INFER and Metaculus already run, giving an external human-crowd baseline to compute Brier skill against — a far more credible comparison than a base rate. Metaculus publishes an aggregate Brier around 0.111 across its resolved history ([Metaculus FAQ](https://www.metaculus.com/faq/)), which sets the bar. **Flag honestly:** episode count is small and each episode is highly idiosyncratic; expect wide intervals and a real risk that the system should abstain on most of them — which, if it does, is a success of the abstention gate, not a failure of the product.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — turns "pressure is working" into a mechanism with a falsifier; (b) time-to-analysis — the "if we add tranche X" question is answered in a run rather than a two-week interagency paper; (c) QA — export-volume estimates from Kpler, Vortexa and TankerTrackers routinely disagree by hundreds of thousands of barrels per day, and this system makes that discrepancy an explicit reconciliation with a confidence consequence instead of a footnote; (d) cost — the monthly macro/designation refresh is automated.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: Treasury OFAC's own analytic shop and the intelligence community's economic analysis; no public program models the survival-constraint mechanism as a simulation. Commercial: Kharon, Sayari and Windward (sanctions-network and maritime analytics, no decision model — [Windward's OFAC shadow-fleet analysis](https://windward.ai/blog/ofac-targets-irans-shadow-fleet-and-weapons-networks/) is representative); Oxford Economics and Verisk Maplecroft for country-risk scores, which are scores, not simulations.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: system-dynamics models are notoriously easy to fit and impossible to validate — the parameter set has more freedom than the data constrains, and the honest mitigation is to freeze parameters before each backtest window and report out-of-sample only. Second failure: the model will be run on a country where the protest data is thin and will not know it — hence the abstention gate on data density. Red lines: this touches domestic populations of a foreign state; the enforcement is that the corpus adapter admits aggregate event counts only, never individual-level records, and the actor schema forbids natural persons as decision nodes (the Supreme Leader is modeled as an office with a role-payoff function, not as a person with a psychology — a deliberate and load-bearing distinction from C-ADV-10 below).
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 now. 5 in 12 months.

### C-ADV-5 — Technoeconomic Retaliation: what Beijing does back, and what it costs

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** When the US tightens an export control, this predicts which counter-control the adversary picks in response, how quickly, and what it does to prices and supply — by computing which retaliation hurts us most per unit of self-harm.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "If BIS publishes rule R adding N Chinese entities on 15 September, what is the probability MOFCOM announces a new or expanded export-licensing regime on a critical material within 180 days, in bands {<20% | 20–45% | 45–70% | >70%}; conditional on that, which material class is most likely, and what is the 90-day post-announcement price band?"
- **Mission and user:** Commerce/BIS policy and enforcement, Treasury, and the intelligence community's economic security analysts; adjacent, the National Security Council's technology-competition directorate. This is the customer's explicitly named requirement — "predict how adversary technoeconomic policy shapes their actions and strategies to better inform US response options." **Today** the retaliation forecast is a one-paragraph annex to a rule package, written by someone with a trade background reasoning from the last two episodes, and the *price and supply* consequence is estimated separately by a different office on a different timeline, if at all.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is a retaliation-selection profile for the adversary's economic-security apparatus. State variables: `chokepointPortfolio` (materials/components where the actor holds >50% global share, with concentration index), `selfHarmBudget` (domestic employment and revenue exposure per instrument), `inventoryCover` (months of buyer-side stock, which sets the delay before pain lands and therefore the perceived efficacy), `substitutionElasticity` per material, `signalingIntent` (whether the goal is deterrence-by-demonstration or actual denial), `wtoExposure`. Knobs: **riskTolerance** sets the minimum acceptable pain ratio (their pain to our pain) the actor will accept — a low-tolerance actor only fires when the ratio is very favorable; **escalationPropensity** shifts instrument breadth, from a narrow case-by-case licensing regime to a blanket ban, at equal expected effect.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Leontief input-output cascade on a bilateral trade-dependency network, plus chokepoint centrality, plus a partial-equilibrium price response, plus an argmax retaliation selector.** For each candidate instrument m: propagate the supply shock through the I-O matrix to compute ΔUS value-at-risk and ΔPRC value-at-risk with substitution elasticities and inventory buffers as damping; PainRatio(m) = ΔUS/ΔPRC; price response Δp = shock/ε with ε the short-run supply elasticity. The selector is a softmax over PainRatio with temperature set by riskTolerance, breadth chosen by escalationPropensity. Fully deterministic given a seed; the stochastic elements are the elasticity draws and the announcement-timing hazard.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over material classes. **Quality-of-Information judge** on trade-data provenance, which matters unusually here because Chinese customs reporting and mirror statistics diverge systematically. **Key Assumptions Check** on substitution elasticity — the parameter that determines whether a control is devastating or a nuisance and the one most often assumed rather than measured. **Devil's advocate** argues for no retaliation (absorb and accelerate indigenization). **Premortem** on the wrong-material outcome. **Red team** may force a rerun with inventory cover set to the pessimistic end. Rubric axes: chokepoint-share evidence, elasticity evidence, inventory-cover confidence, instrument-precedent match, price-band width honesty.
- **The config pack:** `actors/prc-mofcom.econ.json`, `actors/prc-ndrc.econ.json`; `kernels/io-cascade-retaliation.kernel.ts`; `data/io-matrix-bilateral.frozen.json` (versioned and hashed — the matrix *is* the model); `bands/counter-control-180d.bands.json`, `bands/price-90d.bands.json`; `rubric/technoeconomic.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/federal-register-bis.adapter.ts`, `adapters/mofcom-announcement.adapter.ts`, `adapters/comtrade.adapter.ts`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml` (abstain when chokepoint share is estimated from mirror statistics only).
- **Data required:** Entirely unclassified, which makes this the best *demo* candidate. UN Comtrade bilateral trade flows; USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries for share-of-global-production on gallium, germanium, graphite, rare earths, antimony; Federal Register BIS rule texts and Entity List additions; MOFCOM and GACC announcements in Chinese; company inventory disclosures; commodity price series. Classified access adds indigenization-progress intelligence and the internal Chinese deliberation record; useful, not required. **A credible unclassified demo is possible and would be the single most persuasive live demo in a bid — the model can be run publicly and the answer resolves publicly.** *(Note: I could not verify the 2025–26 specific rare-earth and gallium/germanium control timeline this session — the search budget was exhausted before that query ran. Treat the specific episode dates in the backtest set below as **UNVERIFIED** pending a citation pass.)*
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest with as-of cutoffs**, and this domain has an unusually dense episode set — each US control action and each Chinese counter-action is a dated public artifact. Candidate episodes: the October 2022 BIS advanced-computing rule → subsequent Chinese responses; the 2023 gallium and germanium licensing regime; the 2023 graphite controls; the 2024–25 antimony and rare-earth actions (**UNVERIFIED dates**). Cutoff at each US rule publication date, predict the counter-control class and timing. Then a **live post-cutoff** register of standing 180-day questions resolved by MOFCOM announcement — a public, dated, unambiguous resolution source, which is rare and valuable. Metrics: Brier and Brier skill vs. two baselines (no-retaliation-ever; always-retaliate-in-kind), coverage@3 on material class, and interval score on the price band. Because the price band and the categorical band resolve differently, they are scored separately and never pooled.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — a named material and a probability instead of "China will likely respond"; (b) time-to-analysis — the retaliation annex is produced with the rule package instead of after it, which is the difference between informing a decision and narrating it; (c) QA — Comtrade mirror-statistic discrepancies (China's reported exports vs. partners' reported imports) are surfaced as a reconciliation with an explicit confidence consequence; (d) cost — highest of any candidate here, because the repetitive work being displaced (rebuilding a dependency picture per rule package) is enormous and is done from scratch every time.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: BIS's own analysis and the interagency; no known simulation program. Commercial: Kharon and Sayari (entity-network mapping); Exiger and Interos (supply-chain risk graphs — closest to the I-O layer, but they score risk rather than simulate an adversary's choice); S&P Global and Wood Mackenzie for commodity modeling. **The gap is real: nobody named here models the adversary as a chooser.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: the I-O matrix is coarse and lags by years, so a chokepoint that emerged in the last 18 months is invisible; the fix is an explicit staleness field on the matrix that feeds the abstention gate rather than a silent extrapolation. Second: the model will overfit to the tit-for-tat pattern and miss a genuinely novel instrument class (an antitrust action, a rare-earth-free retaliation, a cyber response) — which is precisely what the devil's advocate and the dimensional-outlier detection exist to catch, and it is the case where the outlier scenario is the valuable one. Red lines: none acute — this is trade and policy data with no persons in it. Sanctions and export-control analysis is a core statutory function of the named users.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now (the platform runs; the I-O adapter does not exist). 6 in 12 months, with a real backtest number and live resolutions accruing.

### C-ADV-6 — Counter-COA: red-teaming our own plan and computing which indicator actually discriminates

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** You give it a draft US plan; it generates the adversary's best counter-moves as a branching tree with probabilities, and — the part that matters — computes which observable would actually tell you which branch you're on.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "For COA-2 in the current plan, what are the adversary's three most likely counter-COAs with branch probabilities, and for each pair of branches, which single collection-observable has the highest diagnostic value, measured as bits of mutual information with branch identity?"
- **Mission and user:** COCOM J5 planners and their J2 support, running the action–reaction–counteraction wargaming step of the joint planning process; adjacent, the service war colleges and the Army's Command and General Staff College, which ran AI-enabled wargaming exercises culminating in a November 2025 event ([Small Wars Journal](https://smallwarsjournal.com/2026/01/16/ai-enabled-wargaming-cgsc/)). **Today** this is a room of officers, a map, and a red cell, executing action–reaction–counteraction manually over days, where the red cell's quality depends entirely on who is in the chair and where the resulting indicator list is written by intuition rather than computed.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is a red-cell doctrinal profile. State variables: `forceAvailability` by domain, `warningTime` (adversary's assumed indications lead), `commandLatency` (decision-cycle length under the actor's doctrine — a PLA systems-confrontation profile assumes different C2 fragility than a Russian profile), `acceptableLoss`, `politicalConstraintSet` (what the leadership forbids regardless of military logic). Knobs: **riskTolerance** sets the softmax temperature β on branch selection — a risk-tolerant red cell picks high-variance branches; **escalationPropensity** sets the probability of a domain-crossing response (answering a maritime move with a cyber or space move).
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Expectiminimax over an adversarial game tree with chance nodes**, with adversary node values from a multi-attribute utility model and branch probabilities from softmax(β·U). Then, and this is the distinguishing computation, **mutual information I(observable; branch)** over the observable set, ranking every collectible signal by how many bits it buys you about which branch is live. That number is the Indicators & Warnings product, and unlike the branch probabilities it is *directly checkable after the fact*.
- **What the judge panel is:** The full red-team stack. **Team A/Team B** as two independently-configured red cells with different doctrinal profiles, scored on divergence. **Devil's advocacy** on the highest-probability branch. **Premortem** on plan failure. **Key Assumptions Check** on our own plan's assumptions (this is where the value is — the adversary model is a device for surfacing *our* assumptions). **Red team with standing to force a bounded rerun** when it identifies an unmodeled adversary option. The structured-analytic grounding is the Army's own [Applied Critical Thinking Handbook, formerly the Red Team Handbook](https://www.nsiteam.com/sma-publications/the-applied-critical-thinking-handbook-7-0), out of UFMCS, whose liberating structures and assumption-validation tools map almost one-to-one onto judge roles. Rubric axes: branch exhaustiveness, doctrinal fidelity, indicator diagnosticity (in bits), assumption exposure count, plan-vulnerability severity.
- **The config pack:** `actors/pla-eastern-theater.doctrine.json`, `actors/russia-western-md.doctrine.json`, `actors/irgc-qods.doctrine.json` — each a doctrinal profile compiling a strategic-culture logic into an attribute-weight vector; `kernels/expectiminimax-coa.kernel.ts`; `kernels/diagnosticity.ts` (the mutual-information computation, extracted as its own tiny module because it is the piece that needs a unit-test seam); `observables/collection-menu.yaml`; `rubric/coa-redteam.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/plan-ingest.adapter.ts`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml`.
- **Data required:** The plan itself is classified, always. The doctrinal profiles can be built entirely from unclassified doctrinal literature — the 2020 *Science of Military Strategy*, PLA systems-confrontation writing, published Russian and Iranian doctrine. **An unclassified demo is possible on a public scenario** (for instance, the publicly published CSIS blockade wargame, [Lights Out?](https://www.csis.org/analysis/lights-out-wargaming-blockade-taiwan), or RAND's [gray-zone coercion exercise](https://www.rand.org/pubs/conf_proceedings/CFA2065-1.html)), which lets a bid show the machinery without a facility.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** This is the candidate with the **weakest oracle, and it must be flagged**. The branch probabilities are not scorable — the plan is never executed, so there is no resolution event. Three partial oracles, in descending honesty: (1) **Indicator diagnosticity is scorable** — take historical episodes, compute the model's ranked indicator list as-of a cutoff, and check whether the top-ranked indicators actually preceded and discriminated the observed branch; that is a real, measurable claim and it should be the headline metric rather than branch accuracy. (2) **Synthetic closed worlds** with a known adversary utility function, where the correct branch distribution exists by construction and the abstention and calibration behavior can be measured cleanly. (3) **SME panel** on branch plausibility and on assumptions-surfaced count versus a human red cell on the same plan — an A/B against the manual process, which is the comparison the customer actually cares about, but it is a preference judgment, not ground truth. **Verdict: partially UNSCORABLE on its headline output.** Selling it on branch-probability accuracy would be dishonest; selling it on indicator diagnosticity and assumption-exposure rate is defensible and still valuable.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) efficiency — a red cell that runs in minutes against every COA, not just the favored one; (b) time-to-analysis — the days-long action–reaction–counteraction step compresses, and the I&W annex is computed rather than brainstormed; (c) QA — the two-red-cell Team A/Team B configuration produces a *measured* divergence and the calibration layer's z-score normalization removes the severity bias that makes one red cell chronically harsher than another, which is a real and unaddressed problem in manual wargaming; (d) cost — high, because red-cell time is scarce senior-officer time.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: this is the most crowded lane in the catalog. DIU/Scale AI's [Thunderforge](https://scale.com/blog/thunderforge-ai-for-american-defense) explicitly fields agents that critique war plans, run parallel analyses and flag weaknesses human planners miss ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/03/ai-for-war-plans-pentagon-innovation-shop-taps-scale-ai-to-build-thunderforge-prototype/)); the Air Force issued RFIs for an AI wargaming SaaS platform in August 2025 ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2025/08/13/air-force-wargaming-ai-saas-platform-rfi/)) and for AI for advanced wargaming in integrated force design in November 2025 ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2025/11/24/air-force-ai-for-advanced-wargaming-integrated-force-design-rfi/)); JHU APL stood up a GenWar Lab with a GenWar X cell building adversary personas, with a dedicated facility expected in 2026 ([APL](https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/251030-genwar-lab)); DARPA's SBIR on [improving battle planning through AI](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/improving-battle-planning-through-ai) targets reduced-order models for COA adjudication five orders of magnitude faster than real time; Army SBIR runs an [Automated Course of Action Generation](https://armysbir.army.mil/topics/automated-course-action-generation/) topic; Exia Labs announced a CRADA with West Point in February 2026 for AI red agents simulating near-peer adversaries ([Exia](https://blog.exialabs.com/p/exia-labs-announces-cooperative-r-2b7)). Commercial: Scale AI, Anduril and Microsoft on Thunderforge; [Hadean](https://hadean.com/news/hadean-signs-enterprise-lite-agreement-with-the-uk-ministry-of-defence/) with a UK MoD Enterprise Agreement Lite worth up to £20M and DASA-funded LLM-populated synthetic human terrain; Improbable, with roughly $10.4M of UK military work ([PC Games Insider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/71271/improbable-has-done-104m-of-work-for-uk-military/)). **This is the most contested candidate and the differentiator has to be the measured diagnosticity metric and the abstention gate, not the wargame itself.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: the tree explodes combinatorially and pruning decisions silently determine the answer; and there is a documented behavioral hazard — off-the-shelf language models exhibit escalation bias and arms-race dynamics in simulated wargames, including rare nuclear deployment ([Rivera et al., FAccT '24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03408)), and behave differently from expert humans in wargame simulations ([Lamparth et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03407)). The structural mitigation is that the LLM does not choose actions — the kernel does, from an explicit utility model — and the LLM only sets up and interprets. This is the strongest architectural argument in the whole bid and it should be made here. Red lines: the line between planning support and targeting support. Enforcement: the output schema has no target field, no coordinate field, and no weaponeering field; the domain adapter rejects any ingest containing target-list structures; the product is a probability distribution over adversary branches and a ranked collection list, and there is structurally no function from a run artifact to a fires recommendation.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now. 6 in 12 months on the unclassified demo path; the classified-plan-ingest path is gated on accreditation, not engineering, and 12 months is optimistic for that.

### C-ADV-7 — Coalition Cohesion: who defects, and when

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It simulates a multi-member alliance trying to agree on a response under time pressure, and reports the probability they reach consensus at each response tier by a deadline — plus the ranked list of who holds out.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Following a sub-Article-5 Russian action against a Baltic state, what is the probability that NATO reaches North Atlantic Council consensus on response tier T within 14 days, in bands {<20% | 20–50% | 50–80% | >80%}, and which three members are most likely to be the last holdouts?"
- **Mission and user:** CIA's Europe analysts and the intelligence support to the US mission to NATO; adjacent, EUCOM J2 and State's European Bureau. **Today** this is expert judgment from country analysts, aggregated in a meeting, with the aggregation step — how one analyst's read on Budapest combines with another's on Ankara into a consensus probability — done informally and without any model of the interaction between members.
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is one alliance-cohesion configuration; within a run, each *member* is an agent. State variables per member: `positionOnTier` (continuous, on the response-tier scale), `economicExposure` (energy and trade dependence on the adversary), `electoralProximity` (days to next national election), `publicOpinionSupport`, `bilateralInfluenceWeights` (the edge weights). System-level: `deadlinePressure`, `initiatorAnchor`. Knobs: **riskTolerance** sets members' willingness to absorb economic cost for collective action; **escalationPropensity** sets the initiator's opening tier, which anchors the whole negotiation and — in the model as in life — sometimes produces a *lower* final tier by overreaching.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Bounded-confidence opinion dynamics (a DeGroot/Hegselmann-Krause hybrid) on a weighted influence network, terminated by a unanimity-rule voting check.** Each member's position updates as a weighted average over neighbors whose positions lie within its confidence bound; members outside everyone's bound are structural holdouts. The consensus event is the first timestep at which all positions lie within ε of a tier boundary. Deterministic given a seed; stochasticity is in the initial-position draws and the exogenous shock arrivals (an election result, a gas cutoff).
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over consensus-tier bands. **Key Assumptions Check** on the influence-weight matrix, which is the model's most editorializing input. **Devil's advocate** argues that formal consensus is not the operative variable — that a coalition of the willing acts outside the alliance mechanism, which historically is often what happens and which the unanimity kernel structurally cannot represent unless configured to. **Premortem** on "consensus reached in 48 hours, we said <20%." **Outside-In** on US pressure as an exogenous forcing term. Rubric axes: influence-weight evidence, holdout identification accuracy, mechanism-appropriateness (formal consensus vs. coalition-of-willing), exogenous-shock coverage, deadline-sensitivity.
- **The config pack:** `actors/nato-members.roster.json` (32 member profiles), `actors/eu-council.roster.json`; `networks/influence-weights.matrix.json` (versioned, with a documented derivation); `kernels/bounded-confidence-consensus.kernel.ts`; `bands/consensus-by-deadline.bands.json`; `rubric/coalition.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/eu-council-decisions.adapter.ts`, `adapters/parlgov.adapter.ts`, `adapters/eurostat-energy.adapter.ts`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml`.
- **Data required:** Unclassified and excellent. EU Council decision records and sanctions-package adoption dates with named holdouts; ParlGov for government composition and coalition partners; Eurostat energy-dependence series; national election calendars; Eurobarometer and national polling. Classified adds leadership private positions, which is exactly the gap between stated and true reservation values. **Unclassified demo: yes, and with an unusually clean scoring story — see below.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Historical backtest on a dense, dated, public event series.** Every EU sanctions package from 2014 through 2026 is a resolvable consensus event with an adoption date, a final content tier, and — usually — a publicly identified holdout and a publicly identified concession. That is dozens of labeled instances, which is a far better n than any other candidate here. As-of cutoff at package proposal date; predict adoption-by-day-N and the holdout identity. Metrics: Brier and Brier skill vs. a persistence baseline (packages always pass, eventually) and a duration base rate; coverage@3 on holdout identity; interval score on adoption date. **Live post-cutoff**: the next package is always pending. Evaluation-regime tag `historical_backtest` for the 2014–2026 set, `live_post_cutoff` for pending ones, never pooled.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — a named holdout with a probability instead of "unity may fray"; (b) time-to-analysis — the country-by-country read is aggregated by a stated rule rather than by a meeting; (c) QA — the calibration layer's z-score normalization across judges directly addresses the real problem that some country analysts are chronically more pessimistic than others, and the dimensional-outlier detector identifies the member the consensus is underweighting on one axis, which is the analytically interesting output; (d) cost — moderate.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: NATO's own political analysis; no known simulation program. Commercial: Eurasia Group and Teneo (analyst judgment, no model); Predata (defunct); academic coalition-formation modeling has never been productized for this user. **Genuinely uncontested lane.**
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: the influence-weight matrix is where the analyst's priors enter, and a poorly-justified matrix produces a confident wrong answer — the mitigation is that the matrix ships as a config file with a documented derivation and a required provenance field per edge, so the assumption is auditable rather than buried. Second: bounded-confidence models are prone to spurious fragmentation; the model will over-predict deadlock. Red lines: this models allied governments, which is politically sensitive but analytically routine and legal; the enforcement is that member-state models are role/institution-level with no individual-politician psychology and no personal data, and the corpus adapter admits only public records.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 now. 6 in 12 months — the backtest data density makes this the second-fastest candidate to a defensible real-world number after C-ADV-2.

### C-ADV-8 — Tail Watch: rare-event estimation for nuclear and counterspace thresholds, where the honest answer is usually "insufficient evidence"

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** For the outcomes that have essentially never happened — a nuclear demonstration, a destructive attack on a satellite — it uses rare-event simulation to estimate how likely they are and, more usefully, to show the specific chain of steps that would lead there.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "Conditional on a Taiwan conflict reaching state S (blockade with US kinetic involvement), what is the probability of first destructive counterspace employment against a US national security satellite within 30 days, in bands {<0.5% | 0.5–2% | 2–8% | >8%} — and what is the modal path by which it happens?"
- **Mission and user:** NASIC and the National Space Intelligence Center, Space Force S2/S5, and STRATCOM's plans and policy shop; adjacent, the NIC's strategic-futures group. **Today** this is scenario narrative and expert elicitation — a Delphi round producing a range that nobody can defend and that is frequently just "low but not negligible." The manual process being displaced is the tail-reasoning itself, which humans do very badly and which naive Monte Carlo also does badly (a million runs of a crisis model will show you the tail a handful of times, if at all).
- **What the N simulants are:** Each simulant is a threshold-crossing profile. State variables: `attributionConfidence` (the adversary's belief that a counterspace act would be attributed — the central variable, since deniability is the whole appeal), `dependencyAsymmetry` (their space reliance vs. ours), `debrisSelfHarm` (Kessler exposure to their own constellation), `conventionalDesperation` (how badly the conventional fight is going, which is what actually drives threshold crossing), `nuclearSignallingLadderPosition`. Knobs: **riskTolerance** sets the tail transition-intensity prior; **escalationPropensity** sets the rung-*skip* probability — whether the actor climbs the ladder or jumps it, which is the single most consequential parameter in the entire model and the one about which we know least.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Rare-event Monte Carlo — subset simulation / multilevel splitting with importance sampling** applied to the same ladder CTMC as C-ADV-2 but conditioned into tail states, so the estimator has bounded relative error where naive sampling has none. Output is a tail probability *with an error bar that is itself estimated*, plus the dominant sample paths — the specific rung sequences that carry most of the tail probability mass. The path decomposition is the analytic product; the number is secondary and should be presented as such.
- **What the judge panel is:** This is the panel where the **abstention gate is the headline output**. ACH over tail bands, with INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE as a first-class and frequently-correct verdict. **High-Impact/Low-Probability judge** scores whether the path is coherent rather than whether the number is right. **Key Assumptions Check** on rung-skip, the parameter with no data. **Devil's advocate** argues the deterrence-holds null. **Premortem** written from the far side of the event. **Red team** with standing to force a rerun under a debris-tolerant adversary assumption. Rubric axes: path coherence, parameter-evidence sufficiency (expected to fail often, by design), analogy availability, abstention justification, error-bar honesty.
- **The config pack:** `actors/prc-strategic-support.tail.json`, `actors/russia-space-forces.tail.json`, `actors/dprk-strategic.tail.json`; `kernels/subset-simulation.kernel.ts`; `ladders/counterspace.ladder.json`, `ladders/nuclear-signalling.ladder.json`; `bands/tail-30d.bands.json`; `rubric/tail-risk.rubric.yaml`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml` — **set deliberately aggressive here**; `adapters/swf-counterspace.adapter.ts`.
- **Data required:** Unclassified: the Secure World Foundation's [2026 Global Counterspace Capabilities report](https://www.swfound.org/publications-and-reports/2026-global-counterspace-capabilities-report), which documents Russia's 2021 Nudol direct-ascent demonstration and Chinese DN-2/DN-3 reach to MEO and GEO; the Atlantic Council's January 2026 [Countering Russian escalation in space](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/countering-russian-escalation-in-space.pdf); reporting on the March 2026 Apollo Insight exercise, which put a nuclear-ASAT detonation scenario in focus, and on the Space Force's April 2025 *Space Warfighting: A Framework for Planners* (both via [Exterra](https://www.exterrajsc.com/p/the-wargame-that-writes-requirements) — **primary documents UNVERIFIED this session**); RAND's [Dangerous Thresholds](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monographs/MG614.html) and the [Denial Without Disaster](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2312-1.html) series on keeping a US–China Taiwan conflict under the nuclear threshold. Classified: essentially everything about actual adversary intent here. **Unclassified demo: possible but the least persuasive of the catalog, because the honest output is usually an abstention.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **This is the candidate where the honest answer is nearly UNSCORABLE on its headline quantity and that must be said in the first line of any pitch.** There is one historical instance of nuclear use and zero of wartime destructive counterspace employment; no forecast of a never-observed event can be scored by outcome. What *can* be measured, in descending value: (1) **Synthetic closed worlds** where the tail rate is set by construction — this measures whether the rare-event estimator is unbiased and whether the abstention gate fires correctly, and the existing platform already demonstrates 80/80 abstention on a provably-unanswerable arm, which is exactly the relevant capability. (2) **Near-miss surrogate calibration** on events that *did* happen and are rare-but-observed: the 2021 Nudol test, Russian co-orbital shadowing incidents, DPRK test-launch decisions, GPS jamming escalation. (3) **Path-coherence SME scoring** — not accuracy, a preference judgment, and must be labeled as such. **The product here is not a number; it is a defensible abstention plus a path decomposition.** Sold as a forecast it is indefensible; sold as the thing that tells you honestly when the question cannot be answered from available evidence, it is the strongest demonstration of the platform's differentiator.
- **Benefit categories hit:** (c) QA is the dominant one, and almost the only honest one — this candidate exists to demonstrate that the system knows what it does not know, which is precisely "improve quality assurance... reconcile differences in overall analytic confidence." (b) partial — the path decomposition accelerates scenario development. (a) and (d) are weak here and should not be claimed.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: STRATCOM and Space Force wargaming; RAND's escalation-management series (above); the DIA's *Challenges to Security in Space* (**UNVERIFIED primary link**). Commercial: nothing serious — this is a domain where consulting narrative substitutes for modeling. Academic: recent work finds frontier models exhibit sophisticated reasoning in simulated nuclear crises ([arXiv 2602.14740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14740)), which is interesting and also exactly the result that should not be over-read into a claim of predictive validity.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: importance sampling in a badly-specified model produces a precise estimate of a fictional quantity, and the rung-skip parameter has no empirical basis whatsoever. The mitigation is structural — the abstention gate runs before scoring, and this config sets its threshold so that most real queries abstain. Red lines: producing confident-sounding nuclear-use probabilities for a policy audience is itself a hazard, because a number that survives one briefing becomes a fact in the next. Structural enforcement: the output schema forbids emitting a point probability for any nuclear band — only intervals with the sampling error attached — and every product carries the evaluation-regime tag, which for this candidate will read `synthetic_oracle` and cannot be swapped for `live` by configuration.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 3 now. 4 in 12 months, and it should be pitched as a research line rather than a fieldable product. Overselling this is the fastest way to lose credibility on the other nine.

### C-ADV-9 — Sustainment Ceiling: can the adversary actually afford the campaign they are choosing?

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** It models the adversary's defense-industrial pipeline as a production line with real lead times and bottlenecks, and reports how long they can sustain a chosen level of operations before the ammunition, missiles or refurbished vehicles run out.
- **The analyst question it answers:** "What is Russia's sustainable monthly output of 152mm artillery ammunition in Q2 2027, in bands {<200k | 200–400k | 400–700k | >700k rounds}, and what is the probability they can sustain a fires rate of R rounds/day for ≥180 consecutive days without drawing strategic reserves below X?"
- **Mission and user:** DIA and the service intelligence centers (NGIC for ground forces, NASIC for air and missile), plus CIA's military-capabilities analysts; adjacent, the Joint Staff J2 and allied targeting-policy staff. **Today** this is bottom-up estimation from satellite imagery counts, procurement documents, and defection reporting, assembled in a spreadsheet by a small team, revised quarterly, with the uncertainty expressed as a range whose derivation is not reproducible.
- **What the N simulants are:** This is the candidate where **simulant variation is thinnest and I will say so** — most of the variation is in exogenous parameters rather than in decision behavior, which makes it a weaker fit for a simulate-and-adjudicate platform than the others. What genuinely varies: `reserveDrawdownWillingness`, `civilianConversionRate` (how much civilian industrial capacity the leadership will requisition), `importSubstitutionSuccess` (machine-tool and component workarounds under sanctions), `laborMobilization`, `qualityTolerance` (accepting higher dud rates to raise throughput — a real and observable adversary choice). Knobs: **riskTolerance** maps to reserve-drawdown willingness; **escalationPropensity** maps to civilian-conversion aggressiveness. Those are real decisions with observable consequences, which rescues the candidate, but the kernel is doing more work than the simulants are.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **Queueing / production-network model with capacity constraints, lead times, and a learning curve.** Multi-stage pipeline with stage capacities, work-in-process buffers, and stochastic yield; Little's Law relating WIP to throughput; Wright's-law learning curve on unit cost and cycle time; bottleneck identification by shadow price. Coupled to a simple commitment decision: does the leadership commit to a campaign of duration D given sustainable rate R and reserve stock S? Deterministic integration with seeded yield draws.
- **What the judge panel is:** ACH over output bands. **Quality-of-Information judge** is the most important here, because the input estimates (imagery-derived counts, defector reporting, procurement-document inference) have wildly different reliability and are routinely averaged as if they did not. **Key Assumptions Check** on import-substitution success, the most-assumed and least-evidenced parameter. **Devil's advocate** argues the high band from refurbishment stocks nobody has counted. **Premortem** on a large miss. **Structured Analogies** to historical mobilization episodes. Rubric axes: input-source reliability, bottleneck-identification evidence, substitution-assumption support, reserve-stock confidence, band-width honesty.
- **The config pack:** `actors/russia-mic.production.json`, `actors/prc-mic.production.json`, `actors/dprk-mic.production.json`; `kernels/production-queue.kernel.ts`; `pipelines/152mm.pipeline.json`, `pipelines/cruise-missile.pipeline.json`; `bands/monthly-output.bands.json`; `rubric/industrial-capacity.rubric.yaml`; `adapters/imagery-count.adapter.ts` (ingests counts, not imagery — a deliberate boundary), `adapters/procurement-docs.adapter.ts`; `thresholds/abstain.yaml`.
- **Data required:** Unclassified is surprisingly strong for Russia: RUSI, IISS *Military Balance*, Ukrainian MoD and open-source-intelligence loss-tracking projects, commercial satellite imagery, Russian procurement portal documents, trade data on machine-tool and CNC imports, sanctions-evasion reporting. Classified adds direct production reporting. **Unclassified demo: yes for Russia; much weaker for DPRK.**
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **Backtest against later-revised consensus estimates**, and this oracle is weaker than it first appears and must be flagged. The "truth" for 2023 Russian shell production is not a measured fact — it is a 2025 consensus estimate that is itself contested and was revised repeatedly. So the honest framing is: as-of cutoff at quarter Q, forecast the band, score against the **consensus revised estimate at Q+8 quarters**, and label the evaluation regime as an **SME/analytic-consensus oracle, not ground truth**, in the report — the platform's regime tagging makes that label mandatory rather than optional, which is the point. Where hard truth exists — captured-lot serial-number analysis, which gives genuine production-rate estimates — it is a small but real ground-truth set and should be the primary scoring set even though n is small. Metrics: interval score and coverage on the bands, plus a directional-accuracy score on the revision (did the model move the same direction the consensus later moved?).
- **Benefit categories hit:** (a) accuracy — the bottleneck is identified by shadow price rather than by argument; (b) time-to-analysis — the quarterly re-estimate becomes a re-run; (c) QA — reconciling the systematically-divergent RUSI, IISS and Ukrainian-MoD estimates is exactly the stated QA benefit, and the weakest-link confidence rule stops a well-modeled pipeline from inheriting confidence it has not earned from a single-sourced reserve-stock number; (d) cost — high, because the manual spreadsheet rebuild is the definition of a repetitive analysis task.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** Government: DIA and the service intelligence centers do this today with in-house tooling. Commercial: IISS and RUSI (published estimates, not models); Janes (capability databases); Govini and Exiger for US-side industrial-base analytics, which is the mirror-image product and shows the market exists.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: production models are only as good as the bottleneck hypothesis, and a wrong bottleneck produces a confident wrong ceiling; the shadow-price output at least makes the hypothesis explicit and falsifiable. Second: refurbishment versus new-build is chronically conflated in open sources and the model will inherit that conflation. Red lines: none acute; this is capability analysis on foreign state industry with no persons involved. Note the boundary deliberately drawn in the config: the adapter ingests *counts*, not imagery, so no imagery-exploitation or targeting capability is created inside this system.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** 4 now (kernel family is mature and well-understood). 6 in 12 months for the Russia instance. **Weakest simulant-variation story in the catalog — recommend building it only if a capability-analysis customer asks for it specifically.**

### C-ADV-10 — [BAD IDEA] Leader Digital Twin: a simulated Xi, Putin or Khamenei you can ask questions

- **Pitch (1 sentence):** A high-fidelity personality model of a named foreign leader — trained on their speeches, writings, biography and reported behavior — that analysts can query directly: "what would he do if we did this?"
- **The analyst question it answers:** Ostensibly, "how would Xi Jinping personally respond to a US arms package of size X?" — **and this is the first tell that it is a bad idea, because that question is not resolvable against any quantity.** There is no band, no resolution event, no oracle. Every version of the question that *is* resolvable ("does the PRC announce Y within 90 days") is answered better by C-ADV-3 or C-ADV-5 without the personality layer.
- **Mission and user:** Every senior policymaker who has ever asked "but what does *he* want?" — which is precisely why this idea keeps getting funded and keeps failing. There is no office that can act on the output, because no product line accepts an unfalsifiable individual-psychology claim as evidence.
- **What the N simulants are:** Personality-trait variants — different weightings of risk attitude, ideological commitment, historical-grievance salience, health and mortality salience. **This is the disqualifying weakness: nothing meaningfully varies in a way that can be checked.** Two runs disagree, and there is no procedure — not backtest, not resolution, not SME consensus — that establishes which was right. The knobs (riskTolerance, escalationPropensity) become free parameters with no estimator, so the ensemble spread is an artifact of configuration, not a measurement of uncertainty. A system whose diversity metric is decoupled from any oracle is producing decoration.
- **What the deterministic kernel computes:** **NO KERNEL.** This is the core defect and it is not fixable by effort. There is no dynamical system, no equilibrium concept, and no estimable parameter set for an individual's decision psychology from the available evidence. Every attempt to name one reduces to a weighted-attribute score whose weights are unidentifiable — a scoring function dressed as a model. Whatever the LLM produces *is* the answer, with the seeded-PRNG determinism providing reproducibility of a number that means nothing. Reproducible nonsense is still nonsense; byte-identical artifact hashes are a property of the platform, not evidence of validity.
- **What the judge panel is:** Whatever panel is configured, it inherits the defect. Judges score narrative plausibility, which correlates with fluency rather than accuracy, and z-score normalization across judges removes severity bias from a set of judgments that had no ground truth to be severe about. **The calibration layer would make the output look rigorous, which is worse than looking rough.**
- **The config pack:** `actors/<named-individual>.psyche.json` — and stop there. The platform's actor schema should **refuse to load this file**, because it requires a natural-person identifier as a decision node, which the schema forbids by design in every other candidate in this catalog.
- **Data required:** Speeches, writings, biography, reported behavior, health rumors, and — the moment it gets interesting to a customer — HUMINT on an individual. Which is where the legal problem starts.
- **How accuracy is MEASURED:** **UNSCORABLE.** There is no oracle. Not synthetic (you cannot construct a ground-truth psyche), not historical backtest (the counterfactual "what would he have done" has no resolution), not live (individual decisions are confounded with institutional ones and n=1 per decision), not SME (experts disagree irreconcilably and their disagreement has never been resolved by outcome). **For a customer whose stated benefit is "increase accuracy of insights and predictions," a capability that cannot be scored is not a weak candidate — it is a disqualified one.**
- **Benefit categories hit:** None defensibly. It would be *claimed* against (a) and (b), which is the failure mode: it produces confident, fluent, quotable output that feels like insight, accelerates the production of assessments, and has no relationship to accuracy. It is an efficiency gain in producing text, sold as an accuracy gain in producing judgment.
- **Prior art and incumbents:** The CIA's own Political Psychology tradition — the leadership-profiling analytic line — is the legitimate human ancestor and is explicitly *not* predictive at this granularity. Commercially, LLM "persona" products are everywhere and none has ever published a scored predictive result. JHU APL's [GenWar X](https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/251030-genwar-lab) builds *adversary personas* for alternative-futures gaming, which is the defensible version: personas as a divergence-generating device for exploring futures, explicitly not as forecasts. That distinction — persona-as-explorer versus persona-as-predictor — is exactly the line this candidate crosses.
- **Failure modes and red lines:** Technically: unfalsifiable, unidentifiable, and confidently wrong. Behaviorally worse: it invites anthropomorphic over-trust from senior consumers, and off-the-shelf models are already documented to escalate unpredictably in simulated strategic settings ([Rivera et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03408)) and to diverge from expert human behavior in wargames ([Lamparth et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03407)) — so the psyche layer would be inheriting a known model pathology and labeling it "Putin." Ethically and legally: it requires building a detailed behavioral model of a named living individual, which sits directly on the covert-action-support and targeting boundary and would be an audit finding waiting to happen. **Structural enforcement, and this is the constructive point of including the candidate: the actor schema accepts only organizational and role-level actors with role-payoff functions, the corpus adapter rejects documents tagged with natural-person identifiers, and the run would fail at config load. That refusal is a feature to demonstrate in a bid — "here is a thing our architecture will not let you build, and here is the line of code that stops it."** The closely related bad idea — an autonomous advisor recommending real-time responses during a live crisis — fails for a different reason worth naming: it removes the deliberation window that the entire judge-panel architecture exists to fill.
- **TRL now / TRL in 12 months:** N/A — TRL does not apply to a capability with no measurable performance parameter. Any TRL number attached to this is itself evidence of the problem. **Recommendation: do not build. Carry it in the catalog as the floor, and as the demonstration that the platform's actor schema enforces a boundary rather than merely documenting one.**
